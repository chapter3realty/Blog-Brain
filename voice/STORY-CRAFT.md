# How to write a short story for a page

The skill, short. The research behind every line, with sources and grades, is in `reports/Short stories for retiree buyers.md` (2026-10-10). Read its eight weak-and-strong examples once; they teach more than this page.

## What a story can and cannot do

- People remember a story better than an explanation. That is well proven.
- A story moves what people plan to do a little. Plain numbers move what they believe more. So the facts go in plain sentences on the page, and the story helps the reader picture making the move.
- One false-sounding detail, a sales line or a rhetorical question inside the story makes readers trust it less.
- Never repeat "stories are 22 times more memorable" or anything about oxytocin. Neither holds up.

## Real story first

Look in `stories/stories.json` before writing anything. A real story is the strongest thing a page can have, for readers, for Google ("experience") and under the law. Tell it exactly as recorded. Add no detail. If it reports a money result, put the usual result for most buyers next to it (FTC 16 CFR 255.2). The company never acts in it; "an agent at Chapter3" can.

## Otherwise, a labelled example

**Example:** in bold starts it. Nothing in it says or suggests a client: no "our client", "we helped", no quotation marks, no photo of the people. This label is what makes it legal (FTC 16 CFR 255.0, 465.2). Without it, an invented story is a fake testimonial.

## The shape

One buyer or one couple, first names only. Five to eight sentences.

1. **Before:** their old life and the worry this page answers. Use a real worry: leaving the grandchildren, money lasting, the doctor, knowing no one, a house full of belongings, storms and insurance, stairs later in life, putting it off for years.
2. **But:** the thing that makes it hard.
3. **Therefore:** one small moment where they decide or act. That act is the point.
4. **After:** one concrete outcome the reader can picture: a date on a calendar, a card in a wallet, a spare bed made up. Stop there.

Then, outside the story, one plain sentence on what it means for the reader.

Write the ending first, then open on its opposite.

## The checklist

- Each sentence follows from the one before by "but" or "therefore". If only "and then" fits, it is a list, not a story.
- One detail the reader can picture.
- No named feelings ("nervous", "thrilled", "love it"). Show the act that proves it.
- Three numbers or fewer, rounded, about the people (an age, years in a house). Prices, taxes and fees go outside the story, from a verified row. Never a rate or a payment.
- Chapter3 appears once at most, as an agent doing a service the story bank records.
- Nothing about who lives somewhere or who a home suits. No "adult community", "active adults", "empty nesters", "people like you" (24 CFR 100.75, 100.306).
- No promise: no "never worry", no savings, no value gain.
- Every detail fits the place and the season.
- No metaphor. Only people act.
- Each sentence would mislead no one if an answer engine quoted it alone. Keep local facts out of the story.
- Not another page's story with a new name.

## The difference, in one pair

Weak: "Many retirees worry about leaving their family when they move south. Tom and Diane felt the same way. They were nervous, but in the end they knew it was the right decision. Now they love their new life at the beach."

Strong: "**Example:** For twelve years, Tom and Diane lived ten minutes from their grandchildren in Pennsylvania. Moving to the beach meant the end of Sunday dinners at their house. But they had wanted to retire near the ocean for most of their lives. They decided the new house had to be a place the family would want to visit. They passed on a house with a bigger yard and chose one with a spare bedroom and a pull-out couch. Before they left Pennsylvania, Diane wrote the grandchildren's first visit on the new kitchen calendar."

After it: "If you want family to visit, look for a place for them to sleep before you look at the yard."
