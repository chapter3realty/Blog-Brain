# Outreach emails: how they are written

Signed by Devin Day, sent from info@. Every email is cold, so it gives something before it asks. The owner approves each draft in Gmail before it is sent.

## The method

From *How to Win Friends and Influence People* (Carnegie) and *Never Split the Difference* (Voss):

1. **Their interest first.** The first line is about their work, from something real they wrote or did. Never praise we did not mean, and never invent a detail ("I sent your page to a client" only if it happened).
2. **Say the hard part first (Voss's "accusation audit").** If they will think "another realtor wanting a link", say it before they do.
3. **Name what they feel (Voss's "label").** "It seems like...", "I'd guess your team...".
4. **Give the thing.** One fact they cannot get elsewhere, and the link. They should be able to use it without replying.
5. **Ask a question that is easy to answer "no" to (Voss).** "Would it be a bad idea if...?" "Is it a ridiculous idea to...?" A no feels safe, so people answer.
6. **Let the idea be theirs (Carnegie).** "If it fits your page" rather than "please link to us".
7. **Short.** Under 130 words. No attachments. One link.
8. **Never:** a payment, a gift or a link back for a link; "dofollow"; "guest post"; a fact the page does not prove; a rate or a payment amount; more than one follow-up.

## 1. Local reporter (the flood study)

Subject: 4 in 5 flood-zone homes for sale here aren't flagged

> Hi [first name],
>
> Your story on [their real story, one specific detail] was the clearest piece I've read on why some streets flood and others don't.
>
> You've probably heard buyers say "the listing never said flood zone." It turns out they have a point. We checked every home for sale in Horry County against FEMA's flood maps. Of [1,122] homes in the high-risk zone, only [229] were marked as flood-zone homes in the listing data.
>
> The full numbers are here: [link]. Use any of it, with a link back.
>
> Would it be a bad idea if I sent you one number like this each month?

## 2. Insurance agent (the flood study)

Subject: the call nobody wants to make before closing

> Hi [first name],
>
> I'd guess your office is often the first to tell a buyer the house is in a flood zone, sometimes a week before closing. That's a hard call to make.
>
> We checked every Horry County listing against FEMA's maps. About 4 in 5 homes in the high-risk zone aren't marked that way in the listing data. We wrote it up for buyers, with one line telling them to get an insurance quote before they offer: [link]
>
> If you keep a page for new buyers, it may fit there. And if anything on ours is off from what you see at the desk, I'd want to know.
