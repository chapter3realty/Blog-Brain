**Deciding fact (4c).** Can a buyer in South Carolina learn a house's flood history before closing, and from whom? Yes, in pieces, and the seller holds most of them. The state form asks about floods, flood claims, unclaimed flood repairs and federal flood aid, but only "during your ownership" (row 3). FEMA's claim history and the insurance claims report (CLUE) go only to the owner (rows 8, 9). The county's Florence flood map, its floodplain staff, its permits and its elevation certificates are public (rows 14 to 16). The page can be written as planned.

**Corrections to the live page found here.**
- "sellers of most homes must ... disclose known material problems, which includes known water intrusion and flooding": the statute's list does not name flooding (row 1); the commission's form does, and only for the seller's own years (row 3). Say what the form asks, and what it does not.
- "A CLUE report can show prior water/flood claims" (as if the buyer can pull it): only the owner, an insurer or a lender can (row 9). The buyer asks the seller for it.
- "A home can flood from a burst pipe ... none of which shows up on a flood-zone map": a burst pipe is not a flood for the form or for flood insurance. The form's question 20 covers fire, smoke and water problems, and asks separately about rising water (row 3). Keep the point about drainage and rain, which the county names (row 17), and drop the pipe.
- "Most agents do not check" and "our building study surfaces exactly this": no source. Cut both. Say what we check (rows 14 to 17) and what we ask the seller for (rows 8, 9).
