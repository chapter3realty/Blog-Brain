Batch 2026-10-d, Lane U. Researcher: one agent, 2026-11-01. Columns 9 and 10 are for the verifier. The writer may use rows marked `verified` only. Data files and scripts: `<website>/data/2026-10-d/sell-out-of-state-buyers/` (README.md there says how to rerun).

**Deciding fact (4c).** The page could only be honest if the county's records can tell an out-of-state buyer from a local one. They can. The Horry County parcel layer carries the owner's tax-bill mailing state, and its deed book and page let us confirm the current owner is the buyer on that deed. The pull worked: 30,339 deeds, 13,194 home sales, 12,125 matched to the buyer (row 1). The test of the live page's central claim came back against it: in the same building or subdivision, buyers with out-of-state addresses paid the same middle price as South Carolina buyers (row 7). The page must drop "they pay more".

**Corrections to the live page found here.**
- "Out-of-state buyers make the highest offers" and "why they pay more": not supported (row 7).
- "60%+ buyers relocate from out of state": no source; the county records give about 3 in 10 buyers with a tax bill sent to another state (row 2). Cut the 60 percent.
- "#2 fastest-growing US metro in migration": the metro is #2 of 387 by percent population growth, not by migration (row 10).
- "Once October arrives, buyer traffic ... drops sharply, and it doesn't return until the following February" and the Peak/Slow bars: recorded sales say otherwise. October 2025 had the most out-of-state closings of the year, and December was close behind (row 8).
- "Nassau median $680K+" is close but has no source; the Census figure is $684,700 (row 22). "Highest property taxes in the nation" (NJ): not checked, cut. "19M visitors", "230 sunny days", "more golf per capita", "Top 5 destination": not researched, cut; the page does not need them.
- The two buyer quotes ("Let's write at ask...") are invented. Cut.
