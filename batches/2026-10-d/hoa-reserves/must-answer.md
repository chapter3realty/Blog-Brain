## Who searches this

1. **A buyer looking at a Grand Strand condo**, often from out of state (New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, North Carolina; ledger row 26), often a second-home buyer or an investor. Someone told them to "check the reserves", or the lender asked about the building. They do not know what "percent funded" means, or that South Carolina sets no rule.
2. **An owner** whose board just raised dues or talked about a special assessment, and who wants to know whether the building has saved enough.
3. **A seller** whose buyer's lender is asking for the budget and the reserve study.

What they want first: is this building's savings healthy, in a number they can judge. What worries them: a five-figure bill after closing; a loan that will not close; a building that has put off the roof. What they ask next: what the study should show, what the law requires here, what the lender requires, and what happens if the money is short.

## Why they typed it

- "How much should an HOA have in reserves" is asked by a buyer holding a budget or a study who cannot tell whether $400,000 is a lot. They need a yardstick (percent funded and its bands) and a worked example that turns it into their own bill.
- "South Carolina HOA reserve requirements" is asked by someone who assumes there is a rule, often because Florida has one. The answer that the state sets none is the reason to read the building's own papers.
- "Fannie Mae 15 reserve" is asked by a buyer or seller whose lender mentioned it, or a board member. They need the date, what is measured, and that a building can pass it and still be short.
- "Buying a condo with low reserves" is asked by someone who has found the problem and wants to know whether to walk away. They need what it costs them, and what we would check before deciding.

## The must-answer list, in order

Each item names the ledger rows that answer it. A writer drafts only from rows marked `verified`.

1. **What reserves are, in one plain sentence, and how they differ from the operating budget and a special assessment.** Rows 2, 12. The answer to "operating fund vs reserve fund".
2. **The deciding local fact: South Carolina law does not require a condo or HOA to keep reserves, to fund them at any level, or to have a reserve study.** Row 1. In the body, near the top, not only in the FAQ (judge).
3. **What happens when the money is short: the owners pay the rest.** Row 3. One line, with the link to `/hoa/special-assessments/`, which owns the rest.
4. **How much is enough: percent funded, how it is figured, and the bands (weak, fair, strong) with what each means for a buyer.** Rows 8, 9, 10. The answer to rank 1. Name the source of the bands.
5. **A worked example a stranger can follow: one building, its big parts, what it has saved, what comes due in five years, and the bill per owner.** Rows 28, 29, 30. Labelled as an example. Includes the per-unit bill at 40 and 200 units (judge).
6. **The lender's test: at least 10 percent of the budget into reserves today, 15 percent for loan applications from January 4, 2027, and the reserve study route.** Rows 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17. And the trap: a building can pass the 10 percent line and still be 26 percent funded (row 31). The 15 percent date rests on Freddie Mac's bulletin, which says it is "in alignment with Fannie Mae"; Fannie Mae's own letter could not be opened (Not verified).
7. **Why it matters more here: how many condos for sale are in buildings 30 and 40 years old, and in elevator and oceanfront buildings.** Rows 24, 25. Our own count; no one else has it.
8. **What wears out and when (the parts a reserve pays for).** Row 28. HUD's lives.
9. **What other states require, against South Carolina.** Rows 18 to 23, 26. Florida, New Jersey, Maryland and Virginia, the states many Grand Strand buyers come from or know. Answers ranks 2 and 6.
10. **What a reserve study is, who prepares it, how often it should be updated, and what it must contain.** Rows 4, 5, 6, 7, 14. Answers ranks 3, 7 and 9.
11. **What the HOA fee tells you about reserves (very little).** Row 27. Answers rank 10 in one line.
12. **What we read before you make an offer, and the offer.** Rows 32, 7. The page does the work: never "ask the HOA" or "check the study".
