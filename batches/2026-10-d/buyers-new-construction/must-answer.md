## The reader

A person who wants a brand-new house on the Grand Strand. Often moving here from another state. Has toured or plans to tour a model home. Knows the builder names from signs and ads, not the towns or the rules. Worried about overpaying, about the builder's salesperson, about defects and about hidden costs. The next question after "should I buy new" is "how do I do it without getting burned".

Why they typed it (CRAFT 1): they have seen a model home or a "from $299,990" sign and want to know if it is a good deal, what the sign leaves out, and whether they need their own agent.

## What they would pay an expert to tell them

- Is new a better deal than a house a few years old, here, today?
- Where new houses are, and what they cost.
- What the price on the sign leaves out.
- What goes wrong with new houses here, and what the law gives them when it does.
- How to do it step by step, and how long it takes.

## The deciding fact

Does a new house here cost more than a nearly new one, for the same space, in the same place? The page cannot be written as a decision guide without it. Checked first (ledger row 5): no. A new house lists for about the same price per square foot as a house built 2016 to 2023 of similar size within a mile. Half list for less. The difference is in what the sign leaves out (the tax jump, the extras) and in the wait.
