## The reader

Two readers with one question.
1. **A buyer who wants to rent the home out**, by the night, by the season or by the year: an investor, a second-home buyer who plans to rent the weeks they are away, or a buyer who may move and keep the house. Often from out of state, where the rules differ (Florida protects current owners from new rental bans; South Carolina does not).
2. **An owner who already bought** and just heard the association may change its rules, or got a letter.

What they want first: can the association stop me, and how do I know before I buy. What they know: little; many think the city's rule is the only rule. What worries them: buying for a plan the papers forbid, and a ban voted in after they buy. What they ask next: how many homes here allow it, and which ones.

The reason behind the search: they are about to spend money on a plan that depends on renting, and they want to know who can say no.

## The deciding fact

**Yes. A South Carolina association can limit or ban renting, if the rule is in its recorded papers and clearly worded.** South Carolina law sets no cap on what the rule may say and no protection for owners who bought before a new rule (Florida has both). Courts here enforce a clear ban and read an unclear one in the owner's favor. Two gates decide every home: the city or county, and the association. Our count shows the association is the gate that closes most often. (Ledger rows 1 to 7, 10 to 20.)
