## The reader

Someone from another state (most often North Carolina, New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey or Virginia) who has decided Myrtle Beach is a real option and now wants to know what buying here is like. They have bought a home before, somewhere else. They do not know the towns, the tax classes, the flood maps or that a lawyer runs the closing. Many will buy without living here first. What they would pay an expert to tell them, in order:

1. Is now a good time to buy, or will I overpay? (The headline promises "a buyer's market"; the page must prove it with a number, or say where it is not true.)
2. What does a house or a condo cost here, and where does my budget go furthest?
3. What are the steps in South Carolina, how long, and when can I still walk away?
4. What will it cost me each year besides the mortgage?
5. What could go wrong here that would not go wrong where I live now?

## The deciding fact

The owner's headline says "it's a buyer's market". The page can keep that headline only if a public count shows it. The test is months of supply: homes for sale now divided by homes sold in a typical month. Six months or more is the level many in the trade call a buyer's market. The page must give this number from county deeds and today's listings, split by houses and condos, and say plainly where it is not a buyer's market (if a price band or kind is under that level). Result: facts 2 to 5.
