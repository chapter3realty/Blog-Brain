## Why they typed it

Three readers, one page:

1. **An owner who is behind on dues now**, or who just got a lien letter or a summons. This is the H1 reader ("Unpaid HOA dues"). The likely reason behind "can an hoa foreclose on a home that is paid off" and "can hoa put a lien on your house if you own it": they own the house outright or are current on the mortgage and cannot believe a small dues bill could cost them the house. Behind "what happens if you stop paying": a dispute with the board, and they are thinking of withholding. They want to know how long they have, what it will cost, and the one step that stops it.
2. **A buyer of a condo or a home in an HOA**, worried that the seller's unpaid dues will follow the unit, or that the building has many owners behind. They want to know who pays at closing and what to ask for.
3. **An investor or bargain hunter** who typed "horry county foreclosure list" or "condo foreclosures myrtle beach" and found that some sales are for HOA debts. They want to know what they are buying at an HOA sale (the mortgage stays on it).

What they already know: that HOAs charge dues and can send late notices. What they do not know: that South Carolina's HOA law says nothing about liens or foreclosure (the power comes from the recorded papers and, for condos, the Horizontal Property Act); that the lien sits behind an earlier mortgage; that the bill grows mostly from lawyers' fees; that once a default judgment is entered, the owner may get no notice of the sale; that paying after the gavel is too late; and how often this happens here.

Worry: losing the house over a small bill; a bill that keeps growing; the board's power; buying someone else's debt.

What they ask next: can you help me sell before the sale; can you check the unit before I close; is there a lawyer who handles this.

## The list, ranked

Every item needs an answer from a verified row, or it goes on the owner question list.

1. **Can an HOA foreclose on your house in South Carolina?** Yes. A condo association by statute (27-31-210); an HOA through its recorded covenants, enforced in court as an equitable lien (Bailey; Winrose). It must go to court: the master-in-equity, not a private sale. Rows 1 to 4.
2. **How often does it happen here?** Our count from the county's own sale list: 4 of the 19 cases on the November 2 sale were association debts, three of them condo units; the amounts. Horry County's share of the state's HOA complaints. Rows 18 to 21.
3. **What happens if I stop paying, and how fast?** The steps in order, each tied to its source: late charge and interest under the declaration, the recorded lien, the lawsuit and lis pendens, default, judgment, the advertised sale on the first Monday, the deed. A real South Carolina case's timeline in months. Rows 5 to 12.
4. **How does a small bill become a big one?** Winrose: $250 in dues became a $2,898.67 judgment; attorney fees eight times the dues. Rows 13, 14.
5. **Does the HOA lien come before my mortgage?** No: behind taxes and a mortgage recorded before the dues fell due. SC has no "super lien". What that means for an HOA sale: the buyer takes the house with the mortgage still on it. Rows 3, 4, 15, 16.
6. **What can I do if I am behind now?** Pay before the sale (the county says the case comes off the sale); after the gavel, the association can refuse your money (Wachesaw, a Murrells Inlet case). Answer the summons: a default means no further notice. Magistrate court for money disputes up to $7,500. The SC Bar's referral line and its $50 first half hour. Selling before the sale. Rows 10, 11, 22 to 25.
7. **Can I withhold dues because the HOA is not doing its job?** The condo statute says no owner can exempt himself by not using the common elements or by abandoning the unit; withholding starts the steps above. Row 26.
8. **Can an HOA put a lien on my house for fines?** The HOA Act is silent; it depends on whether the recorded declaration makes fines an assessment. Link to /hoa/violations-and-fines/. Row 27 (not verified beyond "the Act is silent").
9. **I am buying: will I inherit the seller's unpaid dues?** Condo: the buyer is liable with the seller, but only up to the statement the association issues on request (27-31-220); unpaid dues come out of the sale price first (27-31-200). What we check before closing (stories: `hoa-lien-flip`). Link to /hoa/estoppel-and-transfer-fees/. Rows 28 to 30.
10. **Do many owners behind on dues hurt a buyer's loan?** Fannie Mae: no more than 15 in 100 units 60 days late; an association suing an owner for dues does not by itself block the loan. Rows 31, 32.
11. **What happens to unpaid dues when a bank forecloses?** The bank's buyer does not owe the old dues; they are spread across every owner (27-31-210(b)). Row 33.
12. **Does bankruptcy wipe out HOA dues?** Dues that fall due after filing are not discharged while you still own the unit. Row 34.
13. **Is the law changing?** Three 2025-2026 bills (S. 366, H. 3447, H. 5204); none has become law; H. 3447 passed the House 110 to 2. Rows 35 to 37.
14. **Is there a right to buy it back after the sale (redemption)?** Not verified as a statute; the page says only what Wachesaw and the county show: paying after the sale was refused. Upset bids stay open 30 days only when a deficiency is demanded, and none of the four HOA cases demanded one. Rows 9, 11.

Not on the page: an interest rate (Winrose's declaration names one; the page says "interest" only), any address or owner from the sale list, a statute of limitations for HOA dues (not verified), the homestead exemption (not verified for HOA liens).
