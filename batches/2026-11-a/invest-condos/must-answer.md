## Who searched this, and why

An investor, often from out of state, is thinking about one condo on the Grand Strand, usually in an oceanfront building. Prices look low next to their home market. They want to know if a condo here makes money or loses it, and what could go wrong that they cannot see from a listing. They know condos have HOA fees. They do not know how high the fees run here, that a rental pays a higher tax rate, that many oceanfront buildings cannot get a normal loan, or how condo prices behaved in the last crash. They worry about a special assessment, a flood or a hurricane, and a manager who takes most of the rent. Next they will ask: "which building?" and "what would this one make?" That is the offer.

Why they typed it (CRAFT 1, "Ask why this person typed it"):
- "myrtle beach condo prices dropping", "market crash", "will prices go down": they have seen prices fall and want to know if it is a chance or a trap.
- "why are hoa fees so high in myrtle beach": they saw $600 to $900 a month on a listing and want to know what it buys and whether it is normal.
- "condos for sale under $200,000 / under $100,000": they want the cheapest way into a beach rental and want to know what the catch is.
- "best condo buildings": they know the building matters and want someone to tell them which.

## The must-answer list, in order

1. **The verdict for this reader, in the first two sentences.** Is a Myrtle Beach condo a good investment, and for whom? "Usually yes for X, no unless Y", with the conditions. Never "it depends" alone. (A)
2. **How condo prices behaved over time, and where they are now.** The 2006 to 2012 fall, how long recovery took, the change since the 2023 high, against houses over the same years. (C, I)
3. **What condos sell for, and what the cheap ones are.** The middle sale price in the county from deeds, how many sold under $200,000, oceanfront asking prices, North Myrtle Beach against Myrtle Beach. (B)
4. **The yearly cost of one typical oceanfront condo, in dollars:** HOA fee, property tax at the rental rate, the unit's own insurance, with parts named. No mortgage, rate or payment. (D, F, E)
5. **HOA fees: the middle fee, the range, why they are high here, and what they usually include.** (F)
6. **Taxes on a condo you rent out:** the 6 percent rule against the 4 percent home rate, in dollars on the typical condo; lodging taxes the guest pays. (D)
7. **Flood zone and insurance:** how many condos sold sit in a high-risk flood zone, what that means for a loan and for the building's policy. (E)
8. **Can I rent it nightly?** How many condos for sale allow short-term rentals; who owns condos here (how few are anyone's home). (H)
9. **Rental income: what one owner really made,** from the story bank, with its limits, and where to get a number for one unit (the analyzer). (H)
10. **Financing: why many oceanfront buildings cannot get a normal loan,** the rule that causes it, and how many condos for sale accept each loan type. (K)
11. **Which building:** the buildings with the most recorded sales this year, what we check in a building before an offer (dues, reserves, rental rules, loan status, flood zone, litigation). No "best" building. (G, L)
12. **Special assessments and a seller's unpaid dues:** who is liable under SC law, and the link to the special-assessments and reserves pages. (L)
13. **Condo or house:** a decision table on the facts above (price, value record, fees, tax, supply, financing, rental rules). (I)
14. **The good and the hard,** each from a verified row, each hard point with what we do about it. (J)
15. **The next step:** send us a building or a listing and we run the numbers and read the building's papers. (offer)

## The deciding fact (checked first)

Whether a condo here grows in value like a house does. If condo values track houses, the page is a "yes, pick the building" page. If they do not, the page must say a condo here is bought for income and a low entry price, not for growth, and the decision table has to show it. Result: see facts.md rows 1 to 3. Condos fell further than houses after 2006 and took longer to recover, grew less over 25 years, and have fallen about 11 percent since late 2023 while houses held.
