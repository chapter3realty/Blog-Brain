## The reader and their job

A person who has vacationed in Myrtle Beach and types "Myrtle Beach real estate", "Myrtle Beach condos for sale oceanfront" or "Myrtle Beach short term rental zoning map". Most want a condo they can rent some weeks or most of the year; a large second group wants a house to live in or to use as a second home. Both have heard "Myrtle Beach" as one place. They do not know that the city is a small part of what has a Myrtle Beach address, that the city decides nightly rentals by zone, or that condos and houses are two different markets moving in different directions.

**Their job, in one verb: choose.** A condo to rent, or a house to live in, and where in the city each one makes sense.

What they would pay an expert to tell them:
- which homes they can legally rent by the night, shown on a map, and how many of the homes for sale today qualify;
- what condos and houses really sell for, and whether prices are falling;
- whether a typical oceanfront condo pays its own way, in dollars;
- which part of the city to live in, and what each part is like;
- what owning costs each year (tax, flood), and what the HOA papers can hide.

## The deciding fact (checked first)

**Inside the city, nightly rental is a zoning question, and it splits the city by home type** (rows 1 to 5, 8). The city's text: no rentals under 90 days in any R zone except RMV; no conversions; a few dozen grandfathered houses. Our redraw of the city's own visitor-accommodation map on the current zoning lines gives the count: about 3 in 4 condos sit in a zone that allows nightly rentals; about 96 in 100 houses do not. Of the 350 houses and townhouses for sale inside the city today, 76 are in such a zone; of the 668 condos for sale in oceanfront buildings, 667 are.

The page can be written as planned. One limit: the map image is from April 2021 and the PDF and the code's use table were not readable today (facts, Not verified 1). The verifier must confirm C6 (The Market Common town center), MUM and the oceanfront PUDs in a browser. If C6 is not a visitor-accommodation district, row 8's house count drops from 389 to 58 and the finding gets stronger, not weaker.

## The findings no other site has

1. **Our map of where nightly rentals are legal in the city**, redrawn from the city's 2021 visitor-accommodation map onto the city's current zoning layer, with the count of homes and of homes for sale today in each class (rows 4, 5, 8, 9). The judge's first idea. Nobody publishes the count.
2. **Condos and houses are two markets** (rows 12 to 16): only 1 in 8 condos is someone's main home and 2 in 3 condo tax bills go out of state, while 3 in 4 houses are main homes. Condos have about 18 months of supply and Zillow's condo index is about 14 percent below its 2024 high; houses have about 4 months and are flat. That is the honest answer to "is the market dropping": for condos yes, for houses no.
3. **A Myrtle Beach address is usually not Myrtle Beach** (row 25): about 3 in 4 houses and buildings with the mailing address are outside the city; in 29579 (Carolina Forest) 280 of 21,216 are inside, in 29588 (Socastee) none. Fixes the judge's third problem (the live page put Carolina Forest and Socastee "in Myrtle Beach itself").
4. **The oceanfront buildings that trade most, from county deeds** (rows 18, 19): about 460 oceanfront condo resales in the city, middle price about $148,000, 3 in 4 buyers with an out-of-state tax bill; the ten busiest buildings with their sale counts and middle prices.
5. **A worked example on a real local case** (row 32): the typical oceanfront one-bedroom for sale inside the city (about $160,000) against the market's typical one-bedroom rental income (about $17,000), less running costs and the 6 percent tax: about $8,100 a year left for the HOA fee, insurance and any loan. No rate, no payment.
6. **Flood by home type and part of the city** (rows 20, 21): 1 in 4 condos in FEMA's high-risk zone (1,105 in the VE wave zone), 1 in 23 houses; the Meridian neighborhoods are the exception, 2 in 5.
