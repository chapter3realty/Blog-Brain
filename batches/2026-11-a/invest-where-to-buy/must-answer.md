## Who searched this, and why

An investor, often from out of state, who has decided to buy a rental "near Myrtle Beach" and does not yet know where. They may have one strategy in mind (Airbnb, a yearly lease, a furnished monthly rental) or they may be choosing between them. They know the beach and the name Myrtle Beach. They do not know that the Grand Strand is two counties, a dozen towns and a lot of unincorporated land, that each sets its own nightly-rental rule, or that the tax on a rental is about three times the tax on a home you live in. They worry about buying a place they cannot rent the way they planned, about paying a beach price for a yearly rent, about flood and insurance, and about a market already full of other investors. Next they ask: "what would a house there cost, and what would it rent for?" and "send me properties with analytics to prove it" (story `investor-common-question`). That is the offer.

Why they typed it (CRAFT 1, "Ask why this person typed it"):
- "best area to buy airbnb in myrtle beach", "where are airbnbs allowed in myrtle beach", "myrtle beach short term rental zoning map": they read that Myrtle Beach bans nightly rentals in neighborhoods and want to know where they can legally run one before they look at houses.
- "where to buy investment property in myrtle beach", "best area to buy rental property": they want someone to name the places and say why, with numbers, not "it depends".
- "is myrtle beach a good place to invest in real estate", "is myrtle beach a good investment": they want to know if the rent covers the price here at all.
- "north myrtle beach vs myrtle beach", "murrells inlet vs myrtle beach", "surfside beach vs garden city": they have two towns in mind and want the difference that matters to an owner.
- "horry county rental property tax", "conway sc tax rate": they found out the tax is higher on a rental and want the number by town.
- "myrtle beach off season", "off season monthly rentals", "airbnb occupancy rate": they want to know how many months a nightly rental really earns.
- "myrtle beach flood zone map", "is north myrtle beach in a flood zone": insurance cost by area.

## The must-answer list, in order

1. **The answer in the first two sentences.** Where should an investor buy here? It depends on one choice, so answer for each: nightly (North Myrtle Beach citywide, Surfside Beach's two districts, the oceanfront zones of Myrtle Beach, Garden City and the Georgetown resort districts, where the covenants allow), yearly (the inland areas where a year's rent is the largest share of the price: Longs, Socastee, Conway, Little River). Never "it depends" alone. (A, B)
2. **What a house and a condo cost in each area,** from recorded sales, not asking prices. (C)
3. **What each area rents for by the year,** and how big a year's rent is next to the price there. The deciding money fact. (D, B)
4. **Where nightly rentals are legal, area by area, with the rule's source,** and what "short-term" means in each place (under 90 days in Myrtle Beach, Conway and the state tax; under 30 days in Surfside Beach and Georgetown County). (A)
5. **The tax on a rental in each area, in dollars a year,** at the 6 percent rate, on that area's middle house. (H)
6. **How full of other investors each area already is:** the share of homes that are not anyone's legal residence, and the share whose tax bill goes out of state. (B, E)
7. **Flood: what share of homes in each area sits in FEMA's high-risk zone.** (G)
8. **How many homes for sale in each area say nightly rentals are allowed,** and why a listing's word is not enough (covenants, zoning). (A, C)
9. **Nightly income and the season:** what an average nightly rental grosses in each beach town, and how much a June month earns against a January month. (F)
10. **One worked example on a real local case:** the same budget as a yearly house inland and as a nightly condo at the beach, with the yearly money left before a loan. Never a payment or a rate. (B, F, H)
11. **Town against town:** the two or three pairs readers type (North Myrtle Beach against Myrtle Beach; Murrells Inlet against Pawleys Island; Surfside Beach against Garden City), answered by the scorecard. (E)
12. **Which county, and why it matters:** the county line runs through Garden City; Georgetown County has no business license and a 30-day rule. (A, H)
13. **Will the rules change?** The two state bills by number and where they stand, and North Myrtle Beach's workshops. (J)
14. **What we check before an offer:** zoning, the recorded covenants (some communities ban renting at all), the building's rules, the flood zone and the tax line, for the one address. (A, story `hoa-rental-bans-filtered`)
15. **The next step:** send a budget and a strategy, and we send back properties with the numbers. (offer; story `investor-common-question`)

## The deciding fact (checked first)

Two facts decide the page, and both hold:
- **Where nightly rentals are legal.** Each town sets its own rule; every rule was re-read at its primary source (ledger rows 1 to 12). Without it, "where to buy" for a nightly investor cannot be answered.
- **How big a year's rent is next to the price, by area.** If it were about the same everywhere, the page would be a rules page only. It is not: a year of HUD's 3-bedroom rent is about 9 percent of the middle house price in Longs and about 3 percent in Surfside Beach (rows 19 and 20). The page can name places by strategy with numbers.
