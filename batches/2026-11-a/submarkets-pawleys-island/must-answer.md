## The reader

A buyer with money to spend, usually from inside South Carolina or a nearby state, who has heard "Pawleys Island" all their life and is deciding where to buy a beach house or a place to retire on the south end of the Grand Strand. Some will rent it out part of the year. They know the name, not the map. They do not know that "Pawleys Island" on a mailing address mostly means the mainland, that the island itself is a small town of about 480 houses, or how rarely those houses come up for sale.

**Why they typed it.** "Is Pawleys Island expensive" and "why is Pawleys Island so cheap" are the same question: what do I get at my budget, island or mainland? "Is Pawleys Island an island" and "town limits map" mean: which addresses are the real island? "Flood zone", "erosion", "Hazel", "Ian" mean: will my house be there, and what will it cost to insure?

**What they would pay an expert to tell them:** how many island houses exist and how often one sells; what each part costs; what living there is like in winter; the flood, setback and rebuild rules that apply to the exact lot; what the tax bill is as a second home.

## The deciding fact (CRAFT 2)

"Pawleys Island" names two different things. The town on the island is about 480 houses, nearly all in FEMA's high-risk flood zones, most passed down in families; in the last five years about 1 in 11 was bought at a market price. Most homes with a Pawleys Island address are on the mainland, at a quarter of the island's price. Rows 1, 6, 9, 11 and 15 prove it. Verified before anything else: the town polygon, the parcel count and the sale field come from Georgetown County's own GIS.
