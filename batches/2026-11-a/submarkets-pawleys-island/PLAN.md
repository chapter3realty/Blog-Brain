## The reader and their job

A buyer with money who has known the name "Pawleys Island" for years, often from South Carolina or a nearby state, deciding whether to buy a beach house here or nearby, to use, to retire to, or to rent some weeks. They know the name, not the map.

**Their job, in one verb: decide.** Island or mainland, and if the island, which part and at what risk and cost.

What they would pay an expert to tell them:
- how many houses are on the island and how rarely one sells (so they know whether to wait and how fast to move);
- what each part costs: the island, Litchfield's ocean side, the mainland behind the island, west of US 17;
- what winter is like and who owns the houses (by where the bill goes, never by who lives there);
- flood zone, flood insurance cost, the state setback line, and what can be rebuilt after a storm;
- the yearly tax as a second home, in dollars;
- whether they can rent it and what guests pay in tax;
- the island's own rules: building size and height, golf carts, dogs, parking.

## The deciding fact (checked first)

"Pawleys Island" is two places. The Town of Pawleys Island is the island: about 480 houses, all but one in a FEMA high-risk flood zone, about 1 in 5 someone's legal residence, and only about 1 in 11 bought at a market price in five years. Most homes with a Pawleys Island address (ZIP 29585) are on the mainland, where a house sells for about $580,000 to $700,000 at the middle against about $2.45 million on the island. Rows 1, 3, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 13, 15. All from Georgetown County's own GIS, verified as reachable and complete (14,608 parcels; footprints pulled by object id, none skipped).

## The findings no other site has

1. **The count and the turnover** (rows 6, 7, 9, 41). About 480 houses; 10 market sales in the past year; at least 44 in five years; 12 new buildings in seven years. Litchfield and the mainland turn over about twice as fast. This answers the judge's first problem ("no count of how many homes are on the island or how few sell").
2. **The four-way price and ownership table** (rows 8, 10 to 14, 18, 39, 40): island, Litchfield ocean side, mainland behind the island, west of US 17, with DeBordieu for reference. Houses, middle sale, share that is someone's legal residence, share bought at market in 5 years, share in a high-risk flood zone. Nobody has published this split.
3. **Island houses stay in families** (row 40): 6 in 10 last changed hands by a deed under $1,000, against 1 in 3 west of US 17. With the town's own line on third-to-fifth-generation owners.
4. **Pawleys is South Carolina's beach** (row 39): the tax bills go to Columbia, Greenville, Charleston, Mount Pleasant and Florence; only 3 in 10 leave the state (Garden City's ocean side: 57 percent, its own page).
5. **The setback line runs through about 100 oceanfront houses** (rows 21 to 23): a map we drew from the state's 2018 lines and the county's 2024 footprints, plus the rebuild rules in plain words. The deciding fact for an oceanfront buyer, and on no other site.
6. **Flood insurance on the island, from FEMA's own policy file** (row 19): about $4,000 a year at the middle; about $4,800 on a second home; about $1,050 on the mainland; and the $250,000 coverage cap against a $2.45 million house.
7. **Every storm within 50 miles since 1950** (row 26): 44 storms, 7 hurricanes, with Hazel's toll on the south end from the 1972 National Register form (row 27).
8. **The tax bill in dollars** (rows 30 to 33): $2.45 million island house about $11,200 a year as a legal residence and about $34,900 as a second home; the town levies no property tax of its own.
