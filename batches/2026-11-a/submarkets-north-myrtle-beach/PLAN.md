## The reader and their job

A person who has vacationed on the north end of the Grand Strand and is now deciding whether to buy there: a home to live in, a second home, or a house to rent by the week. They have heard "Cherry Grove" and "Ocean Drive" and do not know what separates them, or why one house costs twice another a mile away. They do not know North Myrtle Beach is its own city. Their job: **pick the part of North Myrtle Beach that fits how they will use the home, and know what it will cost to own there.**

What they want first: what each part is like and what a house costs there. What worries them: flooding and insurance, buying next to a row of weekly rentals (or, for an investor, a rental ban), and whether the city is about to change its rental rules. What they ask next: the yearly tax as a home and as a rental, the golf cart and beach rules, the drive to Myrtle Beach and the airport.

The investor is a real but second reader. One section serves them with the rule in force, the parking-space rule, the taxes on a week's rent, and links to /invest/str-rules/, /invest/accommodations-tax/ and /invest/airbnb-income/. The live page was an investor report with a town guide bolted on; the judge called it out.

## The deciding fact (checked first)

**Where you buy inside North Myrtle Beach decides the price, the flood insurance and whether the neighbors live there all year, and the three move together.** Checked from county records before anything else, and it holds (rows 8, 10, 18, 35):
- Cherry Grove: 88 in 100 homes in FEMA's high-risk zone; about 1 in 4 houses someone's legal residence; middle resold house about $695,000.
- Across the waterway (Barefoot Resort and the newer neighborhoods): 2 homes in 5,193 in the zone; about 3 in 4 houses someone's legal residence; about $480,000.
- Crescent Beach, by the ocean: about $410,000, the lowest of the beach sections; 6 in 100 houses in the zone.

The second check, the rule the live page got wrong in its first line: nightly rentals are allowed everywhere in the city with a yearly business license, and no new rule has been adopted (rows 23, 25). The live subtitle's "licensing regime still in draft" goes.
