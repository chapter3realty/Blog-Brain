## The reader and their job

A person who has vacationed at Garden City Beach and now types "Garden City SC homes for sale", "Garden City condos" or "can you Airbnb in Garden City SC". Most want a second home or a condo they can rent some weeks; some want to live here. They do not know that Garden City is not a town, that it has two very different halves, or that the county line runs across the beach a short walk from the pier.

**Their job, in one verb: choose.** Which part of Garden City fits how they will use the home (rent it, live in it, keep a boat), and what that part costs to buy and to own.

What they would pay an expert to tell them:
- which side of Waccamaw Drive to buy on, and what each side costs;
- whether they can rent it nightly, and what the county takes;
- how quiet it is in winter;
- what the water does here, and what flood insurance costs;
- what is different at the Georgetown end.

## The deciding facts (checked first)

1. **Rentals are legal without a town ordinance** (rows 1, 20, 21, 22). Garden City is unincorporated. Horry County's own short-term rental chart lists four filings and no permit. The Georgetown end needs no business license. The HOA or condo rules still decide. One gap: the Horry zoning code itself was not readable (Municode blocks bots); see facts "Not verified". The page can say "Horry County has no short-term rental permit" now; "no zoning limit" waits for that check.
2. **The county line crosses the beach about a quarter mile south of the pier, and most of Waccamaw Drive is in Georgetown County** (rows 2, 3). The live page said "a southern tip". It is about 3.2 of the road's 5 miles.

## The findings no other site has

1. **Who owns each side of Waccamaw Drive** (rows 7, 8; Horry parcels). Fewer than 1 in 10 ocean-side homes is anyone's main home (120 of 1,376 on the 4 percent rate); 57 percent of their tax bills go out of state. West of Business 17, about 3 in 4 houses are main homes. This is the honest answer to "how quiet is it in winter", and the judge's first idea.
2. **Census Day 2020 along Waccamaw Drive, both counties** (row 9): about 4 in 5 homes had no one living in them; about 2 in 3 were seasonal homes. The Georgetown end was quieter (1 in 6 lived in).
3. **What each part sold for, from county deeds** (rows 11 to 16): ocean-side condos about $320,000; houses on the marsh about $1.17 million; other houses east of Business 17 about $570,000; inland houses about $340,000. Replaces the county-wide condo median the judge flagged.
4. **Flood insurance on the strip costs about five times the rest of the ZIP** (row 29, OpenFEMA): about $3,260 a year for a house policy on the strip against about $620 elsewhere in 29576; a condo building's own policy about $17,240 a year, shared through dues.
5. **Our map of the two sides and the county line** (rows 2, 3, 6, 28): drawn from Census, county GIS and OpenStreetMap layers on a public-domain USGS aerial.
6. **High-tide flood days at the nearest tide gauge rose from about 1 or 2 a year to about 8** (row 36), and the Hugo record (up to 90 percent of homes, row 30).
