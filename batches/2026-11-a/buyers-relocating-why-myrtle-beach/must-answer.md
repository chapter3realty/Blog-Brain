## The reader

Someone in another state, often New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, North Carolina, Ohio or Maryland, who keeps hearing that "everyone" is moving to Myrtle Beach. Many have vacationed here. They are deciding whether to start looking seriously. They know the name and the beach. They do not know the county, the towns, the taxes, or that "Myrtle Beach" means about 60 miles of coast and inland towns.

What they want first: is it true, and why. What worries them: that the hype is a sales pitch; that it is overcrowded now; that people regret it and leave; that wages are low; that they are too late. What they ask next: what their own house would buy here, what it costs to live, which town.

Why they typed it (CRAFT 1): they are weighing a move against staying put, and want the reasons other people found, in numbers, before they spend a weekend looking. A seller in Long Island or Charlotte wants to know whether the math works for them. A retiree wants the tax answer. Someone who searched "why are people leaving Myrtle Beach" wants the honest other side before committing.

## The deciding fact

The page promises "four reasons, told with numbers" and that people are moving here. It can be written as planned only if the official counts show that many more households move into Horry County than leave, every year, and that the growth comes from movers rather than births. Checked first: ledger rows 1 to 6 (IRS: 15,075 households in, 9,143 out in the latest file, more in than out in all five years; Census: more deaths than births every year since 2020, so all of the growth is people moving in).
