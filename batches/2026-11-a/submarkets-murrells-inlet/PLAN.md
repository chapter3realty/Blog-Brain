## The reader and their job

A buyer, usually from out of state or from inland South Carolina, who has eaten on the MarshWalk or heard the name, and is now looking at homes with a "Murrells Inlet" address. Many want a home to live in all year or to retire to; some want a creek-front house with a dock; a few want a rental. They know the name, not the map, and they do not know that the address crosses a county line.

**Their job, in one verb: choose.** Which side of the county line, and which part: the Horry side's newer golf and pool neighborhoods, the Georgetown side's older streets near the MarshWalk, or a house on the creek.

What they would pay an expert to tell them:
- which county a house is in, and what that changes (tax bill, schools, rental rules), without a trip to two county websites;
- what a house really sells for on each side, not an aggregator's range;
- what a creek-front house costs, how rarely one sells, whether it can have a dock and whether there is water at the dock at low tide;
- which parts flood, and what flood insurance really costs;
- the yearly property tax in dollars, as a main home and as a second home;
- whether nightly renting beats a yearly lease, by house size;
- what a normal week is like: the MarshWalk, the boat landings, oysters in season, the state park beach, groceries, the hospital.

## The deciding fact (checked first)

"Murrells Inlet" is two places. The Census's Murrells Inlet is all in Georgetown County (row 1). The Murrells Inlet ZIP code crosses into Horry County (row 3), and about 7 in 10 of its homes outside Garden City are on the Horry side (row 4). Neither side is a town (row 2). The county line decides the tax bill (rows 15 to 17), the schools (rows 22, 23) and the rental filings (rows 24 to 26). This is the page's spine, and the judge's first problem ("the county line is the page's best idea ... buried in the 11th section, and there is no map").

## The findings no other site has

1. **Most "Murrells Inlet" homes are in Horry County** (rows 1, 3, 4). About 8,900 of about 12,600 homes outside Garden City; the Census's Murrells Inlet is all Georgetown. Our count from both counties' parcels. Every other site answers "which county" with one word, and half say the wrong one.
2. **Prices by side of the line, from the deeds** (rows 6 to 10). Horry side house about $450,000 (396 sales); Georgetown west of the bypass about $570,000 (113); between the roads about $530,000; condos about $275,000 on both sides. Replaces the "mid $330,000s to $445,000" aggregator band (judge's problem 4).
3. **The creek-front house, counted** (row 9). About 220 houses back onto the marsh, all on the Georgetown side, and about 7 sell a year, at about $930,000 at the middle, about 1.6 times a house west of the bypass.
4. **The same house, two tax bills** (rows 14 to 17). A $450,000 house costs about $560 a year more on the Georgetown side as a main home and about $1,210 more as a second home.
5. **Flood follows Business 17, not the county line** (rows 18 to 21). About 2 in 100 homes west of the bypass, about 1 in 3 between the roads, about 6 in 7 on the creek side. And the real flood insurance bill: about $590 a year at the middle for a house, about $1,080 in a high-risk zone (FEMA's own policy file).
6. **Airbnb vs a yearly lease, by house size** (rows 27 to 29). A 3-bedroom grosses about $5,600 a year more nightly than HUD's fair rent, before cleaning, management and furniture; a 4-bedroom about $25,000 more. Settles the judge's problem 2 (the page contradicted itself) with one comparison.
7. **The dock and the tide** (rows 32, 33, 35). The state's dock rule in plain words (one dock a lot, 75 feet of marsh frontage on a newer lot, deck size by creek width, rebuild within five years), the inlet's 4-foot tide against 2.3 feet on the river, and how rare a listed dock is (9 of 465 for sale). Answers the judge's dock-checklist idea.
8. **Oysters in season** (row 36). Two state shellfish grounds and one public ground in the inlet, open October to May with a $15 saltwater license.
