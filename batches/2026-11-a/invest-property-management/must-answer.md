## Who searched this, and why

Three readers type the same words.

1. **The owner who lives far away.** They own, or are about to buy, a house or condo on the Grand Strand and live in North Carolina, New York or Pennsylvania. They want the price of a manager, in percent and in dollars, and whether it is worth it. They do not know the local firms, the leasing fee, or what South Carolina makes a manager do. They worry about a manager who takes a share and does little, and about a tenant or guest damaging the place. Next they ask: "how do I fire one if it goes wrong?" and "does the rental still pay with the fee in?" (that is /invest/run-the-numbers/).
2. **The buyer of a vacation condo.** The oceanfront building has a rental desk. They want to know what the desk takes, what an outside manager takes, and who files the taxes.
3. **The local owner deciding to self-manage.** They live in Conway or Carolina Forest and want to know what they would save, and what the law makes them do if they run it themselves.

Why they typed it: "property management myrtle beach fees" is a price check before buying or before handing over a house. "Is property management worth it" is the owner weighing the fee against their time and distance. "How to fire a property manager" is an owner unhappy with the one they have. "South carolina property manager license" is half people checking a manager, half people who want to become one (the second group is not this page's reader; one line, no section).

## The must-answer list, in order

1. **What a manager costs here, in one number, and in dollars a year.** The monthly share of the rent on a long-term rental, the leasing fee, the renewal fee, from the fee schedules local firms publish; then what that comes to in a year on a typical 3-bedroom house. (B, H, I; judge: "the page never gives a number")
2. **What a vacation rental manager costs here.** The share of each booking, and what is and is not in it (cleaning, repairs). One value with the site's other pages. (C)
3. **Is a manager worth it, or should I manage it myself.** Decided by distance, kind of rental (long-term, mid-term, short-term, a building's rental program) and number of units. Who actually owns the rentals here and how far away they live. (D; judge idea: decision table)
4. **What a manager does, long-term against short-term.** Plain list of the work, so the reader knows what the fee buys. (G)
5. **What the law makes the owner do whether or not there is a manager.** The deposit is returned with an itemized list within 30 days, or the owner can owe three times the amount; the tenant must be told who the owner or agent is. Brief; /invest/landlord-rules/ owns the detail. (judge: law is most of the live page; cut to what decides)
6. **What the agreement must say, and how to get out of it.** Written agreement; how pay is set; automatic renewal only with a 30-day exit after the first term; renewal pay in underlined capitals on page one; no tie to a later sale listing. (E, K)
7. **Is my manager licensed, and where does the money sit.** Licence or broker licence required for anyone managing for a fee; the manager-in-charge and the trust account; deposits stay in trust until the lease ends; an owner managing their own home needs no licence. We check this for the reader (CRAFT: we do the work). (F)
8. **Who files the taxes on a short-term rental.** Whoever takes the payment; which charges are taxed (the nights, a required cleaning fee); the county hospitality fee inside and outside city limits; the business licence. (C; judge idea: worked booking)
9. **Are management fees tax deductible.** Yes, a rental expense (IRS Pub. 527). One line. (J)
10. **Local firms by town.** We name no firm with a verdict (CRAFT 3; PLAYBOOK). We give the count of firms whose fees we read, what they charge in a range, and the offer to introduce the managers we work with once we are working together (story `no-vendor-names`). (A)
11. **Evictions and extra charges.** Many managers handle evictions; some charge extra or by the hour (story `pm-eviction-fees`). One line in the fees section. (H)
12. **HOA management is a different job.** One FAQ line: this page is about a manager who rents out your home, not the company that runs an association. (G "for an hoa")

## Questions only the brokerage can answer (proposed answers; ask once)

- **The fee range in Chapter3's files for long-term managers.** Only five firms that serve Horry County publish their terms. The judge said: if too few publish, print the range in Chapter3's files as our figure. Proposed: print the published figures as they are (four of five at 10 percent of the rent; leasing fee from nothing to one month), and ask the owner whether that matches what our investors pay. If he gives a range, it goes beside the published one.
- **Do we introduce managers?** `no-vendor-names` says the brokerage works with management companies it can recommend once working with a customer. Proposed CTA: "Ask us which managers our investors use" (no name on the page, no referral talk).
- **What happens to the deposit and the bookings when an owner changes managers.** State law keeps deposits in trust until the lease ends; it does not say how they move to a new manager. Proposed: the page says we read the exit terms with the owner before signing, and leaves the transfer steps out.
