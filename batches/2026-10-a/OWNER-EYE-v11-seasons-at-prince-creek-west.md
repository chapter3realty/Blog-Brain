## What I would send back

1. **"About 10 minutes" to the beach is on the page seven times.**
   The hero tile, the gray line, "Where is Seasons?", the map, the compare intro, the compare card and the FAQ. The hospital time is in the hero tile, on the map and in the text under the map. The airport time is on the map and in its own paragraph.
   "A Publix grocery store is about 5 minutes away, and Tidelands Waccamaw Community Hospital is about 8." "The airport is about 25 minutes away by car." The map right above already says this. Cut the hospital and the airport from the text. Keep Publix in one line, since it is not on the map.
   In the compare intro, cut the beach time (see item 5).

2. **The four cards in "What is there to do" take a whole phone screen for a few words.**
   "Tennis courts" and "Bocce" are a word and an icon in a full-width card. "Outdoor pool / Next to the clubhouse" says what the sentence above the cards just said. "Indoor pool / For the cooler months" makes it sound like the indoor pool is only for winter.
   Put the four in a 2 by 2 grid like the hero tiles, titles only. If the ledger can say the indoor pool is open all year, say "Open all year" under it. If not, just the title.

3. **The flood section leaves two questions hanging.**
   "Outside the zone, a flood policy here usually costs about $600 a year." Why would I buy one outside the zone? Say what it means for me: "Outside the zone, flood insurance is your choice. A policy here usually costs about $600 a year."
   The story: "sees one corner inside the high-risk flood zone. If the house itself is in that zone, their lender will require flood insurance." So was it or not? It is an example, so pick one and say it: "The back of the house is inside that zone, so their lender will require flood insurance. That is a yearly cost they had not planned for." Then the body line and the story line are not the same sentence twice.

4. **The transfer fee is in percent.**
   "There is also a small transfer fee, no more than a quarter of one percent of the price." On a $500,000 house that reads like $1,250, and the ledger (row 40) says it is capped much lower. Give me dollars: "When you buy, you pay the HOA about $700, two months of dues, as a one-time fee." Then the transfer fee in dollars if the ledger supports today's cap. If it does not, say "a small transfer fee" and drop the percent.
   "One bill. Your HOA bill covers Seasons and the larger Prince Creek West area." A buyer does not know there is a larger area. Say what it means for them: "You pay one HOA bill. There is no second bill for the roads and the park outside Seasons." Only if the ledger supports that wording.

5. **The compare intro does not compare.**
   "Seasons is about 10 minutes from the beach, and its clubhouse has both an indoor and an outdoor pool." In a list where both Del Webbs are 4 minutes from the beach, the first thing it tells me is the part where Seasons is farther. Say what the table shows, good first: "Seasons usually sells for less than either Del Webb, and it is closer to the beach than Myrtle Trace."

6. **The golf cart card has a golf flag on it.** Same problem as the pickleball paddle last time. A buyer will think there is golf, and we cannot say yet whether there is. Use a cart icon.

7. **One FAQ still repeats the page word for word.**
   "Can you keep a golf cart in Seasons at Prince Creek West? Yes. Park it in your garage." The rules card says the same thing. Swap it for a question the page does not answer: "How old are the homes in Seasons?" The ledger has it (row 89, verified): built from 2006 to 2016. Put that in the homes section too. A buyer wants to know how old the roof and the air conditioner might be.
