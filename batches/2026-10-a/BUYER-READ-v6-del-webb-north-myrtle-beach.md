# Buyer read, v6: Del Webb North Myrtle Beach

I read this on a phone, top to bottom: ten screenshots and the text. I skipped the site menu. I am 62, from Ohio, and I have never bought in South Carolina. The black "Draft for review" bar sits on top of the end of the first paragraph and the first button on the first screen. I ignored it, because it is a preview bar.

## My notes, in the order I hit them

### 1. I don't know this word

- "Operations Officer" and "Broker-in-Charge" in the byline. I would call them "somebody at the company" and "the boss of the agents". I did not know if a person in operations knows anything about retiring.
- "Pulte". The page says "its builder is still selling" and then "The builder, Pulte". I know the name Del Webb. I did not know Pulte until the page said Pulte sells under the Del Webb name. It is explained, but late.
- "Amenity center" on the neighborhood map. The text says "clubhouse". I think it is the same building. I would call it the clubhouse.
- "Lot". I get it from context. I would say "the piece of land".
- "agreement with the builder" (a link in the 55+ section). I do not know what a city has to do with this. I would call it "a paper the city signed".
- "Tax levies", "deed records", "TIGER/Line", "Horry County GIS" (map captions and the Sources line). I did not stop for them, because they are small print. They are words I do not know.
- "Intracoastal Waterway" on the maps. I half know it. I would call it "the water behind the town".
- Footer: "DSCR financing", "STR analysis", "ROI", "1031 Exchange", "BRRRR Method", "Fix & Flip". I know none of these. See note 10.

### 2. Why are you telling me this?

- "A lender must require flood insurance only for a home inside that zone, under the federal flood insurance law. The zone is on the government's flood map." I only wanted to know if I need flood insurance. The answer is the line before it.
- "The new-home prices are what buyers paid at closing, not the builder's starting prices." This tells me how a number was made. What I wanted was the builder's price. I never get it.
- "That includes the city tax, because the neighborhood is inside North Myrtle Beach." Fine, short. But I did not know a city tax was a thing, so I stopped on it.
- "under the city's agreement with the builder" in the 55+ answer. I wanted "one of you must be 55". I got it, and then a reason I did not need.

### 3. This sounds like a report

- "The builder says" or "The builder lists" or "Pulte says" or "Pulte lists" shows up 6 times: "The builder says the homeowners association (HOA) fee includes lawn care", "The builder lists an indoor lap pool", "Pulte says its warranty covers the structure", "The builder also lists a TV package", "Yes, the builder says." Each time I felt the company telling me "don't blame us if the builder is wrong". It reads like a person covering themselves.
- "OpenStreetMap gives the drive times, with no traffic. FEMA policy data for this ZIP code gives the flood cost." That is a report sentence.
- "Map data: U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line; Horry County GIS." Report.
- "Sources: ... Read October 5, 2026. Educational only, not legal or tax advice. Builder prices change." Report and legal.
- Photo credits: "by JefferyGoldman, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons". Report.
- "Homes that owners resold here in the last year usually sold for about $535,000." This one I liked. It is how a person talks.

### 4. I skipped this

- The whole FAQ at the bottom. Every answer is something I already read higher up: finished or not, lawn care, hospital, city limits, who builds. Five questions, nothing new.
- The Photo Credits block.
- The Sources paragraph.
- The long footer, the "RESPA Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure", and the middle of the dark "Ready to make your next move" box.
- The 55+ paragraph about "Other people in the household can be younger, such as a husband or wife." I did not skip it. I read it because I was worried about it. I am saying so because it was the best two sentences on the page for me.

### 5. Now I want to know...

- After the first screen and short answer: "OK, what does a house cost, and what is the HOA fee?" The page does not answer that yet. It goes to the homes (2 to 4 bedrooms, garage, hurricane covers), then yard care.
- After the new homes section: "How big are they, and which one do I pick?" It says the smallest is about 2,200 square feet. It does not say the biggest, or how many designs. I have no design names, so the button "Tell us the design and the lot you like" asks me for things I do not have.
- After the yard care story: "How much is the HOA fee that pays for it?" Never answered. The page says an agent will get it. See note 8.
- After the clubhouse: "Is there a golf course?" The neighborhood map has a "Golf course" label right next to Del Webb. The text never says a word about golf. Is that a golf course I can use? I could not tell.
- After the location: "How far is the airport?" It says "about 35 minutes", but not which airport. I would guess Myrtle Beach.
- After North Myrtle Beach: "What is the town like in the summer, and is it loud and full?" The page says wide sand beach, beach houses, a fishing pier. That is three things. It did not answer the question.
- After the 55+ section: "Can my grandkids stay a week? Can my daughter move in if I get sick?" Not answered.
- After the price: "Why does a new house cost $100,000 more than a resale?" Not answered. Also, why is the tax example for a $700,000 house when the table says $635,000?
- After flood: "What does wind insurance cost? It is a hurricane place." I get a link: "Read what home insurance costs near the coast." That is not an answer. See note 8.
- After the table: "Why is Myrtle Trace $300,000 and this one $535,000?" The page does not say what I give up. It also does not tell me which one it would pick for me.
- Still being built: "About 400 homes built on about 500 lots." So about 100 more are coming. What is it like to live next to building? The page says "still selling" like it is good news. I wondered about the noise and trucks.

### 6. Too many numbers

- "Not yet. About 400 homes are built on about 500 lots, and the builder is still selling new houses there." Three, and it is the FAQ.
- "About $535,000" and "About $635,000", then "$700,000", then "$3,000", then "$600". That is five money numbers in one stretch (price section and flood section), and I could not tell which one was my number. The $700,000 house and the $635,000 average confused me.
- Kroger / Barefoot / airport: "Kroger is about 6 minutes away, and the Barefoot Landing shops about 9. The airport is the long drive, about 35 minutes." Three numbers in two lines. Hospital and ER lines add 10 minutes and a mile and a half. In the whole "Where is it" section there are five time numbers in text, and the map shows four of them again.
- "$535,000", "$635,000" and "$630,000" are exact. I would say "mid-500s" and "mid-600s". I would not say "five thirty-five".
- The comparison table has three numbers per community times four communities. It was fine as cards, because each card only had two.

### 7. Where is this?

Yes, I can picture most of it.

- First map: I see North Myrtle Beach on the water, with Del Webb a little inland, Kroger, Barefoot Landing, the beach and a hospital all around it with drive times. That helped a lot. I could tell it is not on the beach, but close.
- Second map: I see the neighborhood outline, the amenity center on the left, a golf course at the bottom left, US 17 on the bottom edge and the waterway at the top. That helped me know it is a closed-in neighborhood and the road is right there.
- What the maps did not give me: where North Myrtle Beach sits in South Carolina, how far from Myrtle Beach proper, how far from the North Carolina line. I come from Ohio and I have no picture of the coast.
- Where I wanted a picture and did not get one:
  - A house. There is not one photo of a home in Del Webb. The page says so itself: "These photos show the town around Del Webb, not the Del Webb homes."
  - The street, or the entrance.
  - The clubhouse. The four tiles under "What is in the clubhouse" are cartoon drawings. They look like clip art. They tell me what is inside, but I know it is not the real building.
  - The pool and the lake. I wanted the real ones.
- On a phone the "North Myrtle Beach" photo strip cuts off the second picture at the right edge, and the caption is cut too ("A lake in N..."). I did not know to swipe, so I only half saw the lake photo. When I did look at the text, the credit says "Prices Swamp Run". A swamp does not sound good to me, and I could not tell if it is near Del Webb.

### 8. That's not an answer

- "Does the HOA fee in Del Webb North Myrtle Beach include lawn care?" Answered yes. But how much is the fee? "One of our agents gets the current HOA fee." Ask someone.
- "Want the insurance cost before you sign?" The button says "WE HELP GET INSURANCE QUOTES IF YOU NEED THEM." That is a sentence, and I did not know if it is a button I press. And the cost for wind and the house itself is "Read what home insurance costs near the coast." Read something.
- "See how a home you live in is taxed in North Myrtle Beach and the rest of Horry County." Read something. But I did get "a little over $3,000 a year" above it, so I was mostly OK.
- "Tell us the design and the lot you like." I have not seen either. That is not an answer to what I should do next.
- "Do you need flood insurance?" It says "Usually not, because every lot ... is outside the high-risk flood zone." If every lot is outside, I wondered why it is "usually". That reads like a hedge.
- "What does a home cost?" The page gives me what people paid. It does not give me the builder's price, and it tells me so.

### 9. Why so many dates?

Three dates in all, and two of them are the same day: "Updated October 5, 2026" at the top, "Read October 5, 2026" in the Sources, and "December 2022" in a photo credit. I did not mind. I noticed the photo one only because "Prices Swamp Run (December 2022)" made no sense to me.

### 10. Where is the good part?

First screen: title "What is it like to live in Del Webb North Myrtle Beach?" and the line "New 55+ homes a mile from the beach." Then "a newer 55+ neighborhood of houses, about 4 minutes by car from the beach." Yes, that tells me what I get (a new house, close to the water) and that I can (55+). It starts with the good part. No rule or drawback before it. Good.

It does not tell me what it costs. Price is about two thirds of the way down. There is no drawback anywhere on the page. The only mildly bad thing is "The airport is the long drive."

The footer is the one place that did not feel like it was for me. It says "The Myrtle Beach investor-focused brokerage. DSCR financing, STR analysis, and condo-by-condo ROI data for serious real estate investors." I am retiring, not investing. For a second I thought I was on the wrong site.

The same goes for the buttons. There are five different ones ("SPEAK TO AN EXPERT", "TALK TO A SPECIALIZED AGENT", "WE HELP GET INSURANCE QUOTES IF YOU NEED THEM.", "CALL TO LEARN MORE", "TALK TO AN AGENT"). "Specialized agent" made me ask what a regular agent is.

The name "Del Webb North Myrtle Beach" is on the page 27 times. Every heading wraps to three lines on my phone. It did not stop me, but I felt it.

## What did you want to know first? Where did you find it?

First I wanted to know: can I live there, and is it a house? Found on the first screen. Both answered in the first two lines.

Right after that: what does it cost, and what is the monthly fee? Price: middle of the page (about two thirds down), with a table. Monthly fee: never.

## Three things I liked about this place

1. A house with someone else cutting the grass, and sprinklers, and a TV package, all in the fee.
2. The beach is 4 minutes away, and there is an emergency room open all hours a mile and a half away.
3. Every lot is outside the high-risk flood zone, so a bank does not make me buy flood insurance, and a 10-year warranty on the structure that goes to the next owner.

## Questions I still have, ranked by how much they matter

1. What is the HOA fee each month?
2. What does the builder ask for a new house, and what is the cheapest one?
3. What does homeowners insurance cost on the coast, with wind, for one of these houses?
4. What do the houses look like, and how do I see the real clubhouse and pool?
5. What is it like to live in a place that is still being built?
6. Is there a golf course, and is it part of this?
7. Which airport is "the long drive", and how do I get from Ohio?
8. Can grandchildren stay, and what happens when the 55+ person is gone?
9. Why is a new house $100,000 more than a resale?
10. Which of the four neighborhoods is the one for me?

## Would I call this company?

Maybe, but not first: the offer to read the HOA papers with me is the one thing the builder's own website cannot do, and I would call after I found the HOA fee and the builder price somewhere else.

## Did the story stick?

Yes, more than anything else on the page. After one pass I could tell it back:

- Gary and Lynn, from Dayton, Ohio. I am from Ohio, so that landed.
- Gary mowed the same big yard every Saturday morning for 31 years.
- Lynn did not want a condo. She wanted a garage for his tools and a yard for her flower pots.
- Their one rule: a house, with someone else paid to cut the grass.
- An agent read the HOA papers with them before they signed.
- First Saturday in the new house, Gary took his coffee to the beach at seven.

The coffee at the beach at seven is the part I will remember. It is the whole point of the section in one picture.

Things that did not stick or made me pause:

- It is labeled "Example:", so I wondered if it was a real couple or a made-up one. I did not know which.
- I never learned what the agent found in the HOA papers. The story says they were read, and then moves on.
- Lynn wanted "a yard for her flower pots". That seems at odds with the page saying the yard is taken care of. I understood it, but it made me stop.
- The last line, "put lawn care on your list before you look at floor plans", is useful and I took it as the point.

## Did the pictures and maps help me picture the place?

- Maps: yes. They are the best part of the page for placing it. The drive-time pins gave me the size of the area faster than the text did.
- Beach photos: some. The beach chairs and the Cherry Grove aerial gave me the feel of the beach. But they are not Del Webb, and the page says that.
- Clubhouse drawings: they told me what is inside. They did not help me picture the real place. They felt like a brochure icon set.
- No photo of a house, a street, the clubhouse, the pool or the entrance. For a page about "what is it like to live in" a neighborhood, I never saw the neighborhood.
- The lake photo was half cut off on the phone, so I got little from it.

## Grade for easy to read: 8 out of 10

Short sentences, headings that are questions I would ask, small paragraphs, plain words. It lost points for the repeated long name, the "the builder says" lines, a few words I did not know, and the footer talking to investors.
