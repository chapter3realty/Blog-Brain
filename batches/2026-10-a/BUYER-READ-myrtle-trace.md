# Buyer read: Myrtle Trace (Conway, 55+)

Read as a 62-year-old from Ohio who has never bought in South Carolina.

## Notes as I read

- **This sounds like a report.** "By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge". I don't know who these people are. I skipped it.
- **I skipped this.** "Let us make it simple" and "The short answer". Two labels before I get to anything. I scrolled past both.
- **This sounds like a report.** "The HOA's activities calendar listed about 70 entries a month in fall 2026." A calendar entry count is not something I care about. I want to know if there is stuff to do. It is repeated again in the next section ("about 67 entries in September 2026 and about 71 in October") and a third time in the table and the FAQ. Four times in one page.

## Answers

### What did you want to know first? Where did I find it?

First: "Can I live here at 62, and is it a nice place?" The age answer is in the fifth section and in the FAQ, about the middle of the page. The "is it a nice place" answer is only partly there: a pool, a clubhouse, groups and leagues, in the second section. I never got a feel for the homes or the neighbors. I never found out what the houses look like, how big they are, or what they sell for. "What a house costs" is never answered, not even a range. For me, the page covers what it costs to live here, not what it costs to buy here.

Second: "What does it cost per month, all in?" I got most of it on the first screen: $95 dues, $1,103 tax a year. Insurance is missing.

### Three things I liked

1. The numbers are plain and they are low for me: $95 a month, about $1,103 a year in tax on $300,000, and a hospital less than a mile away.
2. Diane's example. It showed what one buyer pays in the first year, with a number for each piece, and the second-home number.
3. The activities: pool, bocce, shuffleboard, line dancing, bingo, coffee klatch. It sounds like people there do things together.

### Questions I still have, ranked

1. What do homes sell for, and what do they look like (size, bedrooms, garage, age)?
2. What is my total monthly cost with home insurance, and what do I pay for flood and wind coverage if any?
3. Can my spouse stay if she is under 55 and I die or we divorce? Can an adult child live with me?
4. Can I keep my truck? What does "trucks in the garage" really mean?
5. What does the home sell for versus an Ohio house, and is the neighborhood well kept, with a stable board? (The board can raise dues 10 percent a year.)
6. Is it safe? Is it gated or not?
7. How far is the nearest grocery store and the closest doctor? How long to the beach in minutes?
8. Can I play the Burning Ridge golf course, and what does it cost?
9. What are the people like? How many are full-time and how many are snowbirds?
10. What happens on the day I call? Who calls me back? Is there a cost?

### Would I call this company?

Maybe, but not yet: the page gave me costs and rules and treated me like a man who already decided on Myrtle Trace, so I would want to see the homes and the neighborhood first, then call about the home I like.

## Five notes that matter most

1. The first screen leads with money and counts (dues, closing fees, calendar entries, tax), not with "yes, you can live here at 62, and here is what the place is like." The age answer is in section five. A home price or price range and a picture of the homes never appear.
2. Industry words with no plain meaning: "HOA dues", "resale buyer", "capital contribution", "certificate fee", "Zone X" (in the table before it is explained), "common land", "median", "homesites", "recorded", "Register of Deeds", "state's wind and hail insurance pool", "stormwater fee", "homestead exemption".
3. Report voice: "the activities calendar listed about 67 entries in September and about 71 in October" (said four times), "the HOA's 2023 history paper", "policies that began from June 2025 to May 2026 in the whole ZIP code", the legal quote of the age rule, and a Sources line about OpenStreetMap and FEMA data. These explain where facts came from when I only wanted what they mean.
4. Facts that are said without telling me whether they are good or bad for me: no management company, the 10 percent limit on dues increases, the home was built in 1983, the "no minimum" in the youngest-resident column, no gate at the front, and being outside the wind and hail pool. Several of the clearest things are only in the FAQ at the bottom (the gate, who maintains the yard), and the comparison table is hard to read without knowing what "youngest other resident" means.
5. Sections I would scroll past: the rental section (I am buying to live in), the list of small rules, the dated calendar details, the table of other communities, and the closing-cost bar text that ran together. The page asks me to "have us read the HOA papers" before I know whether I like the place, and the page never says what total monthly cost I should expect with insurance.
