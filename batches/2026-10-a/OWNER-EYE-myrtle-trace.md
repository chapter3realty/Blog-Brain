# Owner's eye: Myrtle Trace (v9)

Page: `/buyers/55-plus-communities/myrtle-trace/`. Read 2026-10-11 from the v9 phone screenshots (390 t00 to t10), the desktop screenshots (1280 t00 to t08) and the built page. Written as the owner, by an agent standing in for him. Prompt: `prompts/owner-eye.md`.

**Grade: 7 out of 10. It does not go to the owner yet.**

The top is fixed. There is no photo up there anymore, the icon row is useful, and the short answer gives me the fee, the age rule and what is close. The activity cards look good. The rules cards are easy to read on a phone. Three things keep it from a 9: the homes section has no picture at all while the picture of the homes sits in the wrong section, the story is weak and is not one of the last things, and the comparison leads with the bad.

## What I would send back

### 1. The homes section has no picture, and the picture of the homes is in the wrong place

- "What are the homes like?" is five paragraphs of text and nothing to look at. This is the section a buyer cares about most. I told you before that nobody can picture a house from words.
- The aerial photo ("Myrtle Trace from above in 2023, with its edge drawn in orange and the golf course next door") sits under "What is close to Myrtle Trace?", right after a sentence about the beach and the airport. It does not show the beach or the airport. It shows the neighborhood.
- What I want: move the aerial photo into "What are the homes like?" next to the sentence about the houses and the golf course. If we can get a licensed photo of the houses, a street or the clubhouse, put it there too.

### 2. Too many maps in one place

- On the phone, "What is close" is three pictures in a row: the aerial photo, the area map and the street map. That is almost three screens of maps.
- What I want: keep the area map there, because it shows the beach and the airport. Move the aerial photo to the homes section (item 1). Keep the street map only if it sits next to something it explains, like the hospital and Walmart.
- "Open Myrtle Trace in Google Maps" and "Get directions" do the same thing. Keep one.

### 3. The story is weak, and it is not one of the last things

- Right now the order is: rules, story, comparison table, FAQ. The comparison table is more facts after the story. I want the story after the comparison, just before the FAQ.
- The heading "How does a Chapter3 agent check the fence rules for a buyer with a dog?" is about us, and nobody types that into Google. Make it a question about the buyer, like "Can you fence a yard for a dog in Myrtle Trace?"
- There is no surprise left. The Fences card a screen above already says "None between yards or across the front, and none on lots that back onto the golf course or a pond. An approved dog run is allowed." The story then says the same rule again.
- The agent does not do anything skilled. "Her Chapter3 agent reads the HOA's rules with her and finds..." then "They do allow a dog run". The rules solve it, not the agent. And at the end Pat is the one with the tape measure. Show the agent's act turning the problem, before Pat has her heart set on the golf course house. Use only a service the story bank or an owner answer records.
- "The rules also allow no fence" and "They do allow a dog run": only people act. Say "No fence is allowed" and "A dog run is allowed once the HOA approves it."
- After the story, these two lines say the same thing as the section's opening line: "If you have a dog, read the fence and dog run rules before you choose a house." and "An agent at Chapter3 can read the HOA's rules with you before you make an offer." Keep one sentence after the story.

### 4. The comparison leads with the bad

- "Myrtle Trace is the farthest of these four from the beach, and its homes usually sell for the least." Lead with the good. The fee is $95 a month and the others are about $315 to $445. Say that first, then the beach is 15 minutes.
- Take "(listings)" out of the HOA column. A buyer does not know why it is there.
- "Houses about $400; villas about $445" and "Houses about $665,000; villas about $430,000" are too many numbers in one row. One number per box.

### 5. The homes section reads like records

- "Myrtle Trace was laid out in eight sections from 1984 to 1994." Nobody needs this. Cut it.
- "Most were built from 1984 to 1998" right after "1984 to 1994" is two date ranges in two sentences. Keep one: "Most homes were built in the 1980s and 1990s."
- "Listings show houses of about..." Do not tell them where it came from. Just say it.
- "every home for sale now is on one level" is the best thing in the section for this buyer, and it is buried in the middle. Lead with it: "Most homes are one-story houses with their own yards."
- The golf course: "Owners of those homes accept golf balls and noise from the course." That is the bad. Say the good first, the view of the course, then the golf balls.

### 6. The Conway section is three photos and one sentence

- "Conway is the town next to Myrtle Trace, with a riverwalk on the Waccamaw River and old brick shops on Main Street." Then three photos. On a phone that is two screens of photos for one sentence. It looks like filler.
- What I want: two or three plain sentences on what a buyer does in Conway (the riverwalk, places to eat and shop on Main Street, how far it is). Then two photos, each next to its sentence. The cypress trees photo can go; the riverwalk and Main Street photos show what the sentence says.

### 7. The same facts over and over

- "about 70 events and club meetings a month" is in the short answer and again as the first line of the month section.
- "Myrtle Trace has a Conway address, but it is outside the city limits, so you pay no Conway city tax." sits at the bottom of "What is close", which is about drive times. It is a money fact. Put it in "What does it cost to live here?" It is also in the FAQ, which is fine.

### 8. Smaller things

- Flood: "Federal law requires flood insurance for a home loan only in those zones, though a lender may still ask for it." That is how it works, and they do not care. Say what it means: "You probably will not need flood insurance. If you want it, a policy here usually costs about $550 a year."
- The button "WE HELP GET INSURANCE QUOTES IF YOU NEED THEM." is a whole sentence with a period on a button. Make it short, like "Get an insurance quote".
- "Two leagues" as a card title says nothing. Call it "Bocce and shuffleboard".
- "In Chapter3's experience, many HOAs here do not allow renting a house at all." This is good news for Myrtle Trace. Say it that way: "Many HOAs here do not allow renting at all. Myrtle Trace does, for a year or longer."
- The sources line says "Read October 5, 2026." The date is already at the top. Once is enough.
- Not a send-back, just a question: the big orange line under the title is "A 55+ neighborhood close to the hospital." It is true and it matters, and the hospital is already in the icon row right below it. I would rather the big line say what living there is like: the pool, the ponds, the busy calendar, the low fee.

## What did the page forget that a buyer types into Google?

- **Myrtle Trace homes for sale.** This is the biggest search for this name, and the page has no way to get the current list. Add a CTA near the homes section, like "Get the list of Myrtle Trace homes for sale now", that opens the contact form.
- **Can I play golf at Burning Ridge?** The page says the course is next door and privately owned. The next question is whether owners can join or play. Answer it if a source has it. If not, do not raise it more than once.
- **Can my adult child live with me?** The page says a younger husband or wife can live there and grandchildren can visit. The next question is about adult children and how long guests can stay. Answer it from the HOA rules.
- **Golf carts.** Can you drive a golf cart in Myrtle Trace, and to the stores across 501? Answer it if the rules or the county say. If there is no good source, leave it out.
- **Older homes and insurance.** Most homes were built in the 1980s and 1990s. A buyer will ask whether an older roof changes the insurance. If our insurance page covers it, link it next to the insurance CTA.
