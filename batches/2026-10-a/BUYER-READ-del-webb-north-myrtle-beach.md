# Buyer read: Del Webb North Myrtle Beach

Read as a 62-year-old from Ohio who has never bought in South Carolina.

## Notes as I read

### Top of the page

- **Where is the good part?** The headline asks what a new home costs and answers "From $585,990." The first screen tells me the price, that 408 homes are built, that new ones are still for sale, and that the beach is about a mile away. That is what I came for. It is good. The "short answer" box under it also gives me the dues items and the tax. I did not have to dig.
- **I don't know this word.** "Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge." I would call him "the broker who checked it." Minor.
- **I don't know this word.** "specialized agent" (button). I would say "an agent who knows this community." I do not know what "specialized" means here. Is it a license type?
- **Now I want to know...** Is $585,990 the real price of a house I could buy today, or only a "starting at"? The page says some homesites, upgrades and options cost extra, but it does not say how much extra, even roughly. Not answered.
- **I skipped this.** "Operations Officer" in the byline. I do not know what that has to do with homes. I skipped it.

### What do new homes cost

- **I don't know this word.** "Home design" is fine. "Stardom", "Stellar", "Renown" are names, fine. "From sq ft" in the table header reads odd. I would say "smallest size."
- **I don't know this word.** "Homesites." I would say "lots." I worked it out from context.
- **This sounds like a report.** The table headers and the "2 to 4 bedrooms / 2.5 to 4.5 baths" ranges. A 2-bedroom with 4.5 baths? I cannot tell which version costs $585,990. It reads like a spec sheet.
- **Why are you telling me this?** "Two homes were due to be finished in October 2026... down from $767,965." This is useful. A $68,000 drop on a home. But then I want to know: are these two still available, and are they the ones I can move into soon? "Due to be finished" is vague. Is this a quick-move-in? Not said.
- **I don't know this word.** "Storm fabric for the windows." I think this means hurricane shutters or screens. I would call it "hurricane protection for the windows." I do not know if it works.
- **I don't know this word.** "The 10-year warranty on the structure." Fine, but what does "structure" cover? Roof? Walls? Skipped.
- **This sounds like a report.** "See the current homes on Pulte's Del Webb North Myrtle Beach page." This sends me to the builder. Why would I call this company then? That is a bit odd, but the next box helps.
- **Now I want to know...** What does a buyer's agent do for me when the builder sells the house? Do I pay you? Does the builder pay you? The box says "One of our agents can read the HOA documents with you before you sign Pulte's contract." That is a small reason to call. It does not say what it costs me. Not answered.

### What is there to do

- Liked this section. Pool, indoor lap pool, fitness center, arts and crafts, a lifestyle director, clubs. Short and easy.
- **I don't know this word.** "Lifestyle director." I think it is the activities person. Fine.
- **Why are you telling me this?** "Homes finished in the last few months may not be counted yet." I do not care about the counting. I skipped it.
- **Now I want to know...** Is there a golf course? Walking trails? Is there a shop or restaurant nearby? The page only mentions a pool and a community center. Not answered. Also, what is the fee to use all this? That leads to the next section.

### HOA dues

- **I don't know this word.** "HOA." The page never says what it stands for. I know it from my own state (homeowners association), but a person who did not would be lost. "Dues" is fine.
- **Where is the good part?** It says dues include lawn care and TV. Good. But it does NOT say how much. "Ask the management company for the monthly amount." I came to the page to learn this. The page sends me away. I would be annoyed. This is the biggest gap in the page.
- **I don't know this word.** "Fiber network for internet." Fine, but "Pulte does not say whether the dues pay for it" feels like a shrug.
- **This sounds like a report.** "Ask the management company, Associated Asset Management..." It reads like instructions on a form. Why can the agent not tell me? The page says "in Chapter3's experience, HOAs here commonly charge a transfer fee and a few months of dues in advance." That is good, but give me a number.
- **I don't know this word.** "Transfer fee." What is it? A fee for moving in? I would call it "a fee to take ownership of the house in the HOA."
- **I skipped this.** The "Read what an HOA can charge a buyer at closing" link line.

### Who can live here

- **Why are you telling me this?** "That rule is in the city's 2020 agreement with Pulte. The agreement states: 'at least one member of the household must be at least 55 years of age.'" I wanted: "Is it 55+? Can my wife who is 58 live here?" The first sentence answers me. The quote and the 2020 agreement are for a lawyer. I skipped the second paragraph.
- **I don't know this word.** "Age-restricted" versus "age-targeted." I do not know the difference and it is only in a link. I would want one sentence on it, because it affects whether grandkids can stay.
- **Now I want to know...** Can my grandkids visit for a month? The page says guests are in the HOA rules, "not online." So the page cannot answer. That is honest, but it leaves me without an answer to a real question.

### Renting

- Short. Says the limit is in rules that are not public. I would want to rent it a few weeks a year. The answer is "ask." Not helpful, but honest.
- **This sounds like a report.** "In Chapter3's experience, rental limits differ from one new community to the next, so our agents read each set of documents." It is a sales line more than an answer.

### Property tax

- **Where is the good part?** "$3,000 a year" on a $700,000 home. I did not expect that. In Ohio I pay more. This is a real selling point and it is stated early.
- **I don't know this word.** "Homestead exemption." I would call it "a tax break for people who live in the house." The page explains it a little: apply at 65 after a year living here. I am 62. So I would have to wait. The page says it, which I like.
- **I don't know this word.** "Second home" tax of $9,080. This is a big jump and the page gives it one line. I would want to know why, in plain words: "if it's not your main home the tax is three times higher." It tells me the number but not the reason.
- **Why are you telling me this?** "The city raised its tax rate for 2026, which adds about $140 a year." Fine, short.
- **Liked this.** The Linda and Ray example. Columbus, Ohio, $700,000, ages 67 and 61. That is me. I read this closely. But: Ray is 61 and Linda is 67. Does Ray count for the 55+ rule, and who gets the tax break? It uses Linda. Good, it answered a question I had not asked yet.
- **This sounds like a report.** "Their first tax bill at 2025 rates is about $2,999." Then "$2,784." The cents-level exactness reads like a form. "About $3,000" and "about $2,800" is enough.
- **Now I want to know...** Is the tax bill for the first year based on the price I pay? The page says "2025 rates." I do not know if it will go up when I buy. Partly answered by the link.

### Flood

- **I don't know this word.** "FEMA flood Zone X." I know FEMA. "Zone X" I do not. The page says what it means. Fine.
- **This sounds like a report.** "FEMA's flood map for the community took effect in December 2021." and "single-family flood policies for Zone X homes cost a median of $602 a year, fees included. Half of those policies cost between $460 and $798 a year. They are policies that began from June 2025 to May 2026." This is the heaviest paragraph on the page. I wanted: "Flood insurance runs about $600 a year." "Median", "half of those policies", "began from June 2025 to May 2026" are statistics language. I skipped everything after "$602."
- **I don't know this word.** "State's wind and hail insurance pool." I do not know this. I would call it "the state's backup insurance for beach homes." The page says homes are outside it, which I take to mean regular insurance works. I do not know if that is good or bad. It does not say what I will pay for regular homeowners insurance, which is what I really worry about in a hurricane area. Not answered.
- **Why are you telling me this?** "From the HOA's office, US 17 is about 0.75 miles east." I do not care where the office is.
- **I skipped this.** The "We help get insurance quotes" line after the box. It repeats the box above it.

## Answers

### What did you want to know first? Where did I find it?

First I wanted the price of a house. Found on the first screen, in the headline. Second, the monthly cost beyond the mortgage: dues and tax. Tax was on the first screen and in the middle. The dues amount: never. Third, is it really near the beach: first screen, and again in the middle.

### Three things I liked

1. The price is the first thing on the page: from $585,990, and the three designs go up to $704,590. No hunting.
2. The Linda and Ray example. Two people from Columbus with $700,000. It showed me my own tax bill about $3,000, and what changes after a year.
3. The beach is about a mile, an ER is 1.4 miles away, and the clubhouse has a pool, an indoor lap pool and a lifestyle director. I can picture living there.

### Questions I still have, ranked

1. How much are the HOA dues each month? (Never answered. The page sends me to a management company.)
2. What will homeowners insurance cost, plus flood? (Flood is about $600 a year. Homeowners is not given.)
3. How much do lot choice, upgrades and options add to $585,990? What does a house actually cost when I move in?
4. Can I rent it out for part of the year, and can guests, grandkids and pets stay? (The page says ask for the rules.)
5. Are the two reduced-price homes still available, and can I move in soon?
6. What does your company charge me, and who pays you, if the builder sells the house?
7. Is there golf, and what is nearby to walk to (stores, restaurants)? Can I get to the beach without a car?
8. What does the 10-year structure warranty cover?
9. What is a transfer fee and how much is it?
10. Which hospital would I actually go to, and is it good?

### Would I call this company?

Maybe: the page gave me the price, the tax and the beach distance up front, which earns trust, but it sent me to someone else for the dues, the rules and the rent limits, so I am not sure what the agent adds that I could not find myself.

## Other things that read like a report, not a person

- "Pulte does not say whether the dues pay for it." and "Ask the management company..." appear many times. It reads as if the company has not looked.
- Phrases like "in October 2026" and "at 2025 rates" repeat. A buyer would say "right now."
- Several small headings are questions with "in Del Webb North Myrtle Beach" repeated each time. It feels like a template. By the fourth section I stopped noticing the name.
