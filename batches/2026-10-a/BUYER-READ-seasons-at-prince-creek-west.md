# Buyer read: Seasons at Prince Creek West

Read as a 62-year-old from Ohio who has never bought in South Carolina.

## Notes as I read

### Top of the page

- **Where is the good part?** The first screen answers the question in the title: yes, it is 55+. That is useful. But the next lines are the rule (a resident 55 or older, no one under 18), then a hospital distance, then lease rules and a tax figure. I never learn what it is like to live here before the rules start. The clubhouse and pools are there, but in one flat sentence.
- **I don't know this word:** "permanent resident." I would call it "the person who actually lives there." The page explains it later, in "Who can live in...", but it is first used where I can't tell what it means.
- **Why are you telling me this?** "On a $450,000 home you live in, the property tax is about $1,768 a year." I never said I had $450,000. I don't know why that number, and the top of the page does not say.
- **I don't know this word:** "55+ communities... age-targeted ones." I don't know the difference. The page only offers a link.

### What is life like

- **This sounds like a report.** "In 2013, the builder listed a full-time activities director... Its 2013 list also named a ballroom, a card room, an art studio..." This is a 13-year-old list from a builder. I can't tell whether any of it is still there. I can't use it.
- **This sounds like a report.** "The association's website has a section for its lifestyle office and ambassadors, open to owners who sign in." I can't see it, so it tells me nothing.
- **I don't know this word:** "homesites." I would say "homes." "444 homesites" is a number I can't judge.
- **I don't know this word:** "road and park association" and "43-acre park." Why is a road association charging me dues? I would call this "a second fee." The page doesn't say what I get for it.
- **Why are you telling me this?** The Blackmoor story: clients sold a house in a golf community and bought here. This is the one part that sounds like a person talking. But it tells me nothing about why they moved or whether they like it.
- **I skipped this:** The facts table's "Where" row ("Unincorporated Horry County, with a Murrells Inlet mailing address"). I don't know what "unincorporated" means for me. Later the page says no city tax, which is the only reason I'd care.
- **I don't know this word:** "Zone X... 45 lots touch Zone AE" in the table. No explanation here.
- **Now I want to know:** How much are the dues each month? What does a house cost? The page never says either. The table has "Two months of dues" as a fee, but not what the dues are.

### Who can live here

- **Where I'd stop:** The "2017 copy of its rules." The page says the board recorded newer rules, the latest in December 2025, and they are not online. So the age rule I just read comes from a copy that may be out of date. I feel unsure whether the "Yes" at the top is really true today.
- **Now I want to know:** If my spouse is 58 and I'm 62, are we fine? Page answers yes in effect (one person 55+), but I had to work it out.

### Dues

- **I don't know this word:** "Prince Creek master association," "Highway 17 connector road association." Four associations, one bill. I want to know the total. The page tells me to "ask for the 2026 budget and the current monthly dues." That is the number I most wanted, and the page sends me away for it.
- **I don't know this word:** "shared services such as cable, internet and home monitoring." It says the board can add or cancel them. So do I pay for these now? Unclear.

### Closing costs

- **This sounds like a report.** "The 2017 rules copy says the fee may not exceed $480, adjusted over time." Then the example later says the fee is $1,125 "or the cap if that is lower." I can't tell if I will pay $480 or $1,125 or something else. This confused me.
- **I don't know this word:** "transfer fee." I'd call it "a fee for the sale." "Written statement of the dues, fees and any balance owed" is a form word. I'd call it "a letter with what I owe."
- **Why are you telling me this?** "The seller and the buyer are both responsible for it." Who pays what share? I can't tell.

### Renting

- **I skipped this** mostly. "In prominent type" and "in default under the lease" and "timesharing... unless the board and the developer approve it" are lawyer phrases. I don't plan to rent. Page does say "Yes, one year, in writing," which was the part I'd use.
- **Where I'd stop:** "Our agents read the HOA documents for any rule against a buyer's plan to rent." It's a sales line in the middle of a rule section. Fine, but I don't learn anything.

### Other rules

- **I liked this.** Two pets, fences, golf carts in the garage. Short and plain. These are the rules I'd tell my wife about. The golf cart rule matters.
- **Now I want to know:** Can I park an RV or boat? The page doesn't say. It mentions parking rules from 2022 and says to ask.

### Property tax

- **This sounds like a report.** "Each owner in unincorporated Horry County also pays a county stormwater fee... the county's utility-fee page lists $89.40 a year." That's a source quote. I only needed "about $89 a year on top."
- **I don't know this word:** "homestead exemption." Page says it saves about $196 a year "at 65, after a full year living in South Carolina." I'm 62, so it doesn't apply to me for 3 years. The page does not say that plainly. Joan example helps a little.
- **Why are you telling me this?** The Joan and Walt story. It is the only thing that shows real numbers together, but it is long, and "or the cap if that is lower" and "once Joan claims" read like a worked math problem. The Richmond couple is not me. I would skim it.
- **Good part:** "As a second home, the same house would owe about $5,597." That is a real shock and a real help. In Ohio I'd pay much less. I'd want this near the top if I'm keeping a house in Ohio.
- **Now I want to know:** What do I pay all in per month? The page gives tax, but not dues, insurance or utilities. It does not add them up.

### Flood

- **I don't know this word:** "FEMA flood Zone X," "Zone AE," "mapped flood area," "homesite's center," "letter that took Phase 2 lots 306 to 314 out." I'd call this "flood risk." The sentence "The center of every homesite is in Zone X, the area of minimal flood hazard" is OK once explained. But the string of lot numbers ("16 to 20, 22, 23, 25 to 28, 101 to 121...") is a list I can't use without knowing my lot.
- **This sounds like a report.** "Those policies began from June 2025 to May 2026, across the whole ZIP code." That sentence is a footnote, not an answer. What I want: "Flood insurance here usually costs about $574 a year."
- **I skipped this:** The 2011 FEMA letter paragraph and the lot list.
- **Now I want to know:** What does regular home insurance cost? Is wind or hurricane covered? The page says the state wind pool doesn't sell here. I don't know what that means for me. Is that good or bad? Do I need other coverage? Not answered.
- **Good part:** The offer to get an insurance quote on a specific lot before I offer. That is a real service I'd use.

### Distances

- **Why are you telling me this?** Distances are "by road from the clubhouse at 130 Grand Cypress Way" and "OpenStreetMap estimates." I'd say "about 15 minutes to the beach."
- **Where is the good part?** The beach is "about 6 miles" to a "boardwalk access in Garden City." I wanted to know how long it takes and whether it's nice. Miles only. The airport 15.7 miles: how long? Hospital 4 miles is good to know, and the page gave it at the top.
- The paragraph with the run-together "Tidelands Waccamaw Community Hospital4.0 miAtlantic Avenue beach access..." is a mess, and then the same three are listed again as bullets. I skipped one of them.

### Comparison table

- **I skipped this.** "Youngest other resident: 19 / No minimum in the HOA's rules / Ask the management company." I don't know what the column means. "Del Webb" I recognize as a name, but not why I'd choose it. Two rows say "Ask the management company," so they tell me nothing.
- **Now I want to know:** Which is best for me? The page doesn't say. It shows facts without a view.

### FAQ and end

- FAQ repeats the earlier answers. I'd skip it.
- The "Sources" line is a block of names (FEMA, Medicare Care Compare, SC Code 38-75-310). "For education, not legal advice" reads like a form.

## What I wanted to know first

Whether I can live there (55+) was first, and the page answered it on the first screen. After that, I wanted: what a house costs, what I pay each month, and what it is like. Cost of a house: never. Monthly dues: never (it says ask). What it's like: thin, partly from 2013 and a login page. Total monthly cost: never.

## Three things I liked

1. The age rule is clear and answered right away, and a younger spouse can live there as long as one person is 55+.
2. Two pets, golf carts in the garage, a one-year minimum lease: plain, short rules I can picture.
3. The Zone X flood finding for the center of every lot, the flood insurance median of $574 a year, and that the home is outside the state wind pool area. Also the clubhouse, indoor and outdoor pools, fitness center, tennis and bocce, and a hospital 4 miles away.

## Questions I still have, most important first

1. What are the monthly dues, all four associations together?
2. What do homes here cost, and what is for sale?
3. What will I really pay each month, all in (dues, tax, insurance)?
4. Are the 2024 amenities still there, and what do residents do day to day?
5. Which exact fee do I pay at closing: $480 or $1,125?
6. Do I need hurricane or wind coverage, and what does it cost?
7. How long is the drive to the beach and the airport?
8. Are there newer rules (2025) that change the age or pet rules?
9. Can I park an RV or boat?
10. Why pick this over Del Webb or Myrtle Trace?

## Would I call this company?

Maybe, because the offer to read the rules with me and get a flood quote on a lot is useful, but the page gave me the rules and no monthly cost, so I would not feel I knew enough to call yet.
