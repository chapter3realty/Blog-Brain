Why the North Myrtle Beach page was weaker

  1. Its HOA rules are not online, so it had fewer checked facts. I filled the gap from the city instead: the community center, parks, beach paths, golf cart and dog rules, and drive times. A separate agent checked all 30 new facts. None was wrong.
  2. The old map photo was a 2023 government aerial blown up past its sharpness. A researcher found newer public-domain aerials flown in January 2026, about twice as sharp. All the aerials on the page now come from them.
  3. A town section had been cut because no checked facts existed for it. Now they do, so the section is back.

Why the stories were hard, and the new rule

The Grande Dunes story made the reader count nights against a rule with three parts. Research on memory and on older readers says a reader holds about four things at once. A story that asks for more gets lost. Every story now has to pass a simple test: one want, one problem, one fix, no counting, no "if", 4 to 6 short sentences, and a stranger can tell it back after one read. The page builder now refuses a story that breaks the parts a machine can check. An outside reader read all four once and told each one back correctly. Two places where they stopped were fixed.

What I decided, so you do not have to

  • Renting. The page now says plainly, "Not by the week. Renters here must stay at least 30 days." This comes from the city's approval for the neighborhood. The HOA's recorded rules are not online. If they are stricter, the sentence is still true, and an agent reads them with every buyer before they sign.
  • Golf carts to the beach. The way to the nearest beach path runs along US 17, where carts may only cross. The page says to plan to drive. It makes no claim about the builder.
  • Reviews. The page's writer graded its own work 9. An independent reviewer graded it 8 and found eight fixes. From now on the writer never grades its own page.
