## What I would send back

1. **There is no picture of a home.** The page is about new houses, and the homes section is all text. The only real photos are beach chairs and a pier. I said the names of houses mean nothing to people and photos are easier to understand. Show me a street of finished homes, the front of one house with its garage and the clubhouse from the outside. These have to be our own photos, since builder and listing photos are not allowed. An agent can drive over and take them. Until then, put a drawing of a one-story house with a garage in the homes section, the same way the clubhouse has drawings.

2. **The story has no tension and finds nothing.** "If this HOA is one of them, they would find out only after they sign." That is a maybe, not a problem. Then the agent reads the rule, and the story ends with "Frank and Joyce now decide on the builder's contract with the rental rule in front of them." What does the rule say? The reader never learns. The agent has to find something, and it has to change what they do. The fact ledger has one real thing to find. Row 75 says the city's 2020 plan for the neighborhood says renters must stay at least 30 days. Frank and Joyce plan to rent to vacation renters by the week in the summer. Their agent finds the 30-day minimum before they sign, and they change their plan to a monthly renter or no renter. Keep row 75's wording limit: it is the plan, not the recorded rule. The better fix is to get the recorded rules (see item 4). Then the story can say what the rule is today.

3. **"many HOAs here do not allow renting at all"** That sentence is in the story with no source. Prove it from a fact row or cut it.

4. **The rental question is asked and not answered.** "The HOA's full rules are recorded with the county, but they are not posted online." That tells a buyer we do not know. If the rules are recorded with the county, go get them. The ledger names them: Book 4442, Page 1382, and the 2026 rules at Book 5032, Page 1405, at the Register of Deeds in Conway. Then answer "Can you rent out your home here?" in plain words. Until then, cut that sentence from "Who can live here?" It only raises a worry.

5. **The story is still not one of the last things.** It comes before the comparison table and the FAQ. Move it after the comparison table, so it is the last section before the FAQ. The reader should know the place first.

6. **No buyer types the story's heading.** "How does a Chapter3 agent check the HOA rules on a new home?" is about us. Name the section with the buyer's question: "Can you rent out your home in Del Webb North Myrtle Beach?" Answer it in the first sentence. Then tell the story.
