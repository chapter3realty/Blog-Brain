# Buyer read, v5: Del Webb at Grande Dunes

Read on a phone, top to bottom. I skipped the site menu and the grey "Draft for review" bar that sits over the first paragraph.

## Notes as I read

### 1. I don't know this word

- "Intracoastal Waterway, a channel for boats along the coast". The page explains it, good. But I would just say "the boat canal behind the houses".
- "homeowners association (HOA)". Explained once, then "HOA" is used about 25 times. By the cost section I had to think about it again.
- "the larger Grande Dunes association". I do not know what this is, who runs it, or what it does. It is a second fee and the page never says what I get for it.
- "recorded rules", "recorded a newer set of rules". I do not know what "recorded" means here. I would say "the official rules filed with the county".
- "posted rules, revised January 2019". "Posted" where? I would say "the rules the HOA put on its website".
- "amenity center". I can guess (clubhouse and pools), but I would say "clubhouse".
- "age-restricted and age-targeted". I know neither. The link sentence uses both words and does not say the difference.
- "high-risk flood zone", "federal flood insurance law", "lender must require". I can follow it, but "lender" and "flood zone" are words I would have to look up.
- "villas" versus "single-family houses". I do not know what a villa is here (attached? one floor? shared walls?). Never said.
- "Mexican Train". The page says it is a dominoes game. Fine.
- "day dock", "boat ramp". OK from context.
- "FEMA policy data for ZIP code 29572" in the sources line. Meaningless to me.

### 2. Why are you telling me this?

- "Under the HOA's posted rules, revised January 2019, the dock is open from dawn to dusk." The date and the source are in my way. I only want to know the dock closes at night.
- "Yes to all three, with a few rules, under the HOA's posted rules, revised January 2019." Same. This phrase shows up about four times.
- "At least one resident of every lived-in home ... must be 55 or older. The neighborhood's recorded rules say so." I do not need to know who says so.
- "Under the federal flood insurance law, a lender must require flood insurance only for a home in that zone." I wanted "do I need it, yes or no". The first sentence answers that. This sentence is more than I need.

### 3. This sounds like a report

- "Recorded rules, 2018, Posted rules, revised January 2019, HOA financial report, Horry County deed records, FEMA flood map." A list of documents. Skipped.
- "Drive times come from OpenStreetMap, without traffic. FEMA policy data for ZIP code 29572 gives the flood cost." Report talk.
- "Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)." And the photo credits with "CC0 (public domain dedication), via Wikimedia Commons" and "CC BY 2.0". I know they are required, but they read like legal fine print under every picture.
- "Their surprise is the one-time fee to the HOA when they buy". Fine in a story, but "Their surprise" is an odd phrase.
- The two pages of footer, "Investor Hub", "DSCR Loans", "BRRRR Method", "1031 Exchange". I came for a place to retire. The footer says "investor-focused brokerage ... for serious real estate investors". That tells me I am in the wrong place.

### 4. I skipped this

- The "at a glance" tiles (Beach, Groceries, Hospital, Pools, Beach club, Boat dock). They repeat what the sentence above them and the map said. I looked once.
- The map. I could not tell the pins from each other without squinting. The map says "2 MILES" but I do not see a road or any town name I know. The text under it ("Show the live map") I would skip.
- The "Sources" paragraph.
- The photo credit lines.
- The last two dark call-to-action blocks and the whole footer.
- The FAQ at the bottom. It says the same thing as the sections above it.

### 5. Now I want to know...

- After the first screen: what does it cost? Not answered until about the middle of the page.
- After "Where is it": is it quiet or busy? Is it near the big road, near traffic, near tourists? Not answered. I do not know how far the neighborhood is from Broadway at the Beach crowds.
- After "homes": how big are they? How many bedrooms? One floor or two? Is there a garage? Not answered at all. Only "single-family houses and a section of villas".
- After "homes": what is "the first villa section"? Are there other villa sections that are different? Not answered.
- After "what is there to do": is there a golf course, a gym, a pool with a heater? "Fitness: A fitness and wellness program in the HOA's budget" does not say if there is a gym room. Not answered.
- After "Who can live here": I am 62, my spouse is 60. Fine. But what if one of us is 54? Partly answered by the Carol and Jim example.
- After "cost": what is the monthly HOA fee? What is the beach club fee? What is the "larger association" fee? The page never gives one monthly number. It says "a fee" four times and gives no amount. That is the number I need most.
- After "flood": what about hurricanes and wind insurance? It only talks about flood. Not answered.
- After "pets, golf cart": can I have a bigger dog? "Of any breed", up to three. Answered.
- After "move": how do I buy one? Do I pay cash, do I need a lender? Not covered, and I understand why.
- After "compare": which one is better for me? Not said. The table is cut off (see 6).

### 6. Too many numbers

- "A home in Del Webb at Grande Dunes usually sells for about $630,000. Villas usually sell for less, about $433,000." $433,000 is too exact for something I would say out loud. "About $430,000" is how I would say it.
- "Property tax on a $630,000 home you live in is a little over $2,000 a year." Two numbers, and I do not trust it. $2,000 on $630,000 looks very low to an Ohio buyer (my tax is much more). The page does not say why it is that low. That is good news if true and I do not believe it.
- "Grandchildren and other guests under 19 can stay up to 90 nights in any 12 months." Three numbers. I would read "grandchildren can stay up to 90 nights a year".
- "Carol is 66 and Jim is 58" plus the 55+ rule. Fine, but it is a lot of numbers in two sentences.
- "a fence 4 feet tall", "up to three cats or dogs", "two guest cards", "at least $3,000". Each one is fine alone.
- Beach 4 min, Food Lion 4 min, Hospital 6 min, Airport 20 min, Broadway 8 min. Five times in the page. The same numbers are in the tiles, on the map and in a sentence. Too many repeats.
- Phone problem: the table at the bottom is cut off on the right. I can see "Community, Town, Minutes to the beach" but the last column ("Homes usually sell for") is sliced to one letter. I cannot read the prices for the other places. I did not know I could scroll sideways, and I would not have tried.

### 7. Where is this?

- I can partly picture it. "Myrtle Beach", "4 minutes from the beach", "beside the boat canal". The map helps a little.
- I did not get a picture of the neighborhood. The two photos are of the beach and of Broadway at the Beach at night, and both say "not the neighborhood". I want to see the houses, the pool, the clubhouse. I never see one Del Webb home. I feel I am being shown a different place.
- I do not know what is next to it. Golf? Houses? A highway? The map shows an ocean and a waterway, but no roads.
- Where is the Ocean Club? Is it on the beach? Do I have to drive to it? Page says "beach club" but does not say how far from my house. The phrase "the Grande Dunes Ocean Club" is also tied to "the larger Grande Dunes association", and I am not sure if they are the same thing.
- "Grande Dunes" and "Del Webb at Grande Dunes" are used as if the same thing. I am not sure if Del Webb is one piece inside a bigger Grande Dunes, and the page only half says it.

### 8. That's not an answer

- "Looking at a house or villa in Grande Dunes? Tell us the house or villa you have in mind. One of our agents will read the neighborhood's rules with you before you make an offer." That is "ask someone".
- "The HOA has recorded a newer set of rules that is not online. One of our agents reads it with you before you make an offer." So the rules I just read are old, and the new ones I cannot see. That is the most worrying line on the page. It says the golf cart, fence and pet answers above may be out of date. It is buried at the end of that section.
- "Want the insurance cost before you offer? Ask for a quote on the exact house or villa." The page does give about $550 a year, but then tells me to ask.
- The HOA fee. The page never says the dollar amount. It says "your HOA fee pays for basic lawn care and the activities program" and does not say how much. I would have to call.
- "Read what happens if an HOA adds a rental limit after you buy." A link. I did not ask this.
- "Read where each Grand Strand hospital is and what it offers." A link, and it is a "go read another page" when I wanted one sentence about the hospital. The tile says "Grand Strand Medical Center, about 6 minutes away", which is enough.

### 9. Why so many dates?

I count these dates on the page: "Updated October 5, 2026" at the top, "revised January 2019" four times, "Recorded rules, 2018", "Read October 5, 2026" in the sources. That is about seven. None of them matter to me. "Revised January 2019" makes the rules sound seven years old.

### 10. Where is the good part?

Partly. The first screen has the title, "a 55+ neighborhood with a beach club", and one sentence on "about 4 minutes from the beach by car". That is a good start. But on my phone the grey bar covered the first sentence, and the next screen is "the short answer", which is about a boat canal. The price is not on the first screen or the second. I get no cost until about the 40 percent mark. The "good part" (beach, club, boat dock) is there but I have to scroll past an explanation of what a waterway is.

## The answers

### What did you want to know first? Where did you find it?

First: what does it cost, in total, each month and to buy? Second: is it nice (what do the homes look like)?

- The purchase price: middle of the page ("How much does it cost"), about $630,000 and $433,000.
- The monthly cost: never. The page does not give a monthly HOA fee, a beach club fee, or the bigger association fee. I get one-time fee "at least $3,000" and tax "a little over $2,000 a year". That is not the monthly picture.
- What the homes look like: never. No photo of a home, no size, no bedrooms.

### Three things I liked about this place, from the page

1. The beach is about 4 minutes by car, and the grocery store is also about 4 minutes. I like that.
2. A beach club every owner belongs to, with mahjong, knitting, dinners, wine tastings and holiday events. It sounds friendly and easy to make friends.
3. Pets up to three of any breed, golf carts on the streets, and a boat dock. It sounds like people can live how they want, and the HOA does the lawn and (for villas) the roof.

### Questions I still have, ranked by how much they matter

1. What is the monthly HOA fee, the beach club fee, and the other association fee, all added up?
2. What do the homes look like and how big are they? Can I see inside one? What is a villa?
3. What is in the newer rules that are "not online"? Could they change the pets, golf cart or fence answers?
4. Why is the property tax so low on a $630,000 house? Does it go up if I buy it?
5. Is it quiet or does it have tourist traffic from Broadway at the Beach?
6. What about hurricane and wind insurance, not only flood?
7. What is "the larger Grande Dunes association" and what do I get for that fee?
8. How do the other 55+ places compare on price? (The table is cut off on my phone.)
9. Is there a golf course or a gym room?

### Would you call this company?

Maybe, because they say an agent will read the rules with me and that is what I need, but the footer says they are for "serious real estate investors" and I am a retiree, so I would want to know first that they help people like me.

## Grade for "easy to read": 6 out of 10

Short sentences and plain headings are good, and the question headings match what I would type into Google. It loses points for the repeated "posted rules, revised January 2019" lines, "recorded rules" and "larger association" with no explanation, no monthly cost, no photo of the homes, the price column cut off on the phone, and an investor footer.
