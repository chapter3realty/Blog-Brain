# Buyer read, v5: Del Webb North Myrtle Beach

I read this on my phone, top to bottom. I am 62, from Ohio, and I have never bought in South Carolina.

## Notes as I read

### Top of the page
- **Where is the good part?** Good. The first line says it is a newer 55+ neighborhood of houses, about 4 minutes from the beach. The short answer then tells me about 400 homes are built, the builder is still selling, and one person must be 55 or older. I knew in one screen that I could live here and that it is near the beach. The price is not on the first screen. I had to scroll a long way to find it.
- **I don't know this word.** "HOA" is spelled out once ("homeowners association (HOA) fee"), but it never says what the fee is or how much it is. I would call it "the monthly neighborhood fee."
- **Now I want to know...** How much is it? Not answered in the first two screens.
- **Too many numbers / sounds like a form.** "By Devin Day, Operations Officer, Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge, Updated October 5, 2026." I skipped this line. It is the first date I saw.

### At a glance and Where is it
- **I liked this.** The six boxes (beach, groceries, hospital, pools, lawn care, clubs) are exactly what I wanted. I read all six.
- **Where is this?** The map helps. I can see the ocean, Kroger, the hospital and the airport. But the labels crowd each other. "Kroger about 6 min" runs into the pin and the "Del Webb North Myrtle Beach" label. I could not tell which dot was which. I do not know what town is nearby besides North Myrtle Beach and Myrtle Beach. I wanted to know how far it is from Myrtle Beach proper, and from Charleston or Florence.
- **Why are you telling me this?** "Opens Google Maps here. Zoom in, or drag to look around." That is directions for a button. It is fine, but I would have skipped it.
- **Now I want to know...** "Because the neighborhood is inside the city, you pay a city tax along with the county tax." How much is the city tax? Not answered here. The cost section does give one number, but it does not say which part is city.
- Hospital is "McLeod Health Seacoast, about 10 minutes". Good. I know this is a real name.
- **Where is this? (picture)** The page has no photo of Del Webb itself. Not of a house, the clubhouse or the pool. Both photos are of the beach and a theater, and the page says so in the caption. That is honest, but I wanted to see the houses and the clubhouse. I am being asked to spend $535,000 or more and I cannot see a single home.

### Homes
- **I don't know this word.** "Storm fabric to cover the windows." I would call it "hurricane window covers." I did not know if that is a roll-down thing or a cloth you put on yourself.
- **I don't know this word.** "Pulte" and "Del Webb". The page says Pulte sells under the Del Webb name. Okay. I still do not know who Pulte is, but I understand.
- **I don't know this word.** "Warranty on the structure." I would say "a guarantee on the house itself."
- **Why are you telling me this?** "natural gas service" sits next to the hurricane sentence. I do not know if that is good or bad. Is gas an extra cost?
- **Now I want to know...** What do the houses look like inside? What are the floor plans called, and which has the extra bedroom? Is 2,200 square feet the smallest, and what is the biggest? Not answered. Single floor or two stories? This matters a lot at my age. Never answered.
- **That's not an answer.** The page says "See the homes for sale now on the Del Webb North Myrtle Beach website." That sends me to the builder, which is the opposite of calling this company. Fine, but I still have no idea what the HOA fee is.
- The orange box "Want a new Del Webb home..." says an agent will "read the HOA documents with you before you sign the builder's contract." I liked that. I would never read those by myself. The button text "WANT TO BUY A NEW CONSTRUCTION HOME?" is a question, not a button. It made me pause. "New construction" is an industry phrase to me.

### Things to do
- **I liked this.** Indoor lap pool, fitness room, crafts room, gathering room, and a full-time lifestyle director. That is what a 55+ place is for. "In a normal week you can swim laps indoors, work out and meet neighbors at a club."
- **I skipped this.** The four small boxes repeat the sentence above them. "Swim laps inside the clubhouse" is the same as the line two sentences later.
- **That's not an answer.** "The builder says the city's community center is next door." What is in the community center? Is it open to me? Not said. Also "builder says" shows up many times. By the fourth time I wondered whether the company has seen any of it.
- **Now I want to know...** What clubs? Golf? Pickleball? Tennis? Not answered. The page says "activity groups" and nothing else.

### Area
- **I skipped this.** The "area around" section is two sentences and two photos. The beach photo is nice. The theater photo is a theater with a sign for a Beatles tribute band, dated July 26. That date made me think the photo was an ad. I do not know what a "Barefoot Landing" is until the page says "shops and a show theater." Fine.
- **That's not an answer.** "Read about the hospitals near North Myrtle Beach and what each one offers." I wanted that answer here, not a link. Only the nearest hospital is named.
- No restaurants, no doctor offices, no golf, no churches. For a 62 year old, I would want to know where I get a haircut and a doctor.

### Who can live here
- **Why are you telling me this?** "This is in the city's agreement with the builder." I do not care which paper says it. I care that it is true. This is a rule explained by where it came from.
- Good: a younger husband or wife can live there. I like that.
- **I don't know this word.** "Age-restricted" and "age-targeted". I do not know the difference and the link says it will explain. That is okay, but it is the thing I most wanted to know, since my wife is 57. The page does not say whether she can live here and I cannot (it covers the opposite). It does say "a younger husband or wife can live there with that person." So I think we are fine.
- **That's not an answer.** Can grandchildren stay for a summer? Can my adult kids live in the house? Not here. Linda and Ray's story says an agent reads the "HOA's rules on guests and pets with them", which tells me guests and pets have rules, but not what they are.

### Cost
- **Where is the good part?** This is the section I wanted first. It is more than halfway down the page.
- **Too many numbers.** "Homes that owners resold here in the last year usually sold for about $535,000. The builder's new homes started at about $586,000 in early October." Two prices with a month. That is okay. I understood it. But I do not know why resold homes cost less than new ones, or what I get for the difference. "usually sold for about" is a bit of a hedge, and I had to read it twice.
- **Too many numbers.** "On a $700,000 home you live in, property tax is a little over $3,000 a year, city tax included." Then: "A New York household we worked with sold a $1 million house that had more than $20,000 a year in property tax. They bought a $700,000 house on the Grand Strand and pay about $3,200 a year." Five dollar figures in three sentences. The $3,000 and the $3,200 look like they are the same thing and I did not know why they are different. The New York story is the best part of the page. It feels like a real person. But it is a house in a different place, not Del Webb, so I could not tell what my own bill would be on a $586,000 home.
- **This sounds like a report.** "The builder says the HOA fee includes lawn care and a TV package, and each yard comes with sprinklers. The builder also lists a fiber network for internet." No dollar figure for the fee anywhere. That is the number I wanted most after the home price. Every time the page said "HOA fee" I wanted the amount. It never came. I would assume it is high, or that the page is hiding it.
- **I don't know this word.** "Fiber network." I would call it "fast internet."
- **Why so many dates?** Dates: "Updated October 5, 2026", "early October", "Read October 5, 2026" in the sources, and "July 26" on the theater sign. I counted four. None of them matter to me, except that "early October" tells me prices could be different now.

### Flood

I care that I probably do not need it. I also do not know what a "lender" means here, since I would be paying cash from my house in Ohio. I skipped this line.
- **Too many numbers.** "About $600 a year" is clear and good. But I did not see how much regular home insurance costs, only a link to read about it. Near the coast, I have heard it is high. I wanted a number here.

- The box "We help get insurance quotes if you need them" is useful. The button wording is odd, as it reads as a sentence, not an action.

## Questions asked at the end

**What did you want to know first? Where on the page did you find it?**
First: what does a house cost, and what is the monthly fee? The home price I found in the middle (about 60 percent down the page). The monthly HOA fee I never found. I would say, price: middle. Monthly fee: never.

Second: can I live here with a younger wife, and is it close to the beach? Both on the first screen. That worked well.

**Three things I liked about this place:**
1. Four minutes to the beach, and about a mile. I could picture it.
2. The indoor lap pool and fitness room, with a lifestyle director who sets up clubs. I would use these in winter.
3. Lawn care is included, so I would not mow. Also, the homes are new, so nothing needs fixing.

**Questions I still have, ranked by how much they matter:**
1. What is the HOA fee per month, and what does it not cover? (Not answered. This matters most.)
2. What are the houses like inside? One floor or two? What are the plan names and sizes? (Only "2 to 4 bedrooms," "two- or three-car garage" and "smallest is about 2,200 square feet".)
3. What does homeowners insurance cost? (Only a link.)
4. What is the real yearly tax on a house at the new-home price? (There is a $700,000 example, but not the $586,000 price.)
5. What are the guest and pet rules? Can my grandkids stay? (Never said.)
6. Is there a waiting list or a deposit? How long to build? Can I buy a house that is already built?
7. Which clubs? Golf, pickleball, tennis?
8. What are the other three communities in the table like, and what do they cost? (The price column is cut off on my phone.)
9. What does a doctor, a church, and a good restaurant look like nearby?

**Would I call this company?**
Maybe: the page was honest and told me what to ask, and the "agent reads the HOA papers with you" offer is a real reason, but I would call mainly if I could get the monthly fee, and the investor links at the bottom make me wonder if they work with people like me.

## Counts
- Dates or months on the page: 4 (Updated October 5, 2026; early October; Read October 5, 2026; July 26 in a photo).
- Sentences with more than two numbers: 3 (the $700,000, $3,000 and $3,200 tax lines, and the New York house).
- Questions the page raises and does not answer: HOA amount, guest rules, floor plan, clubs, homeowners insurance cost.
- Photos of Del Webb itself: 0.

## Ease of reading: 7 out of 10
Short sentences and plain words. It is easy to read. It loses points for the missing HOA amount, the table that is cut off on a phone, no photos of the actual homes, and a "Sources" paragraph that reads like a form.
