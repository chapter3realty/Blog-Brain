# Buyer read, v5: Seasons at Prince Creek West

I read this on a phone, slowly, as a 62-year-old from Ohio who has never bought in South Carolina.

## Notes as I read

### 1. I don't know this word
- "55+" in the title is fine. I know what it means.
- "HOA" is spelled out once, halfway down ("homeowners association (HOA)"). The first time I met it was in the table of contents area as "HOA bill" in the cost section. It is fine by then, but I would call it "the monthly neighborhood fee."
- "home lots" ("about 440 home lots"). I would say "homes." A lot is the land, and I do not know if all lots have homes yet.
- "permanent resident" and "lived-in home." I would say "someone who lives there most of the year."
- "age-targeted" (link text). I do not know what that is, and the page does not say.
- "the architectural board approves it." I would call it "the neighborhood committee that approves outside changes."
- "high-risk flood zone." I have heard "flood zone." "High-risk" is clear enough.
- "road and park association," "the larger Prince Creek associations," "the group that owns its 43-acre park." I do not know who these are. I thought I was buying into one neighborhood with one fee. Now there are several groups.
- "one-time transfer fee." I would say "a fee when the home sells."
- "recorded newer rules." I would say "rules that were filed after 2017."
- "Horry County deed records" (in sources). I skipped it.
- "ZIP code 29576" and "middle FEMA policy price." I do not know what "middle FEMA policy price" means. It sits at the very bottom, so I did not mind.
- "Medicare Care Compare." I know Medicare. I did not know there was a "Care Compare."
- "Intracoastal Waterway" on the map. I do not know it, but I did not need it.

### 2. Why are you telling me this?
- "The federal flood insurance law makes a lender require flood insurance only when the home itself is in that zone." I only wanted to know if I need flood insurance. This is how the rule works. I would skip it.
- "Seasons has a Murrells Inlet address but is not inside any town, so there is no city tax." It is a good fact, but it is explained in a way that makes me ask "so who runs the roads and police?" The page does not say.
- "The 2017 posted copy of the rules says so" is repeated in three places. I only need to know the rule once.
- "Your HOA bill also pays your share to the larger Prince Creek associations. You get one bill instead of several." This is explaining how the bills are set up. What I want is the number.
- "Seasons is part of Prince Creek West, a larger group of neighborhoods." I did not ask for the history of the group.

### 3. This sounds like a report
- "The 2017 posted copy of the rules says so." Reads like a legal file.
- "Sources: Posted 2017 copy of the rules, Tidelands Waccamaw on Medicare Care Compare, Horry County deed records..." Reads like a footnote in a study. It is at the bottom, so I do not mind it, but "Read October 5, 2026" and "Drive times are no-traffic estimates from OpenStreetMap" are report words.
- "Yes to pets, golf carts, fences and renting, each with a rule, under the 2017 posted copy of the rules." Reads like a form.
- "Tell us what you need in a home. One of our agents will read the association's rules with you before you offer." Fine and friendly. This is the one place it sounds like a person.
- The "Example: Joan is 70 and Walt is 64..." story. It says "Example:" and "with up to $500,000 to spend." It reads like a sample case in a manual, not like a friend telling me about people. The line above it ("Clients of ours owned a house in Blackmoor") is better. I also do not know what happened in the end: "They buy it knowing what the insurance will cost." That does not say what the cost was or whether they still bought a flood-zone lot.

### 4. I skipped this
- The "at a glance" tiles near the top. They repeat what the two short paragraphs already told me. I looked at them but learned nothing new except the hospital and Publix.
- The map's small print and the "SHOW THE LIVE MAP / Open in Google Maps" block. Two ways to open a map is one too many. I would not click either.
- The two photos' captions. They are long, with license text ("CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons"). The caption does say the pictures are not of the neighborhood, which I respect, but it makes me ask: where is a picture of the actual neighborhood?
- The "Read which hospital serves each part of the Grand Strand" link line.
- The sentence "Many HOAs on the Grand Strand do not allow renting a house at all. Our agents read each HOA's documents for that rule." It is not about this place.
- The Sources paragraph.
- Everything under the dark footer (investor links, DSCR, BRRRR, 1031, AfBA disclosure). This is an investor site, and I am not an investor. It made me wonder if I am on the right site. The footer says "The Myrtle Beach investor-focused brokerage. DSCR financing..."

### 5. Now I want to know...
- After the top: How much is the HOA fee each month? Not answered at the top. Is it in the cost section? Not answered anywhere.
- After the map: What is a house like here? How big? One floor? Not answered.
- After "What is there to do": Is there a calendar of things to do, a social group, a clubhouse event list? "A lawn game to play with your neighbors" is the closest it gets.
- After "Who can live here": Can my adult son move in with us? The page says only that no one under 18 can live there. Partly answered.
- After "How much does it cost": What is the monthly fee? Not answered. What does it cover (grass, trash, water, cable)? Not answered. What does the "one-time fee" cost in dollars? The page says "two months of dues" but I do not know what a month of dues is, so I cannot add it up. The "transfer fee" has no number.
- After flood: What are the 45 lots? How do I tell if I am on one? The page tells me to "look up the home on FEMA's flood map," which is homework.
- After pets/golf carts: Is the neighborhood gated? Are there sidewalks? Is it flat? Not answered.
- After the Joan and Walt story: What did the insurance cost? Did they still buy? Not answered.
- After the comparison table: Which one should I pick? Not answered, but the table helps.
- After the FAQ: Are homes selling fast or slowly? What do the homes look like, and who built them? Not answered.

### 6. Too many numbers
- "The beach in Garden City is about 10 minutes away." Fine.
- "A corner of 45 lots touches the high-risk zone." Plus "about 440 home lots" plus "444 home lots" in the FAQ. I notice two different counts for the same thing, "about 440" in one place and "444" in another. Small, but it makes me wonder which is right.
- "Joan is 70 and Walt is 64... up to $500,000." Three numbers, but they are easy.
- "At least one permanent resident... 55 or older. ...at least six months of each year. No one under 18... up to 60 days a year." Five numbers across four short paragraphs. I had to read it twice to follow it.
- "On a $500,000 home you live in, property tax is about $2,000 a year." Two numbers. Fine.
- "ZIP code 29576" and "Read October 5, 2026" in the sources. I skipped it.
- "43-acre park" is more exact than I need.

### 7. Where is this?
- The map helps a lot. I can see the ocean, the marsh, Publix, the hospital and the airport. The pin sits west of the water, and I can see it is inland.
- The words "south of Myrtle Beach" and "about 10 minutes inland" help.
- I wanted a picture of the neighborhood itself: the clubhouse, the pool, a street with homes. The only photos are of boats in a marina and a sunset at a park, and the captions say they are not this place. They are pretty, but they do not show me where I would live.
- The map's tiny text is hard to read on my phone.
- The table has a missing column on the phone (see 8 below).

### 8. That's not an answer
- "How much does it cost to live in Seasons?" The page answers with the price of a house, a property tax, and two fees, but never the monthly HOA fee. That is the number I came for. The heading promises it and the page does not deliver.
- "Do you need flood insurance?" "Usually not" is an answer. Then "get a flood quote before you offer" and "Look up the home on FEMA's flood map" send me elsewhere for the rest.
- The page says "The association has recorded newer rules since 2017 that are not online. One of our agents reads them with you before you buy." So the rules I just read may be out of date, and the answer is "ask an agent." That makes the whole pets/carts/fences/renting box feel uncertain.
- The comparison table on the phone is cut off. I can see Community, Town and Minutes to the beach, but the "Homes usually sell for" column is cut off at the right edge and I cannot read the prices. I would not know to scroll sideways.
- "Try your own price in the Horry County property tax calculator." That is homework.
- Under "Can you have pets...", "Golf carts: Park them in your garage, never on the street." What if I do not have a garage? Small, but it came up.

### 9. Why so many dates?
- "Updated October 5, 2026" at the top, and "Read October 5, 2026" at the bottom. Two dates. "The 2017 posted copy of the rules" appears three times, plus "since 2017." So the page tells me a date or year about eight times. The 2017 mentions made me think the rules are old. That is the one date that matters to me, and it worries me.

### 10. Where is the good part?
- First screen: I learn it is 55+, near Murrells Inlet, and about 10 minutes from the beach. The sentence and the italic line "A 55+ neighborhood with two pools" tell me what I get. I do not learn the price on the first screen. The top of the page has the title, the byline, one sentence, and then a draft banner covering the rest of the paragraph (that banner is only on the preview).
- The price ("about $500,000") shows up in the middle of the page, in the cost section. The cost is not in "The short answer" or the glance tiles.
- The first thing I see after the title is the byline ("By Devin Day, Operations Officer · Reviewed by Tim Nash, Broker-in-Charge"). It is fine, but I do not know these people.
- The good part (two pools, indoor swimming in winter, tennis, a beach in 10 minutes) is on the first screen and a half. Good. The drawbacks (flood corner, rules from 2017 that may be out of date, several associations) come after. Good order.

## What I wanted to know first
I wanted to know: what does a house here cost, and what do I pay each month?
- House price: found in the middle (cost section, about halfway down). Not on the first screen.
- Monthly fee: never.

## Three things I liked
1. Two pools, one indoors. I am from Ohio and I want to swim in winter.
2. The beach is 10 minutes away and Publix is 5. The map made it easy to see.
3. The pets, golf cart, fence and renting box. Short "Yes" answers I can read in seconds. And it is allowed to rent, which matters if I want to leave for the summer or sell later.

## Questions I still have, most important first
1. What is the monthly HOA fee, and what does it cover?
2. What is the actual dollar amount of the one-time fees (two months of dues, and the transfer fee)?
3. What do the homes look like inside and out? How big, how many floors, who built them? Are there photos of this neighborhood?
4. Are the rules from 2017 still the rules? What changed?
5. Which lots are the 45 that touch the flood zone, and how do I tell? What did Joan and Walt's insurance cost?
6. Who are the "larger Prince Creek associations" and what do they control?
7. Is it gated? Is it quiet? Is it flat and walkable?
8. Are there social clubs or events?
9. What are the prices in the comparison table? (They are cut off on my phone.)
10. How many homes are for sale right now?

## Would I call?
Maybe: the pools and the 10-minute beach interest me, but I would call only after I found the monthly fee somewhere else, because this page never gave it to me.

## Easy to read: 6 out of 10
The sentences are short and the headings are questions, which helps. It loses points for the missing monthly fee under a "How much does it cost" heading, the price column cut off on the phone, the repeated "2017 posted copy of the rules," the several-associations explanation, no photo of the neighborhood, and an investor-style footer on a page for a retiree.
