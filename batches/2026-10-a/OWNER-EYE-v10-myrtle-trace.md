This is a lot better. The homes section has pictures now, and it leads with one-story houses. The three maps are down to one. The comparison leads with the fee. ## What I would send back

### 1. Three things I asked for last time are still there

- After the story there are still two lines: "If you have a dog, ask what you can build in the yard before you choose a house." and "An agent at Chapter3 can read the HOA's rules with you before you make an offer." I said keep one. Keep the second one. The first one says "ask" and does not say who to ask.
- The sources line still says "Read October 5, 2026." The date is at the top. Take it out.
- Conway: I asked for how far it is. The section never says how long it takes to drive to downtown Conway. That is the first thing a buyer wants after "the town next to Myrtle Trace". Re-run the drive time to the riverwalk and have it checked (the ledger's row 90 is marked unverifiable). If it cannot be checked, leave it out, but try.

### 2. The fence answer is not the whole answer

- "Not with a fence between yards or across the front. A dog run is allowed once the HOA approves it." A buyer reads that and thinks he can fence the back yard. On most lots he cannot. The rules only allow a fence around the yard on lots that back onto a road or empty land, and never on golf course or pond lots.
- What I want: "Usually not. Fences between yards and across the front are not allowed, and most lots cannot have a fence around the back yard either. A dog run is allowed once the HOA approves it." The story then shows the golf course lot, which is still new to the reader.
- "the HOA's design rules": say "the HOA's rules for yards". "Design rules" is a term.

### 3. Things still doing the acting

- "wide walkways run between the houses" (month section). Say "There are wide walkways between the houses." And make it its own sentence. Right now it is glued onto line dancing and the craft fair.
- "The ponds collect rainwater, so swimming and boating are not allowed in them." The reason does not matter to them. Say "You cannot swim or take a boat in the ponds."
- "The HOA fee pays to keep up the pool..." (cost section and FAQ). Say "The HOA uses the fee to keep up the pool...".
- FAQ: "The HOA says the back gates stop drivers from using the neighborhood as a shortcut." The gates are doing the stopping. Say "The HOA put gates on the back entrance so drivers do not cut through the neighborhood."

### 4. "The HOA says" twice in the FAQ

- "The HOA says the back gates stop drivers..." and "the HOA says it has no management company." We are the experts. Say it: "The owners run the HOA themselves, with volunteers. There is no management company."

### 5. The month section ends in a grab bag

- "Your house guests can fish too. The ponds collect rainwater, so swimming and boating are not allowed in them. The pool closes for the season at the start of October." Three different things in one paragraph, under four cards that already cover fishing and the pool.
- What I want: put the guests in the Fishing card ("Ponds where you and your guests fish and let the fish go"). Put the season in the pool card ("Open every day 8 a.m. to 9 p.m., until the start of October"). If we know when it opens in spring, say that too; if not, leave the opening out. Cut the rest of the paragraph or keep only the no-swimming line.

### 6. The homes paragraph has too many numbers in one sentence

- "Most were built in the 1980s and 1990s, with about 1,000 to 2,900 square feet and 3 bedrooms, some with 2." That is six numbers and it reads oddly at the end.
- What I want: "Most were built in the 1980s and 1990s. Most have 3 bedrooms and about 1,500 to 2,000 square feet." Two short sentences. The listings support the middle range.

### 7. Smaller things

- The top says the same thing twice in a row. The line under the byline is "Myrtle Trace is a 55+ neighborhood of mostly one-story houses just outside Conway..." and the short answer starts "Myrtle Trace is a neighborhood of about 500 homes just outside Conway." Start the short answer somewhere else: "About 500 homes. At least one person in each home must be 55 or older..." or change the line under the byline.
- The drive times are three times in the first two screens: the icon row, the short answer, and the first line of "What is close". The short answer can keep them for Google. In "What is close", say something the icons do not, like what the beach drive is like, or put the hospital and Walmart on the map instead of repeating the minutes.
- "On a home at that price that you live in, plan for these costs:" Two "that"s. Say "If you live in the home, plan for these costs:".
- "In Chapter3's experience, many HOAs here do not allow renting at all. Myrtle Trace does." Up top it says "In our experience". Pick one way. I would just say "Many HOAs here do not allow renting at all. Myrtle Trace does, for a year or longer."
- The bottom CTA: "One of our agents will go over the HOA's rules and rental policy with you." This buyer is moving in, not renting it out. Say "the HOA's rules and the costs".
- FAQ flood answer: "though a lender may still ask for a policy" is the explanation I asked you to take out of the body. Say "Probably not. If you want it, a policy here usually costs about $550 a year."
- On the phone, the comparison is four cards and ours looks like the other three. Make the Myrtle Trace card stand out so the eye lands on it.
- Not a send-back, still a question: the big orange line is still "A 55+ neighborhood close to the hospital." I would rather it say what living there is like. The line under it already says pool, ponds and the club calendar, so the orange line could say "A pool, 15 fishing ponds and about 70 events a month" and drop the hospital to the icon row where it already is.
