# Buyer read, v5: Myrtle Trace (Conway, 55+)

Read on a phone, top to bottom, as the 62-year-old from Ohio. Site menu skipped.

## Notes as I read

### 1. I don't know this word
- "HOA". The page spells it out at first use, in the "Looking at a home" box ("homeowners association (HOA)"). That is good. Later it just says "the HOA" and "HOA fee" and I half remember. I would call it "the neighborhood association".
- "55+ neighborhood" is fine. I know what it means.
- "builder's original floor plans" in "What are the homes like". I would call it "the house designs when it was built". I do not know what a floor plan has to do with me.
- "lots" in "Owners of those lots must accept golf balls and noise". I would say "homes".
- "high-risk flood zone" and "FEMA's flood map". I know FEMA. I do not know what "high-risk" means in numbers, and the page does not say.
- "city limits" and "mailing address". I get it, but I do not know why it matters until the next clause ("no Conway city tax"). Fine.
- "ZIP code" and "middle policy price" in the small print. "Middle policy price" means nothing to me.
- "deed records" in Sources. I skip it.
- "approved dog run". I guess it is a fenced spot for the dog. Nobody says who approves it.
- "private roads". I do not know if I pay to fix them. The page says the fee covers them, so fine.

### 2. Why are you telling me this?
- "The HOA's posted copy of its rules states it." I only wanted to know the age rule. Who posted it does not help me.
- "Drive times are OpenStreetMap estimates with no traffic." I do not know what OpenStreetMap is. I only care that it is a different number in July.
- "The HOA says the back gates keep out drivers who cut through the neighborhood." That is a reason, I can use it. OK.
- "planned by neighbors on the activities committee". Nice, but not needed.
- "The federal flood law requires flood insurance for a home loan only when the home is in a high-risk flood zone." This is how the rule works. I wanted: "You will not have to buy it." The line above it says that already, so this one is extra.

### 3. This sounds like a report
- "Horry County deed records" and "Read October 5, 2026" in the Sources line. That is a footnote, I skip it.
- "Updated October 5, 2026" under the title. Fine, small.
- "The flood cost is FEMA's middle policy price for this ZIP code." Report talk.
- "Map of Myrtle Trace and the drive by car to the beach, groceries, the hospital and the airport. Map data (c) OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL)." "ODbL" is gibberish to me.
- "Photo: Pubdog, public domain, via Wikimedia Commons" and "CC0 (public domain dedication)". Report talk. I would skip it, but the lines saying "the photo shows the town, not Myrtle Trace itself" are honest and I liked that.
- The comparison section and the flood and tax paragraphs read like a form. The rest reads like a person.
- "In Chapter3's experience, many HOAs here do not allow renting a house at all." This one sounds like a person. Good.

### 4. I skipped this
- The "Myrtle Trace at a glance" cards, after the first two. They repeat the Short Answer and the map.
- The whole map block. The picture map is fine, but the "SHOW THE LIVE MAP" box, the "Opens Google Maps here" line and the "Open Myrtle Trace in Google Maps" link are three things that do the same job.
- The small gray map caption with "ODbL".
- The two photo captions after the first sentence.
- The "Want to know the insurance cost first?" box. The button says "WE HELP GET INSURANCE QUOTES IF YOU NEED THEM." and that reads like a sign, not like a button I press.
- The Sources paragraph.
- The FAQ. Every answer is something I already read above. I stopped at the FAQ.
- Everything after the last orange button. The footer talks about "investor-focused brokerage", "DSCR financing", "ROI data for serious real estate investors". That is not for me. It made me wonder if this company is for me at all.

### 5. Now I want to know... (after each section)
- After the top: Can I afford it, and is it nice? Yes, the price shows up in the cost section. It is not on the first screen.
- After "Where is Myrtle Trace": What is Conway like, is it quiet, is it far from stuff? Answered a few sections later.
- After "What are the homes like": How big are they? How many bedrooms and how many square feet are they now? The page gives only "2 or 3 bedrooms" from the builder's original plans. Not answered. How old are the homes? Not answered. Do they have garages? Not answered. One level or two? Not answered. This is a big hole for me.
- After "What is there to do": Is it friendly, do people actually go? The page gives a list but nothing about who lives there. Partly answered.
- After "area around": Where do I shop, eat, see a doctor? Walmart and the hospital are named. Restaurants and a regular doctor are not.
- After "Who can live": Can my grandkids stay a while? Page says they can visit. For how long, it does not say. Half answered.
- After "How much": What is the total per month? The page gives the pieces (HOA about $100, tax a little over $1,000 a year, flood about $550 if I want it). It never adds them up. I would have to do the math. Home insurance for a normal policy is not given at all. That is a real cost and the page sends me to "ask us for a quote".
- After "flood insurance": What about hurricanes and wind? Not answered. I live in Ohio. I worry about storms at the beach. The page only talks about flood.
- After "pets, rent, fence": Can I have a garden? A garage? A golf cart? Not answered. How many pets? Not answered.
- After "What does a move look like": Is Diane a real person? The page says "Example", so I think it is made up. I do not mind. It did help me. But it only repeats facts already above it.
- After the comparison: Which one is best for me? The page does not say. It only says Myrtle Trace is cheapest and farthest. And the price column was cut off on my phone (see 6 and 10).

### 6. Too many numbers

- Cost section: "$300,000", "$100 a month", "a little over $1,500", "$300,000" again, "a little over $1,000 a year". Five money numbers in four short paragraphs. They are rounded and I can say them out loud. OK, but it is a lot to hold. A little table would help me.
- "The pool is open from 8 in the morning to 9 at night". Fine.
- Map: "about 3 min", "about 3 min", "about 15 min", "about 15 min", "about 20 min". Too many "about"s. I would just want "beach 15 minutes".
- Flood: "about $550 a year" next to "$300,000" next to "$1,000" within a few paragraphs. Fine, but heavy.
- Nothing is so exact I would never say it. "A little over $1,500" is good, it sounds like a person.

- I do not know the road names. If I drive in from the airport, what road do I take?

The photos are of downtown Conway, a nice town. I liked them, but I still do not know what the neighborhood looks like.
- It does not say how close the golf course is, or where Burning Ridge is on the map.
- I wanted a picture of the pond and of a typical house.

## Answers

### What did you want to know first? Where did I find it?
I wanted to know what a house costs and what I would pay each month. I found the house price in the middle (the cost section, about two thirds down). The monthly picture is split into pieces and never added up. Home insurance, I never found.

### Three things I liked
1. The pool, the clubhouse and 70 activities a month. It sounds like I would meet people. Bingo and line dancing are what I would want.
2. The fish-and-release ponds. My grandkids can come and fish. That is a picture I can see.
3. It is cheap for the area (about $300,000), 3 minutes to a hospital and to Walmart, and no city tax. And it is honest about drawbacks: the golf balls, no fences, no sheds, you take care of your own roof.

### Questions I still have, most important first
1. What is my total cost each month, with house insurance, water, power and the HOA fee together? (Not answered; flood only, "ask us" for the rest.)
2. How big are the homes now, how old are they, how many floors, is there a garage? (Not answered. Only the builder's original 2 or 3 bedrooms.)
3. What about hurricanes and wind? Does insurance cover it and what does it cost? (Not answered.)
4. Can I see what the neighborhood looks like? A house, the pool, a pond. (No picture.)
5. How long can the grandkids stay? How many pets? (Not answered.)
6. Which months is the pool open? (Not answered.)
7. How is the traffic and which road gets me to the beach? (Only "15 minutes".)
8. What would it cost to rent here, in case I am not sure? (Not answered; it says renting is allowed for a year.)

### Would you call this company?
Maybe: the page was honest about the bad parts and told me plain things, but the footer says "investor-focused brokerage" and "DSCR financing", and that made me think they are for investors, not for a retired couple from Ohio.

## Grade: 7 out of 10 for "easy to read"
The main text is plain and short, and I could follow it. It loses points for the cut-off price column in the table on a phone, the heavy sources and photo credit lines, the four "go read this" links, the investor footer, and the first number-heavy paragraphs.
