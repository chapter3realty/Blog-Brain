## What I would send back

### 1. You forgot the neighborhood's own amenities

- The page says "Owners share a clubhouse, pools and a dock on the waterway" and then spends a whole section on the Ocean Club.
- The neighborhood has its own clubhouse with an indoor pool and an outdoor pool, a fitness center, tennis, pickleball and bocce courts, a fire pit, an arts and crafts room and a lifestyle director. The posted rules list every one of them (ledger row 42 note; rows 41 and 42).
- A retiree searching this neighborhood types "pickleball". He gets nothing.
- Want: make the first section about what is inside the neighborhood: the clubhouse, pools, courts and the lifestyle director. Put the Ocean Club after it as the extra. These are facts that rarely change and cost nobody anything if one changed (PLAIN-9), so state them plainly.

### 2. "It serves all of Grande Dunes and is not the neighborhood's clubhouse."

- That is a sentence about what something is not. Nobody knows what "all of Grande Dunes" is.
- Want: one plain line, once: "Grande Dunes is a large area of neighborhoods, golf and shops in Myrtle Beach. Del Webb is one neighborhood inside it." Then you can use "Grande Dunes" without confusing anyone. Also drop "the association for all of Grande Dunes". Say "Grande Dunes' own association" or leave it out.

### 3. Too many photos of downtown, none of the homes

- "The beach in Myrtle Beach, from a high floor", "A fishing pier in Myrtle Beach" and "The SkyWheel on the Boardwalk." That is two phone screens of tourist pictures. The Boardwalk is 15 minutes away. A Del Webb owner does not look at the beach from a hotel tower.
- "What are the houses and villas like?" has no picture at all. That is the section that needs one most.
- Want: cut to one photo of the area at most, and only if it shows the beach these owners use. Put a picture in the houses section: our own photo of a street of houses and a street of villas, or a close crop of the aerial with "houses" and "villas" labelled. No listing or builder photos (`rules/images.md`). If we have no house photo we are allowed to use, ask me for one and I will have an agent take it.

### 4. The "Where is it" section has six pictures in a row

- An aerial with the outline, the area map, the close-up map, then three photos. On a phone that is about two and a half screens of pictures.
- The aerial with the orange outline and the close-up map show the same thing.
- Want: keep the area map with drive times and one of the two close views. Cut the rest.

### 5. "Myrtle Beach has a long sand beach, fishing piers and a Boardwalk with a big Ferris wheel, about 15 minutes away."

- This reads like the beach is 15 minutes away. Two lines up you said 4 minutes.
- Want: "The Boardwalk, with its big Ferris wheel, is about 15 minutes away."

### 6. The Ocean Club cards say nothing

- The pictures are generic cartoons. "Classes and dinners" shows a house. "Clubs" says mahjong, knitting and dominoes and shows playing cards. A buyer learns nothing from the pictures.
- Want: either one real photo of the Ocean Club we are allowed to use, or icons with words and no fake pictures. If we keep illustrations, each one shows the thing its words say.

### 7. The beach club and the HOA fee are said too many times

- The beach club is in the hero chip, the short answer, section one, the cost section and the FAQ.
- "$400 a month for houses and $445 for villas" is in the short answer, the cost section, the comparison and the FAQ.
- "An agent reads the rules with you" is in the houses CTA, above the story, below the story and in the bottom CTA.
- Want: say each one once where it belongs. The FAQ answers in one line and links up. Above the story, delete "A Chapter3 agent reads the neighborhood's guest and age rules with you before you make an offer." The line below the story says it.

### 8. The short answer leads with money

- "Each owner also pays, through the homeowners association (HOA) bill, for the Grande Dunes Ocean Club, a beach club. Listings show an HOA fee of about $400 a month for houses and $445 for villas."
- This is a home buyer page. Price comes later and softly (PLAIN-7). That sentence also has an aside in the middle.
- Want: "Your HOA dues include a beach club on the ocean, with a pool and private beach access." The fee goes in the cost section.
- The phone hero chip "Beach club / Paid in your dues" wraps to five lines and makes the row uneven. Want: "Beach club / Included".

### 9. "Listings show" and "Listings entered in 2026 mostly show"

- That is "records show" again (BUYER-3), and the second one puts a year in the body (PLAIN-6).
- "These are the figures sellers entered, not a bill from the HOA." That is us showing our method (PLAIN-8). Cut it.
- "Some listings say the fee includes..." Either we know what the fee includes or we do not say it. The cost section already says what the bill includes.
- Want: "The HOA fee is about $400 a month for a house and about $445 for a villa." Keep "listings" in the sources line. If the fact checker says the wording must carry "listings", put it in the sources line, not the sentence.

### 10. "Pulte, the builder, no longer sells new homes here."

- I told you buyers do not know who Pulte is (BUYER-2).
- Want: "Every home here is a resale now. The builder has finished."

### 11. Property tax: "More than $2,000 a year"

- That is not an answer. On a $665,000 house it is a lot more than $2,000.
- The comparison section then says "Owners there pay a city tax too." and never says how much. That raises a worry and leaves it.
- Want: give a round figure for a house and for a villa you live in, from the tax rows (68 to 71), and say it already includes the city tax. Put that in the cost section and delete the city tax line from the comparison.

### 12. "Pets" and "Boat dock" cards under "Who can live here, and who can visit?"

- A boat dock has nothing to do with who can live here.
- Want: the dock goes with the neighborhood's amenities (item 1). The pet card can stay. Add the guest amenity cards here: each home gets two guest cards for the pools and clubhouse (row 46). That is the visiting answer a grandparent wants.

### 13. "At least one person in every home that someone lives in must be 55 or older"

- "Every home that someone lives in" is clumsy.
- Want: "At least one person living in each home must be 55 or older."

### 14. The story: real tension, weak skill

- The tension is good and true for this place: the grandsons want the whole summer and the rule allows 90 nights in 12 months.
- The skill is weak. "Their Chapter3 agent reads the neighborhood's rules with them and finds..." Anyone can read a paper. The fix is the buyers giving something up: "Nancy plans a shorter summer stay."
- Want: show what the agent knows that the buyers would not. The rule counts nights in any 12 months in a row, not per calendar year, so the agent lays the summer and the Christmas week on a calendar with them before they offer and they see the exact number of nights that fit. End on what they can now do: the boys' dates written on the calendar, inside the rule. Keep "Example:", the present tense and first names. Do not add a fine or a penalty unless a verified row gives one.
- The heading "How does a Chapter3 agent check the guest rule before you buy?" is about us. Nobody types that. Want: "How long can grandchildren stay in Del Webb at Grande Dunes?" Then the story answers a real question.
- The guest rule is already stated in "Who can live here". In the story, do not restate the number as a fact. Let the agent find it.

### 15. Story placement

- It is now seventh of nine. The comparison table comes after it.
- Want: put the comparison above the story, so the story is the last thing before the FAQ.
