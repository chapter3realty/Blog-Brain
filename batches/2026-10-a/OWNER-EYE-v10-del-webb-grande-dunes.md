## What I would send back

### 1. The property tax is still "More than $2,000 a year"

- I sent this back on version 9. It is not fixed.
- On a $665,000 house you live in, the bill is closer to $4,000. "More than $2,000" is true and tells the buyer nothing.
- Want: one round figure for a house and one for a villa you live in, city tax included, from rows 68 to 71. Something like "about $4,000 a year on a house and about $2,500 on a villa, if you live in it." Have the fact checker confirm the two figures before they go in.
- Same problem one row up: "One-time fee to the HOA when you buy: At least $3,000". "At least" is not an answer. The rule is a year of the HOA's regular dues, or half of one percent of the price if that is more. Want: "About one year of HOA dues, paid once when you buy."

### 2. "Everyone else who lives in the home must be 19 or older, under the neighborhood's rules."

- This is the first sentence of "Who can live here, and what are the rules?" "Everyone else" than who? The 55 rule is not in this section anymore. It is only in the short answer, two screens up.
- Want: start the section with the rule itself. "At least one person living in each home must be 55 or older. Everyone else who lives there must be 19 or older." Then the sentence about the 55-year-old dying or moving out.

### 3. Nobody knows what a villa is

- The page says "houses and villas" eleven times and never says what a villa is. A buyer from Ohio thinks a villa is a big house in Italy.
- Want, once, in the houses section: "A villa is a one-story home joined to one other home by a shared wall." (The listings call them semi-detached. Have the checker confirm "one-story" or drop it.)

### 4. "In one villa section, the HOA takes care of the roofs, gutters and outside paint, and those owners pay an extra fee for it."

- "In one villa section" makes the buyer ask which one, and whether the villa he likes is in it. The page never says.
- Want: if every villa is in that section, say "For villas, the HOA takes care of the roofs, gutters and outside paint. Villa owners pay a little more for it." If not, name the section the way a buyer would see it on a listing. The CTA under it can still offer to check the exact home.

### 5. The houses section still has no picture of a house

- Two aerials: the outline of the whole neighborhood, and "A street of homes in Del Webb at Grande Dunes, from above." Both show gray roofs. A buyer cannot tell a house from a villa or what the front of a home looks like.
- Last time I said: a street of houses and a street of villas, or a close crop of the aerial with "houses" and "villas" labelled. Neither is done.
- Want now: label the street crop ("Houses" on one row, "Villas" on another, if both are in the frame). Then ask me for a street-level photo of a house and a villa and I will have an agent take them. No listing or builder photos.

### 6. The first phone screen says the same thing twice, and the beach club three times

- Lead line: "Del Webb at Grande Dunes is a 55+ neighborhood of houses and villas beside the Intracoastal Waterway in Myrtle Beach."
- Short answer, right under it: "Del Webb at Grande Dunes has houses and villas beside the Intracoastal Waterway, a channel for boats along the coast."
- Beach club: the orange sub ("A 55+ neighborhood with a beach club."), the chip ("Beach club / Included") and the short answer, all on one screen.
- Want: cut the lead line under the byline, or make it say something new (how many homes, item "forgot" below). Keep the beach club in the sub and the short answer. The chip can show something else a buyer cares about, like "Pickleball / In the neighborhood".

### 7. The story's skill sentence changes nothing

- "The agent knows that the guest nights are counted over any 12 months in a row, not a calendar year."
- For a summer and a Christmas week, the count comes out the same either way. The reader reads that sentence, waits for it to matter, and it never does. The fix is still the buyers giving up part of the summer.
- The good part: the agent asks how long the boys will stay, counts the nights on a calendar before the offer, and they offer knowing the dates fit. That is the skill. Catching it before they are under contract is the win.
- Want: cut the "12 months in a row, not a calendar year" sentence, or make it matter (for example, the boys come at Christmas and again the next summer, and the agent counts both inside one 12 months). Say once why it matters to do it before the offer: after closing, the rule is the rule. Keep "Example:", present tense and first names. Do not add a fine.
- "Moving from Columbus": Ohio or Georgia? Say "Columbus, Ohio".

### 8. The houses CTA does not match its own heading

- Heading: "See a house or villa with an agent." Text: "One of our agents finds out what the HOA keeps up on that home."
- Want one thing. Either "Ask what the HOA takes care of on the home you like." with that text, or a showing CTA with showing text.

### 9. Under the story, two lines say the same thing

- "If grandchildren will stay with you, count their nights on a calendar before you offer." Then "An agent at Chapter3 can read the guest rules with you before you make an offer." Then the bottom CTA: "An agent at Chapter3 will read the HOA's age, guest and pet rules with you."
- Want: one line under the story, as a CTA: "Tell us the dates your family plans to visit. An agent counts them against the rule before you make an offer." Let the bottom CTA say something else, like seeing the home.

### 10. The comparison line

- "Del Webb at Grande Dunes is the only one of these four with a beach club in the dues, and it is about 4 minutes from the beach."
- Del Webb North Myrtle Beach is also about 4 minutes from the beach, in the same table. The sentence makes it sound like Grande Dunes is the only close one.
- Want: "Del Webb at Grande Dunes is the only one of these four with a beach club in the dues."
- Myrtle Trace shows "$95". Every other fee says "About". Make it "About $95".
- On the phone, "In or near:" reads oddly as a label. "Town:" is enough.

### 11. The Ocean Club cards say little

- "Private beach access / For Ocean Club members." You just said every owner is a member. It says nothing.
- "A big pool / A large outdoor pool and a hot tub." Big, then large.
- Want: "Private beach access / A members-only way onto the sand." "Pool and hot tub / A large outdoor pool by the ocean." Only what the rows support.
- A buyer will ask how far the Ocean Club is from his house. The ledger has no drive time for it (row 65). Get one from the router like the other drive times and add it to this section.

### 12. "Your HOA bill includes basic lawn care and the fee for Grande Dunes' own association."

- A buyer does not know what Grande Dunes' own association is or what he gets for it. It is a fee for a thing he cannot picture.
- Want: say what he gets. "Your HOA bill includes basic lawn care, the beach club and the upkeep of Grande Dunes' shared roads and grounds", if a row supports the last part. If not, "Your HOA bill includes basic lawn care and the beach club." The beach club is the reason he is on this page; say it here too.

### 13. "Day dock / On the waterway, with no boat ramp"

- "Day dock" is a term. The card leads with what it does not have.
- Want: "Boat dock / Tie up your boat on the waterway from dawn to dusk." Leave the ramp out, or put it last.

### 14. Sources line: "HOA rules, posted copy revised January 2019"

- That tells the reader our rules are seven years old. I said do not announce old data (PLAIN-9). The ledger keeps the date. The page does not.
- Want: the link text "HOA rules".
