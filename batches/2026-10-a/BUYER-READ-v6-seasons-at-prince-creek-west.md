# Buyer read, v6: Seasons at Prince Creek West

I read the text and looked at all ten phone screenshots, top to bottom. I skipped the site menu. I ignored the black "Draft for review" bar that sits over the first paragraph, since that is the preview and not the page.

## Notes as I read

### 1. I don't know this word

- **"HOA"**. The page never says what it stands for. It first shows up as "Under the HOA's rules" and then again in the orange box ("reads the HOA rules with you"). I would call it "the neighborhood's rules and fees," and I only half know that.
- **"the larger Prince Creek West associations"** and **"Another Prince Creek West association."** I don't know what these are or how many there are. I thought there was one neighborhood, Seasons. Now there are "associations" inside something bigger.
- **"transfer fee."** I would call it a fee for the sale.
- **"stormwater fee."** I would call it a drainage charge, but I'm guessing.
- **"design committee."** I don't know who they are. I would call them "a neighbors' board that says yes or no."
- **"high-risk flood zone"** and **"federal flood insurance law."** I get "the area that floods most." I don't know the law and don't want to.
- **"Del Webb."** "Both Del Webb neighborhoods" assumes I know what that is. I don't.
- **"Prince Creek West"** itself. The FAQ at the bottom is the first place that tells me what it is.
- **"Brookgreen Gardens."** Is it a garden, a museum, a park? The page doesn't say. Same for "live oaks" under the photo. I would call those "big old trees."
- Byline: **"Operations Officer"** and **"Broker-in-Charge."** I don't know what either person does.
- Sources line: **"Medicare Care Compare," "deed records," "ZIP code 29576," "the middle FEMA policy price."** I skip all of it, but I noticed I didn't know them.
- Footer (site-wide, but I scrolled through it): **DSCR, STR, ROI, BRRRR, 1031 Exchange, AfBA, RESPA, NMLS.** I know none of these.

### 2. Why are you telling me this?

- "Your HOA bill also covers your share of the larger Prince Creek West associations, so you get one bill." I wanted to know how much the bill is. I got how it is sent.
- "Usually not, because the center of every lot in Seasons is outside the high-risk flood zone." I don't know what the "center of a lot" has to do with my house.
- "A lender has to require flood insurance only when the house itself is in that zone, under the federal flood insurance law. That zone is on the government's flood map." This is how a rule works. I wanted to know if my house floods.
- "When the builder sold new homes here, it described open, one-story floor plans." This is what the builder said, not what the homes are like.
- "Seasons has a Murrells Inlet address but is not inside any town, so there is no city tax." I understand "no city tax." I don't know what "not inside any town" means for who plows, who does the trash, who comes if I call 911.

### 3. This sounds like a report

- "Another Prince Creek West association charges a transfer fee of up to a quarter of 1 percent of the price." Reads like a form.
- "Property tax on a home at that price that you live in is about $2,000 a year, with the county's stormwater fee."
- "A lender has to require flood insurance only when the house itself is in that zone, under the federal flood insurance law."
- "Sources: HOA rules, Tidelands Waccamaw on Medicare Care Compare, Horry County deed records, FEMA flood map, Federal flood insurance law. Read October 5, 2026... The flood cost is the middle FEMA policy price in ZIP code 29576."
- "Map data: U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line; Horry County GIS." under both maps.
- The photo credits block ("CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons").
- "Seasons at Prince Creek West and Myrtle Trace are both outside any city, and both Del Webb neighborhoods are inside one." Mild, but it reads like a government sentence.

The rest reads like a person talking. The story, the pools, the grandchildren part and the house rules cards read fine.

### 4. I skipped this

- The flood paragraph that starts "A lender has to require flood insurance..."
- The "Photo credits" block.
- The FAQ at the bottom. Most of it I had already read: hospital, under 55, beach distance, how big.
- The "Sources" paragraph.
- The road numbers on the first map (707, 17BYP, 17BUS, 544, 31). I looked at the pins and drive times and nothing else.
- Everything under the dark footer line: the long list of links and the small print about the mortgage company.

### 5. Now I want to know... (after each section)

- **After the first screen and "The short answer":** What does it cost? Not in this section. I found it four screens down.
- **After "What can you do":** Is the golf course on the map part of this? Can I play it? Not answered. The page only lists the pools, bocce, tennis and fitness center. Tennis has no tile of its own.
- **After "Can grandchildren stay":** If I must live there six months a year, can I keep my house in Ohio and split the year? Partly answered. It says six months, then nothing about what that means for someone who is not moving full time.
- **After "Where is Seasons":** What is the area like to eat out, shop, see a doctor? Hospital and Publix are answered. Restaurants and doctors are not.
- **After "What is Murrells Inlet like":** What would a normal week look like? It is two sentences and three photos. Not answered.
- **After "What are the homes like":** How big, how old, how many bedrooms, what is for sale now? Not answered. It says "open, one-story" and "optional extra room." That is all.
- **After "What does it cost to live here":** How much is the monthly HOA bill? Not answered anywhere on the page. This is the biggest hole for me.
- **After "Do you need flood insurance":** What about hurricanes and wind? I'm from Ohio and that is the thing I'm afraid of. Not answered. The page only talks about flooding.
- **After "House rules":** Fine. It answered what I would ask, except the fence line (see section 8).
- **After "Compare":** Which one is right for someone like me? The table shows price and beach time. It does not say who each one suits.

### 6. Too many numbers

- "Yes, as visitors: anyone under 18 can stay up to 60 days a year, and no one under 18 can live there." Three numbers in one sentence (18, 60, 18).
- "Publix on Highway 707 is about 5 minutes away, and Tidelands Waccamaw Community Hospital is about 8." Three numbers (707, 5, 8). I read "707" as a number to remember and it isn't.
- "Another Prince Creek West association charges a transfer fee of up to a quarter of 1 percent of the price." I would never say this out loud. I would have to do the math on $500,000 to know what it means.
- "Seasons has room for about 440 homes on about 190 acres. More than half of that land is shared by the owners." Fine for two numbers, but I don't do anything with them.
- The first map has about a dozen numbers on it (roads, minutes, miles).

### 7. Where is this?

Mostly yes, and the maps are why.

- The first map put it in my head. Seasons is the pin on the left, inland and west of the water. The beach and MarshWalk are to the right, about 10 minutes. The hospital is a bit south, 8 minutes. The airport is up at the top, 25 minutes. I can see I am not on the ocean.
- The second map shows the entrance, the clubhouse on Grand Cypress Way, a pond, a golf course next to it, and Publix at 5 minutes. I could tell where I would turn in. The street names are very small on a phone. I could not tell homes from ponds without zooming.
- I did not see "Garden City" on the map, but the text says the beach is "in Garden City." I could not point to it.
- The airport has no name. I assume Myrtle Beach.
- "Pawleys Island" in the first photo caption means nothing to me. I only know it is "south."
- I wanted a picture of: the clubhouse, a street in Seasons with the houses on it, and the inside of one home. There is no picture of anything that is actually in the neighborhood.
- The three Murrells Inlet photos (boats, sunset, big trees) helped me feel the area. The row is cut off on the right on my phone. I only saw the boats fully. The second caption ("Sunset at... Park") was cut off and I did not know I could swipe.
- The pool, bocce and clubhouse pictures are cartoon drawings. They are pleasant, and they tell me what is there, but they don't show me what it looks like.

### 8. That's not an answer

- "What does it cost to live in Seasons at Prince Creek West?" It answers with a sale price, a one-time fee, a transfer fee and a tax. It never gives the monthly HOA bill. I felt that missing number.
- "Do you need flood insurance?" Answer: "Usually not." Then: "Some lots, 45 in all, have a corner inside the high-risk zone... get a flood quote before you offer." I don't know which 45 lots, so the answer for my house is "ask someone."
- "If family will stay with you, read the visitor rules and count the beds before you make an offer." That is "read something," though I do like the "count the beds" part.
- "Fences: Yes, once the design committee approves it. It must be as wide as the house." I do not understand "as wide as the house." Is the fence as wide as the house? Is that the most it can be? The least?
- "Want the flood cost before you offer?" The button reads "WE HELP GET INSURANCE QUOTES IF YOU NEED THEM." I read that as a sentence, not a button.
- "Homes usually sell for about $500,000." I don't know if that is the low end, the middle or the top. I can't tell what a smaller or bigger house costs.

### 9. Why so many dates?

Four.

1. "Updated October 5, 2026" under the title.
2. "Every July, Donna and Steve had their grandchildren..."
3. "By the next July, there were bunk beds..."
4. "Read October 5, 2026" in the sources paragraph.

The two July lines are part of the story and did not bother me. The two October 5 lines are the same date. I did not need either one.

### 10. Where is the good part?

On the first screen I learned: it is 55+, it has two pools, and it is about 10 minutes by car from the beach. That is a good first screen. The first big button comes before any facts.

"The short answer" starts with "room for about 440 homes" and then "Every home where someone lives must have a resident 55 or older." That is a rule first. The pools and grandchildren come in the second paragraph.

What it costs is not on the first screen and not in the short answer. It is in the middle: "Seasons homes usually sell for about $500,000." I did not find out whether I can afford it until about the fifth screen. Whether I can buy (I am 62, so yes) was answered on the first screen.

## Your questions, in order

**What did you want to know first? Where did you find it?**

1. Can someone my age live here? First screen. Yes.
2. How far from the beach? First screen, top line: about 10 minutes.
3. What does a house cost? Middle of the page (about screen 5): about $500,000.
4. What do I pay each month? Never. The HOA bill is mentioned and never given a number.

**Three things I liked about this place, from the page**

1. Two pools, one inside for the cooler months, plus a fitness center, tennis and bocce. I would use all of that.
2. Grandchildren can stay up to 60 days a year. That was the first time I felt a page was talking about my life.
3. Beach, Publix, hospital and the MarshWalk are all 5 to 10 minutes away. And there is no city tax.

**Questions I still have, ranked by how much they matter to my decision**

1. What is the monthly HOA bill, and what does it cover (lawn, trash, water, the pools)?
2. What about hurricanes, wind and the full cost of insuring a house here, not only flood?
3. What are the houses like: size, age, bedrooms, and what is the range of prices around $500,000?
4. Can I live here six months and keep my house in Ohio? What does "live there at least six months" mean in real life?
5. Is the golf course part of it, and can I play?
6. How bad is the traffic from May to September? I found "no-traffic estimates" only in the small print at the bottom, and I trust the 10 minutes less now.
7. What is the airport, and can I fly to Ohio from it?
8. Where do people eat and shop in Murrells Inlet? Is it busy and loud in summer?
9. What does the fence rule mean?
10. Who does the town stuff (trash, police, roads) if there is no town?

**Would you call this company?**

Probably yes, because the page was honest about the rules and offered to read them with me, but I would want the monthly HOA bill in my head before I dialed.

## Did the story stick? What do I remember?

Yes. The first one did, the second one did not.

**Donna and Steve (grandchildren).** I remember:

- They lived in Richmond and had their grandchildren for a week every July.
- They wanted to retire near the beach without losing that week.
- Donna asked whether kids could even stay over in a 55+ place. The agent read the visitor rules with them before they made an offer.
- They picked a house with a spare bedroom over one with a bigger kitchen. That choice is what stayed with me, because it is a real trade a person makes.
- The next July there were bunk beds in the spare room and three beach towels on hooks by the back door. The towels are the part I could see.

What stuck is the towels and the kitchen trade. It also made the 60 days a year feel real instead of a rule. It is in a gray box labeled "Example:" and that label made it feel a little like a school lesson for a second. I read it anyway. I wondered why three towels. I assumed three grandchildren, but it does not say.

**The couple from Blackmoor.** I remember almost nothing. They sold a house in a golf neighborhood and bought in Seasons a few minutes away. I don't know why they moved, what they liked, or what they gave up. It did not stick.

## Did the pictures and maps help me picture the place?

- **Maps: yes, a lot.** The first map told me where I would live compared to the beach, hospital and airport. The second told me where the entrance and clubhouse are. The words on them are small on a phone and I had to squint.
- **Photos: some.** The three Murrells Inlet photos (boats, sunset, trees) helped me feel the area. The caption on the big beach photo up top says it is Pawleys Island and "not the neighborhood." I appreciated that, but it also meant the biggest picture on the page is of somewhere else.
- **Drawings:** the pools, bocce and clubhouse are cartoons. They told me what is there but not what it is like.
- **What is missing:** a photo of the neighborhood itself. I never saw a house, a street, or the clubhouse. The "What are the homes like" section has no picture at all.

## Grade for "easy to read"

**8 out of 10.** Short sentences, big type, and headings that are questions I would ask. I lost points on the flood and fee paragraphs, the words I did not know (HOA, association, transfer fee, Del Webb), and one table that left me unsure whether Seasons and Myrtle Trace are "outside any city" or in a town. That table says "Town: Murrells Inlet" for Seasons and "Town: Conway" for Myrtle Trace, while the sentence above it says both are outside any city. I could not tell which was true.
