## What I would send back

1. **The Blackmoor story is in the middle of the page, in the homes section.**
   "An agent at Chapter3 helped clients who owned a house in Blackmoor, a golf neighborhood in Murrells Inlet with no age rule. They sold that house and bought in Seasons, a few minutes away."
   I said the story should be one of the last things. This one is a real story and it is stuck under the square footage, where it says nothing about the homes. Move it down next to Carol and Jim, or into the compare section, where it tells people they can move a few minutes and get the 55+ rule. Tell it the way it is in the story bank. Add nothing.
   Then delete the line after it: "A move into a neighborhood with a 55+ rule can be a few minutes from the house you have now." It says nothing a buyer would miss.

2. **The storm photo.** "A marina in Murrells Inlet in the evening" is a black storm cloud over the boats. This page talks about flood zones. The last thing I want next to that is a storm. It is also the second boat photo in a row. Cut it. The sentence above it names the MarshWalk, so if we have a CC photo of the MarshWalk boardwalk, use that one beside that sentence. Keep the fishing boats.

3. **"What are the homes like in Seasons?" has no picture.** It is the one section where a buyer most wants to see something, and it is all text. Photos should be of the topic. We cannot use listing or builder photos, so this is mine to fix: I or an agent can drive in and take a few photos from the street of the houses and the clubhouse front. Put a line in `templates/owner-questions.md` asking for them. Until then, do not fill the spot with a photo of something else.

4. **Too many map pictures in one section.** "Where is Seasons" has the outline photo from above, the area map, and the street map inside Seasons. The outline photo and the street map show the same thing, the shape of the neighborhood. Keep the area map and one of the other two. On the phone the labels on the maps are small; the buyer reader had to squint.

5. **The homes section sounds like a report, not an agent.**
   "The builder described its designs here as open, one-story floor plans. Some designs had an optional extra room."
   Say it as the expert: "The homes are one story with open floor plans. Some have an extra room."
   "Listings show houses built from 2006 to 2016..." and "Most recent listings are on one level." The first sentence says they are one story, the last one makes me think some are not. Pick what is true and say it once: "Homes were built from 2006 to 2016. Most are about 1,700 to 2,500 square feet with 3 bedrooms."
   "Seasons has room for about 440 homes" is a strange way to say it. Give it the realtor feel: about 440 homes, and ponds, woods and walking paths on more than half the land.

6. **"Listings show" all over the page.** It is in the short answer, the cost section, the FAQ, and "(listings)" sits in three table cells. I told you we are the experts. Say "The HOA fee is about $340 a month" and put the source in the sources line. Take "(listings)" out of the table.

7. **The flood section explains how the law works.**
   "On those lots, what counts is whether the house itself is in the zone. Only then does federal law make a lender require a policy."
   A law does not make anyone do anything, and the buyer does not need the how. Say what it means for them: "On 45 lots, one corner of the lot touches the flood zone. If the house itself sits in it, your lender will require flood insurance."
   Then answer the next question. The page gives the cost of a policy outside the zone (about $600). It never says what it costs if the house is inside the zone, and that is the exact cost Carol and Jim "have not planned for". If we have a verified number, give it. If not, the story and the button already offer the quote, so leave the number out instead of hinting at it.

8. **The cost section explains how the bill works.**
   "Your HOA bill includes your share of the fees for the larger Prince Creek West area, so you get one bill." The buyer cares that there is one bill. Cut the rest.
   "One of the larger Prince Creek West associations also charges a transfer fee of up to a quarter of 1 percent of the price." Nobody knows there are several associations, and nobody can do that math. Say it in dollars: "When you buy, there is also a transfer fee of up to about $1,250 on a $500,000 home." Only if the ledger supports that number.
   "Property tax on a home at that price that you live in is about $2,000 a year, the county's drainage fee included." That is not proper English and the drainage fee is us showing off. "If you live in the home, property tax on a $500,000 home is about $2,000 a year."

9. **Dates in the captions.** "from above in 2023" is in two captions, and "about $340 a month in 2026" in the cost section. Say the date once, at the top. Take the years out of the captions and the fee line.

10. **The flood story should finish the job.** Carol and Jim is good: there is a deadline (offer that night), something they did not see coming (the corner of the lot), and the agent catches it. Two things:
    - Let the agent's skill show more. The agent does not just "know". The agent checks that lot on the flood map before the offer. Say that.
    - "write their offer with the yearly flood cost in front of them" is close to a phrase. Say it literally: "Carol and Jim write their offer knowing what flood insurance on that house will cost each year."

11. **The compare table opens on tax.** "Seasons at Prince Creek West and Myrtle Trace are outside any city, so owners in both pay no city tax." The section is a comparison of four neighborhoods. Open on what a buyer compares first: the beach drive and the clubhouse. The city tax line can stay as the second sentence. Make the Seasons row bold so a reader on a phone finds it.

## What did the page forget that a buyer types into Google?

Checked against the must-answer list for a 55+ page:

1. **Is Seasons gated?** Not on the page at all. The builder called it gated and the site plan shows a gatehouse, but nothing says it is still staffed or working in 2026. If we cannot say it plainly and safely, put it on the owner question list with a date. I can tell you.
2. **Clubs, events, an activity director.** "In a normal week you can swim..." lists equipment, not life. I asked for how many events an HOA holds. No source was found. Put it on the owner question list; I or an agent can find out.
3. **Golf carts.** Carts must go in the garage, not on the street. That is a rule people ask about in a 55+ place. Add it to the house rules cards.
4. **Is there golf?** Blackmoor is a golf neighborhood and the buyer reader asked if Seasons has a course. Say no course in Seasons if that is true, and what is close.
5. **Homeowners insurance and storms.** Seasons is west of Bypass 17, outside the state's coastal wind pool area. That is good news for a buyer worried about hurricanes and insurance, and we have it verified. Say what it means for them in one sentence, in the flood section.
6. **New or resale.** The builder is done. Say plainly that every home here is a resale, so nobody goes looking for a sales center.
7. **What owners find hard.** The page has no kind drawbacks besides flood on 45 lots. The 25-minute airport drive is there. If there is nothing else verified, fine.
8. **Homes for sale now.** No link to listings. The site has no listing search to point to yet, so the call button covers it. Note it on the gap list.
