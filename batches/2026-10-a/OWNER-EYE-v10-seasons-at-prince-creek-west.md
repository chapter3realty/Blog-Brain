## What I would send back

1. **The top of the page says the same thing three times.**
   "Seasons at Prince Creek West is a 55+ neighborhood near Murrells Inlet, with homes on curving streets around ponds."
   "Seasons at Prince Creek West is near Murrells Inlet, south of Myrtle Beach."
   The name and "near Murrells Inlet" are in the headline, the gray line under the byline and the short answer, all on the first phone screen. Cut the gray line, or start the short answer with something new: "Seasons has about 440 one-story homes around ponds, about 10 minutes from the beach." That is the realtor feel I asked for, and the short answer does not have it yet.
   The orange sub, "A 55+ neighborhood where grandchildren can visit.", makes it sound like visits are the best thing here, and like other places do not allow them. Lead with what a buyer wants: the two pools and the beach 10 minutes away. The grandchildren rule can stay in the short answer.

2. **The pools and courts are listed four times in one section.**
   The intro sentence lists them ("In a normal week you can swim indoors or outside, play tennis or bocce, and work out at the fitness center."), the cards list them, the sentence under the cards lists them, and the photo caption lists them. They are also in the hero icons and the short answer. Keep the cards and the photo. Cut the intro sentence or make it about something the cards do not say.
   The card lines say nothing: "Play tennis outside", "A court for bocce games", "Swim outside in the warm months". Either give a fact (how many courts, when the indoor pool is open) or leave just the title.
   The tennis card drawing has a pickleball paddle in it. If there is no pickleball, a buyer will think there is. Use a tennis racket.

3. **"Can you live in Seasons part of the year?" is confusing.**
   "Yes, if Seasons is your main home. Under the homeowners association (HOA) rules, the resident who is 55 or older must live there at least six months a year."
   The answer says yes and then says you have to be there half the year. Say it straight: "Yes, if you live here at least six months a year. One person in the home must be 55 or older."
   Then answer the next question a snowbird asks: can I rent it out while I am gone? The page already has the answer in the rules cards (a lease must be at least a year). Say it here: "You cannot rent it out for the months you are away. A lease here must be at least a year."
   "No one under 18 can live there, though grandchildren can come to stay." This is the third time on the page (short answer, here, FAQ). Cut it here.

4. **The wind sentences explain an insurer, not what it means for the buyer.**
   "Seasons is also outside the coastal area served by the state's backup insurer for wind. That insurer sells hurricane coverage where it is hard to buy."
   Nobody knows what a "backup insurer for wind" is, and the reader has to work out the good news on their own. I asked for one sentence on what it means for them. If the ledger supports it, say it plainly, for example: "Seasons is far enough inland that a regular homeowners policy can include hurricane coverage." If no source we have says what it means for a buyer, cut both sentences and keep the link to the insurance page.

5. **The flood section says the same advice three times.**
   "On those lots, if the house itself is in the zone, your lender will require flood insurance." The story says it again. Then after the story: "If the lot you like is one of the 45 that touch the flood zone, get a flood quote on the house before you offer." Then the box says "Get a flood quote on the lot you like ... before you offer." Cut the line after the story. The story and the box already say it.
   "The middle of every lot is outside the high-risk flood zone on the federal flood map, but on 45 lots one corner touches it." "The middle of every lot" is a detail only a mapper cares about. Say: "Most will not. On 45 of the lots, one corner touches the flood zone."

6. **The Seasons card in the compare section still does not stand out on the phone.** I asked for it bold last time. It is the last card, the same look as the others. Make it bold or shade it, or put it first.
   "Like Myrtle Trace, it is outside any city, so owners pay no city tax." The cost section already says "Seasons is outside any town, so there is no city tax." Say it once. Pick "city" or "town", not both.

7. **Golf is still not answered.** The page names Blackmoor as "a golf neighborhood" right next to Seasons, so the next thing a buyer asks is whether Seasons has golf. Last time I asked for "no course in Seasons, and what is close". If the ledger can support it, add one line to "What can you do": no golf course in Seasons, and the closest course and how many minutes. If it cannot, get the row verified first.

8. **The calls to action are mostly about reading rules.**
   "Read the Seasons age rules with an agent." and the closing box "Know the Seasons rules before you make an offer. ... An agent at Chapter3 reads the age, lease and pet rules with you."
   Nobody comes to this page to have rules read to them. They come to find a house. The closing box should be about buying in Seasons: "Want to buy in Seasons? Talk to a specialized agent." Keep one rules offer at most, in the age-rule section. The flood quote box is good.

9. **The FAQ repeats the page.** All five answers are already in the body. Keep the HOA fee and the beach ones. Swap "Is Seasons at Prince Creek West part of Prince Creek?" and "What hospital is closest" for questions people type that the page does not answer above, such as "Does Seasons have a golf course?" (item 7) and "Are there new homes for sale in Seasons?" (no, every home is a resale).
