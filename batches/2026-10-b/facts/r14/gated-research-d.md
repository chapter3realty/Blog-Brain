## Wachesaw Plantation

**Best non-obvious thing (notes, not copy):** The LPGA history that people tie to the name "Wachesaw" belongs to Wachesaw East, a separate course (row 329). The private club's own record is many Carolinas Golf Association championships since 1987 and the Can-Am Junior Team Matches every March, which its members saved when the event lost its first site (rows 321, 322). Its Interim Equity membership is for owners whose primary home is more than 100 miles away (row 320).

**A normal day (notes):** Practice on the 30,000-square-foot range, play the Fazio course, then eat at Kimbels on the bluff above the Intracoastal Waterway and the 18th green (rows 324, 326). A boat can sit in a wet slip or a dry-stack rack at Wacca Wache Marina (row 327).

**Honest trade-off (notes):** The pool area and Magnolia's are being rebuilt, and the new pool and restaurant are a plan with renderings (row 325). Neither the club nor the POA prints dues or gate rules.

## Wachesaw East

**Best non-obvious thing (notes, not copy):** Four televised LPGA events were played here under three names (row 330). Karrie Webb won the first two, and her 1998 score was 19 under (row 331). In 1999 rain cut the event to 36 holes (row 332). In 2017 a timeshare company bought the course and grill, and the course is part of its Ellington at Wachesaw Plantation East resort (row 334).

**A normal day (notes):** The Toasted Tee opens at 7 a.m. for breakfast and serves until 3 p.m. (row 338). The scorecard sets a pace of 4 hours 30 minutes for 18 holes (row 336).

**Honest trade-off (notes):** The course takes public tee times and is part of a vacation-ownership resort, so outside golfers and resort guests share the clubhouse and grill (rows 334, 335).

## The Reserve

**Best non-obvious thing (notes, not copy):** The marina was built from 1999 to 2001 as an amenity for The Reserve's owners and opened to outside boat owners in 2008. It sits at the southern tip of Sandy Island, where Thoroughfare Creek meets the Waccamaw, and the US Coast Guard designates it a Hurricane Hole (rows 345, 346). Every October the Pawleys Island Festival of Music & Art fills a tent at the golf club (row 349).

**A normal day (notes):** Staff launch the boat from a dry storage building that holds about 200 boats, and it can take gas or diesel on a weekday (rows 346, 347). Then 18 holes on the 7,200-yard Norman course, where a golf membership includes dining and social events (rows 342, 343).

**Honest trade-off (notes):** The golf club (McConnell Golf), the marina (Safe Harbor) and the HOA are three separate operators, and none of the three prints its fees (rows 341, 345, 348).

## Willbrook Plantation

**Best non-obvious thing (notes, not copy):** Six markers along the course tell the land's history, hole by hole (row 353). The Oatland cemetery, by the 8th hole, has 38 graves. One headstone marks Albert Doctor, born in 1839 and died in 1919 (row 354). After rice, wealthy Northerners used the land for duck and quail hunting. Litchfield by the Sea bought it in 1984 (row 358).

**A normal day (notes):** Breakfast in the public clubhouse restaurant, then a round past the chimney of the house rebuilt in 1895 (rows 351, 353). The car's barcode opens the Litchfield by the Sea gates on the way to the Beach Club (row 356).

**Honest trade-off (notes):** The course and restaurant belong to Founders Group International and are open to the public. Owners do not control the tee sheet or the prices (rows 351, 352).
