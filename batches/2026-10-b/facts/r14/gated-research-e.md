## Litchfield Plantation

**Best non-obvious thing (notes, not copy):** The owners own the plantation house. For years the developer controlled the association; the owners won control in court in 2018, foreclosed on the developer's loans and took the 1700s house, its pool and 7.35 acres (rows 360, 361). The house and its oak avenue first appear on a 1794 plat (row 362). The beach house on Pawleys Island is also the association's, at 148 Atlantic Avenue (row 365).
**A normal day (notes):** Owners use the house as their club room: a ballroom upstairs, a terrace over the old rice fields where some watch the sunset, and weddings on some weekends that help pay for upkeep (row 363). The beach house is a drive across US 17 (row 165).
**Honest trade-off (notes):** The original developer still owns 329 acres of the old rice fields next to the community (row 366); and most of what is known about the house today comes from a 2019 news story, so ask the association what is open now.

## Litchfield by the Sea

**Best non-obvious thing (notes, not copy):** Owners can watch the beach from home on three live cameras the association runs (row 376). From November 1, 2026, every vendor needs a registered credential, a QR code for a day or a week or an RFID decal for the year, to pass the gates (row 377).
**A normal day (notes):** Tai Chi at the Beach Club on Wednesday morning, yoga and chair yoga on Thursday, a board meeting or an owner appreciation day now and then (row 379). Two patrol cars now cover the grounds (row 378).
**Honest trade-off (notes):** It is many sub-associations inside one association, so the fee and the rules differ from one section to the next (rows 92, 380).

## Heritage Plantation

**Best non-obvious thing (notes, not copy):** The HOA fee includes cable TV with 150 channels and home internet, and the marina's dock house has a crow's nest with a swing on its roof (rows 369, 371). Owners have run the place since 2002 and say they have never levied a special assessment (row 370).
**A normal day (notes):** Morning league tennis or pickleball by the clubhouse, then the daily sunset gathering on the party deck over the Waccamaw, bordered by cypress trees (rows 369, 373, 374).
**Honest trade-off (notes):** The Heritage Club course is public and membership is a separate cost, not part of the HOA fee; trash pickup is also extra (rows 371, 372).

## DeBordieu Colony

**Best non-obvious thing (notes, not copy):** The owners pay for their own beach. A gated community cannot get public money for renourishment, so in 2017 they voted a beach fund that was expected to raise up to $12 million over 17 years, and they built groins at the south end, where a seawall stands (row 393). The local name "Debidue" comes from the Gullah pronunciation of a Lafayette legend (row 383). In October 2026 the state moved to buy Arcadia East, the 3,492 acres just north of DeBordieu, for conservation (row 394).
**A normal day (notes):** Coffee at the Beanery, a round on the Dye course or a set on the clay courts, lunch at the Gazebo by the sand, a boat out of the community ramp into the creek toward North Inlet; dogs on leash on the beach until 7 p.m. (rows 386 to 388, 396).
**Honest trade-off (notes):** The club is separate from the association: four levels of membership, open only to DeBordieu owners, on top of the association fee (row 385). Boats cannot stay at the community dock overnight (row 388).
