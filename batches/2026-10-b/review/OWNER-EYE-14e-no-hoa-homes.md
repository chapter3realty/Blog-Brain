## Grade: 7 of 10

This is a much better page than the one the owner sent back, and a better 7 than 14d's. Every entry is now 158 to 221 words (median 178, up from 103). Ten of 16 areas have a photo, up from 4. The rules table speaks plain English. Every area has its share chip, its price and its own offer. Read against the owner's sentence, it is close.

It is not a 9 for three reasons the owner would send back:
1. Plantation Point says "every home for sale" has no HOA fee, then "An HOA owns the common areas, and we check what it charges for any home." On a page about homes with no HOA, that reads as a contradiction.
2. The top is still 2.5 phone screens. The map alone is about 1.1 screens on a phone.
3. Several entries lead with history or a landmark, not with why people live there. The body of most entries is a list of places nearby, one fact per sentence. It is accurate, but it is a guidebook voice, not "the best agent in town".

## High findings

**High 1. Plantation Point says it has no HOA fee, then says it has an HOA.**
- The chip: "Listed with no HOA fee: every home for sale".
- The body: "An HOA owns the common areas, and we check what it charges for any home."

A reader who came for "no HOA" stops here and asks: so does it have one or not? The ledger (row 171) shows the HOA owns the common-area parcel; row 117 shows the 7 listings had no fee. Both are true, but the page does not say what they mean together. Round 14d's version said "voluntary"; that word was cut because it rested on listing remarks, and nothing replaced it.
Do instead: research what the Plantation Point HOA requires (whether membership and dues are required by recorded covenants, and what the dues are). Then either:
- say it plainly in one sentence, in the buyer's terms, from the verified row (what the buyer pays, if anything, and what rules come with it); or
- if the HOA's dues or covenants are required, take Plantation Point off this page. A page that promises no HOA cannot include a place with a required one.

**High 2. 2.5 phone screens before the first area.**
Better than 3.2, still over the "about two" the brief sets. On a phone the map is three panels stacked, about 950 px. The guide below it is another 0.4 screen.
Do instead:
- On a phone, make the map short and wide (CRAFT 3, "Measure the top"). Put the three panels side by side in one swipe row, or merge the two coast panels into one and put Conway, Loris and Aynor as a small inset.
- Cut the dek or the H1's second line. "16 areas where most homes have no fee." and "Room for a boat, a truck and a shed, from Little River to Aynor." are two subtitles. Keep one.
- Target: entry 1 within 2 screens of the H1.

**High 3. Many entries lead with history or a landmark, and read as a list of places, not a sale.**
The owner: "speak extensively on all of the benefits of that location and why people like it" and "sell them on the location like a real estate agent". The entries are long now, but most paragraphs are one landmark and one date. Few say what owning a house there gives you, or why people pick it over the next place. The weakest leads:
- **1. Downtown**: "Downtown is the old town by the sea. ... The Withers Cemetery off Collins Street is the only known trace of the early settlement that gave the swash its name." A cemetery is the first fact a buyer gets. The real sale is lower down: the boardwalk a quarter hour on foot, open to bikes and dogs from September to April.
- **4. Plantation Point**: leads with street names ("Its streets share their names with old Georgetown County rice plantations"), then "Room is the reason to choose it. A half-acre lot leaves space for a garden, a pool or a long driveway." That sentence is true of any half-acre lot. Then a run of drive minutes.
- **10. Socastee**: leads with the 1936 waterway celebration, the swing bridge "in the state's historic bridge inventory", and a store closed in 1932. The sale for this reader is in paragraph four: houses around $250,000, the lowest price on the page, two minutes from a county boat ramp on the waterway. Lead with that.
- **12. Garden City Beach**: the heading promises "a free pier walk and free beach parking"; the first sentence is "The county's Stanley Drive landing is about 2 miles by road from the center of Garden City." Lead with what the heading promises.
- **14. Loris**: the Gate City name, 1902, the Bank of Loris in 1907, a carillon, "No other place in the country is named Loris." Trivia. The library that lends cake pans and the hospital in town are the life; the reason to choose Loris is not said.
- **16. Aynor**: leads with the Hoe-Down. The heading's first word is "an acre", and the acre is the reason this reader comes.

Do instead: for each entry, write the first two sentences as the answer to "why do people buy a house here instead of somewhere else?" for a buyer who wants a boat, a truck, a shed, land or no fee. Then the day: where the owner launches, eats, walks, what the weekend is. Keep the history only where it changes what you see or do today (Little River's fishing boats, Conway's live oaks, Myrtle Heights' deep front yards). Cut trivia sentences that sell nothing (low 2). Kinds of fact to research: what owners in each area do on a weekend; what the older houses are like inside the lot (carports, garages, room for a shop); what people who live there say they chose it for, from our agents.

## Medium findings

**Medium 1. The price comes before the sale.**
Each entry opens with two chips, and the second is the price: "Houses with no HOA fee here are listed at about $435,000". The owner's order was share, then "speak extensively", then "one line about the average price". The round 14 brief says the same, and CRAFT 5 puts the price after the trade-off. The owner's complaint was "too hung up on the price"; a bold price pill at the top of all 16 entries keeps price in front.
Do instead: keep the share chip under the heading. Move the price into one plain sentence at the end of each entry, just before the offer: "Houses with no HOA fee in Windy Hill are listed at about $745,000."

**Medium 2. Windy Hill's best fact is not Windy Hill's alone.**
Windy Hill leads with the surf zone: "you can still surf and skimboard here." Crescent Beach then says "the beach is a summer surf zone too, open to boards all day", and Myrtle Heights says "the city allows kayaks and surfboards in summer". The lead a buyer "could not guess" turns out to be true of the next entry.
Do instead: keep the surf zone in one entry only (the one where it is strongest), and give the other a fact only it has. Windy Hill has Barefoot Landing's free day docks three minutes away; that is a better lead for a boat owner.

**Medium 3. "We check" eleven times.**
The owner wanted "we check" instead of "you check". The page now says it in nearly every section: "We check the address." (three times in the rule cards), "We check this for the lot you choose.", "We check the lot's zoning before you buy.", "We check which section a house is in before you make an offer.", "we check what it charges for any home.", "We check the rules for the address, and we check the deed for restrictions before you make an offer.", "we read them before you make an offer.", "We check the deed and the county records for an HOA before you make an offer.", plus "We ask the city" and "we ask the town". repeat-check flags "before you make an offer" in three sections. This is his "extremly repetitive writing" in a new place.
Do instead: say it once, strongly, under the rules cards: "Before you make an offer, we check the town or county rules for that lot, the deed and the county records for an HOA." Let the cards give only the rule. Keep one line in the FAQ answer about listings with a blank fee.

**Medium 4. The map names 6 of its 16 areas.**
The owner said "circle them". They are circled, but Myrtle Heights, Pine Lakes, Windy Hill, Crescent Beach, Ocean Drive, Cherry Grove, Surfside, Garden City, Loris and the country around Loris are bare numbers. Cherry Grove and Surfside are names a reader may already know.
Do instead: give every circle a name pill. Where 5 to 8 sit close together, put the pills over the ocean with a short leader line.

**Medium 5. The rule cards on a phone have icons and no words.**
Each town card has four rows marked only by an icon: a boat, a truck, a key and a house. A reader has to guess that the key means renting and the house means a shed or fence. CRAFT 4: icons with words.
Do instead: put a short label beside each icon ("Boat or camper", "Work truck", "Renting", "Shed or fence"), as the desktop table's column heads do.

**Medium 6. More areas, as he asked.**
"do that for as many areas as possible." Still 16. The Garden City entry itself sends the reader to Murrells Inlet's MarshWalk, but Murrells Inlet is not tested as an area. Ledger row 85 counts 31 no-fee houses at $750,000 and up in Murrells Inlet alone.
Do instead: run the same share test on more candidates: the older streets of Murrells Inlet, older sections of Forestbrook, Bucksport, Green Sea, Galivants Ferry and the small beach towns between Myrtle Beach and North Myrtle Beach. Add each one where most homes show no fee. Describe each by its features only.

**Medium 7. Six areas have no photo (one finding).**
Plantation Point, Windy Hill, Ocean Drive, Little River, Socastee and the country around Loris are text only. These are pending the photo partner. Two photos that are in also miss their entry's point:
- Surfside Beach: the frame is mostly sand and sky, with a beach umbrella; the houses are small at the right edge. The entry sells live oaks, sidewalks and golf carts on town streets.
- Pine Lakes: the clubhouse, with a green umbrella in front. The entry sells half-acre lots with room for a workshop. A street of the older houses would show it.
Do instead: add the Surfside street and a Pine Lakes street of older houses to the partner's shot list, with the six missing areas.

## Low findings

- **Low 1. Verifier and method phrases.** "one of the two oldest of the seven swing-span highway bridges in the state's historic bridge inventory" (Socastee). "a state survey recorded its 1950s cottages" (Garden City). "Look for porches, coquina rock walls and wood awnings" (Myrtle Heights) tells the reader to look; say what they will see. Write what it means for the buyer; leave the method in the ledger.
- **Low 2. Trivia that sells nothing.** "A round, grassy mound marks the spot." (Windy Hill). "Across Hog Inlet, Waties Island has almost three miles of undeveloped beach, with no public access." (Cherry Grove: why tell a buyer about a beach they cannot reach?). "No other place in the country is named Loris." "North Carolina is close, too: Tabor City is about 10 miles away by road." "Farms here once shipped strawberries, sweet potatoes, lettuce and poultry to northern markets." Cut, or replace with a fact about life there now.
- **Low 3. Sibling crossover.** The Pine Lakes caption says "The 1927 clubhouse". SIBLING-OWNERS gives the club's 1927 history to best neighborhoods, which has it. Round 14d praised a 1946 line on why the lots are big; it was struck correctly (no source says holes were sold for homes; `no-hoa-homes-struck.json`), so do not bring it back. Caption the photo by what it shows ("The Pine Lakes clubhouse, a few streets from the older houses") and, if research finds a verified reason the older lots are so large, that is this page's own angle.
- **Low 4. "Its namesake dish is chicken bog."** Loris is not named for the dish, and the festival is the Bog-Off. Say "The town's dish is chicken bog: chicken, rice and sausage."
- **Low 5. Trade-offs that skip the bigger cost.** Windy Hill's trade-off is "The typical lot is small". Ledger row 134 has about half of its no-fee houses in a high-risk flood zone, and Socastee about a third; neither entry says so. Flood is a real cost for a buyer here. Do not add it to every entry; but where it touches half the houses, it beats lot size as the honest line.
- **Low 6. Prices that differ from best neighborhoods.** Cherry Grove: "listed at about $850,000" here, "usually sell for about $695,000" on best neighborhoods. Surfside: $940,000 here, $705,000 there. One is list price of no-fee houses, the other is recorded sales of all houses. A reader who reads both will notice. The sources line says list prices; consider "asking prices" in the chip, or a matching measure.
- **Low 7. Loris photo.** Still half sky with three red traffic lights across the top. Add a better Main Street frame to the partner's list.
- **Low 8. Little River's no-HOA streets are not named.** "these older waterfront streets stand out" but only Mineola Avenue is named. Name the two or three streets where the no-fee houses are.

## What the page forgot that a buyer types into Google

- "land with no restrictions near Myrtle Beach" and "can I put a mobile home on my land": the country entries say nothing about whether a manufactured home is allowed on a county lot. Research the county's rule and say it once in the rules section.
- "well and septic": the country lots around Loris and Aynor. A buyer wants to know whether a house there is on city water and sewer or a well and septic, and what we check. One line in the country entries.

## The best three things: keep them

1. **The rules by town, now in plain words.** "Yes, behind the front of the house or under a carport." "Yes, up to 18,500 pounds." "A boat over 16 feet, or a camper of any size, stays out of the front yard." This is what this reader came for, and no other page in the set has it. The Teresa example under it is clear on one reading.
2. **Entries that sell with facts nobody could guess, for this reader.** Cherry Grove's canals dredged "to give owners boat access to House Creek and the Intracoastal Waterway at high and low tide", and the dredging fee and flood line as its honest trade-off. Surfside's "two boats or trailers at a home" and golf carts on town streets. Myrtle Heights' street ends that "are now public beach paths" and the appearance board as the trade-off. Ocean Drive's free Tuesday shag lessons and Hoskins since 1948. Pine Lakes' "room for a workshop or a boat beside the house". The country around Loris: "Outside the towns, the Solid Waste Authority provides no curbside trash pickup."
3. **The short answer and the honest FAQ.** "Almost every house for sale outside a named subdivision is listed with no homeowners association (HOA) fee, and so are about three in four built before 1990." That changes how a buyer searches. "Longs and the country around Conway look rural, but most homes for sale there have an HOA fee too" and the campground-home answer save a buyer a wasted weekend.

## Five answers in one line each

1. **Does every entry sell the place like the best agent in town, leading with something a buyer could not guess?** No. Downtown (1), Plantation Point (4), Socastee (10), Garden City (12), Loris (14) and Aynor (16) lead with history, a landmark or a distance instead of the reason to live there.
2. **Is there any "facts:" style label line, or field list in prose?** No "facts:" lines. The two chips per entry are label lines, and Plantation Point's run of drive minutes is a field list in prose.
3. **Does every place end with its own offer?** Yes, 16 of 16: "Get the homes for sale in [area]".
4. **Does it look modern (liquid glass), with charts that are simple to understand?** Yes for the glass chips, map card, rule cards and pill offers. There is no chart. The phone rule cards need words beside their icons.
5. **Is any picture unclear?** None is unclear. Surfside (mostly sand and sky) and Pine Lakes (the clubhouse, not the lots) miss their entry's point, and six areas still wait for the partner's photos.
