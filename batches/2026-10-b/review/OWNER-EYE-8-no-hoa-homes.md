## High

**High 1. Aynor, Loris and Conway are one paragraph three times.**
- 1, Aynor: "More than half of the houses for sale were built in 2024 or later, and almost all are listed as not in a subdivision. Most are outside the town limits, so the county's rules apply to a boat or a camper on the lot." Trade-off: "the nearest beach is about 45 minutes away by car".
- 2, Loris: "About 6 in 10 houses for sale here were built in 2024 or later, and almost none are listed in a subdivision. Most are outside the city limits." Trade-off: "the nearest beach, in North Myrtle Beach, is about 35 minutes away by car."
- 6, Conway: "Most of the houses for sale here are outside the city limits and listed as not in a subdivision. About a third were built in 2024 or later." Trade-off: "the nearest beach is about 25 minutes away by car."

Same three facts (built since 2024, not in a subdivision, outside the limits) and the same trade-off sentence. This is "the same question and damn near the same answer". Each town's history paragraph (the Hoe-Down, the Bog-Off, Kingston on the bluff) is good; the second paragraph undoes it. Want: say the inland pattern once, above the three ("Inland, most houses for sale with no HOA fee are new, on their own lot outside any subdivision"), then give each town what only it has: Aynor's acre lots and the Galivants Ferry landing; Loris's hospital two minutes from downtown and its three-quarter-acre lots; Conway's river, the Riverwalk and the older half-acre streets. Give each a trade-off only that town has, from rows we already hold (Aynor: you arrange your own trash pickup outside the town; Conway: inside the city a big business truck cannot park on the street), not the beach drive three times. The beach drive can sit once in the inland line above them.

## Medium

**Medium 2. The boat section is the heaviest block of numbers in the batch, and repeats its own table.** 498 words, 3.7 phone screens. One paragraph: "In Conway, a business truck over 10,000 pounds and longer than 20 feet may not park on a city street. In Surfside Beach, one work van or pickup may park overnight at a home. ## Low

1. "Here the land is the draw." A phrase. "Here you get land: most lots are about an acre."
2. "Little River sits on the Intracoastal Waterway". RULES 2 bans "sits" for a place. "Little River is on the Intracoastal Waterway".
3. Murrells Inlet's trade-off, "the lots are smaller than inland", is obvious. Its own catch is that part of it is in Georgetown County (a different tax bill and rules).
4. Little River's trade-off, "few homes for sale here have no HOA fee, so the choice is small", repeats the 11 percent on the map.
5. The Myrtle Beach aerial caption says "between Kings Highway and the beach", and the label on the photo says "US 17 Business". Same road; a reader does not know that. Use one name.
6. The short answer names no place: "About a quarter of the homes for sale around Myrtle Beach are listed with no homeowners association (HOA) fee." The lede above it does ("Loris and Aynor have the highest share"). See SEO.
7. Cross-page: "owners of about 400 properties in the canal district pay the city a yearly fee" here; "About 415 homes and lots in Cherry Grove" on best neighborhoods. One value.
8. "Is a campground home with no HOA fee a no-HOA home?" The answer never says yes or no.
