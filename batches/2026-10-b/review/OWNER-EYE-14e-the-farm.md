## Findings

### High

**1. The day section is an amenity sheet, not a day, and the page spends more on the HOA and the drawbacks than on living here.**
- Where: "What is a normal day like in The Farm at Carolina Forest?" (198 words, 1.3 screens).
  - "The fitness room is in the HOA's clubhouse, open to owners from 5:30 a.m. to 9 p.m. The HOA also keeps a basketball court, open from dawn to dusk, and a playground." Hours and a list. Every HOA has these.
  - "The activity center, about 400 feet from the large pool, is where the HOA board meets each month." A board meeting is not a reason to live somewhere.
  - "A sidewalk runs along Wheatfield Drive from the north-east corner of The Farm to International Drive." The reader cannot tell why this matters. It is the ledger row pasted in (row 110), not a sentence for a buyer. CRAFT 5: "A verifier gives the fact; the writer gives the sentence."
  - The section uses the HOA as its subject three times in five paragraphs.
- What is good in it, keep: "The large pool curves around two small islands of palms and has a wide deck." "Owners can also reserve it for a private party." The Greenway is now explained: "a walking and biking route from Maine to Florida."
- Meanwhile the cons are 236 words and 2.2 screens, and the rules 206 words and 2.1 screens. The page about living here is still mostly about the HOA and what can go wrong.
- Instead:
  - Write it as a week, in the order an owner lives it, leading with what no one would guess: a spring walk among the flytraps, the trails and the rock wall a few minutes out, the palm-island pool on a summer evening, Publix 2 minutes away, the beach 10 minutes away on a Saturday. Each said once, with what it gives the owner.
  - Say what the sidewalk means, or cut it. Research the kind of fact that would make it a selling line: whether the sidewalk reaches the two Ocean Bay schools at The Farm's north-east edge, so a student can walk, and whether The Farm is in a walk zone or a bus zone.
  - Cut "where the HOA board meets each month" from the day. Keep the private party.
  - Research the kinds of fact that fill a normal week and that no other page has: the nearest places to eat and get coffee by name, the drive to the main places people work (the hospitals, downtown, Conway), whether the ponds allow fishing or have a path, what Community Appreciation Day is.
  - Name places by what the reader knows: "the back entrance by the schools", not "Wheatfield Drive" and "International Drive".

### Medium

**2. The first screen leads with what anyone would guess, and says "two pools" three times.**
- Where: the subtitle "Two pools, cable in the fee, flytrap habitat along its north edge."; the tile "Pools / Two"; the short answer "end in one of the two pools".
- The subtitle is a field list of three items. "Cable in the fee" is a line from the fee table, not a reason to buy. "Habitat" is still a press-release word (14d finding 8).
- The short answer starts the day "in the fitness room". The owner: "if its next to a pond ... gloss over it ... find information that isnt obvious and therefore is intriguing".
- Instead: one subtitle in a buyer's words, about 8 to 10 words, built on what no one could guess, for example the wild Venus flytraps in protected woods next door. Then let the short answer open on the preserve or the 2-minute Publix and 10-minute beach, and give the pools one clause. Drop either the "Pools" tile or the pools from the subtitle.

**3. The preserve still never says what it means for an owner.**
- Where: "In 2025, the state bought the land instead, for the Lewis Ocean Bay Heritage Preserve." The page then says where the preserve's main entrance is, "about 6 minutes by car from The Farm's entrance by the schools."
- The two questions a buyer asks are unanswered: will the woods along the north edge ever be built on, and can I walk in from The Farm, or must I drive 6 minutes? The ledger notes the new tract "will be open" but is not yet (farm-verify-v8, row 106 note).
- Instead: if a verified row supports it, say once that the woods along the north edge are now a state preserve and will stay woods. Research whether the new tract will have an entrance or path on The Farm's side, and when it opens; say it, or say plainly that today the way in is the main entrance.

**4. The pros undersell, and the honest section reads mostly as bad news.**
- Where: the three pros are "The HOA fee is the same $107 a month for every home." "Every home is outside the high-risk flood zone." "There is no city tax." The four cons run 4 to 73 words each.
- These pros are lines from a fee table. The reasons people choose The Farm (the preserve next door, two pools and cable in a $107 fee, Publix 2 minutes away, the beach 10 minutes away, the three schools zoned together) are not in the list a buyer scans for "pros and cons".
- Instead: lead the pros with the best of the page, each in one short line said in new words, not repeating a sentence from above. Keep the flood line. Cut "the same $107 a month for every home", which the cons' second-fee line undercuts.

**5. Dates and numbers pile up in three places.**
- The hunting con: "bows from mid-September, guns from October 21 to January 1, and small game to March 1 ... on burn days International Drive can close between SC 90 and Ocean Bay Elementary." Four dates, one highway number and two road names in one item.
- The FAQ: "D.R. Horton recorded its HOA rules in 2004 and added land until 2017. Most houses were built from 2004 to 2009, and most of the rest from 2014 to 2018. D.R. Horton has not controlled the HOA board since 2019". Six years in one answer. The owner, 2026-10-07: "there's too many numbers you also are mentioning the date so much".
- The cost section: "owners in The Orchards pay about $225 to $255 a month. Owners in Planters Point pay about $450 to $500 a month." Four figures in two sentences.
- Instead:
  - Hunting: one plain range and what it means: deer hunting runs from mid-September to January 1, with guns used from late October; a walker in the fall shares the roads with hunters. Put the other dates in the ledger.
  - Burns: "on burn days, the road past the schools can close for the day". No highway number.
  - FAQ: "Most houses were built from 2004 to 2009, and the rest mostly from 2014 to 2018. Owners have run the HOA board since 2019." Cut the recording year and the land year.
  - Fees: "about $240 a month in all in The Orchards, and about $475 in Planters Point", if the rows allow a midpoint; otherwise keep the ranges and cut the other numbers in that paragraph.

**6. "It does not include internet." states a negative the research has not settled.**
- Where: the cost section: "The fee also covers landscaping and the ponds, trash pickup and the pools. It does not include internet."
- The ledger: row 11, 34 of 45 sellers listed internet as included in the fee. The open-questions list, item 7: "Ask the HOA: does the bulk Spectrum contract include internet as well as TV". The budget names cable TV, which does not prove internet is excluded.
- A buyer will plan a monthly bill on this line. If it is wrong, it is the first thing a new owner notices.
- Instead: confirm from the HOA's own papers (the Spectrum contract, a board minute or the HOA's welcome page) and say it either way, with the providers that serve the streets. Until then, cut the sentence.
