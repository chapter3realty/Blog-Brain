## Findings

### High

**H1. The entries sell public amenities, not the house, and they got shorter.** The owner on this page, first read: "give me a decent amount of information about each community ... focus on what the house and location gives you". Third read: "a disappointing surface level article".
- The page fell from 4,016 words (14c) to 3,330. The middle entry fell from 117 words to 94. Under 90: Forestbrook Estates 68, The Farm 71, Prince Creek 85, Plantation Lakes 85, Avalon 85.
- The golf entries sell things anyone in town can use. "Joey's Clubhouse Grille, a public restaurant". "The course is open to the public" (Prince Creek). "Wishbones Bar and Grill, open to the public". A buyer reads that and asks why they need to live there.
- Most entries say nothing about the house:
  - Tidewater: one line, "Many of Tidewater's houses are along the fairways."
  - Prince Creek: nothing about the homes.
  - Wild Wing: nothing about the homes.
  - Grande Dunes: only the style.
  - Barefoot: "Most homes for sale here are condos."
  - Plantation Lakes: "On a lakefront lot, the water is behind the house."
  - Only Avalon gives size, years and lot ("about 1,600 square feet"), and it puts them in the trade-off.
- Obvious lines stand in for selling. "Its practice area has a putting green, a chipping green and a grass driving range." That is a field list of what every course has, the owner's pond. "Mornings here can start on the sand." "The pier was built in the 1950s."

Do instead, for every entry:
- Keep the lead.
- Add a paragraph on the house itself, from the deed and listing rows: what a typical house is (size, one or two stories, the years built), and what the lots look like (on the fairway, on the lake, wooded).

- Cut the practice-green list and the one-line filler paragraphs.
- Target: no entry under about 120 words, unless what was cut was filler.
