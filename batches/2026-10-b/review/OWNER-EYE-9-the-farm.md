## Medium

**Medium 1. The school map still does not show the sentence beside it.** Text: "Every home in The Farm is inside all three zones, so every home has the same three schools." Caption: "purple shading for Ocean Bay Middle and yellow shading for Carolina Forest High." Opened full size: the land west of The Farm is tinted yellow and carries the "High school zone" label; The Farm itself is gray, with no yellow in it. The "Middle school zone" label sits in a purple band south of a purple line that runs below The Farm. A buyer reads the map as: The Farm is outside the high school zone. A picture is a claim (CRAFT 4), and this one says the opposite of the sentence on a school question.

Want, the simpler map: since every home has the same three schools, shade one area, "Zoned for all three schools", with The Farm's outline inside it, and mark the two Ocean Bay schools and the arrow to Carolina Forest High. Or keep three zones but show the yellow tint inside The Farm too. Caption: "Every home in The Farm, outlined in orange, is zoned for Ocean Bay Elementary, Ocean Bay Middle and Carolina Forest High."

2. Cable TV is named four times (short answer, "The HOA spends the largest share of the fee on cable TV from Spectrum", the con, the FAQ). Keep the short answer, the costs line and the con; cut it from the FAQ answer.
3. "The HOA spends the largest share of the fee on cable TV from Spectrum. The HOA spends the rest on landscaping..." Two sentences open the same way. "The largest share of the fee pays for cable TV from Spectrum. The rest pays for..."
4. "The Farm at Carolina Forest has groceries, schools and the beach close by." (opening the pros). True of most of Carolina Forest. Cut; the pros list says it with numbers.
5. The location map's "Carolina Forest Blvd" and "US 501" labels are not tied to a road line. A short leader line to each road.
