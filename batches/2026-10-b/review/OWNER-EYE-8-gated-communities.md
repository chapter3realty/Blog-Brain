## High

**High 1. One sentence used twice, and one theme in eight entries: rice plantations.**
- 12: "Wachesaw was a rice plantation on the Waccamaw River, and the state's Rice Culture survey of Georgetown County lists it."
- 16: "Litchfield was a rice plantation on the Waccamaw River, and the state's Rice Culture survey of Georgetown County lists it."

Same sentence, same shape, same source cited in the copy. The citation is also "records show" in other words (RULES C: "Do not write records show, according to"). And the theme runs on: lede "from old rice plantations on the Waccamaw River", short answer "several sit on old rice plantations along the Waccamaw River", 13 "on a former rice plantation", 15 "Enslaved people grew rice here ... wrecked the rice fields' dikes", 16, 18 "on land that was part of former rice plantations ... among live oaks and the old rice fields", 19 "rice planters built a colony of summer homes". The word "rice" appears 13 times. This is exactly what the owner meant by "youll get hung up on one thing and then its all you ever want to talk about".

Want: tell the rice history once, in full, where it is most striking (Willbrook, with the cemetery and the plantation house ruins). Elsewhere give what only that place has: Litchfield's Avenue of Oaks and its 1700s house; Wachesaw's Kimbel's on the bluff and the marina; Heritage's marina and the 500 houses between river and ocean. Drop "the state's Rice Culture survey ... lists it" from the copy; it belongs in the sources line.

## Medium

**Medium 1. A second device in ten entries: "[designer] course, opened in [year]".** Tidewater "the course opened in 1990"; Barefoot "four golf courses at the same time in April 2000"; Grande Dunes "Its Resort Course, designed by Roger Rulewich, opened in 2001"; Prestwick "designed by Pete Dye and his son P.B. Dye, opened in 1989"; Bellwood "a Tom Fazio course that opened in 1999"; Wachesaw "with its Tom Fazio course, was founded in 1985"; Wachesaw East "It opened in December 1996"; The Reserve "designed by Greg Norman, opened in 1998"; Willbrook "Dan Maples' golf course opened in 1988"; Heritage "Dan Maples' Heritage Club course opened there in 1986". Ten of nineteen entries open or turn on the same fact shape. A golf buyer cares about the course; a designer and year in every entry reads as a spreadsheet column. Keep it where it is a story (Barefoot's four on one day, Tidewater's best new course of 1990, Wachesaw East's LPGA years); elsewhere say what the course is like to live beside, or what the club offers owners.

**Medium 2. Five entries are still thin.** Body words, without caption and facts line: Covington Lake East 48, Bellwood Landing 56, Grande Dunes 58, Wachesaw East 58, The Bluffs on the Waterway 60.
- 7, Covington Lake East: "built from about 2008. Many houses back up to its ponds, and the streets have sidewalks. It has a pool and a clubhouse. The shops and restaurants of Carolina Forest Boulevard are less than a mile away." Sidewalks, a pool and shops nearby are true of most Carolina Forest neighborhoods.
- 11, Bellwood Landing: the headline and first paragraph are about a nearby course ("near the course that hosted Tom Watson's 2000 win"), not the neighborhood. Its own facts are "Some homes are on ponds" and a nearby park.
- 6, The Bluffs: no builder, no year, no history.
- 4, Grande Dunes: "Burroughs & Chapin launched Grande Dunes." Two short paragraphs for the most expensive, best-known place on the list.
Want: who built each, when, what the land was, and one thing a visitor notices at the gate.

**Medium 3. The headline answer to "where" is a town span.** H1 "Where are the gated communities in Myrtle Beach?" First answer sentence: "Gated neighborhoods near Myrtle Beach run from North Myrtle Beach to Pawleys Island." Hero line: "From North Myrtle Beach to Pawleys Island." The owner: "one of your answers is myrtle beach ... name some roads some communities". The first sentence names no community; an answer engine lifting it gives the reader nothing. Want: "Gated neighborhoods near Myrtle Beach include Grande Dunes, Barefoot Resort, Tidewater Plantation, Prestwick and DeBordieu Colony, from North Myrtle Beach to Pawleys Island."

**Medium 4. Myrtle Trace's front entrance is told four times, and one section exists only to repeat it.** Entry 8 facts: "gates only at the back entrance on Myrtle Ridge Road; the front entrance on Burning Ridge Road is open." Section "What should you know about the gate before you buy?": its one sentence is "In Barefoot Resort and Grande Dunes only some sections are gated, and Myrtle Trace has gates only at its back entrance." (both already in entries 3, 4 and 8). The story: "That entrance has no gate, and the gates are only at the back." FAQ: "Myrtle Trace near Conway has gates only at its back entrance." Want: cut the section's sentence, keep the story under a heading that says what it shows, and keep the FAQ.

**Medium 5. Filler in the pros.** "Many have a pool, a clubhouse or a dock that the owners share." True of almost every HOA neighborhood, gated or not. Cut, or say what only gated places here have (a mile of private beach, a river marina).

**Medium 6. The first offer is a different, smaller box than the last one.** First: a pale card with "Get this week's list of homes for sale in gated neighborhoods." in bold body-size text. Last: the navy box with a large headline. Same finding on all five pages.

**Medium 7. Twenty aerials that each say only a name.** Each entry's aerial has one label: the neighborhood name with a pin. Wachesaw, Wachesaw East, The Reserve, Willbrook, Litchfield Plantation, Heritage, Bellwood and Myrtle Trace look alike at phone width: trees, fairways, roofs. None shows what its entry is about: Litchfield's Avenue of Oaks, Willbrook's old rice fields and plantation ruins, Waterway Palms' clubhouse on the point and its boat ramp, The Reserve's marina, The Bluffs' boathouse. The best neighborhoods page labels the pool or the canals on each aerial; this page does not. Want: one or two labels per aerial for the feature the paragraph names (CRAFT 4: "Label what the reader must find"). The photo partner list should start with the Avenue of Oaks, the Litchfield by the Sea beach club, the Prestwick and Tidewater gatehouses.

## Low

1. "Every occupied home must have a resident 55 or older." appears word for word in entries 8 and 10.
2. "a golf cart ride away" in 2, 14 and 15.
3. "A gate controls who drives in. It is not a guard at each home: what a gate does." The link text reads as a broken sentence. "It is not a guard at each home. Read what a gate does below." or cut the link.
4. "The state's crime data site shows crime reported to each police department and sheriff, by year and type." It points the reader to look it up. Fair housing keeps us from rating, so this is allowed by CRAFT 1, but it is the only reader to-do left.
5. A hero tile is price: "Homes sell for: About $480,000 to $2 million".
6. The first map shows dots 6, 7, 11 and 13 with no name, crowded at the bottom left.
7. "Is there a gated community in Surfside Beach?" The answer never says no. "No. Prestwick, just outside the town, is gated."
8. No trade-off line in any entry. The pros and cons cover the gate in general; a buyer still wants each place's catch (Prestwick's course is public; The Reserve's club is separate from the home; Litchfield by the Sea's fees differ by section). Several entries already say this in passing.
9. "Get this week's list" promises a weekly send. Fine if the agents do it; ROUND-7 asked to promise no time we have not verified.
10. At about 48 phone screens this is the longest page in the set. High 1 and medium 1 cut more words than medium 2 adds.

## The story, read once

Joan wants a 55+ home behind a gate; the Myrtle Trace house she likes has no gate at its front entrance; the agent shows her Seasons, which has one, and she offers there. I retold it after one read. It passes. Its shape matches the no-HOA story (the house fails the want, the agent shows homes elsewhere, an offer), see the batch file.
