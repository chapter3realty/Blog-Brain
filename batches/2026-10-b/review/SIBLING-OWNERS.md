# Batch 2026-10-b: one home for each fact (round 14c)

The owner, third read: "the same information was given to gated communities as you gave to affordable communities they dont care about the same things." The round 14 reviewers found the same facts, often in the same words, on two or three pages. This file decides which page owns each fact. Another page that names the same place gives a different angle, written for its own reader. It never gives the same fact in other words.

## The angle of each page

| Page | Its reader | What it says about a place |
|---|---|---|
| Gated communities | Has money and wants privacy, service and a club | The club, the course and its history, the water and the marina, how the gate works, what the fee does, the social calendar |
| Best neighborhoods | New to the area and choosing by what they love | A normal day there for someone who loves that thing: the walk, the morning, the weekend. One fact no other page has. |
| No HOA | Wants freedom: a boat, a truck, a shed, renting, no fee | What you can keep and do at home, the town's rules, the land and the older character |
| Affordable | Budget or first-time buyer | What the money buys: the house itself, the amenities, the commute, room to grow |
| The Farm | Considering The Farm | Everything about The Farm. Other pages give it one line and a different fact. |

## The rule for next time

Before writing, list every place that also appears on a sibling page in the batch. Assign each shared fact to one page, by which reader cares most. Then each page gives its own angle. Run the sibling check: search each place name across the batch's specs and read the sentences side by side (CRAFT 8, item 6).
