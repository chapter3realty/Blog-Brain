## Medium

**Medium 1. Straight-line distances, in five entries.** `repeat-check`: "mile" in 12 of 20 entries; "straight line" 6 times.
- Tidewater: "Measured in a straight line, Cherry Grove's public boat ramp is less than a mile from the middle of the neighborhood. By road it is about 5 miles."
- Seaside: "A Walmart Supercenter, a Lowe's and the shops of Gator Hole Plaza are less than half a mile from the middle of the neighborhood in a straight line. Main Street's nearest block is 0.45 mile from the same spot, also in a straight line."
- The Bluffs: "In a straight line, the Carolina Forest bike park is 0.8 mile away."
- Covington Lake East: "In a straight line, shops and restaurants lie within a mile."
- Golf and Yacht Club: "Enterprise Landing, a public boat ramp on the Intracoastal Waterway, is about a mile and a half away in a straight line."

CRAFT 2: "For a place the reader drives to (a store, a school, a park, a ramp), give the drive time by car, not the straight-line distance." The Tidewater paragraph spends two sentences to say the ramp is 5 miles by road. "0.45 mile from the same spot" is a measuring note. Want: one drive time where the place matters ("Cherry Grove's boat ramp is about 10 minutes by car"; "A Walmart, a Lowe's and Gator Hole Plaza are a few minutes away, and Main Street is close enough to walk" only if a row measures the walk). Cut the rest. Three entries also give miles to a beach (The Reserve "a drive of under 3 miles", Willbrook "about 3.4 miles away", Litchfield Plantation "about 4 miles away by road"); the table already gives every beach drive in minutes. Keep Litchfield Plantation's, where the beach house is the point.

**Medium 2. Records-and-sales notes read like a data sheet.**
- "Six sales were recorded in the past year." (Seaside, in the facts line)
- "Few homes come up for sale: only four sold in the past year." (The Bluffs)
- "Houses listed here in the past year were built from 2000 to 2006." (Wachesaw East)
- "The 12 houses listed here were built from 2005 to 2019." (The Reserve)
- "Few homes sold here in the past year, so the $1 million middle price is a rough guide." (Willbrook)
- "Few homes sold, so the price of about $1 million is a rough guide." (Litchfield Plantation)
- "Its property owners association recorded its gate access rules with the county in 2019." (Seaside)
- "Its owners association recorded a gate policy with the county in 2024, and the main gate is on TPC Boulevard." (Bellwood Landing)

The same "rough guide" hedge twice, the same "recorded ... with the county in [year]" shape twice, and four sentences that count sales. The reader wants what the homes are, not how many deeds we read. Want: say what it means. "Houses here were built in the early 2000s." "Homes rarely come up for sale here." Give a thin price once as "about $1 million" and put "where few sold, the price is a rough guide" once in the sources line. Cut the two "recorded ... with the county" sentences; the facts line already says there is a gate.

**Medium 3. Attribution and old listings in the copy.**
- "The facts: listings mark Calais, Palermo, Tuscany Village and Villa Venezia as gated." and "Living Dunes and Siena Park have no gate marked."
- "a gatehouse at the entrance on Bay Road; listings describe security at the gate day and night" (Golf and Yacht Club)
- "In 2013 the builder also listed a ballroom, a card room, an art studio, a billiards room and a full-time activities director." (Seasons). A 2013 sales listing stated in today's page; CRAFT 2 "Time: is an old description stated as true today?"
- "A local paper expected it to bring at least 10,000 people." (Seaside)
- "Michie said the project was the most extensive archaeological research done on any South Carolina plantation." (Wachesaw). A superlative carried by "said".
- "By one local history, Waccamaw Native Americans and English colonists would have traded animal skins there for beads, tools, salt and rum." (Wachesaw)

CRAFT 5: "No 'records show', 'according to' ... If a verifier allows a fact only with attribution, find a stronger source, state the part fully supported, or cut it. Never paste the hedge." Want: "Calais, Palermo, Tuscany Village and Villa Venezia are gated; Living Dunes and Siena Park are not." if the rows allow it, else name only the three the price covers. "A guard is at the gate day and night" if the row allows it, else "a gatehouse at the entrance on Bay Road". Seasons: name only what the clubhouse has today (indoor and outdoor pools, a fitness center), or cut the 2013 list. Seaside: "The Society of Stranders' Fall Migration, a shag festival, fills North Myrtle Beach each fall." Wachesaw: "Archaeologists from the University of South Carolina dug here before the neighborhoods were planned; some of their finds are in the clubhouse." and "The bluff where Kimbel's stands was a trading post where Waccamaw people and English colonists traded skins for goods." only as far as the row supports; otherwise cut the trade line.

**Medium 4. Litchfield by the Sea says one thing twice and adds an unexplained fee.**
- "A new vehicle barcode costs $25. We send the homes for sale in each section on request."
- "The HOA fee and the prices differ from section to section and from unit to unit."
- Facts line: "The HOA fee differs by section and unit."

The fee-differs sentence is said twice, one line apart. "A new vehicle barcode costs $25" means nothing to a reader who has not been told the gate reads a barcode on each car. Want: "Owners' cars get a barcode for the gate, $25 each." Cut the middle sentence; keep the facts line. Move "We send the homes for sale in each section" into the offer box, where it is an offer.

**Medium 5. Covington Lake East still says nothing only it has.** Body: "Covington Lake East is a small neighborhood of brick houses in Carolina Forest, built since the mid-2000s. Its curving streets lie among ponds, and most lots are about a quarter of an acre." "The streets have sidewalks, and many of the houses have natural gas." "It has a pool and a clubhouse on land its owners association holds. In a straight line, shops and restaurants lie within a mile. Coastal Carolina University takes about 12 minutes to reach by car." "It has no golf course or boat ramp of its own."

"shops and restaurants lie within a mile" is true of most of Carolina Forest. The university drive matters only to a student or a parent of one. Round 9 asked for who built it and what an owner does there; neither is here. Its one real draw, the lowest HOA fee on the page (about $90), is in the heading and the facts line only. Want: who built it and when, from the county's plat or deed rows; what the clubhouse and pool are used for; and say the fee plainly as the reason to pick it ("For a gate, a pool and a clubhouse, the fee is about $90 a month, the lowest of the twenty."). Cut the shops and university sentences.

**Medium 6. Four entries still have no trade-off.** ROUND-10 asked for "one trade-off line each". None in Seaside, Wachesaw Plantation, Wachesaw East and Heritage Plantation. Want, from rows already held where they exist: Seaside, few homes for sale (medium 2 already has the count); Wachesaw, club membership is a separate cost; Wachesaw East, the drive to the beach (about 12 minutes in the table); Heritage, the HOA fee of about $325 a month, among the higher fees here.

## Low

1. "among the highest on this page" twice (Grande Dunes, DeBordieu), and Leatherleaf's fee "among the highest of the twenty". Talks about the page. Give the price; the table ranks.
2. "Its plan was patterned after Charleston's Battery district, with Charleston-style architecture as the blueprint." Says Charleston twice, and "blueprint" is a figure of speech. "Its plan copies Charleston's Battery district, and its houses are built in the Charleston style."
3. "Willbrook was part of a grant of 24,000 acres made in 1711. Enslaved people grew rice here; the 1850 census counted 149 at Willbrook." With the next sentence's "from 1893 to 1911", that is six numbers in three sentences. CRAFT 5: "Three numbers in a paragraph at most." Keep 1711 and 149.
4. The first map still shows dots 6, 7, 12 and 14 with no name, crowded at the bottom left.
5. "Get this week's list" promises a weekly send. Fine only if the agents do it.
6. "The state's crime data site shows crime reported to each police department and sheriff, by year and type." The one reader to-do left. Allowed by fair housing.
7. The Heritage aerial's top label is "Caledonia Creek", a name the entry never uses; the entry is about the Waccamaw River marina. Label the marina, or name the creek in the text. The Wachesaw aerial's label is "Golf course"; the entry is about the club and Kimbel's bluff.
8. Leatherleaf's caption, "with the Love course on every side", is repeated word for word in the body sentence under it. Cut it from one.
