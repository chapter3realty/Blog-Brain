## High findings

**H1. The page is called "Where are the gated communities", and most entries never say where they are.**
- Location: 12 of the 18 entries never name a town, an area or a landmark a newcomer knows: 3 Barefoot, 4 Grande Dunes, 5 Waterway Palms, 6 The Bluffs, 8 Prestwick, 10 Bellwood Landing, 11 Wachesaw, 12 Wachesaw East, 14 Willbrook, 16 Litchfield by the Sea, 17 Heritage and 18 DeBordieu. Prestwick, Seasons and Barefoot are placed only in the FAQ. A reader new to the area does not know that Wachesaw or Bellwood Landing is anywhere near Murrells Inlet.
- The map does not help. It sits after the price table, about 30 screens down, and shows four circles ("North Myrtle Beach", "Myrtle Beach", "Surfside to Murrells Inlet", "Pawleys Island to DeBordieu") with no entry numbers. The guide's last row sends the reader to it: "On a map: where the four areas are, after the places."
- Do instead:
  - Put the area in each entry's first or second sentence, in words a newcomer knows: the town, and one landmark from a verified row (the river, the beach, the MarshWalk). One clause, not a sentence of its own.
  - Or put a small label for each area above its first entry ("North Myrtle Beach", "Myrtle Beach", "Surfside to Murrells Inlet", "Pawleys Island to DeBordieu"). The entries already run north to south, so four labels would do it.
  - Either way, write the entry numbers in or beside each map circle, so the map answers "which ones are near me".

**H2. Four entries still do not sell the place, and "talk extensively" was his word.**
- 5, Waterway Palms (69 words): "its clubhouse stands on a point in a pond, with water on three sides." The ramp paragraph is good. Then the price. Nothing on the homes, the gate, a normal day or a trade-off.
- 7, Myrtle Beach Golf and Yacht Club: it opens with "the HOA's Community Watch desk is covered 24 hours a day, seven days a week. The gatehouse at the entrance has its own phone line." A phone line is not a reason to live somewhere. The RV and trailer storage and the public ramp 4 minutes away are what a gated buyer could not guess, and they come second.
- 9, Seasons at Prince Creek West (71 words): "the HOA fee includes internet, trash pickup and lawn care." It leads with the fee, which is the price-first habit he named ("then mention price"). It has no club, no event, no normal week and no trade-off. The heading says "55+" twice: "(55+): a 55+ fee that covers internet and lawn care".
- 10, Bellwood Landing (65 words): "Bellwood Landing has room." Then lot sizes and a creek. It has no gate, no day there, no trade-off, and no word on what the creek lots look onto.
- Do instead:
  - Research each one the way Heritage and Wachesaw were researched: who built it and when, what the land was, the homes themselves, what owners do on a Saturday, how the gate works.
  - Golf and Yacht Club: lead with the RV and boat storage inside the gate and the ramp 4 minutes away. Put the watch desk in one clause and cut the phone line.
  - Seasons: lead with the clubhouse life (one club, event or class from its own calendar). Put the fee in the price sentence.
  - Bellwood and Waterway Palms: one more paragraph each of what only that place has. Give each an honest trade-off.
  - If nothing a gated buyer would pay to know turns up for Bellwood, cut it. Eighteen is not a target.
