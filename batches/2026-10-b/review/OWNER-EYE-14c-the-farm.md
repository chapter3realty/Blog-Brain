## Findings

### High

**1. The day section reads like the amenity rules, not like a day.**
- Where: "What is a normal day like in The Farm at Carolina Forest?" The first three paragraphs:
  - "Besides the fitness room in the clubhouse, owners share a basketball court, open from dawn to dusk, and a playground."
  - "The large pool covers about 7,400 square feet. The pools are open from April 15 to October 15, with no lifeguard. Each home can bring up to six guests, with the owner there."
  - "Owners can reserve the activity center for a private party of up to four hours, for a fee. The HOA's events include a fall yard sale and a Community Appreciation Day."
- These are the HOA's rules for the amenities: hours, limits, fees. A basketball court, a playground and the size of a pool are what the owner means by "gloss over it". The paragraph also opens on "Besides", which leans on the short answer and does not stand alone if quoted.
- The two paragraphs that sell are fourth and fifth: the library and recreation center campus with "a full gym and a rock climbing wall", and the Bike and Run Park, "7 miles of mountain bike and hiking trails", "South Carolina's first official East Coast Greenway trailhead".
- Instead:
  - Lead the section with the trails and the recreation center, the two things a buyer could not guess. Then the pools, in one sentence that says what an owner does there.
  - Gloss the court and the playground in a few words, or cut them.
  - Move the rules (no lifeguard, six guests, the party fee) to the HOA rules cards, or cut them. "No lifeguard" is a rule a buyer needs, but not as a selling line.
  - Make the events something an owner does, not a list of two names. Research the kind of fact that fills a normal week: what the Community Appreciation Day is, whether the ponds have a walking path or sidewalks, whether the preserve edge can be reached on foot.

### Medium

**2. No picture shows The Farm at eye level.**
- Where: all three pictures are labelled aerials (`img1.png` the overview, `img2.png` the location, `img3.png` the school zone). They are good maps. But a reader picturing a life here sees no house, no street, no pool and no activity center.
- The photo partner's list already has The Farm first: the entrance sign, a typical street, the large pool, the small pool, The Orchards and a pond (`photo-request-abdulla.html`).
- Instead: when the photos come, put the typical street in the homes section and the large pool in the day section, each under its sentence. Keep the overview aerial and the location map. Until then this is waiting on the partner, not on the writer.

**3. The preserve leads, but the page never says what it means for a day here.**
- Where: "In 2025, the state bought them for the Lewis Ocean Bay Heritage Preserve instead, and plans to open them for outdoor recreation." Then: "The state keeps it healthy with controlled burns."
- "Plans to open them for outdoor recreation" is vague: no trail, no entrance, no time frame. "The state keeps it healthy with controlled burns" reads as reassurance. A buyer hears "burns" behind the house and wonders about smoke.
- Instead:
  - Research the kind of fact that makes it daily life: where the nearest public entrance or trail to the preserve is today, what a visitor can do there, and when the flytraps bloom.
  - Say what it means that the woods along the north edge are a preserve. If a verified row supports it, say that this land will not be built on.
  - Either say what the burns mean for owners (from a verified source), or cut the sentence.

**4. One fact said four and five times, and one device used four times.**
- Where:
  - The school zone, five times: the short answer ("Every home is zoned for Ocean Bay Elementary, Ocean Bay Middle and Carolina Forest High"), the schools section's first sentence, the map caption, the map label "Zoned for all three schools", and the table.
  - "Before you make an offer" or "before they make an offer", four times: the roof and air conditioner, the pool minutes, the example, and the HOA rules line.
  - "For any home (or lot) you like", three times: "We check the current zones for any home you like", "We read the HOA's rules and latest minutes for any home you like", "We check the Corps' map for any lot you like".
  - The state's 353 acres, three times: the subtitle, the first section and the map caption. Publix at 2 minutes and the beach at 10 minutes, three times each: the tiles, the end of the day section ("Publix is about 2 minutes away by car, and the beach about 10.") and the map labels.
- `repeat-check --strict` passes. It cannot see a fact said again in other words.
- Instead:
  - Schools: keep the short answer line and the table. Cut the section's first sentence and shorten the caption to what the map shows.
  - Vary the "we" lines, or fold two into one. One "before you make an offer" is strong; four is a template.
  - Cut "Publix is about 2 minutes away by car, and the beach about 10." The tiles and the map already say it.

**5. The school map has a label with no point.**
- Where: `img3.png`. "Carolina Forest High, about 15 min west" floats in the top left, over woods, with no pointer. A reader can take it to mean the school is there. The blue zone runs far past The Farm, so the map's one point, that every home is in it, takes more than a second to see.
- Instead: drop the high school label (the table gives the drive), or put it at the edge as an arrow pointing west. The caption can say the one point: "All of The Farm (orange) is inside the zone (blue)." Or cut the map: the point is one sentence and a table.

**6. The HOA rules section says renting and pets twice, and hides its best rule in the example.**
- Where: "the rules allow you to rent out your home and keep up to three dogs and cats." The cards right below: "Renting: Allowed, with no limit ..." and "Pets: Up to three dogs and cats in all."
- The rule a buyer could not guess, "No lot in The Farm may have a private swimming pool", is only in the example.
- Instead: open the section with the no-pool rule, in one plain line, and let the cards carry renting and pets. Keep the example; it then shows the rule at work.

**7. An FAQ that is true of every home in the county.**
- Where: "How is the property tax set when you buy in The Farm?" "After you buy, the county values the home at about what it would sell for on the open market. In an ordinary sale, that is close to the price you paid. The new value first shows on the next year's tax bill."
- The round 14 review asked for this to go. It is a sentence that would be true of any place. The useful number, about $1,300 a year on a $350,000 house, is already in the cost table.
- Instead: cut it, or replace it with a question buyers type about The Farm. The kinds of fact to research: which internet providers serve the streets (the page says the fee "does not include internet" and stops), and the range of house prices from low to high.

**8. The page's one-line hook is too long for the hero.**
- Where: the subtitle sits inside the H1, at the H1's size (64 px at 1280). It is 16 words. At 1280 it runs three lines of large orange italic and nearly touches the H1 (`screen-1280-0.png`). On a phone it runs four lines. The sibling subtitles are 7 to 9 words.
- Instead: cut it to about 8 words, for example the flytraps and the preserve. The 353 acres and the hospital plan stay in the first section, where they are told well.

**9. The sibling pages still say The Farm's facts, and one Farm paragraph is another page's lead.**
- Where:
  - Affordable, Sawgrass East leads with: "the county's Carolina Forest Recreation Center has a gym, a fitness room and a climbing wall, and the county library shares its campus." This page's day section says: "the county library and the Carolina Forest Recreation Center share one campus ... The recreation center has a full gym and a rock climbing wall." It is the same fact in close words.
  - Best neighborhoods gives The Farm cable TV in the fee, the two pools, about $350,000, $107 a month, the second fee for townhomes and condos, and $2,600 a year. That is this page's content again, not one line and a different fact.
  - Gated: "The land was part of Carolina Forest, which the Army Air Force used as a bombing and gunnery range from 1942 to 1948." SIBLING-OWNERS gives the bombing range to The Farm.
- Instead: the fixes are on the sibling pages (their writers are mid-edit). Add the recreation center and library to SIBLING-OWNERS and decide one owner. For a Farm reader, the trails and the Greenway trailhead are the better lead anyway, so this page can give the recreation center one short clause.

## Best three things (keep them)

1. **The opening is the sale.** The state's tract and the flytraps in the subtitle and the first section, the $160 million hospital plan the state bought out, the bombing range told once and honestly, and the street names ("from Scarecrow Way to Pumpkin Patch Lane"). No other page has these. They come in the first screen and a half.
2. **The cons with what we do.** "The large pool has sunk into the ground more than it should, and the HOA board has not decided whether to repair or replace it." Then: "We read the board's latest minutes on the pool before you make an offer." Honest, specific, and in the voice of the best agents in town. The same for parking and the second fee.
3. **The example and the plain numbers.** Rachel and Luis and the no-pool rule: I could retell it in one sentence (a couple wanted a pool in the yard, the agent read the rules before the offer, private pools are not allowed, they bought anyway and plan a hot tub). The cost table, "$1,300 + $1,300 = about $2,600 a year", is a number a buyer can plan with. The homes section is now short: what the homes are, the sizes, the age, the price in one sentence, then the offer.
