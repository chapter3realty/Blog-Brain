This is the best version of this page so far, and the first one that is not a 7. Three of the four things that held it back are fixed:
- The top is now 1.98 phone screens, under the brief's two.
- Plantation Point, with its "no HOA fee, then an HOA" contradiction, is gone.

The map is one panel, the rule cards have words, and "we check" is said once where it counts. Murrells Inlet's north end is a real area for this reader.

It is not a 9 for one reason the owner has sent back twice, and a few smaller ones. ## High finding

**High 1. Many entries are still a stack of one-fact lines, and few say why people choose the place.**

The owner asked to "speak extensively on all of the benefits of that location and why people like it", and said "don't do the facts: thats not how a human talks". The leads now do this well. Below the lead, many entries fall back to the same three fields, each its own short paragraph: when the houses were built, the lot size, and the share within half a mile. repeat-check counts "acre" in 9 of 16 entries and "built" in 7. These are the lines he would send back:

- **1. Downtown**: "Most of the houses for sale here with no HOA fee are within half a mile of the beach." / "The older houses on Collins, Chester and Dunbar Streets date from the 1950s and 1960s." / "Lots are small here, typically about 0.2 acre." Three paragraphs, three fields.
- **5. Crescent Beach**: "Most of the houses were built before 1990, on streets such as Hillside Drive, Holly Drive, Perrin Drive and Strand Avenue." / "The typical lot is small, about 7,600 square feet."
- **9. Socastee**: "Lots are about 0.2 acre, and most houses for sale were built from 1979 to 1986, in Rosewood Estates and Cimerron Plantation."
- **11. Garden City Beach**: "Most of the houses for sale here are within half a mile of the shore. Garden City grew up in the 1950s." / "Huntington Beach State Park is known for birding and surf fishing."
- **12. Murrells Inlet**: "Most of the houses were built from the late 1960s to the early 1990s, on lots of about a quarter acre. Murrells Inlet is not a town, and part of it is in Georgetown County."
- **13. Conway**: "Billy Witherspoon Landing is a 6-minute drive. In town, the houses for sale are on a little over a third of an acre, and most were built before 1990."

Each line is true. Together they read like a spreadsheet row, and none says what the fact means for someone who wants a boat, a truck, a shed or no fee. Pine Lakes shows the right way: "typically almost half an acre, more than twice a downtown lot. There is room for a workshop or a boat beside the house." That turns a lot size into a reason to buy.

Do instead, in each listed entry:
- Turn the lot size into what fits on it, from the rule cards already on the page (a boat beside the house, a shed in back, room for a work truck), or cut it where it means nothing. Where a small lot is the trade-off (Downtown, Crescent Beach), say what it rules out for this buyer, in one line, as the trade-off. Say lot size this way in the entries where it is the point (Pine Lakes, Loris, the country, Aynor), not in all nine.
- Say the age of the houses only where it changes what you get (a carport, a garage apartment, a porch), as Myrtle Heights does. Cut it elsewhere.
- Add one or two sentences on why buyers choose this place over the next one. Kinds of fact to research: what our agents hear from buyers who bought there, what an owner does on a Saturday, and what the older houses have on the lot (a carport, a detached garage, a side yard gate).
- Join the short lines into paragraphs a person would say out loud. Keep the length: the entries must not get shorter.

## Medium findings

**Medium 1. The map names 11 of 16 areas, and Loris and the country around it are hard to tell apart.**
Crescent Beach (5), Ocean Drive (6), Surfside (10), Loris (14) and Aynor (16) are bare numbers. The large outline north of Conway has "14" inside it, and the bold label "15 Around Loris" sits beside it with no circle of its own. A reader cannot tell which shape is the town and which is the country. Aynor's outline is the second largest shape on the map and has no name.
Do instead: give every circle a name pill, as 13 and 15 have. Put Crescent Beach, Ocean Drive and Surfside pills over the ocean with a leader line. Mark Loris the town as a small circle with its own "14 Loris" pill inside the "15 Around Loris" outline.

**Medium 2. The short-term rental cards still give non-answers.**
This is the one column a renter came for, and four of seven cards do not say yes or no:
- Horry County: "County zoning does not name it, and short stays pay county taxes."
- Aynor: "The town's zoning does not name it."
- Surfside Beach: "Only in two zones."
- Myrtle Beach: "Only in the resort and business zones and one residential zone."
- Loris: "Not a listed use in city zoning, and a use not listed is not permitted." That means no; say "No."

The owner's "entries that nobody will ever understand" is about exactly this kind of cell.
Do instead: answer each card in the reader's terms: "Yes", "No", or "Only on [streets or areas a reader knows]". Research what the county and Aynor actually allow for a short stay at a house today (a permit, a business license, or nothing), and say it. Name Surfside's two zones and Myrtle Beach's residential zone by where they are, not by their count.

**Medium 3. Pine Lakes says "every home for sale", then names a section with a fee.**
- The chip: "Listed with no HOA fee: every home for sale".
- The last body line: "One section is different: the newer Preserve at Pine Lakes has an HOA fee."
- The best neighborhoods page's Pine Lakes entry is The Preserve and Pine Lakes Estates, "about $600,000" with "an HOA fee of about $95 a month."

This is a mild echo of the Plantation Point problem. A reader who sees both pages sees one name with two answers.
Do instead: name this entry by its own streets ("Pine Lakes' older streets") in the heading, the map pill and the guide. Make the Preserve line say it is not part of this entry: "The Preserve at Pine Lakes, the newer section by the club, has an HOA fee and is not counted here." Then add the honest trade-off the entry lacks; it is the only entry with none.

**Medium 4. More areas, as he asked.**
"do that for as many areas as possible." The count is 16, the same as the last three versions. Murrells Inlet was added and Plantation Point cut. 14e named candidates that were not tested: older sections of Forestbrook, Bucksport, Green Sea, Galivants Ferry, and the beach streets between Myrtle Beach and North Myrtle Beach.
Do instead: run the same share test on each candidate. Add each one where most homes for sale show no fee, with a full entry. Twenty is a better answer to "as many as possible" than sixteen.

**Medium 5. Seven areas have no photo (one finding).**
Pine Lakes, Windy Hill, Ocean Drive, Socastee, Surfside Beach, Murrells Inlet and the country around Loris are text only, pending the photo partner. Do instead: keep all seven on the partner's shot list, with a street of the older houses in each, and add Loris Main Street without the traffic lights (low 7).

## Low findings

Say the fact: "Every house for sale in Crescent Beach is less than half a mile on foot from a public beach access." Leave the method in the ledger.
- **Low 2. Trivia that sells nothing.** "South Carolina made it the official state dance in 1984." "In the Ocean Drive and Crescent Beach area, a pavilion and amusement park drew crowds who danced the shag." "Brookgreen Gardens, created in 1931 by the sculptor Anna Hyatt Huntington and her husband on four former rice plantations." "In the twentieth century, a fleet of charter and party boats grew up at the inlet." "Horry was long a county of small family farms, and its farms still have tobacco barns." "The Main Street library has been in its building for 50 years." "State law names the third Saturday in September for it." Conway's "steamer terminal from about 1880" and "an 1897 fire". Cut each, or say what it gives an owner today.
- **Low 3. Road numbers and a far landing.** "between US 17 Business and the creeks of the inlet" (Murrells Inlet) and "Chris Anderson Landing on SC 9, about half an hour away" (the country around Loris). Name places by what a reader knows. A landing half an hour away is not a benefit; give the closest ramp, or cut the line.
- **Low 4. Sentences true of any place.** "Its low-density neighborhoods have mature trees and quiet roads." (Surfside). "Huntington Beach State Park is known for birding and surf fishing." (Garden City, with no distance). Replace each with a fact only that place has, or cut it.
- **Low 5. The trade-off shape.** "The trade-off is" opens five trade-offs, and flood is the trade-off in six entries (Windy Hill, Cherry Grove, Socastee, Surfside, Garden City, Murrells Inlet). Flood is the honest cost there, so keep it. Vary the opening, as Ocean Drive's "Crowds come with it" does.
- **Low 6. Prices that differ from best neighborhoods.** Cherry Grove "about $850,000" here and "about $695,000" there. Surfside $940,000 and $705,000. Pine Lakes $930,000 and $600,000. One is list prices of no-fee houses, the other recorded sales of all houses. Say "asking" in this page's price line, or match the measure.
- **Low 7. Loris photo.** Still about half sky, with three red traffic lights across the top. Ask the partner for a Main Street frame at eye level.
- **Low 8. Socastee leads with price.** The heading "Socastee: the lowest prices, 2 minutes from a boat ramp" and the first words "Socastee has the lowest prices on this list". Every other entry keeps price last. Lead with the ramp two minutes away and the county's rules for what you keep at home; let the last line say it is the lowest price on the page.
- **Low 9. Windy Hill's music line.** "In October, its stage has live music on Friday and Saturday afternoons." One month reads as a calendar note. Give what runs most of the year, or cut it.

## What the page forgot that a buyer types into Google

- "can I put a mobile home on my land near Myrtle Beach": the country entries say nothing about whether a manufactured home is allowed on a county lot. Research the county's rule and say it once in the "Outside the towns" card.
- "well and septic": a buyer of a country lot around Loris or Aynor wants to know whether a house there is on public water and sewer or a well and septic tank, and what we check. One line in the country entries.

Both were in 14e's list and remain.

## The best three things: keep them

1. **The top.** The short answer, a slim offer, one map and a guide by feature ("A boat at home, or a boat ramp within 10 minutes", "Room to spread out"), and the first area under two phone screens. It is the fastest top in the set. "Almost every house for sale outside a named subdivision is listed with no homeowners association (HOA) fee, and so are about three in four built before 1990" changes how a buyer searches.
2. **The rule cards and the Teresa example.** Plain answers with labels: "Yes, behind the front of the house or under a carport." "Yes, up to 18,500 pounds." "Yes, up to two boats or trailers." No other page in the set has this, and it is what this reader came for. The Teresa example tells in one reading why a Myrtle Beach address can rule out a boat, and what the agent did.
3. **Leads and trade-offs written for this reader.** Windy Hill: "you can tie up a boat at the docks free for the day." Cherry Grove's canals dredged "to give owners boat access to House Creek and the Intracoastal Waterway at high and low tide", with the dredging fee and flood as its honest trade-off. Surfside's "two boats or trailers at a home" and golf carts on town streets. Pine Lakes' "room for a workshop or a boat beside the house". Myrtle Heights' avenue ends that "now give public access to the sand" and the appearance board as the cost. The country around Loris: "the Solid Waste Authority provides no curbside trash pickup." Ocean Drive's "Crowds come with it".
