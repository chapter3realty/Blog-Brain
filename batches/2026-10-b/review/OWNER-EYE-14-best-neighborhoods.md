## Grade: 7 of 10

A clear step up. The entries now carry facts a buyer could not guess: Sports Illustrated planned at Pine Lakes, the 1,780-pound tiger shark off Cherry Grove Pier, four courses opened on one day at Barefoot, a swing bridge moved from Edenton. Every place ends with its own offer. The photos are real aerials of each place, labeled, and the guide sorts by feature.

It does not reach 9 for four reasons:
- the preamble is still 3.2 phone screens;
- six entries still read as amenity lists and drive-time lists, not a day in the place;
- the page ends with about 9 phone screens of price tables, the same shape the gated and affordable pages use;
- three entries (Berkshire Forest, Deerfield, Forestbrook Estates) do not sell the place at all.

## Findings

### High

**H1. The preamble is 3.2 phone screens.** H1 at 213 px, entry 1 at 2,905 px. The same idea is said three times before the reader reaches anything:
- subtitle: "Fifteen places, by what each one does best.";
- dek: "Fifteen neighborhoods from North Myrtle Beach to Murrells Inlet: a normal day in each, one honest trade-off, and the price.";
- short answer: "We sorted 15 neighborhoods from North Myrtle Beach to Murrells Inlet by what each does best."

Do instead:
- Cut the subtitle and the dek.
- Make the short answer answer, not describe the page. The FAQ already has the answer: "For walking to the beach, Cherry Grove and the ocean side of Surfside Beach. For golf, Pine Lakes and Tidewater Plantation ..." That sentence is the short answer, but it lists the names, so keep it to the best one or two picks and let the guide carry the rest.
- On a phone, put the map before the guide or cut it to a small strip, so the 15 names do not appear twice in a row.
- Target: entry 1 within 2 screens.

**H2. The short answer says nothing.** "The best neighborhoods in Myrtle Beach are the ones built for what you want to do every day." That is true of any town. Replace it with picks and the reason for each (see H1).

**H3. Three entries do not sell the place.**
- **14. Berkshire Forest.** It leads with a pool ("Berkshire Forest's first pool complex has a water slide"). Then builder history: "Centex Homes developed the first sections, such as Crestwood, Sutton Place and Cambridge. Pulte Homes later added land from two neighboring developments." Then a school drive time. Then a comparison to another entry: "earlier than most of those for sale in Waterbridge, built since 2023." Nothing here makes a buyer want to live there.
- **15. Deerfield Plantation area.** The middle of the entry is a court case: "The court sent the case to a special referee, who found no rule on that land that kept it a golf course. The state Court of Appeals upheld that finding." Then a building site and a beach access with "three parking spaces, for golf carts only". It reads as a warning.
- **12. Forestbrook Estates.** It has no photo, the only entry without one. It reads as a list: a school list ("Every home here is zoned for Forestbrook Elementary, Forestbrook Middle and Socastee High"), a Food Lion, a boat ramp 5 miles away. Then it runs itself down: "The neighborhood has ponds, not a lake. Its houses cost a little more than The Farm's, but they are newer."

Do instead: for each, research what an owner does there on a Saturday and what only that place has, then lead with it. Kinds of facts to look for:
- the lot sizes and the tree cover from county GIS (the guide sells Deerfield on "wooded streets" and Berkshire on "room for the money", but no entry gives a lot size);
- what the HOA calendar holds;
- the closest landing, trail or park, with what you do there;
- for Deerfield, what the new houses on the old South Course are like.

Keep the court case to one line inside the trade-off, or cut it. Ask the photo partner for a Forestbrook Estates shot.

**H4. The page repeats the gated page and the Farm page.** The owner: "the same information was given to gated communities as you gave to affordable communities they dont care about the same things."
- Tidewater: "That year Golf Digest and Golf Magazine both named it the best new public course in America", the beach cabana at Cherry Grove and the guard house on Little River Neck Road are all on the gated page.
- Grande Dunes: the Ocean Club, "126 wet slips" and "The two-mile Waterway Trail is open to the public" are all on the gated page.
- Barefoot: the beach cabana and the free shuttle are on the gated page.
- The Farm: the flytrap preserve lead is on the Farm page and on the affordable page.
- Cherry Grove: the flood zone and the $1,700 dredging fee are on the no-HOA page, in the same words.

Do instead:
- Choose facts for this page's reader, someone choosing by the beach, golf, a boat, a walkable center, new homes or space, and leave the gated page its own facts.
- For Tidewater, Barefoot and Grande Dunes, lead with what a normal day there gives a buyer who does not care about the gate.
- Cut the three "Our guide to gated communities ..." links inside entries 4, 5 and 8. One line under the guide is enough.

**H5. The page ends with about 9 phone screens of prices.** The owner on the no-HOA page: "its too hung up on the price".
- The comparison table (4.8 screens on a phone, the longest section on the page) repeats each entry's price line word for word.
- Then "What does a home here cost each year, besides the mortgage?" (2.2 screens) gives a tax and flood breakdown for three places.
- That cost section has the same shape as the gated page's "What does a gated neighborhood cost to live in each year?" and the affordable page's "How much property tax will you pay?". That is "Every page was the same."

Do instead:
- Cut the yearly cost section from this page and link to the cost guide in one line.
- If the table stays, make it compact on a phone (one line per place, not 15 cards) with the columns this reader chooses by: what it does best, the drive to the beach, the price.
- Or cut the table, since each entry already ends with its price.

### Medium

**M1. The dek promises "a normal day in each", and most entries do not give one.** Only Waterbridge ("card nights, bunco, mahjong, a book club and potluck Thursdays") and Market Common (the Saturday farmers market) show a day. The others list amenities: "Owners share pools with hot tubs, courts for tennis, pickleball and bocce, a fitness center and a clubhouse" (Tidewater). "That amenity area has two pools joined by waterfalls. The clubhouse has a gym, a great room with a fireplace, a reading and card room and a kitchen" (Plantation Lakes). The owner praised "what a normal week is like (clubs, events, activities)" on 2026-10-11. Do instead: in each entry, replace the amenity list with one or two sentences of a real week there, from the HOA newsletter or calendar, or what our agents know. Gloss the pool in three words.

**M2. Drive-time lists are field lists in prose.** repeat-check: "minute" in 9 of 15 entries, "away" in 9, "mile" in 8. Quotes:
- Wild Wing: "Coastal Carolina University is about 10 minutes from Wild Wing by car, Conway Medical Center about 8, and the Conway Riverwalk about 15. The ocean is about 20 minutes away by car."
- Prince Creek: "the entrance to Huntington Beach State Park is 12 minutes by car. A Publix is about 2 minutes away."
- The Farm: "a Publix is 2 minutes by car and the beach about 10."
- Three "is about N miles away by road" sentences: Tidewater, Forestbrook, Deerfield.

Do instead: keep one drive time per entry, the one this reader chose the place for. The beach drive is already in the table.

**M3. "We check" became a device.** Five entries close a paragraph the same way:
- "We check the flood zone of any home you like before you offer."
- "so we check which one a home belongs to before you offer"
- "so we compare the fee on each home you like"
- "so we ask about a space before you offer"
- "we find out which homes include it"

Do instead: keep it in the two places where it matters most (Waterbridge's full boat storage; Barefoot's many associations). Cut the rest.

**M4. Pools carry the guide and the pictures.**
- Guide labels: "The Farm at Carolina Forest: two pools", "Berkshire Forest: a water slide", and the group name "New homes and big pools".
- Photos whose label is a pool: Tidewater "Pool", Wild Wing "Pool complex", Plantation Lakes "Pools", Waterbridge "Pools and clubhouse", The Farm "Large pool", Berkshire "Pools".

Do instead:
- Rename the group to what the places do best, by feature.
- Give each a guide label a buyer could not guess.
- Crop or label each photo on the entry's lead: the marsh at Tidewater, the lake shoreline at Plantation Lakes, the paddle lakes at Waterbridge. Send the photo partner a shot list for the places where an aerial cannot show the lead.

**M5. Two photos do not show their entry's lead.**
- Grande Dunes leads with the Ocean Club and the marina. Its photo shows the houses of Palermo and Villa Venezia, and the waterway is not labeled.
- Waterbridge leads with "lakes to paddle". Its photo shows a clubhouse in a circle of streets, with no lake labeled.

Do instead: use or crop a view that shows the marina and the paddle lakes, labeled.

**M6. Grande Dunes says less than the gated page, and the two disagree.** This page: "The Ocean Club offers residents private oceanfront access and dining, and we find out which homes include it." The gated page: "a household membership to the Ocean Club comes with the home at no extra charge." One site, one value. Do instead: state the verified fact once, in the same terms on both pages, and cut the hedge.

**M7. Pine Lakes contradicts itself on a quick read.** "On the older streets near the club, the homes for sale have no HOA fee." The next sentence: "the HOA fee is about $95 a month." Do instead: say plainly that the two outlined sections pay about $95 and the older streets around them pay none. Or cut the older-streets line here; it belongs to the no-HOA page, which covers Pine Lakes.

**M8. Cherry Grove is missing from "A boat on the water".** The no-HOA page says the city dredges the Cherry Grove canals "so owners on the canals can take a boat to House Creek and the Intracoastal Waterway". That is the best fact a boater could want. Here, the canals appear only as a trade-off (flooding and a dredging fee). Do instead: list Cherry Grove under the boat group as well, and sell the canal dock in the entry, worded differently from the no-HOA page.

**M9. Unclear pictures.**
- Wild Wing: the "Golf course" label sits on houses and grass, and half the frame is cleared brown land. A reader cannot tell what to look at.
- Deerfield: "Old North Course land" sits on trees and roofs. The open bands are hard to see at phone width.
- The Farm: "Large pool" sits over the dark pond, so the pond reads as the pool.

Do instead: move each label onto its subject, or crop tighter. For Deerfield, outline the old course land.

**M10. The map labels are too small on a phone.** The labels on the numbered map are about 9 to 11 px tall at 390 px, and nine of the 15 dots have no name. CRAFT 4: "card labels, map labels, captions, table headers" are body size. Do instead: on a phone, show the map at full width with the numbers larger, or drop it below the guide as a small strip.

### Low

- **L1.** Two price sentences in Barefoot ("a condo pays about $680 a month in HOA fees", then the price line) and in Market Common ("A townhome here pays about $300 to $680 a month", then the price line). Fold each into one sentence.
- **L2.** Plantation Lakes: "The beach access nearest by car has no parking, and the next one, with a pay lot, is about the same distance away." It is hard to follow, and Deerfield uses the same beach-parking shape. Say the drive to a beach with parking, once.
- **L3.** "The clubs also offer memberships." (Grande Dunes). Filler. Cut it, or give the fact a buyer needs about joining.
- **L4.** The yearly cost sentence appears twice, word for word: in the cost section and in the FAQ. This goes away if H5 is done.
- **L5.** Cherry Grove: "The nearest public beach access is about 0.1 mile away." From where? Say "a short walk from most of these streets", if the row supports it.
- **L6.** The preview file has no viewport meta tag. A phone renders it as a desktop page. Check that the built page in the website repo has one; the live template should.

## The best three things: keep these

1. **The leads that only this place could have.** Pine Lakes: "Sports Illustrated was planned at Pine Lakes." Cherry Grove: "In 1964 a 1,780-pound tiger shark was landed from it, a world record at the time." Barefoot: "four golf courses ... all opened on the same spring day in 2000", and a bridge "moved here from Edenton, North Carolina". Surfside: a free pier "open until 1 a.m.", and the Floral Beach history. This is the "find information that isnt obvious" the owner asked for.
2. **An offer in every entry.** "Book a showing in X", in a glass box with that place's price in one plain sentence, linked to the contact form with the place named. This is exactly what he asked for.
3. **The guide plus map on desktop, and the labeled aerials.** On desktop, six feature groups sit beside a numbered map, one unit with no repeat. Pine Lakes (outlined sections beside the course), Cherry Grove (beach, canals, marsh) and Market Common (homes, Grand Park, the old runway) each say their point in a second. Waterbridge's club calendar is the "normal week" he praised on 2026-10-11.

## One-line answers

1. **Does every entry sell the place like the best agent in town, leading with something a buyer could not guess?** No. Berkshire Forest (a water slide), Deerfield (the old courses, then a lawsuit) and Forestbrook Estates (a garden plot, then schools) do not. Grande Dunes leads with what its guide label already says.
2. **Is there any "facts:" style label line, or field list in prose?** No label lines. Yes, field lists in prose: the drive-time lists in Wild Wing, Prince Creek and The Farm, and the amenity lists in Tidewater and Plantation Lakes.
3. **Does every place end with its own offer?** Yes, 15 of 15, each linked to /contact/?place= with the place named.
4. **Does it look modern (liquid glass), with charts that are simple to understand?** The look is modern glass. There is no chart. The comparison table becomes 15 stacked cards, 4.8 screens long on a phone, and the map labels are too small.
5. **Is any picture unclear?** Yes. Wild Wing (the golf label sits on houses), Deerfield (the old course land is hard to see) and The Farm (the pool label sits on the pond). Grande Dunes and Waterbridge do not show their lead.
