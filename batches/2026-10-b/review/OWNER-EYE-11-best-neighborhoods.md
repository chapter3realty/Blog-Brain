## Medium

**Medium 1. Plantation Lakes lost its point.** Entry 8:
- "Plantation Lakes is built around a large lake, with miles of shoreline, off Carolina Bays Parkway."
- "Its developer was American Mining Company, and the owners' association holds about 185 acres of common areas."

Round 10 read "with about 15 miles of shoreline". The ledger's verifier gave that wording as safe (row 218's note, from the association's site). "Miles of shoreline" is the weakened fact the brief warned of: it now says nothing a reader can picture. "Its developer was American Mining Company" raises a question the entry never answers: why would a reader care who developed it, unless it explains the lake? The entry also has no fact about life on the water beyond "Many homes back onto the lake, and the amenity area has a dock."

Want: "Plantation Lakes is built around a lake with about 15 miles of shoreline, off Carolina Bays Parkway." If a verified row says the lake was dug by the mining company, say that in one plain sentence ("The lake was dug as a sand mine before the homes were built"). If not, cut the developer.

**Medium 2. Two adjacent entries end on the same ranking sentence.**
- The Farm (7): "Of the four Carolina Forest neighborhoods here, The Farm has the lowest house prices, and its HOA fee is lower than the usual fee in the other three."
- Plantation Lakes (8): "Of the four Carolina Forest neighborhoods here, Plantation Lakes has the highest house prices."
- Forestbrook Estates (11): "Its houses cost a little more than The Farm's, but they are newer."

The owner's word: "youll get hung up on one thing". Two entries in a row open their last paragraph with the same eight words, and a third compares itself with The Farm. The reader must also work out which four. Plantation Lakes' line is not a trade-off a buyer feels; its price is already in the facts line. Want: The Farm keeps "Townhomes and condos at The Farm cost less, but they pay a second HOA fee on top of The Farm's." and drops the ranking line (its price and fee are in the facts line and the table). Plantation Lakes gets a real trade-off from a row already held: "The beach is about 20 minutes away by car, the longest drive of the four Carolina Forest neighborhoods here" or "the nearest beach access has no parking, so owners drive on to the next one" (row 227). Forestbrook Estates keeps its line.

**Medium 3. Pine Lakes and Prince Creek lost their trade-off, and Prince Creek ends on a list of notes.**
- Pine Lakes (10): round 10 ended "Few homes in the two sections come up for sale." It is gone. The entry now has no trade-off, and at 93 words it is one of the two shortest. Its history paragraph also lost a year: "and later took the name Pine Lakes" (was "in 1946").
- Prince Creek (15): "Bellwood Landing, behind its own gate, sells for more, about $850,000. A Publix is about 2 minutes away by car. Builders sold new townhomes here in the past year." Three facts with nothing to join them. "Builders sold new townhomes here in the past year" is a sales-records note, and it replaced the trade-off "but few homes are sold by their owners".

Want: Pine Lakes: "Homes in the two sections rarely come up for sale." Prince Creek: "A Publix is about 2 minutes away. Bellwood Landing, behind its own gate, sells for more, about $850,000." Then one trade-off line: "Few homes here come up for resale; most sales are builders' new townhomes."

**Medium 4. The FAQ disagrees with the page's own table.** FAQ "Is Carolina Forest or Myrtle Beach better to live in?": "The ones in the city of Myrtle Beach are about 5 to 10 minutes away." The table now gives Grande Dunes "About 12 minutes" (changed this round to match the gated page), and Grande Dunes is in the city. CRAFT 5: "One page, one value." Want: "about 5 to 12 minutes away".

**Medium 5. Measuring and records notes came back in four places.** CRAFT 5, "A verifier gives the fact; the writer gives the sentence."
- Waterbridge (5): "About 90 new houses were sold here in the past year, mostly by builders, and some of its newest streets are still filling in." Want: "Builders are still finishing its newest streets, so some homes are beside building work."
- Berkshire Forest (9): "River Oaks Elementary School is on Augusta Plantation Drive, about 0.1 mile from the Berkshire Village section." Round 10 said "next to". A distance to a school from a section is a surveyor's note. Want: "River Oaks Elementary School is at the edge of the neighborhood, on Augusta Plantation Drive." (row 228 says "at the edge").
- The Market Common (12): "Grand Park, with picnic shelters, ball fields and a playground, is about 0.1 mile from the nearest homes." With Cherry Grove's beach, that is "about 0.1 mile" three times.
