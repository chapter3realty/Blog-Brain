## Medium

**Medium 1. An absolute from one self-interested source.** Entry 3: "No other developer in the United States has opened four new courses at once." The only source is the resort's own history page (ledger row 116), and the row's verifier gave a safe wording without it. STANDARD FACT-4: a "most" or "only" needs two sources that agree. Want: cut the sentence; "opened four golf courses at the same time in April 2000" already says what is unusual.

**Medium 2. The pros say the same thing twice, one line apart.** "Some are on the ocean or have a marina on the Waccamaw River." Then: "You can live on the ocean, on the Intracoastal Waterway or on an old plantation." Want: one line: "You can live on the ocean, on the Intracoastal Waterway, by a river marina or on an old plantation."

**Medium 3. "The county does not repave" is told in two sections in a row.** Yearly cost: "the streets behind a gate are usually private, so the county does not repave them. We check how much the HOA has saved for that". Then the cons: "The county does not repave private streets." "We check the HOA's savings, so a one-time charge to every owner, a special assessment, is less likely." Want: keep it in the cons only, and give the yearly-cost section only the cost line and the flood check.

**Medium 4. The story still retells a facts line, and shares its shape with the no-HOA story.** Entry 8's facts line: "gates only at the back entrance on Myrtle Ridge Road; the front entrance on Burning Ridge Road is open." The story: "That entrance has no gate, and the gates are only at the back." A reader who read entry 8 knows the turn before it comes. The shape (the house fails the want; the agent shows homes elsewhere; the buyer offers there) is the no-HOA story's shape too. CRAFT 6: "Stories in a set must not share one shape." Want: change this one, since no-HOA's story carries a rule the reader needs. A different gated story could carry a fact the page does not otherwise give, such as what the gate means for guests or deliveries at one place (Litchfield by the Sea's registered guests), or the private streets and the HOA's savings. If the story stays, cut "the front entrance on Burning Ridge Road is open" from the facts line, so the story has its turn.

**Medium 5. Sixteen of twenty aerials say only the name.** Wachesaw, Wachesaw East, The Reserve, Willbrook, Litchfield Plantation and Heritage look alike at phone width: trees, fairways, roofs. The Willbrook aerial plainly shows the old rice-field squares at its top left, unlabelled, while the entry is about them. Want: one label per aerial for the feature the entry names: "Old rice fields" (Willbrook), "Avenue of Oaks" (Litchfield Plantation), "Marina" (The Reserve, Heritage), "Clubhouse" (Wachesaw), "Beach Club" (Litchfield by the Sea). CRAFT 4: "Label what the reader must find." The photo partner list should start with the Avenue of Oaks, the Prestwick and Tidewater gatehouses and the Litchfield by the Sea beach club.

**Medium 6. The two cheapest gated places in the batch are on the affordable page, not here.** The affordable page names Parkview ("a gated community off 17th Avenue South", about $190,000) and Myrtle Beach Golf and Yacht Club ("a gated community off Bay Road", about $225,000 in the older part), and its entry 5 says "Our guide to gated communities near Myrtle Beach covers more." This page covers neither. Its "Homes under $550,000" legend starts at Myrtle Trace, about $300,000, and its short answer says "the 19 gated neighborhoods near Myrtle Beach", as if that were all. The gated ledger has no row for either place. A buyer on a budget who wants a gate is the reader this page can help most, with places only research finds. Want: verify the gate at both (the affordable ledger may already hold it), add them as entries with a facts line, add them to the under-$550,000 legend, and say "among the gated neighborhoods" rather than a fixed count unless the count is the full set.
