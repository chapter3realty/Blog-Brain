## Grade: 9 of 10

This page meets the bar. It does what the owner asked for this page, in the order he asked:
- many areas, in and around the city;
- each one circled and named on one map;
- a plain line on how many homes there have no fee;
- several paragraphs on what living there gives you;
- one line on the price;
- an offer to see the homes.

The 14f high finding is fixed in the entries it named. Downtown's built years and lot size are gone or turned into a reason. Conway's lot size now says what fits on it. Murrells Inlet's built years became the inlet's oyster roasts and the MarshWalk. The rental cards answer yes, no or "only in some zones". The map names 15 of 16 areas. Pine Lakes now matches its chip.

What is left is polish. A few single-fact lines remain, seven areas still wait for photos, and there are some small items. None of it is the kind of fault he sent the page back for.

It is not a 10 for two reasons. Some entries still read as a list of good facts more than as an agent saying why people choose the place. And seven of sixteen areas have no picture.

## Findings

No high findings.

### Medium

**Medium 1. A few single-fact lines remain, set on their own.**
The 14f fix landed in Downtown, Crescent Beach, Murrells Inlet and Conway. Two of the entries it named keep one field-style line:
- Socastee: "Most are 1980s houses in Rosewood Estates and Cimerron Plantation."
- Garden City: "Garden City did not develop until the 1950s, and most of the houses for sale here are within half a mile of the shore. The county's Stanley Drive landing is a 5-minute drive."

Other entries keep a one-sentence paragraph that names a place without saying what it gives an owner:
- Little River: "Hurricane Juel's, with a view of the waterway, has been family owned since 1945."
- Myrtle Heights: "Sea Captain's House, a restaurant in a 1930 beach cottage, is about a 9-minute walk from the middle of Myrtle Heights."
- Downtown: "A trail with a wooden boardwalk is beside Withers Swash, one of the city's oldest waterways, from Third Avenue South to Broadway and Collins Street."
- Windy Hill: "On 41st Avenue South, a pocket park with garden plantings, two pergolas and a walking path is on the site of the old water tower."

Do instead:
- Socastee: say what the 1980s houses give this buyer, such as a carport, a garage or a side yard for the boat, from the listings. Or join the subdivision names to the ramp sentence ("From Rosewood Estates and Cimerron Plantation, Rosewood Landing is 2 minutes by car").
- Garden City: join the landing line to the pier lead as a boat owner's day. Say the half-mile share as the walk to the sand. Cut "did not develop until the 1950s" unless it says something about the houses.
- The others: fold each landmark into the paragraph about a normal day there (breakfast, a walk with the dog, an evening out). Keep the entry length.

**Medium 2. Seven areas have no photo (one finding, pending the photo partner).**
The seven are Pine Lakes' older streets, Windy Hill, Ocean Drive, Socastee, Surfside Beach, Murrells Inlet's north end and the country around Loris.
Do instead: keep all seven on the partner's shot list. Ask for a street of the older houses in each, and a boat beside a house where the entry promises one (Socastee, Pine Lakes, the country around Loris).

### Low

- **Low 1. The map leaves 12 unnamed.** Every other ring has a pill. "12" at the bottom, beside "11 Garden City", is bare. Do instead: add a "Murrells Inlet" pill over the ocean with a leader line, as Garden City has.
- **Low 2. The Little River aerial's labels.** The "Salt marsh" pill sits over trees and yards on the left. The marsh is at the lower right, beside the waterway label. A big paved lot is the first thing the eye finds. Do instead: move "Salt marsh" onto the marsh, and label the docks on Mineola Avenue, the fact the lead sells.
- **Low 3. A forward reference and loose "Most".** Crescent Beach: "Few of them need flood insurance for a mortgage. In Cherry Grove, almost every home does." This compares to an entry the reader has not reached, and it says Cherry Grove's trade-off twice. Crescent Beach's "Most are older beach houses" and Socastee's "Most are 1980s houses" also start with no noun. Do instead: cut the Cherry Grove sentence, and name the subject: "Most of the houses for sale are older beach houses".
- **Low 4. Trivia that sells nothing.** Two lines still do not say what they give an owner today:

  Cut each, or join it to something an owner can do now.

  - Cherry Grove: "It has a two-lane boat launch" (inside a longer sentence) and "It is also the way in to Heritage Shores Nature Preserve".

  Name the ramp and the preserve in each.
- **Low 6. - **Low 8. The calendar device.** Most entries carry a festival or holiday line, and the Fourth of July appears in four of them:
  - Cherry Grove's fireworks;
  - Surfside's golf cart parade and its fireworks rule;
  - Garden City's boat parade;

  Each is true and local. Together they become a slot. Do instead: keep the two strongest (Surfside's golf cart parade, Garden City's boat parade), and replace the others with something an owner does on an ordinary week.
- **Low 9. What a buyer types into Google, still missing.**
  - "can I put a mobile home on my land": add one line in the "Outside the towns" card, from the county's rule.
  - "well and septic": add one line in the country entries on public water and sewer versus a well and septic tank, and say that we check it.

## The best three things: keep them

1. **The top.** These five things come before the first area, in under two phone screens:
   - the short answer: "Almost every house for sale outside a named subdivision is listed with no homeowners association (HOA) fee, and so are about three in four built before 1990";
   - a slim offer;
   - one map with every area named;
   - a guide by feature: "A boat at home, or a ramp within 10 minutes", "Within half a mile of the sand", "Room to spread out", "A small town with old streets".

   It is the fastest top in the set.
2. **The rule cards and the Teresa example.** No sibling has anything like them, and they are what this reader came for:
   - "Yes, up to 18,500 pounds."
   - "Yes, up to two boats or trailers, but a licensed trailer over 21 feet counts as one per home."
   - "No, it's not a listed use in city zoning."

   Teresa's boat and the city's front-yard rule show in one reading why the address matters and what our agent did.
3. **Leads and trade-offs written for someone who wants freedom.** Each one is a reason to buy that a buyer could not guess, with its honest cost:
   - Downtown: "Even so, a lender will not require flood insurance on any of them."
   - Windy Hill: "Come by boat, and you can use the finger docks free for the day."
   - Cherry Grove: canals dredged for access "at high and low tide", with "about $1,700 a year for the dredging" as the cost.
   - Surfside: "up to two boats or trailers at a home" and golf carts on the streets.
   - Pine Lakes: "There is room for a workshop or a boat beside the house."
   - Loris: the library that lends cake pans.
   - The country around Loris: "no curbside trash pickup there, so you hire a hauler".
   - Ocean Drive: "Crowds come with it."
