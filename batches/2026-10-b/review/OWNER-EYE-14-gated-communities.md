## High findings

**H1. The first community is 3.2 phone screens down, and the map repeats the list.**
- Location: from the H1 to "1. Tidewater Plantation". The dek ("What each gated community does best, from a beach club at Pawleys Island to a marina with a dock master, then what homes cost.") says what the page will do, not anything new. The map card ("North Myrtle Beach, Numbers 1 to 3 ... Pawleys Island to DeBordieu, Numbers 15 to 20") is a second pass through the same 20 numbers right after the guide.
- Do instead:
  - Cut the dek. The subtitle and the short answer already say it.
  - Put the map inside the guide, or cut it. On a phone, either show each area name as a small heading inside the guide ("North Myrtle Beach: 1, 2, 3"), or move the map below the price table, where "where are they" is a reference, not a gate.
  - Make the guide denser: one line per group, names as short chips, and move "More on this page" to after the entries.
  - Target: entry 1 within about 2 screens (1,700 px) of the H1 at 390 px.

**H2. 18 of the 20 entries have no picture.**
- Location: only Litchfield by the Sea (18) and DeBordieu (20) have a photo. The page is the longest of the five and the only one where a reader scrolls 17 entries of text without seeing the place. The brief asks for "the architecture and the views"; the page cannot show either.
- Do instead:
  - Send the photo partner a shot list for the gated page now (`templates/photo-request-email.md`): the gatehouse, the clubhouse or club, the marina or dock, and one street of homes, for each of the 20. Start with the ones whose lead is a thing you can see: Heritage's crow's nest dock house, The Bluffs' boathouse clubhouse, Litchfield Plantation's Avenue of Live Oaks and plantation house, Waterway Palms' boat ramp, Wachesaw's Kimbels on the bluff.
  - Until his photos arrive, give each entry one labeled public-domain aerial of the place itself, the way best-neighborhoods does. Each label must point to the thing the entry leads with (the ramp, the marina, the club on the beach), placed from coordinates.
  - Do not reuse best-neighborhoods' aerials for Tidewater, Barefoot and Grande Dunes as they are; their labels show what that page says, not this one.

**H3. The same trade-off, and the same amenities, in entry after entry.**
- Location, the trade-off slot:
  - 1. "a golf membership costs extra"
  - 4. "That club is a separate membership."
  - 10. "The course has been fully public since 2016, so golfers who do not live here play it too."
  - 13. "Club membership is a separate cost on top of the HOA fee"
  - 15. "each is a membership of its own, apart from the HOA"
  - 16. "open to the public, so the neighborhood does not set the tee times"
  - 19. "Golf membership is not part of the HOA fee"
  - 20. "Club membership is separate from the association and its fee"
  - and again in Cons: "a golf or beach club is often a separate cost".
- Location, the amenity roll call: "tennis and pickleball courts and a fitness center" (1), "a hot tub and parking for golf carts" (2), "tennis and pickleball courts, a fitness center and sidewalks throughout" (5), "tennis and pickleball courts and a playground" (6), "a gate, a pool and a clubhouse" (7), "a large pool, a smaller pool and a tennis center" (10), "indoor and outdoor pools and a fitness center, plus tennis and bocce" (11), "its own large pool, tennis courts, workout room and clubhouse" (14). He said: "if its next to a pond ... gloss over it". A pool and courts are the pond of a gated page.
- Do instead:
  - Say once, near the top: at most of these, the golf or beach club is a separate membership from the HOA. Then research what joining costs at each private club (initiation and yearly dues, from the club, a member, or our agents) and give that number where it exists. The research notes (`facts/r14/gated-research-a.md`) say the Grande Dunes dues sit behind a login; ask our agents or the club's membership office.
  - Give each entry a trade-off only that place has: Dye Estates' pond rules and unbuilt lots, The Bluffs' S.C. 31 edge, DeBordieu's beach fund, Litchfield Plantation's rice fields still owned by the developer. Where a place has no trade-off of its own, leave the slot out rather than use the membership line.
  - Keep a pool, courts or a clubhouse only where it is unusual (Heritage's Har-Tru league mornings, Wachesaw's eight clay courts). Cut it everywhere else.

**H4. Four entries are written for the affordable reader, not the gated one.**
- 2, Seaside Plantation: the lead repeats its headline and its guide label ("Main Street is less than half a mile away"). The rest is about Main Street's festivals, then "a Walmart Supercenter, a Lowe's and Gator Hole Plaza are a 3- to 5-minute drive". The neighborhood itself gets "the owners association owns the lakes, about 13 acres in all" and "a hot tub and parking for golf carts".
- 7, Covington Lake East: leads with the fee, then "brick houses ... curving streets among ponds ... about a quarter of an acre ... sidewalks ... natural gas", a field list in prose. The trade-off is what it lacks: "It has no golf club or boat ramp of its own."
- 9, Myrtle Beach Golf and Yacht Club: leads with "For an HOA fee of about $95 a month" and "Community Watch desk coverage 24 hours a day, seven days a week. The HOA also has maintenance coverage on weekdays." "The community calendar has categories for bingo, yoga, Zumba and mahjong" is a note about a web page, not a week there.
- 8, Myrtle Trace: leads with elevation and never says what it means for the buyer. Then "The pool logged more than 4,000 visits in the 2026 season" and "the board voted not to pursue a change for now; the current ten-year period of the rules ends in 2033", which read like board minutes.
- Do instead: research these four the way the strong entries were researched: who built it and when, what the land was, what an owner does on a Saturday, what the gate is like and how a guest gets in. Lead with that. For Seaside, lead with something inside the gate and give Main Street one sentence. For Myrtle Trace, say in one plain line what high ground means for the buyer, only if a verified row supports it (flood zone or insurance). If a place still has nothing a gated buyer would pay to know, consider cutting it from the page.

## The best three things: keep them

1. **The leads that only research finds.** Barefoot's guest process ("The guest calls from the keypad, and the owner presses 9"), the Ocean Club with the Grande Dunes home, the Wachesaw dig, Willbrook's markers and the Oatland cemetery, Litchfield Plantation's owners taking back the house in 2018, Heritage's crow's nest and sunset deck, DeBordieu's beach fund and "Locals say 'Debidue'". These are what he asked for: "find information that isnt obvious and therefore is intriguing and valuable."
2. **One offer per place, and a price in one plain sentence.** "Homes at Tidewater usually sell for about $625,000. The HOA fee is about $230 a month." then "Book a showing in Tidewater Plantation", linked with the place named. Twenty times, with no "facts" label.
3. **A guide by feature, never by people.** "A marina with staff", "A boat ramp at home", "History you live inside", "Lots of nearly half an acre". It lets a stranger choose without knowing a single name, and it is fair-housing safe.
