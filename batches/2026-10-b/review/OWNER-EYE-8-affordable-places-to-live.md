The owner asked for roads, and the access roads (SC 905, Bay Road, SC 707, Glenns Bay Road) answer him: they tell a buyer where to drive. Want: keep the access road in every entry; keep inside street names only at Victory Point (and in the facts if a buyer needs them to search); use the line for something only that place has.

## Medium

**Medium 1. A builder-sale sentence in five entries.** "Lennar built and sold nearly every house recorded here in the past year." (5) "D.R. Horton built and sold every townhome recorded here in the past year" (6) "Every house recorded here in the past year was a Lennar sale." (7) "Lennar built most of the townhomes sold here in the past year." (9) "D.R. Horton built nearly every townhome sold here in the past year" (12). The fact matters once: the cheapest new homes near Myrtle Beach are national builders' entry homes. Say that once above the list; in each entry give what that builder's homes there have (the smart-home package at Amber Bluff is a good example).

**Medium 2. "On this list" comparisons, seven times.** "the one place on this list inside the city" (1), "the fastest pace on this list" (7), "the highest on this list" (8), "Unlike the new neighborhoods on this list" (11), "the lowest on this list" (11), "the closest place on this list to the ocean" (12), plus "Homes here cost the most of the twelve, and the beach is the closest." (12, which also repeats the sentence before it). RULES 4: write about the house, not the page. Want: "the only one of the twelve inside the city", and cut the second Glenns Bay sentence.

**Medium 3. Every entry has a seven-row table that repeats its paragraphs.** "Parkview in short: Homes usually sold for / What you get / Size / HOA fee / Drive to Coastal Grand Mall / Drive to the beach / Schools". Twelve of them: 84 rows, most with a number. The paragraphs already say the HOA fee and what it includes ("The HOA fee includes water, sewer, internet and the grounds." then the table: "About $325 a month, with water, sewer, internet and the pool"). The owner: "too many numbers"; ROUND-7: price "in one line". Want: one facts line per entry (price, homes, fee, beach minutes), and the schools and mall drive in the comparison chart or one table after the list.

**Medium 4. Six entries say little beyond the builder and the streets.** Parkview, Sawgrass East, Birchwood, Indigo Grove, Polo Farms and The Cove at Glenns Bay. For example, Polo Farms: the roads, three street names, "every sale here in the past year was a resale", one-story houses, and "Its HOA fee is the lowest on this list." Why are these the cheapest? What is the catch? That is the deep answer this page can give and others cannot: small two-bedroom units in Parkview, a $450 fee in Wynbrooke, a 40-minute drive from Birchwood to the mall. Want: one line per entry on why the price is low, from rows already held.

**Medium 5. No trade-off per place, and flood is not on the page.** The must-answer list for this page (PLAN.md) includes "what the trade-off is (drive, flood, older homes, HOA)". Drive and HOA are in the tables; "flood" appears only in the first offer ("We check ... the flood map"). Coopers Bluff is between SC 544 and the Waccamaw River; Wynbrooke and Sawgrass East are on ponds. Want: a trade-off line per entry, and flood where a verified row says it matters.

**Medium 6. No yearly cost besides the mortgage.** CRAFT 1: "on any page about where or what to buy, give one line for the typical home: about $X a year besides the mortgage". The tax section gives "about $970 a year" for a $240,000 home, and the fees are in the tables, but nothing adds them. For a buyer on a budget this is the number that matters: Wynbrooke at $250,000 with a $450 fee costs far more a year than Polo Farms at $255,000 with a $20 fee. Want: one line per entry or a column: "about $X a year in HOA fee and tax".

**Medium 7. The first offer is a different, smaller box, and talks about the form.** First: a pale card, "See what your budget buys in these neighborhoods." in bold body-size text, then four sentences ending "The form asks your name, email and phone." The last is the navy box. Same box finding as the other pages; the form sentence talks about the page (RULES 4). Cut it.

**Medium 8. Method in the body.** "We joined the county's deed records to its map of lots, so every sale has a neighborhood. We left out condos and sales far below a home's value, and kept neighborhoods where at least 10 homes sold." CRAFT 5: "Method and attribution go in the sources line." The owner: "talking about all the details and exact things just sound like you're flexing your smarts." Want: "These are the twelve neighborhoods near Myrtle Beach where homes sold for the least in the past year." The method is already in the sources line.

## Low

1. Six of the twelve entries have no picture (Parkview, Birchwood, Amber Bluff, Indigo Grove, Polo Farms, Glenns Bay). The six aerials have no labels; "Wynbrooke, off SC 707, from above." shows townhomes but does not mark SC 707. Label the road and the pool, and put these six on the photo partner's list. (Not medium only because the price chart and map carry the page's main fact.)
2. The short answer says "townhomes in Parkview, Wild Wing Plantation and Carolina Forest". Entry 3 is Sawgrass East; Carolina Forest is an area. Name the neighborhood.
3. Cross-page, for the verifier: "Wild Wing was once a golf resort with several courses. Three of them closed in 2004. The Avocet course ... is still open" here; best neighborhoods says "With the nine-hole Hummingbird course, the club has 27 holes." The reader of both pages sees two stories of what is still open.
4. The down-payment section has eleven numbers in four paragraphs ("$115,000 ... $134,000 ... 640 ... $450,000 ... 4 percent ... $250,000 ... $10,000 ... 10 years"). Keep the $10,000, the income limit and the price cap; link the rest.
5. "You apply for that rate; it is not automatic." The owner does have to apply. "We remind you to apply after closing" would keep the we-do-the-work voice, if the agents do that.
6. The H1 says "affordable places to buy"; many readers type "cheapest places to live near Myrtle Beach". "Cheapest" is only in an FAQ. Use it once in the short answer.
