## High

**High 1. Two entries are still "surface level", and the reader cannot tell why they are on a best list.** `repeat-check` body words: Forestbrook Estates 35, Deerfield Plantation area 50 (range 35 to 154, median 104).
- 11, Forestbrook Estates, the whole body: "Lennar developed Forestbrook Estates, starting in 2018. Houses, and some townhomes, are built around ponds." "On the same grounds as the pool is a community garden with garden plots. Listings say golf carts are allowed."
- 13, Deerfield Plantation area, the whole body: "The Deerfield Plantation area is a wooded neighborhood near Surfside Beach. Its association owns the lakes and canals that run between the homes." "Beach parking takes planning. The closest beach access has parking for golf carts only, and the next one, with a pay lot, is a short drive away."

The owner asked for "a decent amount of information about each community" and "multiple paragraphs focusing on the special features and history". These read as a builder, a pond and a pool. "Listings say" is also the attribution hedge CRAFT 5 bans. Want, for each: who built it and when, what the land was, what a first visitor notices, and what an owner does on a normal day, from new ledger rows. For Deerfield: when it was laid out (the 1970s and 1980s houses say it is one of the older planned neighborhoods on the list), what the lakes and canals are used for, how far the walk or golf cart ride to the beach is. For Forestbrook Estates: what is close by on Forestbrook Road and SC 707, and why it is a "lower price" pick (it is in the legend under that want). If the research finds nothing more, replace the entry with a place that has a story.

## Medium

**Medium 1. Seven of fifteen entries have no trade-off.** ROUND-7 asked for "the trade-off, in one line" in each entry, and PLAN.md lists "the honest trade-off of each" as a must-answer. Entries with one: Cherry Grove (flood), Barefoot (which association), Wild Wing ("the farthest of the fifteen"), Grande Dunes ("The beach accesses closest to it have little or no parking"), Pine Lakes (few for sale), Deerfield (beach parking), Surfside (flood), Prince Creek (few resales). Entries with none: Tidewater, Waterbridge, The Farm, Plantation Lakes, Berkshire Forest, Forestbrook Estates, The Market Common. Want: one plain line each, from rows already held where they exist. Examples: The Farm, "townhomes and condos pay a second HOA fee" (already in its facts line; say it as the trade-off); The Market Common, "the HOA fee on a townhome is about $300 to $700 a month"; Waterbridge and Plantation Lakes, the beach drive of 15 to 20 minutes.

**Medium 2. The story's key fact comes after the story, behind an offer line.** The story ends "Megan makes her offer knowing what the house will cost her each year." Then: "An agent at Chapter3 can send you three neighborhoods that fit what you want, and the homes for sale in each." Then: "About 415 homes and lots in Cherry Grove pay that yearly fee for dredging the canals, about $1,700 now." "that yearly fee" points back across the offer line, and "now" is a date word in the body. Want: put the fee sentence under the yearly-cost table, as the note it is ("Cherry Grove canal homes also pay the city about $1,700 a year to dredge the canals; the totals above do not count it."), then the story, then the offer. The story then has its fact in place and ends the section.
