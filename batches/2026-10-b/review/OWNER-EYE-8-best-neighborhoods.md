# Owner's eye, round 8: /buyers/best-neighborhoods/

Reviewer: a fresh agent. I did not research, write, edit or review any page in this batch before. Read on 2026-10-14 from `scratchpad/preview/b/best-neighborhoods.html` (built 03:08). My screenshots, at 390 and 1280 px after a slow scroll, are in `scratchpad/oe-b8/`: `best-neighborhoods-390/` and `-1280/` (every screen and all 16 figures), the full pages, `cta/` (each offer box), `sheets/` (contact sheets) and `best-neighborhoods.txt`. I opened every map and aerial full size.

This is the page the owner read first: "too jumpy and alot about price instead of the amenities ... focus on what the house and location gives you", then on the second read "the same question and damn near the same answer".

**Grade: 7.** The shape is now what he asked for: the opening, a numbered map, then 15 entries, each a headline, paragraphs, one labelled aerial and a facts line. Many entries now carry real history (Cherry Grove's 1950 inlet and the 1964 shark, Pine Lakes and Sports Illustrated, the Air Force base, The Ark rice plantation). But one high and nine medium findings are left. The biggest: five entries are still two short paragraphs with nothing a buyer could not find in a minute, and the first screen still leads with price.

## High

**High 1. Five entries are still "surface level".** Body words, without captions and the facts line: Plantation Lakes 58, Berkshire Forest 55, Forestbrook Estates 50, Prince Creek 69, Waterbridge 104 but with no history. ROUND-7 set about 120 to 200 words; the owner asked for "multiple paragraphs focusing on the special features and history of that property".
- 9, Berkshire Forest, all of it: "Berkshire Forest's association pool complex, with a water slide, is beside a lake, with a tennis court, a basketball court and a play area next to it." "A Publix is a short drive away. Summer traffic is the drawback: the drive to the beach takes about 15 minutes with no traffic, and longer in July."
- 11, Forestbrook Estates, all of it: "Forestbrook Estates is mostly houses, with some townhomes, around ponds, and the association has a pool." "Rosewood Landing, a county boat ramp on the Intracoastal Waterway, is about 2 miles away. No home here is in a high-risk flood zone, so flood insurance is a choice, not a lender's rule."
- 8, Plantation Lakes: a pool on a point, courts, then "Its houses for sale are the largest on this page, and they cost more than in the other Carolina Forest neighborhoods here."
- 15, Prince Creek: "Prince Creek's sections border TPC Myrtle Beach, a golf course designed by Tom Fazio." Then a Publix, a hospital, a park.
- 5, Waterbridge: "Water is the theme in Waterbridge" and "the newest sections are still filling in". No builder, no start year.

Each reads as a pool, a store and a drive time. Want, for each: who built it and when (the deed index gives the first lot), what the land was, one thing a first visitor notices, and what an owner does there on a normal day. Prince Creek can borrow nothing from gated entry 11 (that history is the TPC course). This is the same root cause as ROUND-8: the research for these five came back as fields, not features.

## Medium

**Medium 1. The first screen still leads with price.** Two of the three hero tiles are prices: "Walk to the beach: Cherry Grove, about $695,000" and "A lower price: The Farm, about $350,000". The lede promises "with home prices, the homeowners association (HOA) fee and the drive to the beach." The short answer gives two prices in its first three sentences. The owner, on this page: "alot about price instead of the amenities". Want: tiles that say what each pick gives ("Walk to the beach: Cherry Grove", "Pools and a lower price: The Farm", "Golf: Pine Lakes, Tidewater, Barefoot") and a lede that says what living in each gives you. Keep one price line in the short answer.

**Medium 2. The first offer is a different, smaller box than the last one.** First: a pale card, thin orange edge, headline "Tell us what you want nearby. We will send three neighborhoods that fit, with the homes for sale in each." in bold body-size text. Last: the navy box with a large serif headline. ROUND-7 and CRAFT 3 ask for the same component. The wording is good; the box is not yet "more of a CTA".

**Medium 3. The short answer and the first FAQ open with a hedge.** "The best neighborhood in Myrtle Beach depends on what you want close by." and "The best neighborhood depends on what you want nearby." CRAFT 5 names "it depends on your needs" as filler. It is also the sentence an answer engine lifts. Want: "The best neighborhoods in Myrtle Beach for most buyers are Cherry Grove to walk to the beach, The Farm for pools at a lower price, and Pine Lakes, Tidewater Plantation and Barefoot Resort for golf."

**Medium 4. Price and "on this page" comparisons in the entry bodies.** The facts line already gives price and fee. These repeat them as a comparison, and talk about the page (RULES 4):
- "The HOA fee here is one of the higher ones on this page for a house." (1, its own paragraph)
- "the HOA fee on a condo here is several times what most houses on this page pay." (3)
- "Its houses for sale are the largest on this page, and they cost more than in the other Carolina Forest neighborhoods here." (8)
- "The HOA fee on a townhome is high for this page" (12)
- also "closer than anywhere else on this page" (2), "the farthest on this page" (4), "Of the four Carolina Forest neighborhoods on this page" (5).
Want: cut the price comparisons; the table after the list does that job. Say the others without "this page" ("the closest beach walk of the fifteen").

**Medium 5. A device in seven entries: the flood line.** "Almost every home here is in a high-risk flood zone" (2), "the only one with any homes in a high-risk flood zone" (5), "No home here is in a high-risk flood zone" (11), "a few homes are in a high-risk flood zone" (12), "none of its homes is in a high-risk flood zone" (13), "About 1 in 4 homes here is in a high-risk flood zone" (14), and the FAQ. CRAFT 5: "a flood line ... becomes a template when it appears in every entry." Want: keep it where it changes a decision (Cherry Grove, Surfside ocean side); put the rest in one sentence after the table: "Of the fifteen, only Cherry Grove and the ocean side of Surfside Beach have many homes in the high-risk flood zone."

**Medium 6. A second device in nine entries: "[a park], with [features], is [distance] away".** Heritage Shores "under a mile away" (1), Russell Burgess "a few blocks away" (2), Atlantic Center "about a mile away" (4), the Grissom Parkway trail "a few blocks away" (10), Rosewood Landing "about 2 miles away" (11), Grand Park "a few blocks from the nearest homes" (12), Fuller Park "under 2 miles away" (13), Fuller Park again "a few blocks away" (14), Burgess Park "a few miles away" (15). A nearby county park is true of almost any neighborhood. Keep it in two or three entries where the park is the point (Market Common's Grand Park, Cherry Grove's preserve); use the words for history elsewhere.

**Medium 7. Phrases and obvious sentences.**
- "The cost is the beach: about 20 minutes by car" (4) and "Summer traffic is the drawback:" (9): a colon set-up phrase the owner has banned in other forms. Say "The beach is about 20 minutes away by car, the farthest of the fifteen."
- "Water is the theme in Waterbridge" (5): a sales phrase.
- "A public beach access is a public path to the beach. Some have parking and some do not." Obvious.
- "Barefoot Resort is not a hotel, and The Farm is not a farm." Entry 7's headline then says "streets named for the farm".

**Medium 8. "What is it like to live here all year?" is filler.** "Life here goes on all year." "Hurricanes can reach this coast." "The highways we use, a few miles inland, avoid the stop lights on the main road along the coast." Each is true of the whole coast and says nothing about these fifteen places. Either give what our agents know (which of the fifteen are quiet in January, which fill with renters in July) or cut the section and keep the hurricane link in the FAQ.

**Medium 9. The yearly-cost section explains HOA fees in general.** "The HOA fee covers what the neighborhood shares. For a house, it usually includes the common areas, and often trash pickup and a pool. A condo fee usually also includes the building's insurance, water and sewer." True of any HOA anywhere. The table above it is the answer. Cut, or name what the fee buys in these three places (The Farm's includes cable TV and trash).

**Medium 10. The story repeats an entry nearly word for word.** Entry 13: "The closest beach access has parking for golf carts only, and the next one, with a pay lot, is about 8 minutes away by car." Story: "It has parking for golf carts only. The agent then shows her the next access, which has a pay lot about 8 minutes from the house." The story has no surprise, and its ending ("Megan knows where she will park each morning") has little tension. Want: cut the parking sentence from entry 13 and let the story carry it, or pick a story the entries do not already tell.

## Low

1. "Tidewater Golf Club, a par-72 public course credited to Ken Tomlinson": "credited to" is a hedge; the gated page states it.
2. "In the 1920s George J. Holliday renamed it Floral Beach ... in 1952 new owners named it Surfside Beach ... It became a town twelve years later." The reader must add. Say "in 1964".
3. "We check which association a home belongs to before you offer, because that decides what it can use." A fact does not decide (RULES 2). "...because each association has its own pools and clubs."
4. The Deerfield aerial has no labels; at phone width it is green texture. The Prince Creek aerial is half a construction site.
5. The overview map puts "Barefoot Resort" and "The Farm" labels far from their dots, near Conway; the leader lines are long and cross.
6. The title tag is "Best Neighborhoods in Myrtle Beach: Prices and Map". Price in the headline is what the owner said scares people. "Best Neighborhoods in Myrtle Beach: 15 Places to Live, With Map" says the same and searches the same.
7. "Is Carolina Forest or Myrtle Beach better to live in?" The answer never says which is better for whom. One clause fixes it: "Carolina Forest is better for a lower flood risk and newer homes; the city is better to be close to the beach."
8. The page is about 44 phone screens, the second longest of the set. Cutting mediums 4 to 9 shortens it more than high 1 lengthens it.

## Photos

Fifteen labelled aerials, one per entry, each showing its caption (the pool, the canals, the North Tower pool, the outlined sections). Fifteen aerials in a row look alike; the photo partner's ground shots (the Cherry Grove pier, the Pine Lakes clubhouse, Market Common's Grand Park, Wild Wing's bird-name street signs) would change the page most.

## The story, read once

Megan wants a cheaper house near the beach to drive to each morning; the nearest access has parking for golf carts only; the agent drives her there and shows her the next one with a pay lot. I retold it after one read. It passes the simple test. It fails "say something new" (medium 10).

## SEO and AEO

- H1 "What are the best neighborhoods in Myrtle Beach?" is the search. The first answer sentence is a hedge (medium 3).
- Each numbered H2 names the place and what it is best for. Good.
- ItemList with 15 items is in the page; every anchor exists.
- FAQ answers stand alone, except low 7.
- To rank and be quoted more: fix medium 3 so the lifted sentence names places; add "Is [place] a good place to live?" phrasing to two or three entry first sentences for the places people search by name (Market Common, Carolina Forest); the history facts in entries 2, 10, 12 and 14 are the kind answer engines quote, so give each place one.

## What I would send back

High 1, mediums 1 to 10.
