## Grade: 7 of 10

This is the best version of this page so far. Every place ends with its own offer, the "facts:" lines are gone, the chart lists each place's best thing beside its price, and four entries (Parkview, Victory Point, Wynbrooke, Polo Farms) sell the place well. It does not reach 9 for four reasons:
- the reader still scrolls three and a half phone screens before the first place;
- three entries repeat what the gated, best neighborhoods and Farm pages already say;
- four entries lead with drive times instead of something a buyer could not guess;
- six entries say almost nothing about the house itself, and the owner asked to be sold on "the amenities of the house and location".

## Findings

### High

**H1. 3.5 phone screens before the first place.**
- Where: from the H1 (213 px) to "1. Parkview" (3,197 px).
- The dek "homes usually sold for about $190,000 to $255,000, all within about 20 minutes of a beach" says the same range as the short answer's next sentence: "Homes in them usually sold for about $190,000 to $255,000 last year".
- The chart takes 1.7 screens, and its 12 bars run from $190,000 to $255,000, so they look almost the same length. On a phone they add height and no information.
- Do instead:
  - Cut the dek, or make it the one line that is not in the short answer.
  - Make the chart rows one line each on a phone: name, best thing, price, and no bar. Or keep the bars and start the scale near $150,000, so the differences show, and say so.
  - Shorten the chart title "What homes usually sold for in the past year, lowest first, with the best thing about each place" to a few words.
  - Target: entry 1 within about two screens.

**H2. Three entries repeat the sibling pages, which is the owner's "same information" complaint.**
- 5. Golf and Yacht Club leads with "Enterprise Landing, a public boat ramp off Bay Road onto the Intracoastal Waterway, is about a four-minute drive". The gated page's entry 9 has the same landing and the same four minutes. It also has Heron Point closing in 2014, the 24-hour community watch desk and D.R. Horton's Sandpiper Place. This page has all four.
- 3. Sawgrass East leads with "Lewis Ocean Bay Heritage Preserve ... South Carolina's largest Venus flytrap population". The Farm page and best neighborhoods entry 13 ("two pools and a flytrap preserve next door") use the same fact. The Farm borders the preserve. Sawgrass East is 15 minutes from it, so the fact is weaker here than on the other two pages.
- 2. The Fairways at Wild Wing repeats the Avocet course, "Larry Nelson and Jeff Brauer", from best neighborhoods entry 7.
- Do instead:
  - For Golf and Yacht Club, sell what a $225,000 buyer gets that the gated page does not cover: what the older houses are like inside, the RV and trailer storage, what the HOA's amenities cost compared with the fee.
  - For Sawgrass East, research what only Sawgrass East has: its history, its clubhouse, what the townhomes are like inside, what an owner does there on a Saturday.
  - Keep each shared fact on the one page where it sells best, and link to it from the others.

**H3. Four entries lead with drive minutes, not with something a buyer could not guess.**
- 2. Fairways: "Conway Medical Center is a six-minute drive from the Fairways. Horry-Georgetown Technical College and Coastal Carolina University are each about a seven-minute trip."
- 8. Amber Bluff: "Coastal Carolina University and Horry-Georgetown Technical College are each a six-minute trip from Amber Bluff, and Conway Medical Center a seven-minute one." This is the same three places in the same shape as entry 2.
- 6. Forestbrook Village: "Forestbrook Elementary and Forestbrook Middle are each four to five minutes from Forestbrook Village by car." A buyer can guess this from the name.
- 3. Sawgrass East: a preserve 15 minutes away (see H2).
- The whole page leans on drive minutes. repeat-check counts "minutes" 19 times, "drive" 10 and "trip" 8, plus "minutes away" in 7 of the 12 entries.
- The owner: "youll get hung up on one thing and then its all you ever want to talk about."
- Do instead:
  - Lead Fairways with the golf. Its own history is already in paragraph 2: "Wild Wing was once a golf resort".
  - Lead Amber Bluff with the smart-home package or game days at Brooks Stadium, a mile away.
  - Find Forestbrook Village's own lead: what the land was, what the townhomes have that resales nearby do not, the pool and playground.
  - Use drive minutes in at most two or three entries, where they are the striking fact (Parkview's four-minute beach drive).

**H4. The house itself is missing from half the entries.**
- The owner: "people want to be sold on the amenities of the house and location."
- 1. Parkview says only "gated townhomes ... around ponds and a pool". It does not say how many bedrooms or floors, how big the townhomes are, or whether they have a patio or a garage.
- 2. Fairways and 11. Wynbrooke say nothing about the inside of the homes.
- 12. Polo Farms has "one-story houses", "fenced" yards and "several have their own pool", which is good. It does not give sizes or bedrooms.
- 5. Golf and Yacht Club and 3. Sawgrass East give only square feet.
- Do instead: for each resale place, research the typical bedrooms, baths, floors, square feet, garage or carport, patio or porch, and the year built, from the county parcel records and listing data. Sell those in one or two sentences a person would say. This is also the "room to grow" item in the audience brief.

### Medium

**M1. A three-chip field row closes every entry.**
- "Usually sold for / HOA fee / Fee and tax a year" appears 12 times. It is not a "facts:" line, but it is the same three fields in every entry, and the price never appears in a sentence.
- "Fee and tax a year: About $4,700" (Parkview) cannot be read in a second. It is the HOA fee times 12 plus the tax.
- Do instead:
  - Put the price in one plain sentence in the entry, as CRAFT 5 asks: "Townhomes here usually sell for about $190,000, and the HOA fee is about $325 a month, which covers water, sewer and the pool."
  - If the chips stay, keep two and rename the third, for example "HOA fee and tax, a year".

**M2. Five aerials show woods, pasture or dirt where the homes are now.**
- 4. Birchwood: the orange dot sits in trees.
- 6. Forestbrook Village: the dot sits in woods.
- 8. Amber Bluff: the dot sits in woods north of US 501. Most of the picture is highway and campus.
- 10. Indigo Grove: the dot sits in a pasture.
- 9. Villas at Sandridge: the dot sits on a cleared lot at the edge of the frame.
- The captions say "before the houses were built", but a first-time buyer sees trees and does not see a home. Victory Point's speedway aerial is the exception. It is clear and makes its point.
- Do instead: send the photo partner a shot list for these five (`templates/photo-request-email.md`): the street of finished homes, the pool or amenity, the entrance. Until his photos arrive, cut the aerials whose point cannot be seen in a second.

**M3. There is no ground photo of any home or amenity.**
- All 12 pictures are labelled aerials of the same kind. The page sells "the amenities of the house" and never shows one.
- Do instead: add at least one ground photo per entry from the photo partner: Parkview's pool, a Polo Farms fenced yard, the Wynbrooke ponds, the Victory Point street signs ("Talladega Drive", "Pit Crew Drive").

**M4. After the entries, the list of places appears four times.**
- Two maps ("Map of the six neighborhoods farther out", "Closer map of the six"), then a plain numbered list of all 12 names with nothing beside them (phone screens 29 to 30), then the table.
- The owner: "you list every neighborhood twice in a cluttered and messy way."
- Do instead:
  - Cut the plain list of names. The map labels and the table already name every place.
  - Keep one map, or one pair if the two scales are needed, and the table.
  - Consider moving one small map into the selection guide, as ROUND-14 allows.

**M5. Filler, and sentences only a verifier would write.**
- 3. Sawgrass East:
  - "For a buyer who would pay those bills anyway, the gap is smaller than it looks." This reasons with the reader about a fee. Say the fee and what it covers once.
  - "Resales here went for less than the new townhomes at Victory Point, an eleven-minute trip." The chart already says this. Cut it.
  - "the only one with long-term viability" is a verifier's phrase.
- 5. Golf and Yacht Club:
  - "From mid-April to mid-October, the pool opens at 8 a.m. for lap swimming, then exercise, then open swim until 8 p.m. or sundown." Pool hours do not sell a home. Cut it, or reduce it to one line about the pool.
  - "Our guide to gated communities covers the gate." This is a pointer in place of a sale.
- 12. Polo Farms: "Our guide to homes with no HOA covers places with no fee at all." This is a pointer in the middle of an entry. Move it to the "Where else" section.

**M6. The same fee sentence recurs.**
- "The HOA fee covers water, sewer, the grounds and the pool" (1), "The HOA fee includes internet, and usually water and sewer" (2), "though it usually includes water and sewer" (3), "It usually includes water, sewer and the insurance on the buildings" (11). repeat-check counts "water" in 7 of 12 entries.
- Do instead: say what fees usually cover once, at the top or in the FAQ. In an entry, mention the fee only where it is unusual (Polo Farms at $20, Wynbrooke the highest).

**M7. "Where else can you look for less?" is a list of names with nothing about them.**
- "Oak Forest and Forestbrook near Socastee, Meadows Edge off SC 544, the Reserve at Long Bay near Longs and McCormick Townhomes."
- The owner: "the list of communities ... nobody knows anything about them."
- Do instead: give each name its one best thing, as the chart does, or cut the names and keep one sentence with the price band.

**M8. The North Myrtle Beach Park and Sports Complex is sold twice.**
- It leads entry 9. Entry 10 repeats it: "The North Myrtle Beach Park and Sports Complex is 11 minutes away."
- Do instead: cut it from entry 10 and use the space for the homes (H4).

## The best three things: keep them

1. **7. Victory Point.** The lead is history a buyer could not guess: "The track opened in 1958 as RAMBI Raceway, and cars ran their last laps there in 2020. Several streets have racing names: Speedway Street, Talladega Drive and Pit Crew Drive." The aerial of the old oval is the clearest picture on the page. The trade-off is honest and specific: "Another owner plans 194 townhomes for rent on about 20 acres of the old speedway land beside it."
2. **1. Parkview and Whispering Pines.** "It was originally part of the Myrtle Beach Air Force Base, and its tee markers feature the squadron emblems. Residents of Horry, Georgetown and Brunswick counties play a round there for $45 plus tax, with a cart." This is local knowledge that sells daily life for a $190,000 buyer.
3. **An offer for every place, and a chart that sells.**
   - "Book a showing in Wynbrooke" and the same for each of the other 11, each linked to the contact form with the place named.
   - The chart puts each place's best thing beside its price ("MarshWalk 5 min, beach 9 min", "one-story house, $20 HOA fee"), which is what the owner asked for.
   - Keep, too: Wynbrooke's MarshWalk lead ("live music every night and no cover charge"), and Polo Farms' honest flood paragraph with "we look up the lot on FEMA's flood map".

## One line each

1. **Does every entry sell like the best agent in town, leading with something a buyer could not guess?** No. 2 Fairways, 3 Sawgrass East, 6 Forestbrook Village and 8 Amber Bluff lead with drive minutes. 4 Birchwood leads with a builder's feature list ("granite counters, stainless steel appliances"), which 8 Amber Bluff repeats.
2. **Any "facts:" label line, or field list in prose?** No label line or field list in prose. Each entry ends in a three-chip field row ("Usually sold for / HOA fee / Fee and tax a year"). See M1.
3. **Does every place end with its own offer?** Yes, all 12: "Book a showing in [place]", linked to `/contact/?place=[place]`.
4. **Does it look modern (liquid glass), with charts that are simple to understand?** Mostly. The glass background, cards and chips look modern. The chart is clean, but its bars are nearly equal and it is 1.7 phone screens tall. At desktop width 40% of the page is empty.
5. **Is any picture unclear?** Yes, five: Birchwood, Forestbrook Village, Amber Bluff and Indigo Grove show woods or pasture, and Villas at Sandridge shows a cleared lot, where the homes are now.

## What the page forgot that a buyer searches for

- What the homes are like inside, at each place: bedrooms, size, one story or two, garage (H4).
- "Cheapest new construction near Myrtle Beach" is answered only in the FAQ. The chart could mark which places are new homes.
- What the HOA fee pays for, in plain words, said once.
