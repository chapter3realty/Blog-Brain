## Medium findings

**M1. Six of the 18 places say nothing about the gate, anywhere on the page.**
- The brief's third item is "how the gate works, whether it is staffed, and how guests get in". The new Gate column was the right fix, but six cells are empty and those six entries are silent too: 2 Seaside, 5 Waterway Palms, 6 The Bluffs, 10 Bellwood Landing, 12 Wachesaw East, 13 The Reserve.
- The Reserve sells for about $1.3 million. A buyer at that price will ask first whether a guard is at the gate.
- Three filled cells do not answer the question either:
  - "Gate phone line" (Heritage) says how to call, not whether anyone staffs it. The entry adds only "the association lists a separate phone line for the main gate."
  - "Car barcode" (Willbrook) and "Guest passes" (DeBordieu) say how owners or guests get in, not who is at the gate.
- Do instead: research the six missing gates (staffed, code, call box, barcode) from the associations' own documents and fill the cells. Write each cell as who or what is at the gate: "Guard, 24 hours", "Gatehouse", "Keypad", "Call box", "Barcode, no guard". Do not add a gate sentence to every entry; 14e and 14f both warned that a slot in every entry becomes the next repetition. The table is the place.

**M2. The two rebuilt entries speak in a verifier's words.**
- 9, Seasons: "The builder listed a ballroom, a card room, an art studio and a billiards room there, with a full-time activities director, when the homes were selling."
  - A reader asks: is the ballroom still there? Is there still a director? The headline promises "a clubhouse built for a social calendar", and the entry gives no event from today's calendar.
  - CRAFT 5: "If a verifier allows a fact only with attribution, find a stronger source, state the part fully supported, or cut it. Never paste the hedge."
- 10, Bellwood: "the middle lot is close to half an acre". No agent says "the middle lot". It is the median, said in a statistician's voice.
- Barefoot keeps the 14f L1 line: "Two Dye Estates homes resold in the past year, at about $1.4 million and $2.5 million."
- Do instead:
  - Seasons: find what the clubhouse holds today and one thing on its current calendar (a club, a class, a yearly event) from the association's own pages or newsletter. State it plainly. If only the builder's list can be verified, cut the list and lead with today's pools, courts and bocce.
  - Bellwood: "Most lots are close to half an acre, and seventeen are an acre or more."
  - Barefoot: "Dye Estates homes have resold for about $1.4 million to $2.5 million."

**M3. Pictures: 13 of 18 entries have none, and four of the five are aerials (one finding; free photos exist for only a few places, and the rest wait on the photo partner).**
- No picture shows a club, a marina, a gate or a house up close.
