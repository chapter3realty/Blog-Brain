## Findings

### High

**1. The section that answers the H1 is the thinnest on the page.**
- Where: "What is a normal day like in The Farm at Carolina Forest?" It is 100 words in three paragraphs. In 14c it was 186. The rules were cut, as asked, but nothing new went in. CRAFT 5: "Never hand back an entry shorter than you found it unless what you cut was filler."
- What it says now:
  - Two of three paragraphs are county places outside The Farm, each told as a drive from a road a buyer does not know: "about 3 minutes by car from the Farmers Rest Drive entrance", "about 4 minutes by car from the same entrance".
  - The third is a list: "owners have two pools, open from mid-April to mid-October, plus a basketball court and a playground. The HOA's events include a fall yard sale and a Community Appreciation Day."
  - "Its parking area is a trailhead on the East Coast Greenway." A buyer from out of state does not know what the East Coast Greenway is.
- What it lost: the short answer promises "the fitness room at 5:30 a.m.", and no other line on the page says where that room is or that the fee pays for it. The overview map labels the "Activity center", and no sentence says what it is. The ledger has both (rows 60, 62, 64: the clubhouse with a fitness room, the activity center owners can reserve for a party, the large pool's deck by a pond). The best neighborhoods entry now uses the pool deck; this page does not.
- What it never had: the strongest daily fact on the page sits in the schools section, untold. "Both Ocean Bay schools are at The Farm's north-east edge, by Wheatfield Drive." The table then gives "About 6 minutes" and "About 4 minutes" from the entrance, so the reader cannot tell whether a child can walk.
- Meanwhile the HOA rules section is 232 words, and the cost section is 160. The page about living here is still mostly about the HOA.
- Instead:
  - Write the day as a day, in about 180 to 250 words: the fitness room and the pools inside The Farm (what the fee gives you), the trails and the rock wall a few minutes out, the preserve on a spring morning, the beach 10 minutes away on a Saturday. Lead with the one an owner could not guess.
  - Say the East Coast Greenway in plain words once: what it is and where it goes from that trailhead.
  - Name places by what the reader knows: "the main entrance by the large pool", "the back entrance by the schools", not "Farmers Rest Drive" and "Wheatfield Drive".
  - Research the kinds of fact that fill a normal week: whether there are sidewalks or a path from the Wheatfield side to the two Ocean Bay schools, and whether children in The Farm walk or ride a bus; whether the ponds have a walking loop or allow fishing; what the Community Appreciation Day is; what the activity center holds. Put the answers in this section, not in rules.

### Medium

**2. No picture shows The Farm at eye level.**
- Where: all three pictures are aerial maps (`nat-0.png` the overview, `nat-1.png` the location, `nat-2.png` the school zone). They are clear maps. But a page about what it is like to live here shows no house, no street, no pool and no preserve.
- The shot list is already with the photo partner, with The Farm first (`photo-request-1.md`: the entrance sign, a typical street, the large pool, the small pool, The Orchards, a pond or the walking path).
- Instead: when the photos come, put the street in the homes section and the large pool in the day section, each under its sentence. This waits on the partner, not the writer.

**3. The hunting line is honest and plain, but it does not say what it means, and it ends on a hint.**
- Where (cons): "The preserve is a state wildlife management area, and hunting is allowed there in season. Deer season runs from mid-September to January 1." Then: "We show you on the map how far any house is from the preserve."
- What is right: it is in the cons, after the good news, in two short sentences, with no alarm words. It does not hide the fact behind the flytraps. I would keep it.
- What is wrong:
  - The first section tells the reader to "park and walk its dirt roads". Eight screens later the reader learns hunters use the same land, and nothing connects the two. The question a buyer asks is: can I still walk there in the fall, and is the land right behind the north-edge homes part of it?
  - "Deer season runs from mid-September to January 1" lumps bows and guns together. The ledger (row 108) splits it: archery from September 15, guns from October 21 to January 1. Small game runs to March 1, so the page's one date range also ends two months early.
  - "We show you on the map how far any house is from the preserve" implies distance matters for safety without saying why. It reads as a warning we will not finish.
- Instead:
  - Say what it means in one plain line, from verified rows: when guns are used, and what a walker does in those weeks.
  - Research the kinds of fact that answer it: which days of the week the preserve is closed to hunting, if any; what SCDNR asks hikers to wear in gun season; whether the 353-acre tract along The Farm is open to hunting now (row 108 says the regulation does not say); any rule on hunting near homes.
  - Make the "we" line concrete: "We tell you which houses back onto the preserve." Row 108 is still "pending verification"; it must be verified before the page ships.

**4. The first section mixes the sale with two trade-offs, and the bombing range is told twice.**
- Where: "What makes The Farm at Carolina Forest different?" After the preserve:
  - "On burn days, International Drive can close between SC 90 and Ocean Bay Elementary because of the smoke."
  - "The Farm's own land has a past: from 1942 to 1948, the Army Air Force used it as a bombing and gunnery range. Its streets now follow a farm theme, from Scarecrow Way to Pumpkin Patch Lane."
- The bombing range then gets its own section at 13.7 screens, which answers the question buyers type. The first mention is the same fact, and it sits next to the street names, so a fun detail follows a bombing range.
- The burns line names two roads and a highway number the reader does not know, and does not say what it means for a home (is there smoke in The Farm?).
- Instead:
  - Keep the bombing range in its own section only. Let the street names stand alone, or with why the land is called The Farm, if a source says.
  - Move the burns to the cons, beside hunting, as one "the preserve next door" trade-off. Say it in places the reader knows ("the road past the schools can close for a few days"). Research how often the burns happen and whether smoke reaches the homes; say that, or cut the line.
  - Say what the preserve means for an owner, once, in the first section: if a verified row supports it, that the woods along the north edge will not be built on.

**5. The private-pool rule is said twice in nearly the same words.**
