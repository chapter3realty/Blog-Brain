## Findings

### High

**H1. Five entries no longer say what the home is.** The owner asked for "the amazing things about the location and property". Round 14d asked the writer to restate the home facts in an agent's voice. Instead they were cut (spec commit 30dd4e5, after the 14d preview was built). What was lost, and what is left:
- 1. Parkview. Cut: two stories, two or three bedrooms, vaulted ceilings, a covered front porch and a screened back porch, the main bedroom downstairs. Left: "around ponds and a pool for owners."
- 3. Sawgrass East. Cut: three bedrooms over two stories, a one-car garage, the main bedroom downstairs, a loft. Left: nothing about the townhome.
- 5. Golf and Yacht Club. Cut: houses built in the 1980s, two or three bedrooms, two baths, about 1,350 square feet. Left: "most of them on eighth-acre lots ... all been one story."
- 11. Wynbrooke. Cut: two stories, a screened porch, most with a loft, some with a garage. Left: "around ponds, and some back onto the water."
- 12. Polo Farms. Cut: three-bedroom houses with an attached two-car garage. Left: "one story."
- Do instead: put each home back, in one sentence an agent would say, from the rows that held it. Use "most" or "some" where a feature is not on every home, and keep the listing count in the ledger. For example: "Most Sawgrass East townhomes have three bedrooms over two floors, a one-car garage and the main bedroom downstairs; some have a loft." Cutting a repeat is half the fix (CRAFT 5); the other half was the home.

**H2. Two entries still do not sell their place.**
- **3. Sawgrass East.** The headline and the chart label sell a pool: "townhomes with a pool and a clubhouse", "pool and clubhouse". The lead is "about 255 townhomes, set around three small lakes that the HOA owns. Owners share a pool and a clubhouse", which is what the owner told us to gloss. The middle paragraph defends the fee and gives the reader homework: "Compare the fee with what those bills would cost you, not with a lower fee somewhere else." The day is a sentence true of any place with a pool: "A Saturday here can be the pool in the morning and the beach after lunch." Round 14d asked for research on what only Sawgrass East has. Nothing new came.
- **5. Myrtle Beach Golf and Yacht Club.** It leads with street names: "some street names are golf courses: Augusta Crescent and Pebble Beach Crescent. Others are birds". A first-time buyer learns nothing they would pay for. The next sentence is hard to follow: "Its own course and yacht club are gone, but both are still near." The day is two drive times (Osprey Marina, 11 minutes; Indigo Creek, 14). The house facts were cut (H1).
- Do instead: research the kind of fact that sells each one and lead with it.
  - Sawgrass East: who built it and when, what owners use the clubhouse for, what is a short walk or ride away, and what the fee saves an owner in plain dollars. Say the fee point once, as what it covers, without telling the reader to compare.
  - Golf and Yacht Club: what the price buys (a 1980s one-story house on its own lot, inside a gated community, for about $225,000; the gate in one clause, as the sibling table allows, without the gated page's watch-desk facts), and what an owner does on a Saturday there. Rewrite the course sentence plainly: what was there, and what an owner uses now.

## The best three things: keep them

1. **7. Victory Point.** "Victory Point is built on part of the old Myrtle Beach Speedway. The track opened in 1958 as RAMBI Raceway, and cars ran their last laps there in 2020." The labelled aerial of the old oval makes its point in a second, and the trade-off is honest and specific: "Another owner plans 194 townhomes for rent on about 20 acres of the old speedway land beside it."
2. **1. Parkview's course and the agent's promise.** "It was originally part of the Myrtle Beach Air Force Base, and its tee markers feature the squadron emblems. Residents of Horry, Georgetown and Brunswick counties play a round there for $45 plus tax, with a cart." Then: "Few of these townhomes come up for sale in a year, so one of our agents watches for each new listing." That is research no buyer would find, and the voice of the best agent in town. Keep it with Wynbrooke's "live music every night and no cover charge" and Indigo Grove's new open-space paragraph.
3. **The chart and the close of every entry.** A frosted card, thin bars on desktop, a clean list on a phone, a best thing beside every price, as he asked. Every entry ends with a sentence a person would say and its own offer: "Townhomes in Wynbrooke usually sold for about $250,000 last year, with an HOA fee of about $450 a month." then "Book a showing in Wynbrooke".

## One line each

1. **Does every entry sell the place like the best agent in town, leading with something a buyer could not guess?** No. 3 Sawgrass East (a pool, a clubhouse and lakes) and 5 Golf and Yacht Club (street names) do not; 2 the Fairways is thin; five entries no longer sell the home itself (H1).
2. **Is there any "facts:" style label line, or field list in prose?** No label lines and no field lists. Three "on the market lately" lines keep a trace of the report voice (L1).
3. **Does every place end with its own offer?** Yes, 12 of 12: a price sentence and "Book a showing in [place]".
4. **Does it look modern (liquid glass), with charts that are simple to understand?** Yes. Frosted cards, pill buttons, a clean chart; four chart labels need to be benefits (M4).
5. **Is any picture unclear?** No. All four make their point; the Conway photo needs its caption to say why it is there (L3), and eight entries wait on the photo partner (M1).
