# Buyer read: The Farm at Carolina Forest

Who I am: I am 39, my job is moving me from Charlotte to the Myrtle Beach area, a coworker told me to look at Carolina Forest, I saw a house for sale in "The Farm" and searched "The Farm at Carolina Forest HOA". I do not know real estate words.

Page read: /submarkets/carolina-forest/the-farm/ (preview), at 390 px wide, top to bottom. About 20,800 px tall, roughly 15 phone screens.

## First screen

Title: "What is it like to live in The Farm at Carolina Forest?" Then four boxes: beach about 10 minutes, groceries about 2, emergency room about 5, HOA fee $107 a month. Then "About 1,600 homes and two pools, near the beach." This is good. I searched for the HOA and the $107 is on the first screen, before I scroll. Nobody had to tell me to look for it.

## Notes as I read

### 1. I don't know this word
- "HOA" is used from the first screen and never explained. I searched for it so I half know it (a fee for the neighborhood), but I could not say what the HOA does or who runs it. The page never says "the homeowners association".
- "The Orchards" and "Planters Point": the page says what they are ("townhomes", "condos"). Fine. But I do not know the difference between a townhome and a condo, and the page does not say.
- "special assessment": "The board can also add a one-time charge, called a special assessment, by a majority vote." The page explains it. Good. But it is buried in the FAQ at the bottom.
- "county's value": "Property tax: About $370 a year for each $100,000 of the county's value". I do not know what "the county's value" is. The next paragraph says "The county's value for a home is not the price you pay for it." That tells me what it is not. It does not tell me what it is, or how to guess it for the house I saw. I would call it "the tax number".
- "high-risk flood zone on FEMA's flood map": I know FEMA. "High-risk flood zone" I follow. "Federal law does not require flood insurance for a mortgage on these homes": I half follow. I do not know what "a mortgage on these homes" being exempt means for me. Does it mean I do not need it, or I should still get it? The page says "If you want a policy" so I guess optional.
- "Range III" / "Corps": "The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers lists its projects..." "Range III" is a name I have no picture of.
- "county roads": "its streets are county roads". I do not know why that matters. Is it good or bad? The FAQ explains it only in terms of a gate.
- "Heritage Preserve": I read it as a park. Probably is. Fine.
- "zoned for": ordinary word, but I do not know what it means here. It means my kid goes to those schools, I guess. I only know that because of the heading "Which schools is The Farm zoned for?"
- "zoning" in "it fits the zoning" (home business). Different meaning from "zoned for schools". Confusing.
- "closed them out in 2017": I would call this "finished the cleanup".
- "stormwater fee": I would call it "a drainage charge".
- "survey of your lot": I would call it "a drawing of your lot".

### 2. Why are you telling me this?
- "Each drive time is from the entrance nearest the large pool. In our experience, rush hour adds 5 to 10 minutes." The first sentence is how the number was made. The second is useful. I would only keep the second.
- "The county's value for a home is not the price you pay for it." Tells me what a number is not.
- "A home you rent out pays about $1,200 a year for each $100,000." I do not plan to rent it out. I skipped it. It is in the middle of a paragraph about my taxes and I had to slow down to see it did not apply to me.
- "The state Department of Natural Resources bought the 353 acres along The Farm's north side for the Lewis Ocean Bay Heritage Preserve." I get that there is woods and a park next door. The acres and the agency name I do not need.
- Photo captions: "The orange line is the edge of the neighborhood." Useful. The photo credit block at the bottom, with "joined three NOAA map tiles", I skipped. I understand it is a legal need.

### 3. This sounds like a report
- "Sources: HOA documents, HOA budget, County deed records, School zone lookup, FEMA flood map, County tax levies, Army Corps of Engineers map data. Not legal or tax advice." Reads like a form. I skipped it.
- "Prices are the middle of recorded sales in the past year." And "Drive times are OpenStreetMap estimates with no traffic." Report voice. I do not know what OpenStreetMap is. This footnote is the only place that tells me how old the prices are.
- "The Corps' map for the lot you like" and "Home sizes and ages... are from recent listings." Report voice.
- "U.S. government aerial photo" as the credit on each image: fine, short.
- "The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers lists its projects on the land where The Farm's homes are as complete." Reads like a record. See below under old bombing range.
- The rest of the page does not read like a report. The pros and cons read like a person.

### 4. I skipped this
- The "Sources" and "Photo credits" blocks.
- "These places are also a short drive away" list: I read Recreation Center, Bike and Run Park, and skipped Tanger, Coastal Grand, International Drive Fields.
- "The HOA holds events such as a fall yard sale and a Community Appreciation Day. Every year, The Farm also joins other Carolina Forest neighborhoods in a food drive." Pleasant, but I skimmed it.
- The Lewis Ocean Bay paragraph.
- The "Fences and sheds", "Pets" and "Home business" cards. I read "Pets" because I have a dog. I would not read the rest unless I was buying.
- The four "Ready to make your next move on the Grand Strand" / investor footer sections. See last section.

### 5. Now I want to know... (after each section)
- After the top and short answer: What does a house cost here? The page tells me later (Costs section, middle). Not on the first screen, and not in the Short Answer. I wanted a price next to "$107 a month". I had to scroll about nine screens.
- After "The homes": What is the difference between a house here and a townhome in terms of what I get? Partly answered (sizes, bedrooms). I wanted to know how old the houses are in a way that matters (do they need work?). The page says "mid-2000s" and "mid-2010s" and stops. Is a house built in 2005 good or bad? Not answered.
- After "Pools and things to do": Can I use the pools in the summer only? Yes, April 15 to October 15. What happens when it is cold? Not said, but I can guess. Is the pool "settled" (the Cons section says the large pool "has settled")? I do not know what that means and the Pools section does not mention a problem. That is a surprise I found much later.
- After "Where it is": How bad is traffic to get to the beach? "In our experience, rush hour adds 5 to 10 minutes." Answered, short. How far from Charlotte, and which road do I take to get here? Not answered. Can I get to Charlotte quickly? Not answered. I would want the coast to Charlotte drive, once.
- After "Schools": Are those schools any good? Not answered. The page only says where they are. I am 39 and I may have kids. I would have wanted a short word on it, or a plain "we do not rate schools; here is where to look".
- After "Costs": What is my monthly total for a $350,000 house? I have the pieces (HOA $107, tax about $370 per $100,000 so about $1,300 a year on a $350,000 house, wait, I am not sure that is how it works, flood $600). I would need a calculator. The page gives the pieces and never adds them up. Also: can I get a loan? Not answered, and I understand that is not the page's job.
- After "Pros and cons": Is the large pool going to cost me extra? "The HOA board has not decided whether to repair or replace the large pool, which has settled." Do I get a bill? The FAQ says the board can add a one-time charge. The two are not tied together. I connected them myself.
- After "HOA rules": What if I want to put up a fence? "Needs the HOA's approval first." How long does that take? Not answered.
- After "Old bombing range": Is it safe? That is the only question that matters. The page says the projects "are complete" and that Range III "touches the edges of some of The Farm's open land, but not any home lot". I think the answer is yes, but I cannot tell whether I should be worried. See below.

### 6. Too many numbers
- "The Orchards has about 200 townhomes, and Planters Point has about 70 condos." Two numbers, OK.
- "Houses in The Farm at Carolina Forest usually sell for about $350,000. Townhomes and condos usually sell for about $230,000 to $240,000." Three numbers. Fine, I would say it that way.
- "In all, owners pay about $225 to $255 a month in The Orchards and about $450 to $500 in Planters Point." Four numbers. I could not hold that in my head. It is a range of two ranges.
- "The HOA spends the biggest part of the fee, about $42 a month for each home, on cable TV from Spectrum." Fine, but I do not need $42.
- "A home you rent out pays about $1,200 a year for each $100,000. The Farm is outside the city limits, so there is no city tax. A county stormwater fee of $7.45 a month is on the tax bill too." $7.45 is a number I would never say out loud. 
- "The state Department of Natural Resources bought the 353 acres" 353 is too exact.
- "Open April 15 to October 15, from 9 a.m. to 9 p.m." Four numbers plus months. Fine in a card.
- "Fitness room... open from 5:30 a.m. to 9 p.m." Fine.
- "You get a courtesy notice first. The HOA then charges a $25 fine, then a $50 fine, and then $50 a week until the problem is fixed." Fine.
- "To add a gate, about three out of four owners would have to agree to ask a court first." OK.
- "Property tax: About $370 a year for each $100,000 of the county's value" is a hard one: three things (370, 100,000, and a hidden value) for one number.

### 7. Where is this?
- The aerial photo with the orange line and labels (Small pool, Large pool, Activity center, Orchards, Planters Point, Entrance) is good. I can see where things are inside the neighborhood.
- The big map with the beach, airport, hospital, "Myrtle Beach" and highway numbers: I can tell it is up the coast from the beach, with a long way to the airport. Good. But I could not tell where it is in relation to Myrtle Beach downtown, and "Carolina Forest" on the map sits on a road labeled 31. I do not know the roads. I am not sure which side of the city I am on. I get "about 10 minutes from the beach" so I do not mind.
- The second map has the Publix and the entrance. Good.
- The street photo ("A street of houses in The Farm, from above") shows rooftops. It tells me the houses are close together. That is the only picture of what a house looks like, and it is from above. I never see a house from the street. I wanted one picture of a typical house. I understand that may not be allowed.
- I wanted a map that shows Charlotte direction or the way in. Not there.
- No photo of the pool from the ground either, only from above. The pool photo is from above and has dark brown trees, in winter. It does not look inviting, even though the page says "two pools".

### 8. That's not an answer
- "An agent can get the latest HOA board minutes for you before you make an offer." Appears in the pros and cons next to the pool problem. I wanted to know what the board is leaning toward, and the answer is "ask an agent".
- "An agent gets the total fee for you before you make an offer." Same, for townhomes and condos. But the page already gave me $225 to $255 and $450 to $500, so I do not need the agent for that.
- "An agent at Chapter3 can get the current rules and read them with you before you make an offer." The page already gave me the rules in cards. This sounds like I should not trust the cards. Mixed message.
- "An agent at Chapter3 can show you the Corps' map for the lot you like." This is the answer to "is the land safe": see a map that I am told about and not shown. That is an "ask someone" answer.
- "Before you make an offer, type the home's address into the district's school zone lookup." The page tells me to go look it up and does not say where. There is a link to "how school zones work" but the lookup itself is not linked in the text I read. I would have to leave.
- "The district can change its zones." OK, but then what do I do? The answer is the lookup.
- Questions the page asks and answers: all of the headings. Good. The FAQ answers are short and direct.

### 10. Where is the good part?
Yes. The first screen tells me what I get (homes, pools, near the beach), a drive time to the beach, groceries and the ER, and the HOA fee. Before any rule or drawback. This is the strongest part. It does not tell me the price of a house, but it tells me the thing I searched for.

## The old bombing range section
I did not expect this. I came for HOA fees and I find a heading that says "Was The Farm at Carolina Forest part of an old bombing range?" Honest answer: Yes. I did not know what to feel. The page tells me:
- yes it was a bombing and gunnery range from 1942 to 1948,
- the Army Corps says the work on The Farm's land is done (2017),
- there is still work in another part of Carolina Forest (Range III) and it "touches the edges of some of The Farm's open land, but not any home lot".

What I wanted next: is it safe for my family, and do I need to do anything? The page does not say "you do not need to do anything" or "this is safe". It says what the Corps did. It ends with "An agent can show you the Corps' map". I read this as "it is probably fine but we will not say so". That is the most nervous I got on the page, and it is buried near the bottom. I was glad it was there. I would also not want it on the first screen.

"Is the land safe" is the question this page raises and then does not answer in plain words.

## Questions answered, in order
**What did you want to know first?** The HOA fee for The Farm, and what it covers. Found: first screen ($107 a month in the box), and again in the Short Answer ("includes cable TV and trash pickup"). Second: what a house costs. Found: middle (Costs section, about nine screens down). Never on the first screen.

**Three things I liked about this place, from the page:**
1. A Publix about 2 minutes away and the beach about 10.
2. The $107 fee includes cable TV, trash pickup and two pools.
3. The Recreation Center and Bike and Run Park are 3 and 4 minutes away, and the aerial photos show a lot of ponds and open space.

**Questions I still have, ranked:**
1. What does a home cost me per month all in (price, HOA, tax, insurance)? The page gives pieces and never adds them.
2. Is the old bombing range a risk for my home? "Complete" and "an agent can show you a map" are not an answer.
3. Is the large pool going to cost owners money? "Settled" is not a word I know in this context, and the extra-charge answer is somewhere else (FAQ).
4. Are the schools good? Page lists only names and drive times.
5. What does a house here look like from the street, and is a 2005 house in good shape?
6. How long does it take to get from here to Charlotte?
7. What is the "county's value" for the house I saw, and how do I look it up?
8. What does the HOA actually do? Who do I call, and how do I pay?

**Would you call this company?** Maybe: the page was straight with me about the fee and the drawbacks and told me what to ask before I buy, but it gave me an agent for every hard answer, and the footer says they are "investor-focused", which is not what I am.

## I came for ___ and the page never told me.
I came for **what it would actually cost me each month to own a house here, all in**, and the page never told me. It gave me the HOA fee, a tax rate per $100,000 of a number I cannot find, a flood price and a house price, and never put them together.

Second one I would write: I came to know **if it is safe to live on an old bombing range** and the page never plainly said.

## Header, footer and site-wide boxes
They seemed to be for someone else.
- The black box at the bottom, "Ready to make your next move on the Grand Strand? Whether you are buying, selling, or investing...", is about the whole region and says nothing about The Farm. I skipped it.
- The footer says "The Myrtle Beach investor-focused brokerage. DSCR analysis, STR analysis, and condo-by-condo ROI data for serious real estate investors." I do not know any of those words. Footer link groups are "Invest", "Strategies" (DSCR Loans, BRRRR Method, Fix & Flip, 1031 Exchange, Multifamily Investing) and "Calculators" (Rental Deal Analyzer, Capital Gains). This made me wonder whether I was on the right site for buying a house to live in. It made me trust the Farm page a little less.
- The "Call if you have any questions: 854.333.2135" bar at the top is fine. The 854 area code is not a Myrtle Beach number (843 is). I noticed because I know Carolina numbers are 843 and 704. I did not know if it was a real local office.
- The footer has "RESPA Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure... BrickWood Mortgage (NMLS #189497)". I do not know these words and I skipped it.
- The last-section "TALK TO AN AGENT" button in the dark box has no visible button shape, just text. It looked like a label.
- The two page CTAs ("TALK TO AN AGENT" under Short Answer and "GET THE LIST" with "Tell us your price range") are good and I would have used GET THE LIST. The page asks me for a price range, and I would not know it, because the page told me the price late.

- The page says "Every home is zoned for Ocean Bay Elementary, Ocean Bay Middle and Carolina Forest High." I do not know which are far. The schools table is a good answer.
