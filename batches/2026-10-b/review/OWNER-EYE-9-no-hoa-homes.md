## Medium

**Medium 1. Loris says the Bog-Off twice, in paragraphs out of order.** The whole entry body:
- "Loris is a small city with City Hall downtown on Walnut Street. On the third Saturday in October, downtown fills for the Loris Bog-Off, a festival centered on chicken bog: chicken, rice and sausage cooked together."
- "Loris has its own hospital, McLeod Loris, in town. Its lots fall between Aynor's acre and Conway's half acre."
- "Loris grew up around a railroad depot, on a line a lumber company laid to carry timber, and became a town in 1902. Its town clock is on Main Street at Meeting Street, and the Bog-Off brings vendors, a car show and two stages of live music."

The Bog-Off is told in the first and third paragraphs, with the hospital and lots between. City Hall's street is a fact nobody needs. Want, in this order: the history ("Loris grew up around a railroad depot ... became a town in 1902. Its town clock is on Main Street at Meeting Street."), then the Bog-Off once, in full ("On the third Saturday in October, the Loris Bog-Off fills downtown with vendors, a car show, two stages of live music and chicken bog: chicken, rice and sausage cooked together."), then the hospital and the lots. Cut the Walnut Street sentence.

## Low

1. "City Hall was the county courthouse, finished in 1825 and reputedly designed by Robert Mills." (Conway). "reputedly" is a hedge (owner: "eradicate the hedges"). State the part that is sure: "City Hall was the county courthouse, finished in 1825."
2. "The town limited building density, and through the 1980s single-family houses remained the norm. Most houses for sale here are that close" (Surfside). "that close" points to the heading, two sentences back, past a sentence on density. "Most houses for sale here are within half a mile of the beach".
3. "Outside Carolina Forest, the houses for sale are mostly older: almost half date from before 1990." (Myrtle Beach). "mostly" and "almost half" disagree. "almost half of the houses for sale outside Carolina Forest date from before 1990."
4. FAQ: "a boat over 16 feet may not sit in the front yard". "may not be kept in the front yard", as the story says.
5. Deed restrictions and the closing attorney are told twice: cons, "A home with no HOA can still have deed restrictions. The closing attorney's search finds them before you buy.", and the boat section, "The closing attorney's title search finds any deed restriction on a boat or a truck before you buy." Keep the boat one; the con can be the first line alone.
6. "Every home for sale in Carolina Forest has an HOA fee." appears under the map, again in "gain and give up" ("In Carolina Forest, every home for sale has an HOA fee"), and as a table row. Keep the table row and the one under the map.
7. "On FEMA's flood map, none of the 400 homes we sampled inside the town is in a high-risk flood zone." (Aynor). "we sampled" is method in the body. "No home inside the town of Aynor is in a high-risk flood zone" if the row allows it; otherwise "almost no home".
8. "The numbers on the map match the list below." and "Two fees of the same size can cover different things." Talk about the page, and a sentence true of any HOA. Cut both.
9. North Myrtle Beach and Surfside entries retell history the best-neighborhoods page also tells (the 1968 merger, Cherry Grove's canals and the 415-property fee, The Ark and Floral Beach). See the batch file.
