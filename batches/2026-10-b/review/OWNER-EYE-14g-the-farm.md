## Findings

### High

None.

### Medium

**1. The top tells the preserve purchase twice, then hedges, and never says what it means for an owner.**
- Short answer: "Conway Medical Center planned a $160 million healthcare facility on the woods along The Farm's north edge. The state bought those woods for a nature preserve instead."
- Half a screen later: "The 353 acres along The Farm's north edge are set to join the Lewis Ocean Bay Heritage Preserve."
- Then the hedge: "Under state law, a heritage preserve may not be used for any private purpose. That protection starts once the new land is dedicated to the preserve, which the state has said it will do."
- The 14f version said what it meant ("no one may build houses there"). The verifier struck that as wider than the statute (farm-verify-v10, D1), and nothing plain took its place. A buyer now reads a land deal, then a law, and has to work out for himself that the woods behind the north edge will stay woods. That is the payoff of the page's best fact.
- The short answer also does not answer its own question. The H1 asks "What is it like to live in The Farm?" and the answer is a hospital that was not built, plus "A gym, a library and trails are 3 to 4 minutes away by car", which the day says again in full.
- Instead:
  - Short answer: open with one sentence that says what living there is, from rows already on the page (houses with yards, two pools and cable in a $107 fee, the Ocean Bay schools at the edge, the flytrap woods along the north edge). Then the hospital story as the surprise. Cut the gym line; the day has it.
  - "What makes The Farm different": do not tell the purchase again. Start on the flytraps and what the preserve gives an owner.
  - The law: ask the verifier for the strongest plain sentence row 114 supports about what can and cannot be built on a heritage preserve, and say that, in the owner's terms. Cut "That protection starts once ... which the state has said it will do"; "set to join" already carries the timing, and the sources line can carry the rest.

**2. The day is a list of drive times, with the measuring point in the sentences.**
- The day has seven drive times in nine paragraphs: the preserve "about 6 minutes by car from The Farm's entrance by the schools", the hospital "about 12 minutes", rush hour "5 to 10 minutes", Publix "a 2-minute drive", the library "about 3 minutes by car from the entrance nearest the large pool", the trails "about 4 minutes by car", the beach "about 10 minutes by car from the entrance nearest the large pool, and about 12 from the middle of The Farm."
- Said again elsewhere: the beach (10 minutes) and Publix (2 minutes) are in the tiles, in the day and on the location map, three times each. The emergency room is "about a 5-minute drive away" in the sentence directly above the map that labels it "Emergency room, about 5 min".
- Two start points have names in the copy ("the entrance by the schools", "the entrance nearest the large pool"), and the school table says "Drive from the entrance" without saying which.
- CRAFT 5: "A verifier gives the fact; the writer gives the sentence ... The method (how it was measured ...) goes in the ledger and the sources line, not the entry." And: "A map, a chip and a sentence that all say '10 minutes' are three statements of one fact." The sources line already says "Drives start at the Farmers Rest Drive entrance, without traffic", and the map intro says it once. That is enough.
- Instead:
  - Cut "from the entrance nearest the large pool" from the day (both places) and "from The Farm's entrance by the schools". The start point is stated once, at the map and in the sources line.
  - Keep the drive times the tiles and the map do not already give (the preserve, the library and the trails). Give the beach and Publix without a number in the day ("River Oaks Pizzeria is next to the Publix"), or take them off the tiles. One home per number.
  - The emergency room: the map label or the sentence, not both.
  - The school table: name the entrance in the column heading, or drop the column and say the walk (finding 3).
