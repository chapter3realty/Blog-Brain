### Medium

**M1. The guide leaves out new homes and space, and its golf group does not hold together.** The brief: "choosing by what they love: the beach, golf, boating, a town center they can walk, new homes, space and value." Golf holds 6 of 15, two with taglines that are not golf ("Barefoot Resort: a swing bridge", "Grande Dunes: a South Florida look"). "Golf at the door" holds Pine Lakes ("only 13 lots border it") and Prince Creek ("about 19" of 640).
Do: from facts already on the page, add "New houses" (Waterbridge, Grande Dunes, Deerfield's South Course) and "Room to spread out" (Wild Wing's third of an acre, Plantation Lakes' half acre, Deerfield). Rename the golf group "Golf close by", or move Pine Lakes and Prince Creek. Give each tagline the group's reason.

**M2. Four entries spend a paragraph on history where the day should be.**
- Cherry Grove: "In 1950 an inlet was filled to join Futch Beach to the Nixon family's Cherry Grove land ... to form the city of North Myrtle Beach."
- Surfside: "George J. Holliday later renamed it Floral Beach for his wife, Flora. New owners gave it the name Surfside Beach, after a town in Florida."
- Deerfield: "A special referee found no such restriction on the land, and the state Court of Appeals affirmed in 2012." A court reporter, not an agent.
- Forestbrook: "once known as Kingsland. Lennar began building it in 2018, and every house the county approved is now built."

Keep history that gives the buyer something today (Sports Illustrated, Warbird Park, the swing bridge).
Do: cut each to a clause at most. Put in what an owner does on a normal day (where they eat, fish or walk), from a verified row. Deerfield: one plain sentence on what the old golf land means now.

**M3. Three entries have no normal day.**
- Tidewater leads with "a neck of land between the Intracoastal Waterway and Cherry Grove Inlet, with salt marsh along its edges" and never says what that gives an owner. Then street names and the decade built.
- Grande Dunes: "Most houses here have a porch for the evening after." Filler.
- The Farm: open land, square feet, a drive, then a link away.

Do: research what the marsh and inlet give a Tidewater owner (a view, a launch, a walk). Give Grande Dunes a day from a fact gated does not own. Give The Farm one line from its guide's rows. Cut the porch sentence.

**M4. A new slot: stories and the porch.** Stories in 8 entries (Pine Lakes, Tidewater, Plantation Lakes, Waterbridge, Market Common, Avalon, Deerfield, The Farm). A porch in 5: "a porch and a deck", "a porch and a fireplace", "a porch for the evening after", "about half have a porch", "Most of the houses have a porch." The cure for 14e's count slot grew the next one.
Do: keep the house line where it sells (Cherry Grove's raised beach houses, the Mizner look, Pine Lakes' primary bedroom down, Deerfield's one-story brick, Avalon's fenced yards). Vary or cut the rest. Add the FAQ a buyer types, "Which neighborhoods have one-story homes?", answered from rows on the page.

**M5. Forestbrook's golf-cart line comes from nowhere.** "Our agents check the HOA rules on golf carts before you make an offer." Nothing says why carts matter here. Its lead, a garden plot by the pool, is the weakest on the page.
Do: state the rule from a verified row, or cut the line. Find a lead a buyer would choose this place for.

**M6. One sibling repeat.** Prince Creek: "a freshwater lake where alligators are easy to spot." Affordable: "Huntington Beach State Park, with three miles of beach and a lake where you can see alligators, is 10 minutes away."
Do: give the park to one page in SIBLING-OWNERS. Give the other its own day.
