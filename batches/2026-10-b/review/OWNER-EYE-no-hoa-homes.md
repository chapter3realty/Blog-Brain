# Owner's eye: /buyers/no-hoa-homes/

Reviewer: a fresh agent that did not research, write or edit this page. Read on 2026-10-13 from `scratchpad/preview/b/no-hoa-homes.html`. Screenshots: `scratchpad/oe-b/no-hoa-homes-390/`, `-1280/`, `raw/`.

**Grade: 7.** It does not go to the owner yet. With gated communities, this is one of the two weakest pages in the set.

What works:
- The short answer gives the share of homes with no fee (about a quarter), the price and where to look.
- The "where" table now adds lot size, which is new and useful.
- The "why do they cost more" section answers a real question.
- The trash and town-limits material, and the four-step "how to check" list, are good.

What holds it back: round 2 rebuilt the page around one cold reader, a boat owner. The first section is now five phone screens of town parking codes, before the reader learns where the homes are. Most people who type "Myrtle Beach homes no HOA" want where the homes are and what they cost first. Boats are one reason among several.

## Cold reader's gap: answered?

The reader came for "where I can park my boat and work truck and nobody can tell me no."
- **Answered fully, and more than fully.** That is the problem: the answer now leads the page.

## What I would send back, most important first

1. **Put "where" first and shorten the boat section.**
   - The first H2 is "Can you keep a boat or a work truck at a home with no HOA?" at 698 words and about 3,700 px on a phone, the longest section on the page. It comes before "Where are the homes with no HOA fee".
   - The owner: "lead with what a buyer would want to know" and "a huge portion of the content is technical knowledge that the customer wouldn't really care to learn about."
   - Wanted:
     - Order: where and lot sizes, why some cost more, pros and cons, then the boat and truck section, then what you take care of.
     - Keep the boat and truck table.
     - Cut the four paragraphs under the table that repeat it: "Myrtle Beach counts a boat over 16 feet as a recreational vehicle...", "Conway wants a boat in a side yard...", "Work trucks have limits in the towns too...", "On county roads outside the towns...".
     - Keep the one line on the day to load and unload, and the deed-restriction paragraph.
   - The subtitle "Where to look, what it costs and where the boat can go" should drop the boat. The page is for every buyer who wants no HOA.

2. **A sentence out of place.** "Conway's street ban covers only work vehicles over 10,000 pounds and 20 feet long." It sits alone after the closing-attorney paragraph and has nothing to do with it. Cut it, or put it in the Conway row of the table.

3. **The story is in the middle of the page.**
   - The Dale example sits right after the parking rules, about a third of the way down. The owner: "the story should be one of the last things in the page."
   - Move it near the end, before the FAQ.
   - It also shares the batch's one arc (the agent reads a rule before the offer, and the buyer makes the offer). See OWNER-EYE-batch.md.

4. **Flood is missing.**
   - The page sends no-HOA buyers to North Myrtle Beach and Surfside Beach, more than half no-fee, and shows a Cherry Grove photo.
   - The best-neighborhoods page says almost every Cherry Grove home is in a high-risk flood zone, with flood insurance of about $1,300 a year.
   - A reader who saves "about $1,100 a year" in HOA fees (this page) may pay more than that in flood insurance near the water.
   - PLAN.md lists flood under "what you pay for yourself instead".
   - Wanted, in the "why some cost more" or pros and cons section: "Many homes with no HOA fee near the beach are in a high-risk flood zone. With a mortgage, those homes need flood insurance. Check the flood zone before you offer." Use the same Cherry Grove figure as best-neighborhoods if you give one.

5. **Houses do not ask.**
   - "A house with no HOA fee usually asks about $515,000"; "Houses with no HOA fee ask more in every area"; "asks about $985,000".
   - Wanted: "is usually listed for about $515,000."

6. **"Show" is a listing-site word.** "show no homeowners association (HOA) fee", "Most show no fee" (hero tiles), "show no HOA fee" (several times). Wanted: "have no HOA fee" or "are listed with no HOA fee", as best-neighborhoods now says.

7. **One picture of the subject, and it is borrowed.**
   - The page has 2 figures: a map and the Cherry Grove aerial, which also appears on best-neighborhoods.
   - Nothing shows the page's own claim, "Loris and Aynor have the most room, usually half an acre or more."
   - Wanted: a NOAA or NAIP aerial of an ordinary street outside Aynor or Loris with no HOA, with wide lots and a driveway or side yard labelled, captioned with the fact ("Lots outside Aynor are often an acre or more").
   - Give the Cherry Grove photo's caption a no-HOA fact, or drop it.

8. **Method sentences the cold reader skipped are still here.**
   - "The lot sizes for Murrells Inlet, and for Surfside Beach and Garden City, rest on fewer houses, because many there are in Georgetown County."
   - "Some homes in campground communities near the beach show no HOA fee. The owner leases the land under the home and pays a yearly lease. We left them out of every count on this page."
   - Cut both. If the campground homes matter, put one line in the FAQ: "A home on leased land in a campground community has no HOA fee, but you pay a yearly lease for the land."

9. **An idiom.** "A good habit: check it twice a year and after big storms, clear out debris and keep the plants in check." "Keep the plants in check" is a phrase, and the owner: "No phrases of any kind at all ever." Wanted: "cut back the plants."

10. **Fractions nobody says aloud.** "seven tenths of an acre", "three tenths of an acre", "a sixth to a fifth of an acre." Wanted: "about three quarters of an acre", "about a third of an acre", "a small lot, about a sixth of an acre".

11. **Jargon in the FAQ.**
    - "A vehicle used in the owner's business is exempt from that street ban where no sign bars parking." Wanted: "A work truck may park on the street where no sign says otherwise."
    - "voluntary association or community dues" (how to check). Wanted: "a club you can choose to join and pay."

12. **The date in the sources line.** "Homes for sale in the local MLS, on October 13, 2026." CRAFT 5 allows one date, in the byline. If the build stamps this date by machine, keep it. If it was typed, remove it ("Never type a date").
