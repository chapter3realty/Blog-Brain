## Changes I would ask for, most important first

1. **Medium. The story has no problem the reader can feel.**
   - "Example: Rosa owns a work truck and a small boat, and she wants a home with no HOA. Her Chapter3 agent searches the homes for sale in her price range with no HOA fee listed. In one listing, the home pays community dues. The agent takes that home off her list. Rosa tours the rest on her first weekend in town."
   - Retell: Rosa wants no HOA, one listing had dues, the agent took it off, she tours the rest. The truck and boat never come back. "Community dues" is a word to look up (STORY-SIMPLE test 7), and the page itself says some dues are for "a club you can choose to join and pay", so the reader cannot tell why that home was dropped. Nothing is at stake, so it shows no skill.
   - Wanted: the boat or truck meets a real limit from this page, the agent catches it, and Rosa ends up where she can keep it. For example: "Rosa wants a home with no HOA where she can park her work truck and her boat. She likes a house with a Myrtle Beach address. Her Chapter3 agent finds it on the county map: the house is inside the city, where a long boat cannot sit in the front yard. The agent shows her houses just outside the city, where the county lets her keep the boat anywhere on her lot. Rosa makes an offer on one with a wide side yard." Run it through `tools/story-check.js`, and say "a long boat" only if the row allows it without the number.

2. **Medium. "Why do homes with no HOA fee cost more in some areas?" has twelve numbers and a reason that does not fit.**
   - In 272 words: $515,000, $415,000, 1 in 3, about half, 2000, 1 in 10, 4 in 10, $985,000, $525,000, $1,100, $1,300 and 1 in 4. The owner: "it is a very hard read because numbers are exact and there's too many numbers."
   - "Many of them are older houses" is given as a reason they cost more. A reader expects older to cost less. The real reason on the page is that many are near the ocean.
   - Wanted: "Houses for sale with no HOA fee are listed at about $515,000, against about $415,000 with a fee. Many of them are near the ocean." (The page already supports this: about 1 in 3 is on the ocean side of Business 17.) Cut "About half were built before 2000, against about 1 in 10 houses with a fee" and the Surfside paragraph of comparisons, or keep one Surfside sentence with one number. Keep the flood paragraphs as they are.
   - Also the heading says "in some areas", and the first sentence says "in every area that has them". Wanted heading: "Why do homes with no HOA fee cost more?"

3. **Low.** The boat table mixes units and trade words: "One commercial; none over one ton", "None over 18,500 pounds", "Over 10,000 lb: not on the street", "One van or pickup, 21 ft, 8 ft high", "One, up to 2 tons". Use one unit style ("pounds", "feet") and say "one work vehicle" for "one commercial".
4. **Low.** The Cherry Grove photo is the same NOAA view as on best-neighborhoods, without its labels. It sits beside the flood paragraph. Add the same three labels, and give the caption the flood fact the paragraph needs: "Cherry Grove from the air. Almost every home here is in a high-risk flood zone."
5. **Low.** "A good habit: check it twice a year..." The colon lead-in is a phrase. Wanted: "Check it twice a year and after big storms. Clear out debris and cut back the plants."
6. **Low.** "Homes for sale in the local MLS, on October 13, 2026" in the sources line. If the build stamps this date, keep it. If it was typed, remove the date ("Never type a date").
7. **Low.** "Is a campground home with no HOA fee a no-HOA home? No." The answer then says it has no HOA fee. Wanted: "It has no HOA fee, but you pay a yearly lease for the land under it."
8. **Low.** "Our guide to the most affordable places to buy near Myrtle Beach compares the towns." It sits at the end of a flood paragraph, where it does not belong. Move it to the "where" section.
