# Owner's eye: /buyers/best-neighborhoods/

Reviewer: a fresh agent that did not research, write or edit this page. Read on 2026-10-13 from `scratchpad/preview/b/best-neighborhoods.html`. Screenshots at 390 and 1280 px after a slow scroll: `scratchpad/oe-b/best-neighborhoods-390/`, `best-neighborhoods-1280/`, and the full-size photos in `oe-b/raw/`.

**Grade: 7.** It does not go to the owner yet.

What works: the first screen gives three wants with a place and a price each. The "fits what you want" boxes are the best part. The yearly cost table answers the cold reader's main gap. The Pine Lakes and Surfside aerials are good, labelled pictures of the place itself. The 55+ table matches the approved live Myrtle Trace page exactly.

What holds it back: a hedge on a cost, legal wording repeated three times, an overview map with eight unnamed dots and no "Myrtle Beach" label, a story that copies an approved one, and length. It runs 4,600 words over 41 phone screens. The approved Myrtle Trace page has 1,771 words.

## Cold reader's gap: answered?

The reader came for "what a real month of owning costs, and what it is like to live there all year."
- **Cost: answered** for three neighborhoods, in a table with a total row ($2,600 to $5,600 a year).
- **All year: only just answered.** "What is it like to live here all year?" has 118 words, and three of its four sentences start "In our experience".

## What I would send back, most important first

1. **The hedge on a cost.** "Many Cherry Grove homes also pay a flat fee of about $1,700 a year, which appears to be the city's canal-dredging charge. Only some homes pay it, so the yearly totals above do not count it."
   - "Appears to be" is a hedge, and the owner said "eradicate the hedges."
   - A reader who plans by this number needs to know who pays it.
   - Verify it from the city (which homes pay, and what it is for) and state it: "Homes on the Cherry Grove canals pay the city about $1,700 a year for dredging the canals." If it cannot be verified, cut both sentences.

2. **Legal wording, three times.** "A federally regulated or federally backed lender must require flood insurance on a mortgage for a home in one." This appears in the map section and in the yearly cost section, and "On a mortgage from a federally regulated or federally backed lender..." appears in the checklist.
   - It is industry language. The owner's own model: "the house requires flood insurance because it sits in a flood zone."
   - Say it once, at first use: "If you take out a mortgage, your lender will require flood insurance on a home in a high-risk flood zone. A cash buyer does not have to buy it."
   - After that, say "needs flood insurance."

3. **The overview map ("Where are the best neighborhoods in Myrtle Beach?").**
   - Dots 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 15 have no name on the map, so on a phone the reader scrolls to the list and back. The cold reader flagged this, and it is unchanged.
   - The "Grande Dunes" label sits between dot 6 and dot 10, closer to 10, which is Pine Lakes.
   - The city of Myrtle Beach is not labelled. The map labels Conway but not the city in the page's title.
   - Wanted: a name beside every dot (short names are fine: Barefoot, Waterbridge, The Farm, Plantation Lakes, Berkshire, Pine Lakes, Forestbrook, Prince Creek), a "MYRTLE BEACH" place label, and the Grande Dunes label placed against dot 6 only.

4. **The story copies an approved one.** "Example: Lisa wants to walk to the beach every morning... Her Chapter3 agent checks the flood map... The agent gets her an insurance quote that week. Lisa knows the full yearly cost..."
   - This is the live Seasons story (Carol and Jim: check the flood map, get a quote, offer knowing the cost) with the names changed.
   - All five stories in this batch also share one arc: the agent checks something before the offer, and the buyer makes the offer (see OWNER-EYE-batch.md). CRAFT 6 says stories in a set must not share one shape.
   - The story also adds nothing the cost table above has not already said.
   - Wanted: a story with a different shape and a real choice between two neighborhoods. For example, a buyer who wants the beach and a lower price is shown the Deerfield Plantation area, where the closest beach access has parking only for golf carts. A real story from `stories/stories.json` comes first if one fits.

5. **"What is it like to live here all year?" reads as three notes in a row.**
   - "In our experience, nothing closes in the off-season..." "In our experience, rush hour adds..." "In our experience, Highway 31 and Highway 22 avoid the stop lights on US 17."
   - Use "In our experience" once.
   - Replace the road numbers with what the reader knows: "the back roads we use avoid the stop lights on the main beach road."
   - Add one plain line on the off-season, such as quieter roads and open restaurants, only if a verified row supports it.

6. **Grande Dunes does not match the gated page.**
   - This page: "$1.2 million to $2.9 million", fee "About $200 to $400".
   - Gated page: "About $1.5 million", fee "About $200 to $375 a month".
   - Both say "three sections". This page does not name its sections; the gated page names Calais, Palermo and Villa Venezia.
   - Use one set of sections and one price and fee on both pages, or name the sections here so the reader sees they are different places.

7. **Tidewater's gate is stated more strongly here than on the gated page.**
   - This page: "Tidewater Plantation has a guard house at its gate, where visitors check in" (also in the FAQ).
   - Gated page: "Gatehouse: A building at the gate. Ask us about hours."
   - "Guard house" promises a guard. Use the gated page's verified wording: "a gatehouse at its entrance."

8. **Length.** At 41 phone screens this is the longest page in the batch by ten screens.
   - Cut the FAQ to the five questions people search. "Best areas to live", "North Myrtle Beach" and "Surfside Beach" each repeat a section word for word.
   - Cut sentences that only report method: "The prices in the table are for two parts of Pine Lakes"; "Builders sold new homes in these sections for about $530,000 to $1.1 million, less than homes sold by their owners."
   - Cut the trade-off lines that do not fit the place. For example, "the nearest beach access has no parking" for Plantation Lakes, which is inland: that buyer drives to a beach lot anyway.

9. **"The trade-off is the price. Only about 17 homes sold here in the last 12 months, so prices vary."** (Pine Lakes.)
   - The sentence says "price" and then talks about how many homes sold.
   - Wanted: "The trade-off is the price: Pine Lakes costs more than most places on this page, and few homes come up for sale."

10. **Market Common photo.** A second orange outline at the right edge of the photo has no label, so the reader cannot tell what it is. Label it, or crop it out.

11. **Cherry Grove photo.** It has no labels. One "Beach" label and one "Canals" label would let a phone reader find the two things the caption names.

12. **Small cells.**
    - In the cost table, Cherry Grove's HOA cell "None on more than half listed" does not read as English. Wanted: "None on most homes".
    - The hero tile "Golf: Pine Lakes, Barefoot Resort" leaves out Tidewater, which the short answer names. Name the same three in both.

## The story, read once

Retell: Lisa wants to walk to the beach, the house she likes needs flood insurance, so the agent gets her a quote before she offers. It is easy to retell. It passes the simple story test, but it lacks tension and repeats an approved story (item 4).

## What did it forget that buyers type in?

- "Myrtle Beach neighborhoods map." A map is here, but there is no Google Maps link. The approved Myrtle Trace page has one ("Open Myrtle Trace in Google Maps"), and CRAFT 4 asks for it.
- "Is Myrtle Beach a good place to live?" No FAQ answers it plainly.

## Fair housing

No sentence describes a place by who lives there or calls a place safe or unsafe. Calling Seasons and Del Webb 55+ communities is a statement of the age rule, which is allowed.
