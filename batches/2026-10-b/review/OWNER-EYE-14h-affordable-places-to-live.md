### Medium

**M1. Photos.** Eight of twelve entries have no picture, and no picture on the page shows a home. The page sells houses, and the reader never sees one. This waits on the photo partner. `batches/2026-10-b/photo-request-2-affordable.md` still says "Not sent yet"; the owner can send it, or connect Gmail. One finding, as instructed.

**M2. Two entries still do not sell their place.**
- **10. Indigo Grove.** The entry now says where it is, which fixes 14g's point. But it leads with directions and school names: "Indigo Grove is off SC 90, about 12 minutes by car from North Myrtle Beach and 13 from the beach. Its homes are zoned for Riverside Elementary and North Myrtle Beach Middle and High." Then the selling paragraph is about ponds: "It holds three new ponds, one with a small island of trees". The headline is "new houses around 22 acres of ponds and woods", and the chart label is "new, ponds and woods". He said: "if its next to a pond ... gloss over it". Then a sentence that is true of every Lennar house on the page: "Lennar's limited warranty covers the workmanship for a year, the electrical, plumbing and mechanical systems for two, and the structure for ten." Do instead:
  - Lead with the benefit already in the entry, said for the buyer: most lots back onto open land, not another house's yard. Put that in the headline and the chart label, not the ponds.
  - Give the school zones and the drive in one short line after that.
  - Cut the warranty sentence, or replace it with something only Indigo Grove has. Kinds of fact to research: the nearest beach access and what is there, a park or landing nearby, what an owner does on a weekend off SC 90.
- **4. Birchwood.** The water tower is gone, as asked, but the place sentence is still empty: "Long Bay Club, a golf course, is in Longs too, and North Myrtle Beach and its beaches are about 20 minutes by car." "A golf course" tells the reader nothing about it, and "too" points to nothing. The rest is the builder's list ("granite counters, stainless steel appliances, 9-foot ceilings"), which is true of most new houses. Do instead: say what Long Bay Club is known for, or replace it with what living off SC 905 near Longs gives an owner. Kinds of fact to research: the course's designer or what it is known for, the river or a landing nearby, the town's own events or places. Keep the quarter acre; it is the real sell.

**M3. A new repeated trade-off: "Myrtle Beach is far", three times.**
- Birchwood: "The long drive is to Myrtle Beach: Coastal Grand Mall is about a forty-minute drive, by the Carolina Bays Parkway."
- Indigo Grove: "Myrtle Beach itself is farther off: Coastal Grand Mall is about 35 minutes by car."
- Villas at Sandridge: "You give up time: Myrtle Beach is about a half-hour drive."

Same trade-off, same shape (a short label, a colon, a drive time), two of them to the same mall. This is the pattern CRAFT warns about: the cure for one repeat grows the next. Round 14f cut drive minutes; round 14g cut the occasion-place-minutes shape; this round added the colon trade-off. Do instead: keep it once, in Birchwood, where the drive is longest. Sandridge already has a second trade-off ("The monthly dues are also higher than at the other new communities here"); let that one stand alone. Give Indigo Grove a different honest trade-off from the research (the kind: a lot detail, building still under way, or what the fee does not cover), or none if the research finds none.

**M4. The dek is still there, and now it says nothing.** "Affordable places near Myrtle Beach worth a closer look." It restates the H1 and adds "worth a closer look", which is the kind of line he calls AI filler. Round 14g's dek at least named what the money buys ("a garage, a pool or a new house"). The top is still 2.26 screens. Do instead: cut the dek. That puts entry 1 near 2.1 screens. If the template will not build without a dek, give it the one fact the reader most wants first, from the page's own numbers, such as "Homes for about half the Myrtle Beach price, within about 20 minutes of a beach."
