## Findings

### High

**1. Daily life starts at 4.9 screens.**
- Where: the order. H1, the subtitle, four tiles, the dek, the short answer, an offer and the contents list come first. Then "What are the homes like" runs about two screens on lot counts, square feet, lot size, house age, condo ownership and the developer's board seats. Only after that does "What is there to do" begin.
- Instead:
  - Make the day the first section after the offer box.
  - Start the short answer with a day here: the fitness room at 5:30 a.m., the pool until 9 p.m., the trails and the rock wall a few minutes away.
  - Put the homes after the day, shortened to what a buyer pictures (house, townhome or condo; one or two stories; a fenced yard).

**2. The best thing a buyer could not guess is buried at 7.0 screens.**
- Where: "Where is The Farm" says: "The preserve is home to South Carolina's largest wild population of Venus flytraps. The state manages it with controlled burns." Before that comes "Conway Medical Center had once meant the 353 acres along its north edge for a $160 million healthcare facility."
- This is the page's strongest sale. The state bought the woods behind the north streets for a preserve, and they hold the state's largest wild population of Venus flytraps. The sibling best-neighborhoods entry leads with it. This page puts it after the schools-map lead-in and the drive-time explainer.
- The subtitle mentions "land the state bought for a nature preserve", but no picture shows the preserve. The location map labels Publix, the ER and the beach, not the preserve.
- Instead:
  - Lead the page, and the day section, with the preserve: what it is, who owns it now, and that the state plans to open it to the public.
  - Label the preserve on the location map.
  - Research the kind of fact that makes it daily life: where the nearest public entrance or trail will be, and what a visitor can do there.

**3. The opening says the same things three times, and the themes repeat after that.**
- Where:
  - The subtitle: "Two pools, the beach 10 minutes away, and land the state bought for a nature preserve on its north edge."
  - The tiles: "Beach About 10 minutes", "Pools Two, plus a fitness room", "HOA fee $107 a month".
  - The short answer: "two pools, a fitness room ... The beach is about 10 minutes ... The state bought the land along its north edge".
- Across the page: "state bought ... north edge" 4 times, "cable TV" 5, "two pools" 3. "Finished / every lot has a home" 3 times (the dek, the homes section, the pros). The pool settling and the "one-time charge" 3 times (things to do, cons, FAQ).
- Instead:
  - Say each fact once in the opening: the tiles carry the numbers, and the short answer says what they mean for a day here.
  - Cut "finished" from either the dek or the homes section, and "Every lot is built" from the pros.

**4. Reporter sentences and field lists in prose, about one topic: the HOA.**
- Where:
  - "D.R. Horton has not controlled the HOA board since 2019, when it gave up its right to appoint a majority of the board." This sits in the homes section, right after the street names.
  - "In Planters Point, each owner owns a condo unit and shares the buildings and land with the other owners."
  - "The HOA budgets about $6,500 a year for social events."
  - "The largest share of the fee covers cable TV from Spectrum. The rest covers landscaping and the ponds, trash pickup, the management company, electricity, savings for repairs, the pools and insurance."
  - "After you buy, the county values the home at about what it would sell for on the open market. In an ordinary sale, that is close to the price you paid. The new value first shows on the next year's tax bill."
- This is the owner's "hung up on one thing": the reader came to picture living here and gets the association's paperwork.
- Instead:
  - Say the fee once, in a sentence a person would say: "$107 a month, and it pays for your cable TV, your trash pickup and both pools."
  - Cut the budget line list, the board-control sentence and the tax-assessment mechanics. They are FAQ or ledger material. If a buyer asks who runs the HOA, one FAQ line covers it.
  - Turn "$6,500 a year for social events" into what happens: the events themselves.

### Medium

**5. Verified daily-life items are left off this page.**
- Where: ledger row 60 (verified) lists a basketball court, a playground and a clubhouse with the fitness room. Row 62 gives the court's hours (dawn to dusk). The best-neighborhoods page names the basketball court and the playground for The Farm; this page does not.
- Instead: add them to the day. Also research the kinds of fact that make a normal week: whether there are sidewalks or a walking loop around the ponds, and what the yearly events are, without dates.

**6. Every picture is an aerial, and one works against the sale.**
- Where:
  - Picture 2, captioned "Houses on one street in The Farm, close together, many with fenced back yards." It is a winter shot of brown yards. "Close together" is a drawback said in the caption, and the point of the picture is not clear in a second.
  - Picture 3, the pool aerial: a dark pond fills two thirds of the frame, and the pool is a small shape in the top right corner.
- Instead:
  - Send the photo partner a shot list: the large pool deck in season, a typical street at eye level, the activity center, the Bike and Run Park trailhead and the preserve's edge.
  - Until then, cut picture 2, and crop picture 3 to the pool and the deck.
  - Keep the labelled overview aerial and the location map.

**7. Bad news sits inside the selling section.**
- Where: "What is there to do" ends with two paragraphs on the sunk pool: "The large pool has sunk into the ground more than it should ... By a majority vote, it can add a one-time charge". The same point is in the cons ("Keep money aside for a possible one-time charge") and the FAQ. The round 8 reviewer flagged this same theme as told four times.
- Instead: state it once, in the cons, with what we do about it ("We read the board's latest minutes on the pool before you offer"). Cut it from the day section. Keep the FAQ line about fee raises without restating the pool.

**8. Three exits send the reader away from the place being sold.**
- Where:
  - "If you want a gate, see which neighborhoods near Myrtle Beach are gated." This is directly above "Book a showing in The Farm".
  - After the cost offer: "If you would rather not pay an HOA fee, see ... For lower prices, see ...".
  - After the pros and cons: "To compare The Farm with other places ... For neighborhoods built for buyers 55 and older, see our guides to 55+ communities." The Farm is not 55+.
- Instead: one short "Other places to compare" line near the end, before the FAQ. Cut the 55+ link from this page.

**9. The reader is still given chores, and some sentences say the obvious.**
- Where:
  - "Keep money aside for a possible one-time charge."
  - "Plan for an internet bill on top of the fee."
  - "Count both fees when you compare homes."
  - "If you ever find something that looks like old ammunition ... Do not touch it, move away and call 911."
  - "Its wide deck is beside a pond."
- Instead:
  - Say what we do: "We show you both fees side by side for every townhome and condo."
  - Name the internet providers that serve The Farm (a kind of fact to research) instead of "plan for a bill".
  - Cut the pond clause.
  - In the bombing-range section, keep the plain answer: the cleanup under every home is finished, and Range III touches no home lot. Cut the 911 how-to, which reads as alarm on a page meant to sell. Or keep it as one short line at the very end of that section.

**10. A date in the body copy, which goes stale this month.**

- Instead: "Every fall, The Farm joins other Carolina Forest neighborhoods in a food drive, now in its 16th year." No dates in the body (CRAFT 5).

**11. The offers do not all carry the place, and the box repeats word for word.**
- Where:
  - All three "Send me the homes for sale" buttons link to plain `/contact/`. Only "Book a showing in The Farm" links to `/contact/?place=The%20Farm`.
  - The box text "See homes for sale in The Farm. Tell us your price range. We will send you the homes for sale in The Farm and show you the ones you like." appears three times. The middle copy is a lighter, different card.
- Instead:
  - Add `?place=The%20Farm` to every offer link.
  - Keep the dark box at the top and at the end.
  - Make the middle offer the place pill, with a different line, for example "Book a showing in The Orchards or Planters Point" after the townhome and condo prices.

**13.** At 390 px the four tiles wrap to three lines each ("Two, plus a fitness room"), so the row looks cramped. Shorten the tile text to "Two pools" and "$107 a month".

**14.** The HOA rules are five label-and-text cards (Renting, Fences and sheds, Parking and boats, Pets, Home business). The owner praised icon cards with words (2026-10-11), so keep them, but cut "Home business", which no one asked for.

**15.** The example (Rachel and Luis and the no-pool rule) is labelled and clear, and I could retell it in one sentence. The tension is mild. Keep it; it is in the right place, near the end.

**16.** What a buyer googles and the page does not answer:
- the drive to Myrtle Beach International Airport (on the must-answer list for a place);
- the range of recent house prices, low to high, not only the typical one;
- which internet providers serve the neighborhood.

These are kinds of fact to research, not claims.
