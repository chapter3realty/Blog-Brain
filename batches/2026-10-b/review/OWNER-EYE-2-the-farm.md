## Changes I would ask for, most important first

1. **Low.** "For a house of about $350,000 that you live in, plan on about $2,600 a year... That covers the HOA fee and the county property tax." Then: "The county also adds a stormwater fee of about $90 a year to the tax bill." A careful reader still gets $2,700. Fold it into the tax: "Property tax: about $1,400 a year, with the county's stormwater fee." Then the total is about $2,700 here and on best-neighborhoods, or leave the $90 out of the copy and keep $2,600 on both. Change both pages together.
2. **Low.** "The Defense Department or a later owner may keep monitoring that land." A reader asks what that means for them. Cut it. The next paragraph and the 911 line say what matters.
3. **Low.** The sinking pool and the one-time charge are told four times: the things-to-do section, the cons, the story and the FAQ. Cut the second paragraph of the things-to-do section ("The board has not said what the work will cost. By a majority vote...") and let the cons and the story carry it.
4. **Low.** "The large pool has sunk into the ground more than it should, which engineers call settling." No reader searches "settling". Wanted: "The ground under the large pool has sunk, and the pool has sunk with it."
5. **Low.** "To add a gate, about three out of four owners would have to agree to ask a court first." It answers a question nobody asks after "No". Cut it. The FAQ answer ends at "public roads owned by Horry County."
6. **Low.** "FEMA's flood map" is the only abbreviation not spelled out once (CRAFT 5). Wanted: "the federal flood map (FEMA's)".
7. **Low.** "Most of the houses have about 1,450 to 3,200 square feet." Wanted: "about 1,500 to 3,200 square feet."
8. **Low.** "Before they make an offer, their Chapter3 agent reads the HOA's documents with them" uses the opening of three live stories ("Before she makes an offer, her Chapter3 agent reads..."). The arc is new, so this is polish. Wanted: "Their Chapter3 agent gets the HOA's latest board minutes for them."
9. **Low.** The street and pool aerials are winter frames, brown and bare. Correct, but not inviting. A summer NAIP frame of the same view would look better. The overview aerial is already a summer frame.
