# Batch 2026-10-b, round 8: the owner's second read

The owner's words are in voice/VERDICTS.md (2026-10-14, second read). Read CRAFT 5, "Say something new, every time" and "We do the work", before you change anything. The owner asked us to fix the root cause, not one sentence: "understand the root cause and root issue ... learn this skill not a hard code".

## The root cause, in one paragraph

Our research gathered the same few fields for every place: price, fee, minutes, the gate, the share with no HOA. A writer with only those fields says them again in every entry, in the same sentence shape, and fills the gaps with safe filler. The reader gets "the same question and damn near the same answer" and "low quality surface level" copy. The fix is deeper, different research for every place, then copy that says each thing once.

## For every page

1. **Research what makes each place different.** For each place, find its special features and its history: who built it and when, what the land was, golf course designers, the river or marsh it sits on, landmarks, festivals or traditions tied to the place, what an owner does on a normal day. Use primary sources: county records, the HOA's or club's own pages, the town's history pages, and news archives. Add rows marked `pending verification (round 8)`.
2. **Say each thing once.** The page's theme (the gate, no HOA, the price) appears once at the top and once in each entry's facts line. It does not appear in every paragraph. Read your page for sentence shapes repeated from entry to entry ("Most houses for sale here with no HOA fee are..."), and vary them or cut them.
3. **Cut sentences that say nothing:**
   - sentences that would be true of any place;
   - obvious or reassuring sentences ("Your own doors, locks and alarm are still yours to choose");
   - hedges.
4. **We do the work.** Never tell the reader to check, ask, verify or look something up themselves. Say what we check: "We check the flood map, the deed and the HOA papers for any home you like." Cut sections that teach the reader to do the agent's job.
5. **Go past what a buyer could find in a minute.** Name streets, subdivisions and landmarks. Give what the county records show. A town name alone is not an answer to "where".
6. **SEO and AEO stay first.** Keep each page's main question as the H1 and answer it in the first lines. Each numbered entry is a heading a search engine can quote, with the place's name in it. Each FAQ answer stands alone. If the kit can add ItemList schema for the numbered entries, add it.
7. **Length:** deeper is not longer. Cut repetition first, then add the new facts.
