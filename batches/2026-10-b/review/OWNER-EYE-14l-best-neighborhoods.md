### Medium

**M1. Grande Dunes and The Farm still sell no day.**
- Grande Dunes: a building style, a course award from 2009, then "Most houses for sale here are new or not yet finished." Nothing on what an owner does on a Tuesday.
- The Farm: "A buyer can choose a one-story or a two-story house here." True of any place. Then "Our guide to The Farm at Carolina Forest has its pools, its schools and its HOA rules." The reader is sent away for the selling.

Do: for Grande Dunes, research what owners use day to day (the club, the waterway, a walk, a place to eat) that the gated page does not already own. For The Farm, bring one line of daily life from its own guide's rows and cut the one-story sentence.

**M2. Tidewater sells places that are not Tidewater.** The short answer makes it the golf pick, and the guide says "houses on the fairways". The entry leads with "a neck of land between the Intracoastal Waterway and Cherry Grove Inlet, with salt marsh along its edges", then gives a paddling trail that "runs 30 miles, from Socastee to Little River" and a park "about 17 minutes from Tidewater by car". Both are true of any house on that stretch of waterway.
Do: lead with the golf, since that is why it is the pick. Then research what the marsh and the inlet give an owner from their own street (a view, a launch, a walk). Cut the trail and the park.

**M3. A new slot: "The trade-off is", in 6 of 15 entries.**

- "The trade-off of a public course" (Tidewater)
- "The trade-off is the condo fee" (Barefoot)
- "The trade-off is a townhome's HOA fee" (Market Common)
- "The trade-off is the yard" (Forestbrook)
- "The trade-off is age" (Avalon)

`repeat-check` does not see it. Two of them are a fee that the price line and the table already give (14k L6, still open).
Do: keep a downside only where it changes a choice (Cherry Grove's flood insurance, Tidewater's golfers, Deerfield's building site), and say it the way an agent would, with no label. Cut the two fee trade-offs.
