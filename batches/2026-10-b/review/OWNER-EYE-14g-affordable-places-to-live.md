## Grade: 8 of 10

This is the best version of the page so far. Three parts of his sentence are now met:
- **The homes.** Every entry says what the home is: size, bedrooms, garage, porch, one story or two.
- **The graph.** It lists a benefit beside each price, in a frosted card.

## Findings

### High

None.

### Medium

**M1. Photos.** Eight of twelve entries have no picture, and no picture on the page shows a home. The page sells houses, and the reader never sees one. This waits on the photo partner (`batches/2026-10-b/photo-request-2-affordable.md`); send the shot list if it has not gone. One finding, as instructed.

**M2. Four entries do not sell their location.** He asked for "all the amazing things about the location and property".
- **10. Indigo Grove** never says where it is. The entry covers the 22 acres, the Lennar plans, the flood zone, the warranty and trash pickup. A reader finishes it without knowing the town or anything near it. Only the FAQ says "off SC 90", and only the table says 13 minutes to a beach. Do instead: say where it is in the first paragraph. Then give the best thing nearby, from the kind of fact that sells the others: what an owner does near SC 90 and North Myrtle Beach on a weekend, the closest beach access, the schools it is zoned for.
- **12. Polo Farms** gives one benefit (fenced yards, some with pools). The rest is what it lacks and what went wrong:
  - "The neighborhood has no shared pool or clubhouse; its common land is two ponds, some woods and the strips along the highway."
  - "floodwater filled the streets and gutted homes across the neighborhood."

  Keep the Florence paragraph. It is honest, and "we look up the lot on FEMA's flood map" is the right voice. Cut the common-land sentence; a buyer does not need to be told about highway strips. In its place, research what an owner likes about living west of Longs: what is close, what the land is like, why people choose it over a townhome at the same price.
- **6. Forestbrook Village.** The only location sentence is "For a night out, Broadway at the Beach is 12 minutes away." Anyone in Myrtle Beach could guess it. Do instead: one fact about the Forestbrook area that a buyer would not know, such as what is on Forestbrook Road, the schools it feeds, or the river or park nearby.
- **4. Birchwood.** "The turn off SC 905, southwest of Longs, is beside the tall water tower" gives directions and sells nothing. Cut it. Say what living near Longs gives an owner, beside Long Bay Club.

**M3. The cure for drive minutes grew a new sentence shape.** Round 14f's M2 is mostly fixed: groceries are now in one entry (Fairways' Aldi), and several minutes now come with what you do there. But six entries now use the same sentence: an occasion, then a place, then the minutes.
- Parkview: "On a weekday evening, the shops, restaurants and movie theater at The Market Common are a ten-minute trip."
- Sawgrass East: "For a workout or a library book, the county's Carolina Forest Recreation Center and library share a campus, a six-minute drive from home."
- Birchwood: "After work, Long Bay Club, a golf course in Longs, is a seven-minute trip."
- Golf and Yacht Club: "For a day on the water, Osprey Marina on the Intracoastal Waterway is within about 15 minutes by car."
- Forestbrook Village: "For a night out, Broadway at the Beach is 12 minutes away."
- Amber Bluff: "On game days, Brooks Stadium, where the Chanticleers play football, is about a six-minute drive."

Nine of twelve entries still carry at least one "N-minute" sentence. Do instead: keep this shape in the two entries where it is strongest (Amber Bluff's game days, Sandridge's wake park). Elsewhere, give the fact about the place itself, the way Wynbrooke does: "a half-mile boardwalk along the marsh, lined with restaurants, with live music every night and no cover charge." The minutes can live in the table.

**M4. 5. Golf and Yacht Club still headlines its street names.** The lead is now right: "about 470 houses behind a gate, most of them one story". The house facts are back. But the headline sells "one-story houses on streets named for golf courses". The chart label is "one-story, golf streets", which does not explain itself. The day is still two drive times (Osprey Marina, Indigo Creek). The page's own facts give a stronger headline: a one-story house behind a gate, with a pool, a hot tub, tennis courts and a community center, for about $225,000 and a $95 fee. Do instead: headline and label what the price buys. Move the street names to one clause or cut them. Replace one drive time with what owners use the community center and courts for.

**M5. The top is 2.26 screens, and the dek is still there.** This is the third round to ask. "Affordable neighborhoods near Myrtle Beach where a budget still buys a garage, a pool or a new house." The subtitle above it already says "Twelve neighborhoods, and the best thing about each." On a phone, the hero alone takes about one screen. Do instead: cut the dek. That puts entry 1 near 2.1 screens.

## The best three things: keep them

1. **7. Victory Point.** "Victory Point is built on part of the old Myrtle Beach Speedway. The track opened in 1958 as RAMBI Raceway, and cars ran their last laps there in 2020." The labelled aerial of the old oval makes its point in a second. The trade-off is honest and specific: "Another owner plans 194 townhomes for rent on about 20 acres of the old speedway land beside it." And "Lennar lists Victory Point as sold out for now, so we watch for resales" is an agent at work.
2. **1. Parkview, home and place together.** It has everything he asked for in one entry:
   - the home: "about 1,050 to 1,350 square feet ... vaulted ceilings, a covered front porch and a screened back porch";
   - a fact no buyer would find: "It was originally part of the Myrtle Beach Air Force Base, and its tee markers feature the squadron emblems";
   - what the fee buys: "water, sewer, grounds care and the pool";
   - the agent's promise: "one of our agents watches for each new listing".

   Keep it with Wynbrooke's "live music every night and no cover charge" and Amber Bluff's "control the front door lock, the thermostat and the video doorbell from your phone".
3. **The chart and the close of every entry.** The chart is a frosted card with thin bars on desktop and a clean list on a phone. A benefit sits beside every price, as he asked. Every entry ends with a sentence a person would say, then its own offer: "Townhomes in Wynbrooke usually sold for about $250,000 last year, with an HOA fee of about $450 a month." Then "Book a showing in Wynbrooke".

4. **Does it look modern (liquid glass), with charts that are simple to understand?** Yes. It has a warm translucent background, frosted cards, pill buttons with an arrow chip, and a chart that is easy to read. Three chart labels need work (L2).
