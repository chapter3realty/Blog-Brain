## Grade: 7 of 10

This is the first version of this page that does what the owner asked in his own words. It has 16 areas, up from 8. Each area opens with how many homes have no fee, has one price line, and ends with its own offer. The map circles every area. The boat and truck rules by town are useful, and no other page has them.

It does not reach 9, for five reasons:
- the reader scrolls 4.1 phone screens before the first area, the longest of the five pages;
- the entries are still built from the same fields (the fee share, city or county rules, flood, lot size, price), and the owner asked for "extensively on all of the benefits of that location and why people like it";
- two of the four things this reader wants (the right to rent, a shed or a workshop) are not on the page;
- 9 of the 16 areas have no picture;
- the map shows numbers, not names.

## High findings

**High 1. 4.1 phone screens before the first area.**
Where: from the H1 to "1. Downtown Myrtle Beach". The map card alone is 1.8 screens on a phone: "The whole area", then "A. Myrtle Beach, Socastee and Garden City", then "B. North Myrtle Beach and Little River", stacked.
Do instead:
- Cut the dek, "16 areas of older beach streets, river towns and country land, where you can keep a boat or a work truck at home." The H1's second line already says "16 areas", and the short answer says it again ("most are in the 16 areas below"). That is three times in one screen.
- On a phone, show one map. The two coast close-ups can sit side by side at 1280 px, but on a phone use one map at most one screen tall, or put the close-ups after the guide.
- Keep the short answer to two of its three paragraphs. The boat paragraph is said again in the guide and the boat section.
- Target: entry 1 starts within about two screens of the H1.

**High 2. The entries still follow one set of fields, not the place.**
Most entries are built from the same five fields, in sentences: the fee share, then city or county rules, then flood, then lot size, then price.
- The county line: "It is outside the city limits, west of Kings Highway, so the county's rules apply" (4); "Little River is not a town, so the county's rules apply" (9); "Socastee is not a town, so the county's boat and truck rules apply" (10); "Garden City is not a town, so the county's rules apply" (12).
- The city line: "Myrtle Heights is inside the city, so the city's limits on boats and work trucks apply" (2).
- Flood appears in 8 of 16 entries.
- Trash pickup appears in 3 entries and the FAQ.
- repeat-check counts "boat" 28 times and "county" 27 times.

This is the owner's "dont get hung up on the same 5 topics." The rule facts are good, and the page already has a section and a table for them. In the entries, they take the room the owner asked for: "speak extensively on all of the benefits of that location and why people like it."

The entries with almost no daily life:
- **4. Plantation Point.** Every sentence is a field: no fee, county, lot size, street names, era, boat rule, flood, beach, price. Nothing says what a day there is like, or why people choose it.
- **1. Downtown Myrtle Beach.** The Boardwalk is something every buyer already knows. "Nearby, a row of new houses was listed, also with no HOA dues" is a listing note. Nothing says what living a few blocks from the ocean with no fee gives you.
- **5. Windy Hill.** "It was one of four beach towns that joined in 1968 to form the city" is true of Cherry Grove, Crescent Beach and Ocean Drive too. After that come Barefoot Landing, flood and price.
- **12. Garden City Beach.** The heading promises "between the ocean and the marsh", but the body never says what the marsh side gives you. It has a landing, the MarshWalk drive, flood and price.
- **15. The Country Around Loris.** It has a lot size, the hospital, trash and price. Nothing says what the country gives you.

Do instead: move every "city or county rules apply" sentence out of the entries. The boat section and its table already carry the rules. Where a rule is the selling point, keep it, as in Surfside's "two boats or trailers at a home". Spend the room on what an owner does there. Kinds of fact to research, per place:
- the nearest launch and what water it reaches;
- where owners eat or fish;
- the park or the street event;
- what the older houses are like inside (porches, carports, garages, workshops);
- what the land allows that a subdivision would not.

Cherry Grove, Little River, Ocean Drive and Loris already do this. Use them as the model.

**High 3. Two of the four things this reader wants are missing.**
The round 14 brief lists: "a boat, an RV or a work truck at home; a shed or a workshop; the right to rent; land and older character, with no fee." The page covers the boat, the truck, the land and the older houses. It says nothing about:
- renting the house out, long term or short term, with no HOA to forbid it;
- building a shed, a workshop or a detached garage, or putting up a fence.

These are what a no-HOA buyer types into Google next.
Do instead: research the town and county zoning on short-term rentals in single-family zones, and on accessory buildings and fences (setbacks, size limits). Then add one short section after the boat section, or fold the facts into the entries where they differ (inside a city versus the county). Each fact needs a verified row. If a town forbids short-term rentals in some zones, say so plainly; the reader needs it.

**High 4. Nine of 16 areas have no picture.**
Where: entries 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 10, 12 and 15 are text only. At 1280 px they are a narrow column of text with nothing beside it. Best neighborhoods has 14 pictures for 15 places, and affordable has 12 for 12.
Several of these places are asking for a photo:
- Myrtle Heights' 1920s to 1940s Craftsman houses;
- Ocean Drive's Main Street and the Horseshoe;
- the Ingram Dunes trail in Crescent Beach;
- the Socastee Swing Bridge;
- the county landing at Garden City;
- a country lot near Loris with a boat or a shop beside the house.

Do instead: send the photo partner a shot list for these nine places (`templates/photo-request-email.md`), with what each photo must show. Until his photos arrive, use a labelled aerial with one lot outlined, as the Aynor entry does, where it shows the entry's point.

## Medium findings

**Medium 1. The map has numbers, not names.**
The owner said "circle them". The circles are there, but a reader must match each number to the guide below. The number badges are about 10 px on a phone.
Do instead: put each area's name in a glass pill beside its circle (`placesMap` with `glass: true`). Use short names: "Cherry Grove", "Conway". Keep the numbers inside the pills.

**Medium 2. Every entry opens with the same sentence, with the verbs rotated.**
"list no HOA dues", "was listed with no HOA fee", "pays no HOA dues", "list no HOA fee", "is listed with no HOA dues", "show no HOA fee on their listings", "carry no HOA dues", "come without an HOA fee", "is free of HOA dues", "carry no HOA fee", "has no HOA fee", "shows no HOA fee in its listing", "list no HOA dues", "show no HOA fee". The owner asked for the share, so it must stay. Sixteen variations of one sentence read as a machine avoiding a repeat checker.
Do instead: give each entry a glass chip under its heading: "No HOA: almost all", "No HOA: most", "No HOA: about 2 in 3". That says it the way the owner asked, once, in the same place every time. The first sentence is then free to lead with the best thing a buyer could not guess (CRAFT 5).

**Medium 3. The same sentence twice in Cherry Grove.**
The caption says "Almost every home here is in a high-risk flood zone." Four paragraphs later, the text says "Almost every home here is in a high-risk flood zone." The caption also puts the bad news on the picture that sells the canals.
Do instead: the caption says what the photo shows ("Houses with their own docks on the Cherry Grove canals"). The flood line stays once, in the trade-off.

**Medium 4. The Pine Lakes aerial does not show its point.**
The heading says "half-acre lots by the city's first golf course". The photo shows US 17 Business, a traffic circle, wooded lots and a row of condo buildings on the beach. There is no golf course in the frame and no lot outlined. The condos are the biggest thing in the photo, and they are not what this page sells.
Do instead: crop to the older streets and outline one half-acre lot from county parcels, as the Aynor photo does. Put the golf course in the frame and label it if it can be placed from real coordinates. Or ask the photo partner for a street view of one of these houses.

**Medium 5. The Little River aerial is mostly treetops and a condo marina.**
The biggest buildings are the red-roof condos and their marina at the lower left, which are almost certainly a named community with a fee. The "old fishing village" houses are hidden under trees. The "Salt marsh" label sits on the edge of the trees, not on the marsh. The large paved lot at the top right may be the county landing the entry names; if it is, it is the one thing worth labelling.
Do instead: crop away the condo marina. Label the landing if its coordinates confirm it. Or use a ground photo of the Mineola Avenue waterfront from the photo partner.

**Medium 6. The Surfside picture is out of date, and the entry leaves out the pier.**
The aerial shows the pier with cranes on it, and the caption says "from the air in 2023". CRAFT 5 says no dates in captions, and a construction photo dates the page. The best neighborhoods page sells the same place on its pier: free to walk, 814 feet, two restaurants. This entry never mentions the pier at all.
Do instead: replace the photo with a current one (the photo partner), or drop the year from the caption. Give the pier one sentence, without repeating the best neighborhoods wording.

**Medium 7. Sentences that read like a verifier's notes or a listing snapshot.**
- "From the middle of the ocean-side streets, the nearest public beach access is about a minute by car." Nobody drives a minute to the beach. Say it as a walk, if the row supports a walk.
- "Two of the houses for sale are from that era, and a new oceanfront house was listed beside them." (2)
- "Two of the houses for sale are on the ocean side of Kings Highway." (3)
- "Nearby, a row of new houses was listed, also with no HOA dues." (1)
- "Half of these houses are listed for less than about $420,000, and the new ones on Mineola Avenue at $450,000." (9)

These describe one day's listings. They will be wrong next month, and they sound like a report.
Do instead: state what the place has ("Some of the original 1930s houses still stand beside new oceanfront ones"), and keep the price to one plain sentence: "A house here usually lists for about $420,000."

**Medium 8. Obvious or filler sentences.**
- "McLeod Loris, the town's hospital, is in town." It says the same thing twice and adds nothing.
- "Myrtle Heights is inside the city, so the city's limits on boats and work trucks apply." This is a rule, not a reason to live there.
- "Inside the city, a big business truck may not park on a city street." (Conway) The table already says it.

Do instead: cut them, or replace each with a fact only that place has.

**Medium 9. The same places carry different numbers on two linked pages.**
- Cherry Grove: here "about $850,000 at its asking price"; on best neighborhoods, "usually sell for about $695,000".
- Surfside, ocean side: here "about $940,000"; on best neighborhoods, "about $705,000".

One page uses asking prices and the other recorded sales. A reader who clicks between them sees two answers for one place, which is the owner's "same information" problem turned around.
Do instead: use the same measure the sibling pages use (recorded sales), or say "asking price" in every price line on this page. Use the same rounding.

**Medium 10. The FAQ leaves the reader to find out alone.**
"Does a listing with no HOA fee mean the home has no HOA? Not always. A blank fee means no fee was listed. A few homes listed with no fee are in a neighborhood with a club you can choose to join and pay."
The answer stops where the reader's worry starts.
Do instead: end with what we do: "We check the deed and the county records for an HOA before you make an offer." The opening offer box already promises this.

**Medium 11. Areas the owner asked for and the brief named, but missing.**
The brief: "the country around Conway, Loris, Aynor and Longs." Longs and the country around Conway are not on the page. Conway is in town only.
Do instead: research the share of homes with no HOA fee for Longs and for the country around Conway, from the same MLS and county data. Add each one that holds up as an entry, with its own offer. Do the same for any other older Grand Strand area the data supports, such as the older streets of Murrells Inlet.

## Low findings

- **Low 1.** "Unlike Cherry Grove, few houses here would need flood insurance" (entry 6) points to entry 8, which the reader has not reached. Say it without the comparison.
- **Low 2.** "15. The Country Around Loris" is in title case. The other headings are in sentence case: "the country around Loris".
- **Low 3.** "Crescent Beach: almost no HOA, and a maritime forest". The heading spends half its words on the fee, which the opening sentence says again. Lead with the forest and the 30-foot dunes.
- **Low 4.** "Galivants Ferry Landing, a county ramp on the Pee Dee River". The county page says Pee Dee. The river at US 501 at Galivants Ferry is widely called the Little Pee Dee. Have a verifier check a second source; a local reader will notice.
- **Low 5.** The price section's cards read as a field list on a phone: "Houses for sale: About 60 / What and where: Older houses in Conway and Socastee". The rule cards are the same: "A boat, camper or trailer: Over 16 feet: not in the front yard", with two colons in one line. Give the cards plain phrases: "About 60 houses, mostly older houses in Conway and Socastee"; "Boats over 16 feet: not in the front yard".
- **Low 6.** The 16-foot city rule is said three times: in the table, in the Teresa example ("a boat that long") and in the FAQ. The county's "no rule on where" line is said about seven times. Say each rule once in the section, and let the FAQ answer in one sentence that points there.
- **Low 7.** In the preview, the site header renders as an empty cream band, 133 px tall, on a phone. This is likely the preview, not the page. Check it on the live build.

## The best three things: keep them

1. **The short answer's insight:** "Age is the best sign. About 3 in 4 houses for sale built before 1990 have no HOA fee. Of those built since, fewer than 1 in 5 do." A buyer could not have guessed it, and it changes how they search.
2. **The boat and work truck rules by town, with the county's rule beside them.** This is what this reader actually came for, and no sibling page has it. The Teresa example makes it concrete in three sentences. Keep the table and the example; just say each rule once (low 6).
3. **The entries that sell like an agent who knows the place.**
   - Cherry Grove: the city dredges the canals so owners "can take a boat to House Creek and the Intracoastal Waterway at high and low tide".
   - Little River: "its boatmen began taking visitors fishing offshore, the first boats in South Carolina to do it for a fee"; "boats can cross the bar at any time of day".
   - Ocean Drive: the shag and the free Thursday concerts.
   - Loris: the Bog-Off and what chicken bog is.
   - The Aynor aerial with one acre lot outlined is the clearest picture on the page.

   Also keep the 16 named offers at the end of each area.

4. **Does it look modern (liquid glass), with charts that are simple to understand?** Mostly. The glass field, map card, rule cards with icon chips and pill offers are modern. There is no chart. Nine text-only entries make long stretches look plain.
5. **Is any picture unclear?** Yes. Pine Lakes shows condos and a highway, not half-acre lots or a golf course. Little River is treetops and a condo marina, with a misplaced "Salt marsh" label. Surfside shows the pier under construction.
