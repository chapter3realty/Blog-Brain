## Medium

**Medium 1. Forestbrook Estates is still surface level, and its new sentences read like corrections.** The body:
- "Forestbrook Estates was formerly known as Kingsland. The county rezoned the land in 2017, and Lennar began building soon after. All of the 500 houses the county approved are built, and townhomes stand beside them."
- "The houses are built around ponds. On the same grounds as the pool is a community garden with garden plots, and listings say golf carts are allowed here."
- "Rosewood Landing, a county boat ramp on the Intracoastal Waterway, is about 2 miles away in a straight line, and about 5 miles by road. The neighborhood has ponds, not a lake."

The third paragraph spends two sentences on a ramp 5 miles away, and "The neighborhood has ponds, not a lake." is a verifier's correction pasted into the copy; the paragraph before already says ponds. "listings say" is the attribution CRAFT 5 bans. "formerly known as Kingsland" tells the reader nothing about the place. The reader still cannot tell why it is on a best list. The legend puts it under "A lower price", but at about $395,000 it costs more than The Farm, Deerfield and Berkshire Forest.

Want: say why a buyer picks it, in one plain line ("a newer Lennar house with a pool and garden plots for about $395,000, about 14 minutes from the beach"). Then what an owner does there (the garden plots, the golf-cart rule, stated plainly if a row allows it), and what is close by on Forestbrook Road and SC 707, as a drive. Cut the ramp paragraph and "ponds, not a lake". If the research has nothing more, ROUND-10 allows replacing the entry.

**Medium 2. The Prince Creek price leaves out a neighborhood the entry names.** Entry 15: "Bellwood Landing, Creekhaven, Longwood Bluffs and Wilderness Pointe are among the neighborhoods of Prince Creek West." and "Homes usually sell for $315,000 to $620,000". The gated page, entry 12: Bellwood Landing "Homes usually sell for about $850,000". The ledger's Prince Creek row (row 147) is ten resales in the other sections; Bellwood's eight sales were $730,000 to $1.2 million. A reader of both pages gets two prices for Bellwood. Want: name only the sections the range covers, or add "Bellwood Landing, behind its own gate, sells for more, about $850,000." Same change in the side-by-side table row.

**Medium 3. Three entries end on the same beach-parking trade-off, and one of them is no trade-off at all.**
- Grande Dunes: "The beach accesses closest to it have little or no parking."
- Plantation Lakes: "The beach access nearest by car has no parking; the next one, with a pay lot, is about the same distance away."
- Deerfield: "The closest beach access has three parking spaces, for golf carts only. By car, owners use the next access, which has a pay lot."

This is the "hung up on one thing" the owner named, in the trade-off slot. Plantation Lakes' line says the next access is "about the same distance away", so the reader loses nothing. Want: keep Deerfield's (golf carts only is the striking one). Give Plantation Lakes a real trade-off from a row already held, such as the 20-minute drive to the beach (the table has it) or its price, the second highest after Grande Dunes. Grande Dunes already has the price in its facts line; its trade-off can be the building: "Across Grande Dunes, most houses for sale are just built or still being built," which the entry already says.

**Medium 4. Two sentences read as a data sheet where a trade-off or a picture of the place should be.**
- Tidewater: "Two-bedroom condos in the Clubhouse Villas list at about $780 to $800 a month in HOA fees, and other units in the same buildings list higher and lower." The second half says nothing. Tidewater's real trade-off is on the gated page: "Tidewater is on the Intracoastal Waterway, not the ocean." Want: "The condos pay a much higher HOA fee than the houses, about $800 a month for two bedrooms." Or use the ocean line.
- Cherry Grove: "From a home near the middle of the neighborhood, a public beach access is about 0.1 mile away, the closest of the fifteen." "From the same spot, Russell Burgess Coastal Preserve, with a walkway and a pier, is under half a mile on foot." The measuring point is method. Want: "From most homes here, the beach is a short walk, about 0.1 mile. Russell Burgess Coastal Preserve, with a walkway and a pier over the marsh, is under half a mile."

**Medium 5. One page, two values: The Market Common's townhome fee.** Entry 12: "Townhomes here list at about $300 to $680 a month in HOA fees". Side-by-side table: "About $300 to $700 for a townhome". CRAFT 5: "One page, one value." Want: one figure in both places.

Reads like a deed summary. "The homeowners association owns and looks after the 80 acres of lakes and open land" says what it means for the owner.
