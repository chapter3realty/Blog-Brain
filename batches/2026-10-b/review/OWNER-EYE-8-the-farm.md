## Findings

**Medium 1. The first offer is a different, smaller box than the last one.** The first offer, after the short answer, is a pale card with a thin orange left edge. Its headline "Get the list of homes for sale in The Farm." is bold body text, the same size as the sentence under it. The last offer is the navy box with a large serif headline. ROUND-7 asked for "the same boxed offer component as the last one on the page", and CRAFT 3 says "Use the same box component as the last offer." The owner asked to "make the first CTA more of a CTA". On a phone it still reads as a note. Want: the navy box, large headline, the same button, right after the short answer. (Same on all five pages; see the batch file.)

**Medium 2. One theme told four times: the sunk pool and the special assessment.**
- Things to do: "The large pool has sunk into the ground more than it should, which engineers call settling. The HOA board has not decided whether to repair it or replace it." Then: "By a majority vote, it can add a one-time charge to owners, called a special assessment."
- Cons: "The large pool has sunk, and the HOA board has not decided whether to repair or replace it." / "Plan for the chance of a one-time charge to owners, called a special assessment."
- Story: "The minutes show the large pool has sunk, and the board has not decided how to fix it. Owners may have to pay a one-time charge for the work."
- FAQ: "The board can also add a one-time charge, called a special assessment, by a majority vote."

"Special assessment" is defined three times. The story reveals nothing the reader has not read twice, so it has no surprise. Want: the full fact once (things to do), one short con line, and the story. Cut the definition from the con and the FAQ.

**Medium 3. The school map does not show the sentence beside it.** Text: "Every home in The Farm is inside all three zones, so every home has the same three schools." On the map the zones are thin lines with no fill. "Middle school zone" sits below a purple line that runs south of The Farm, so The Farm looks outside it. "High school zone" sits beside a yellow line at the top right, far from The Farm. A buyer cannot tell which side of a line is inside. Want: light fills for each zone (or one fill for the area all three share), with The Farm clearly inside, and each label inside its zone.

**Medium 4. The list page knows more history about The Farm than The Farm's own page.** Best neighborhoods, entry 7: "D.R. Horton recorded The Farm's HOA rules in 2004 and added land in stages after that. In 2019 it gave up its right to appoint most of the HOA board. The farm theme runs through many of the street names: Scarecrow Way, Haystack Way, Pumpkin Patch Lane, Wheatfield Drive." The Farm page says only "D.R. Horton developed The Farm in sections." The owner asked for "special features and history". Want: the start year, the owners taking over the board, and the farm street names in "What are the homes like", in two sentences.

**Low 1. The first screen says "two pools" three times.** Hero line "About 1,600 homes and two pools, near the beach." Lede "with two pools, an activity center and a playground." Short answer "has two pools, an activity center, a clubhouse ... and a playground." The lede and the first short-answer sentence are near copies. Cut the lede or make it say what the short answer does not (where it is).

**Low 2. Cable TV is named five times** (short answer, costs, pros, cons, FAQ) and "outside the city limits" three times. Fine in the FAQ; trim one each from the body.

**Low 3. Filler.** "Its cons are costs and HOA rules to plan for." "These are the main points of the HOA rules, which the HOA board can change." "The district can change its zones." Each is true of any HOA neighborhood. Cut.

**Low 4. One rule line gives the owner a step.** "Send a land surveyor's drawing of your lot with the request." Want: "The request needs a land surveyor's drawing of the lot. We get a copy of the survey from your closing."

**Low 5. The location map's road labels float.** "Carolina Forest Blvd" and "US 501" are not tied to a visible road line. A leader line to each road would help a buyer who does not know them.
