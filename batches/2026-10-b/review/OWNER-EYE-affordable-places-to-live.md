## What I would send back, most important first

1. **Conway has two prices that do not fit together.**
   - "A typical home in Conway is worth about $290,000." Then: "In Loris, Aynor and Conway, a typical home is a house, not a condo." Then: "In Conway, a house usually sells for about $265,000."
   - If the typical home is a house, the reader asks why a house sells for $25,000 less than the typical home.
   - Wanted: one Conway figure in the "what $250,000 buys" section. Either drop the $265,000 sale price, or say in plain words why it differs ("Houses that sold in Conway in the past year went for about $265,000, a little under the typical value, because more of them were older"). Use the second only if a verified row says so.

2. **Method in the reader's way.**
   - "A typical home value is our measure of price on this page. It is Zillow's estimate of what a home in the middle of the market is worth. It is not a sale price, so a home can sell for more or less." The owner: "we are the experts... just present the facts to them with a few sources."
   - Wanted: one line: "These are what a typical home in each place is worth today. A home can sell for more or less." Zillow goes in the sources line and the chart caption.
   - Also cut "by Georgetown County's tax estimator" from "A typical home you live in pays about $2,300 a year, by Georgetown County's tax estimator."
   - Also cut "Values are lower in small inland places such as Green Sea, a place of about 40 homes, but few homes sell there." The cold reader skipped it, and it gives no number.

3. **The story has no want and little at stake.**
   - "Kim is buying her first home near Conway. She likes two houses at about the same price, and one of them is inside the city limits... Kim makes her offer on the house outside the city."
   - It passes the simple story test on length and numbers. But it fails test 1: there is no want, and nothing is at stake, so it does not show skill.
   - It also shares the batch's one arc (see OWNER-EYE-batch.md).
   - Wanted: give Kim one want ("Kim wants to keep her yearly bills as low as she can"), and say the difference in words if a verified row supports it ("about $300 more a year inside the city").
   - Better, if it fits the page: a down-payment story. A first-time buyer who thinks she must save for years learns from her agent about the $10,000 program and buys this year. That is the page's strongest piece of news, and it has a real want and a real fix. The agent's act must be a service on record. Chapter3 does no financing, so the agent sends her to an SC Housing lender and does not arrange the money.

4. **The call-to-action line after the short answer.** "Tell us your price range and the places you like. The form asks for your name, email and phone, and has a box for your price range." It says "price range" twice and talks about a form. Wanted: "Tell us your price range. We send you the homes for sale in each place. The form asks for your name, email and phone."

5. **Longs and Aynor have no picture.** These are the two places the cold reader knew least.
   - If a license-clean Commons photo of Aynor's town center exists, add it.
   - If not, add a NOAA or NAIP aerial of a typical street of houses in Longs, labelled. Longs is where new homes are being built, which is what that section says.
   - Low priority, but it would make this page's pictures carry facts and not only scenery.

6. **Homeowners insurance is not mentioned.** The cold reader asked for it, and best-neighborhoods links "Our guide to insurance near the coast." Add that one link in "What do you give up", next to the older-homes line, which is already about insurers.

7. **The town photos show the town, not homes.** The harbor, Main Street and the riverwalk are true and well chosen. Keep them. If an aerial of a typical $250,000 street exists for one town, it would show what the page is about better than scenery (owner: "the photos should be of the topic we are talking about").
