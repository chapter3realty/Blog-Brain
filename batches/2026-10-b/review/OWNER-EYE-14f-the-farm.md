## Findings

### High

None.

### Medium

**1. The line under the H1 sells what any neighborhood has.**
- Where: the subtitle "Two pools for summer evenings, and the beach for weekends."
- Pools and a beach 10 to 12 minutes away are true of most of Carolina Forest. It is the first thing a reader sees, and it could be about any other place. The owner: "if its next to a pond ... gloss over it ... find information that isnt obvious and therefore is intriguing". The 14e reviewer asked for this line to be built on what no one could guess; the field list is gone, but the subject is the same.
- The page already has the line it needs, two screens down: the woods behind The Farm were meant for a hospital and will now stay woods for good.
- Instead: one subtitle of about 8 to 12 words on the preserve next door, for example on the flytrap woods along the north edge that no one may build on. Let the pools and the beach stay in the day, where they are already sold well.

**2. The top three sections say the same things two and three times.**
- The preserve purchase:
  - short answer: "The state bought those woods for a nature preserve instead."
  - next section, first line: "The state says the 353 acres along The Farm's north edge will become part of the Lewis Ocean Bay Heritage Preserve."
  - the location map caption and the first pro say it again.
- The fitness room:
  - short answer: "A day here can start in the clubhouse fitness room."
  - the day: "On weekdays, residents can start with a workout in the clubhouse fitness room, which opens at 5:30 a.m."
- The rock wall, the library and the trails, three times in nearly the same words:
  - short answer: "A library, a rock climbing wall and the county's trails are 3 to 4 minutes away by car."
  - the day: "the county library and the Carolina Forest Recreation Center share one campus, about 3 minutes by car ... The center has a rock climbing wall and a full gym" and "the county's Bike and Run Park has 7 miles of mountain bike and hiking trails, about 4 minutes by car".
  - pros: "A gym, a rock wall, a library and trails 3 to 4 minutes away."
- `repeat-check` flags the last one: "[number] to [number] minutes away" in 3 sections.
- A reader on a phone meets the fitness room twice within 1.3 screens, and the preserve purchase twice within one screen. This is the fault the owner named on his second read.
- Instead:
  - Short answer: keep the hospital story and add its payoff in one sentence (the woods along the north edge will stay woods). For the daily-life line, use one image the day does not repeat, or point to the day in different terms (the walk among the flytraps, the swim after work).
  - "What makes The Farm different": do not restate the purchase. Start on what is new there: the flytraps, the state law, the street names.
  - Pros: say each in new words and fewer of them, for example "A rock wall, a library and 7 miles of trails within 4 minutes."

**3. The day still has no place to eat and no drive to work.**
- Where: "What is a normal day like in The Farm at Carolina Forest?"
- CRAFT 5 says the normal day is "What an owner does on a Saturday, where they eat, where they launch the boat, how long the drive to work is." The day names no restaurant, no coffee, and no drive to the places people work. Publix "2 min drive" is in the tiles but not in the day. The 14e reviewer asked for the same research; it was not added.
- "The HOA's events include a fall yard sale and a Community Appreciation Day." is the weakest line in the day. A yard sale is not a reason to move. Say what Community Appreciation Day is, or cut the line.
- Instead: research the kinds of fact that fill a week and that no sibling has: the nearest places to eat and get coffee, by name and drive; the drive to the main places people work (the hospitals, downtown Myrtle Beach, Conway), with rush hour. Put Publix in the day in one clause.

**4. The schools sit at The Farm's edge, and the page does not say whether a child can walk.**
- Where: "Both Ocean Bay schools are at The Farm's north-east edge." Then the table: "Ocean Bay Elementary ... About 6 minutes", "Ocean Bay Middle ... About 4 minutes".
- A parent reads "at the edge" and then a 6-minute drive, and asks the obvious question: can my child walk, or does a bus come? The 14e reviewer named it in "what the page forgot". It is still open.
- Instead: research whether The Farm is in the district's walk zone or bus zone for each school, and whether a path or sidewalk reaches the schools from inside The Farm. Say it in one sentence. If the drive from the main entrance is the honest answer, say why the drive is longer than the distance.
