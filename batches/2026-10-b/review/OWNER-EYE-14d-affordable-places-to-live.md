## Findings

### High

**H1. Seven entries describe the homes as a sample we looked at.**
- 1. Parkview: "The townhomes we've seen for sale here have two stories ... and the three-bedroom one we saw had the main bedroom downstairs."
- 2. Fairways: "Every Fairways townhome we found for sale had its own garage".
- 3. Sawgrass East: "The two Sawgrass East townhomes we found for sale were three-bedroom, two-story homes".
- 5. Golf and Yacht Club: "each house we saw for sale had a porch or a patio."
- 8. Amber Bluff: "The ones we have seen for sale all share one three-bedroom floor plan".
- 11. Wynbrooke: "The ones we found for sale had two stories and a screened porch".
- 12. Polo Farms: "The two we saw for sale had two baths and an attached two-car garage."
- This is the research method in the copy (CRAFT 5: "The method ... goes in the ledger and the sources line, not the entry"). It reads like a reporter's notes, the voice the owner sent back with "don't do the facts: thats not how a human talks". "The two ... we found" also tells the reader we looked at two homes, which weakens the sale.
- Do instead: say what the homes are, as an agent who knows them. "Sawgrass East townhomes have three bedrooms over two floors, a one-car garage, and the main bedroom downstairs." Where a feature is not on every home, say "many" or "some". Keep the listing count in the ledger. Use "we" for what our agents do for the buyer, not for how the facts were gathered.

**H2. Four entries do not sell their place.** The owner: "like you're selling the best place in the world for each location."
- **2. The Fairways at Wild Wing.** It leads with a garage, which a buyer can guess. Paragraph two gives directions: "The townhomes are off Wild Wing Boulevard, inside Wild Wing Plantation on US 501." Paragraph three is an Aldi and a hospital. "Wild Wing still has 27 holes of golf" makes the reader ask what was lost, and tells them nothing about playing it.
- **3. Sawgrass East.** Nothing here is only Sawgrass East: "Owners share a pool and a clubhouse, and the townhomes are around small ponds." "Sawgrass East is off Carolina Forest Boulevard, a little over two miles up the boulevard from US 501." Then it sends the reader away: "the table after the entries shows what it usually includes." Round 14c asked for research on what only Sawgrass East has. The recreation center was cut, as asked, but nothing new took its place.
- **5. Myrtle Beach Golf and Yacht Club.** "One story means no stairs" is the obvious talk the owner said "should be banned". After it comes an amenity list: "the HOA has a pool and hot tub, tennis courts and a community center." The entry never says what a day there is like.
- **10. Indigo Grove.** The headline sells "22 acres of open space", but the entry never says what is in it: trails, a pond, a field, or woods. "Lennar sells it as The Overlook" leaves the reader unsure which name to search. Then a grocery store and trash pickup.
- Do instead: research the kind of fact that sells each one, and lead with it.
  - Fairways: what playing Wild Wing is like for an owner, from a fact the best page does not use; what the HOA covers besides internet.
  - Sawgrass East: who built it and when, what the clubhouse is used for, what is within a short walk or ride, and why its fee is high.
  - Golf and Yacht Club: what an owner does on a Saturday there; what the community center hosts. Keep the gate to one clause, as now.
  - Indigo Grove: what the 22 acres hold and how owners use them; what Longs and the river nearby give an owner. Say which name the buyer will see on listings once, plainly.
  - Each entry: one paragraph on a normal day there (CRAFT 5, step 2).

**H3. Eight of twelve entries have no picture, and none shows a home.**
- Pictures: Parkview, Victory Point, Wynbrooke and Polo Farms, all labelled aerials. Round 14c counted seven pictures; three more aerials were cut (Fairways, Sawgrass East, Golf and Yacht Club). Cutting unclear aerials was right, but the entries are now text only.
- The owner's own shape for a list page: "a headline for that community and a few paragraphs and photos and then the next" (2026-10-14). The page sells "the amenities of the house", and the reader never sees a house, a porch, a pool, a garage, the MarshWalk or the wake park.
- Do instead: send the photo partner the request in `batches/2026-10-b/photo-request-2-affordable.md` now (CLAUDE.md: "ask the photo partner first"). Ask for one ground photo per entry: a street of homes, or the amenity the entry leads with (the Sandridge wake park, the MarshWalk near Wynbrooke, the Victory Point street signs, the Birchwood lots). Until they arrive, a clear labelled aerial of the amenity an entry sells is better than none, but only if its point shows in a second.

### Medium

**M2. Two pictures say less than the text beside them.**
- 11. Wynbrooke: the aerial sits right under the MarshWalk paragraph, but its labels are "Lowes Foods" and "Publix". The entry's best thing is the MarshWalk; the picture sells two grocery stores.
- 12. Polo Farms: Simpson Creek is said three times in a row: the photo label, the caption "with Simpson Creek along its southwest side", then "Simpson Creek runs along the neighborhood's southwest side." "Off SC 905" is in the caption and the next paragraph.
- Do instead: for Wynbrooke, use a picture of what the lead sells (a photo partner shot of the MarshWalk), or move the aerial down beside the grocery sentence. For Polo Farms, cut the creek sentence from the paragraph and keep it in the caption, or the reverse.

**M3. The map has no town names.**
- The map after the entries shows twelve numbered circles, road shields, "Atlantic Ocean" and one town, "Red Hill". A buyer new to the area cannot tell where Myrtle Beach, Conway, Longs, Little River or Murrells Inlet are, so the map does not answer "where is each place" at a glance.
- Do instead: label the towns the entries name (Myrtle Beach, Conway, Longs, Little River, Murrells Inlet, North Myrtle Beach), and put a short place name beside each number.

**M4. The FAQ says the same things about Parkview twice, and repeats the section above it.**
- "What is the cheapest neighborhood ...": "Parkview is a group of townhomes off 17th Avenue South, inside the city of Myrtle Beach." "Its older townhomes usually sold for about $190,000."
- "Which affordable neighborhood is closest to the beach?": "Parkview, a townhome community off 17th Avenue South in Myrtle Beach ... Its older townhomes usually sold for about $190,000 in the past year."
- The entry's lead already says "off 17th Avenue South, inside the city of Myrtle Beach".
- "Do you have to be a first-time buyer ...?" repeats "You do not have to be a first-time buyer" from the section just above.
- Do instead: merge the two Parkview answers into one, or give the beach question a different answer with new facts (the next-closest places, Sandridge and Wynbrooke). Cut the first-time-buyer question, or answer it with the one new fact, Palmetto Home Advantage, in one sentence.

**M5. Daily life is mostly drive minutes.**
- repeat-check: "trip" in 6 of 12 entries, "drive" in 5, and three sentences end "a six-minute drive" (Parkview's airport, the Fairways' hospital, Amber Bluff's stadium).
- Most entries give a list of places and minutes, not what an owner does there. Wynbrooke shows the difference: "a half-mile boardwalk along the marsh, lined with restaurants, with live music every night and no cover charge."
- Do instead: in the middle entries (Birchwood, Forestbrook Village, Amber Bluff, Indigo Grove), replace one minutes sentence with what an owner does on a weekday evening or a Saturday nearby, from a verified row.

## The best three things: keep them

1. **7. Victory Point.** "Victory Point is built on part of the old Myrtle Beach Speedway. The track opened in 1958 as RAMBI Raceway, and cars ran their last laps there in 2020. Several streets have racing names: Speedway Street, Talladega Drive and Pit Crew Drive." A buyer could not guess it, and the labelled aerial of the old oval is the clearest picture on the page. The trade-off is honest and specific: "Another owner plans 194 townhomes for rent on about 20 acres of the old speedway land beside it."
2. **1. Parkview's golf course.** "It was originally part of the Myrtle Beach Air Force Base, and its tee markers feature the squadron emblems. Residents of Horry, Georgetown and Brunswick counties play a round there for $45 plus tax, with a cart." This is selling with facts only research finds. Keep it with Wynbrooke's "live music every night and no cover charge" and Polo Farms' honest Florence paragraph.
3. **The chart and the close of every entry.** The chart puts each place's best thing beside its price, as the owner asked: "4 min to the beach, golf", "new house, quarter acre", "one-story, $20 HOA fee". Each entry ends with a sentence a person would say and its own offer: "Townhomes in Wynbrooke usually went for about $250,000 last year, with an HOA fee of about $450 a month." then "Book a showing in Wynbrooke".

## One line each

1. **Does every entry sell the place like the best agent in town, leading with something a buyer could not guess?** No. 2 Fairways (a garage), 3 Sawgrass East (a pool, a clubhouse and ponds), 5 Golf and Yacht Club (one story and an amenity list) and 10 Indigo Grove (open space never described) do not.
2. **Is there any "facts:" style label line, or field list in prose?** No label lines. Golf and Yacht Club's "a pool and hot tub, tennis courts and a community center" is close to a field list, and "we found for sale" is the research method in the copy (H1).
3. **Does every place end with its own offer?** Yes, 12 of 12: a price sentence and "Book a showing in [place]".
4. **Does it look modern (liquid glass), with charts that are simple to understand?** Yes. Frosted cards, pill buttons and a clean chart: a list with prices on a phone, thin bars on desktop. The map needs town names (M3).
5. **Is any picture unclear?** None is unclear. The Wynbrooke aerial sells grocery stores under a MarshWalk paragraph (M2), and eight entries have no picture (H3).
