## The reader

A buyer who plans to rent the home out: by the night, by the season or by the year. Some are investors. Some are second-home buyers who want to rent the weeks they are away. Some may move and keep the house. Many are from out of state, where the law differs. Florida protects owners who bought before a new rental ban; South Carolina has no such law.

A second reader already owns here and has heard the board or the owners may vote on a rental rule.

What they want first: can the association stop me, and how do I know before I buy. What they know: little. Many think the city's rule is the only rule. What worries them: buying for a plan the papers forbid, and a ban voted in after they buy. What they ask next: which communities here allow it, and what the rule says in a real place.

Why they typed it: they are about to spend money on a plan that depends on renting, and they want to know who can say no. "myrtle beach hoa rules" and "carolina forest hoa rules" are offered with named places (Myrtle Trace, The Farm, Golf and Yacht Club, Surfside Beach Club, New Clear Pond, Carolina Trace): people look up one community's rules before they buy.

## The deciding fact (checked first)

**Yes. A South Carolina HOA or condo can limit or ban renting, if the rule is in its recorded papers and clearly worded.**
- South Carolina's HOA Act says a governing document must be recorded to be enforced (27-30-130).
- The condo law says the master deed states any limits on leasing (27-31-100(h)).
- Neither law caps a rental rule or protects owners who bought before a new one.
- SC courts enforce a clear rule (a Columbia condo, 2015) and read an unclear one in the owner's favor (a Hilton Head house, 2017).
- A change needs the vote the papers require (2015 case).

Ledger rows 10 to 18. Checked before anything else; it holds.
