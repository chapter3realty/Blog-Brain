## The reader

Two people type this. First, an owner in a dispute: a fine, a rule, a board that will not answer. They want to know what the law lets the HOA do and who will make it stop. Second, a buyer, often from North Carolina, New York, New Jersey or Florida, who wants to know what protections South Carolina gives before they buy into an HOA or a condo. Both assume a state agency polices HOAs, as many expect from home. Neither knows that South Carolina has a short HOA law and that the recorded documents decide almost everything.

Why they typed it (CRAFT 1): "can my HOA do this?", "who do I complain to?", "is this rule even legal?", "what am I agreeing to if I buy here?". Their next questions: can it fine me, can it take my house, can it raise dues, can it stop me renting, can I see the books, can I sue.

Checked first (2026-11-10): SC Consumer Affairs' HOA page says "While South Carolina does not have a comprehensive law detailing how a homeowners association must operate, associations must follow the South Carolina Homeowners Association Act. Certain other laws may apply as well" (facts row 1); the live code shows the Act's Article 1 has six sections (recording, budget notice, records, magistrate court, construction, short title) and no change since 2019 (rows 4, 6).
