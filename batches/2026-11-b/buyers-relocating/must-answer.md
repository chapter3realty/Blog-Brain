## The reader

Someone in another state who has mostly decided to move to the Myrtle Beach area, or is close to it, and now wants to know where to start. The largest groups by home purchases are North Carolina, New York, Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Virginia (deed counts, facts row 6). Many have vacationed here. Many will buy before they move, or on one trip.

- **What they want first:** a short, true reason the move works for people like them, and the order of the work: what to read, what to decide, how to buy from where they live now.
- **What they already know:** the name and the beach. Not the towns, the roads, the county, the tax rules or how a closing works here.
- **What worries them:** buying a house they have not stood in; picking the wrong town; a closing they cannot attend; a job change and the loan; surprise costs (insurance, the car tax, the 6 percent rate on a home not yet filed as their residence).
- **What they ask next:** which town, what it costs, the first-month paperwork, and what changes for their own state's taxes.

Why they typed it (CRAFT 1): "relocating to Myrtle Beach" and "Myrtle Beach relocation guide" are typed by a person who wants one place that lays out the whole move. "Relocation realtor" and "relocation assistance" are typed by a person who wants help doing it. Both want the plan and a team, not a sales pitch.

## The deciding fact

The hub can lead with "you can buy before you move" only if South Carolina lets a buyer sign and close from another state. It does (facts rows 10 to 15): an offer signed electronically has the same effect as ink (S.C. Code 26-6-70); a deed or mortgage signed before another state's notary, with two witnesses, records here (30-5-30(B)(5)); the SC Bar's ethics opinion 05-16 lets the attorney close by mail when "buyer and seller wish to conduct a real estate closing by mail". The one limit: South Carolina has no video notarization, so the signing is in person, before a notary where you live.
