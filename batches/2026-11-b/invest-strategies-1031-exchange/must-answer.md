## The reader

An owner of a Grand Strand rental, most often a condo, who lives in another state and is about to sell, or a seller from another state who wants to buy here. In Horry County, 58 percent of condos have their tax bill mailed outside South Carolina (facts row 39), so this reader is common. They know a 1031 "defers the tax". They do not know South Carolina will hold back part of their money at the closing table, that one box on a state form stops it, or that a December closing has less than 180 days. They fear losing the deferral on a technicality and losing money they cannot get back until they file. Next question after "does it work here": "what do I need to do before I list, and what can I buy with the money".

## The deciding fact

Does South Carolina's nonresident withholding apply to a seller who is doing a 1031 exchange, and how is it avoided? Checked first. Yes, unless the seller gives the buyer the state's affidavit. Form I-295, item 16, has three boxes for a like-kind exchange (facts row 7). SC Revenue Ruling #09-13 sets out what happens with a qualified intermediary: the withholding is worked out and handed to the intermediary, and it is never paid to the state if the exchange is completed (row 10). ## Must-answers, ranked

1. **Does a 1031 exchange work in South Carolina, and does the state add rules?** Yes. SC uses the federal Internal Revenue Code and does not set 1031 aside. The one state step is the withholding at closing for an out-of-state seller (rows 5 to 13, 19).
2. **What does South Carolina withhold when an out-of-state owner sells, and how does an exchange change it?** 5.21 percent for a 2026 sale, of the gain with Form I-295 or of the price less selling costs without it. With box 16 ticked and a qualified intermediary, nothing goes to the state at closing. Worked Myrtle Beach example (rows 1 to 18, 43).
3. **What are the 45-day and 180-day deadlines, and can they be extended?** Calendar days from the sale's closing, running together; the return due date can cut the 180 short for a late-year sale; only IRS disaster relief moves them, and every South Carolina county got it after Hurricane Helene (rows 23, 24, 36).
4. **How do I name the replacement property by day 45?** In writing, signed, sent to the intermediary; three properties, or the 200 percent or 95 percent rules (rows 27 to 29).
5. **What can I buy here with the money?** What is for sale today in Horry County by kind of home and price, and what sold in the past year (rows 41, 42). Show the homes.
6. **What if I keep some of the cash?** The cash is taxed up to the gain; the state withholds on that part only (rows 11, 25, 43).
7. **Can I exchange a beach condo I also use myself?** Yes, inside the IRS safe harbor: owned 24 months, rented 14 days or more at a fair rent in each year, personal use no more than 14 days or 10 percent (row 34).
8. **Who holds the money, and can my agent or attorney do it?** A qualified intermediary. Anyone who was your agent, attorney or accountant in the past two years cannot be one, other than for exchange work (rows 30, 31).
9. **Can I sell in another state and buy in South Carolina, or the other way?** Yes; real property in the US is like-kind to any other US real property (row 26). SC withholding applies when the SC property is sold.
10. **How does a reverse exchange work?** Short: buy first, a parking company holds title, 5 business days, 45 and 180 days (row 37). Keep it short: 26 showings a month, but a small group of readers.
