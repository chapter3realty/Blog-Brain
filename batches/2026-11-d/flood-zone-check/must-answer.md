## Who searches this, and why

A buyer who has found a house, or is about to look, and wants to know one thing before they fall for it: is this address in a flood zone? Behind the search is a fear of a surprise. The lender asks for flood insurance after the offer, and the bill changes what the house costs to own. Many assume the listing would have told them. The suggestions show that belief: "zillow flood zone", "zillow not showing flood zone", "is zillow flood zone accurate", "zillow filter by flood zone", "zillow flood map gone". The page answers the question they type, and the one they did not think to ask: can I trust the listing to tell me?

What they would pay an expert to tell them:
1. The answer for this address, in two minutes, from the official map.
2. What the letters mean for them: does a lender make me buy flood insurance?
3. Whether the listing or a home search would have warned them (our study: usually not).
4. What to do before the offer, so the insurance is a number and not a surprise.

## The must-answers, in order

1. **Is my house (this house) in a flood zone?** Type the address in our lookup at /buyers/property-records/; it reads FEMA's flood map and gives the zone in plain words. FEMA's Map Service Center is the official source and prints the map panel.
2. **Would the listing have told me?** Usually not. Of 1,122 Horry County homes for sale whose map pin is in FEMA's high-risk zone, 229 come up when our home search filters for flood zone; about 4 in 5 do not (rows 1 to 4). The flood-zone line in listing data is not a substitute for FEMA's map.
3. **What does my zone mean?** AE, A, VE and the others are high risk: a 1 percent or greater chance of flooding each year, which adds up to about 1 in 4 over 30 years. X is moderate or minimal. A loan from a federally regulated lender in a high-risk zone needs flood insurance; in X, no federal rule requires it.
4. **When do I find out, if I do nothing?** The lender must tell you in writing, before you sign the loan. That is after your offer is accepted.
5. **What should I get before I offer?** The zone, an elevation certificate if one exists (the county posts the signed ones it has), and a flood insurance quote. We do all three for any address you send.
6. **Is Horry County's flood map new?** The current FEMA map for the county took effect on December 16, 2021 (row 13). FEMA says its maps are updated over time; check on the day.
7. **Does a condo in a high-risk building need its own flood policy?** Short answer, one line: most of the high-risk homes for sale are condos (833 of 1,122). The building's policy and the unit owner's lender rules are on /hoa/master-insurance-ho6/ (owner of "condo flood insurance Horry County"). Only a link here.

## The deciding fact (checked first)

Does the listing data our search reads fail to flag most homes in FEMA's high-risk zone, on a fresh pull? Yes.
