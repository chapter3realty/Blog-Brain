## The questions a reader asks next (the page)

1. **Can I buy before I sell, and will a lender let me carry both?** ("can i buy a house before selling mine", "do i have to sell my house first before buying another", "qualifying for mortgage before selling house", "two mortgages at once", "bridge loan to buy house before selling", "bridge loan vs heloc", "heloc to buy house before selling", "buy before you sell program"). The answer: yes, if a lender approves you with both homes, or you pay cash. The lender counts both house costs unless the old home is under contract with its financing contingency cleared. A bridge loan or a home equity line is the lender's decision; no rate, no payment. Rows 9 to 11.
2. **How does an offer that depends on selling my house work here?** ("home sale contingency", "contingent on sale of buyers home", "sale contingency clause", "contingent offer on house", "kick out clause", "72 hour kick out clause"). The South Carolina REALTORS sale contingency addendum (SCR504): two deadlines, the seller keeps marketing, a notice (SCR505) that puts the buyer "on the clock", the buyer's four choices. Rows 12 to 16.
3. **Can I sell and stay in my house for a while after closing?** ("rent back after closing", "seller rent back", "rent back agreement", "how long can a seller rent back", "post closing occupancy agreement", "leaseback"). The standard SC contract hands over a vacant house and the keys at closing, so a rent-back is a separate written agreement; a buyer with a regular home loan must move in within 60 days of signing. Rows 17, 18.
4. **How long does each way take here, and what do most owners here do?** ("selling and buying a house at the same time", "simultaneous closing", "how long from contract to closing", "how long does it take to close on a house in sc", "how long does it take to sell a house in myrtle beach"). Our own count from county deeds of owners who sold and bought here within a year: about a third each sold first, bought first, or did both within a week; how long each group carried two homes or was between homes. Zillow's listing-to-contract and contract-to-closing days for the area. Rows 1 to 8.

## The deciding fact the reader needs to act

Whether they can afford to own both homes for about three months (the typical stretch for owners here who bought first, row 4), or can live somewhere else for about two months (the typical gap for those who sold first, row 5). Everything else on the page (the contingency, the rent-back, the lender rule) is a tool to shrink one of those two gaps.
