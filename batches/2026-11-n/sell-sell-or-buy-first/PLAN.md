## The reader and their job

An owner in Horry County who wants to move to another home here: up to a bigger house, down to a smaller one or a condo, or into a new home from a builder. Many are retirees or empty nesters; some are families who outgrew the house. They own one home, often with a loan on it, and the money from selling it is part of how they buy the next one. They have read the national advice ("it depends") and want to know what people here actually do, how long each way takes, and how not to end up with two house bills or no place to live.

What they fear: owning two homes for months; selling and having nowhere to go; losing the house they want because their offer depends on their sale; a buyer who will not let them stay a few weeks.

**Job, in one verb: decide** (which order). Kind of page: decision guide (CRAFT 3, "Decide": lead with the conditions for each answer; the visual that does the work is a decision table, here three cards on a phone). Main relation in the content: **options** with their time and risk.

## The deciding fact for the reader

The two gaps. Owners here who bought first owned both homes for about 3 months in the middle case; owners who sold first were between homes for about 2 months (rows 4, 5). A third of owners here avoided both gaps by closing both in the same week (row 1). Everything else on the page (the lender rule, the sale contingency, the rent-back) is a tool that shrinks one gap. The reader picks the gap they can live with.

5. **Can I buy a house before I sell mine?** Yes, if a lender approves you with both homes counted, or you pay cash. Plain list under thin rules, three items:
   - A regular home loan backed by Fannie Mae counts the payment, taxes, insurance and any HOA dues of both homes, until your house is under contract and your buyer's loan approval is no longer a condition (row 9).
   - If you keep the old house as a second home, its costs count; if you rent it, the lender's rules decide how much of the rent counts (row 10; link /sell/rental-property/).
   - A bridge loan borrows against the house you still own; it cannot be secured by the new home, and the lender must show you can carry both homes and the bridge loan at once. Whether you qualify, and on what terms, is the lender's call (row 11). A home equity line of credit (HELOC) is another loan against your home, also the lender's call.
   No rate, no payment amount, anywhere.
   **Mid-roll offer 2:** headline "Find out if you can own both homes for a while"; sentence: "Our affiliated lender, BrickWood Mortgage, a separate company, can tell you what you qualify for with both homes counted."; button "Have BrickWood check what I qualify for" → /contact/ with "lender" named. The inline Affiliated Business Arrangement Disclosure follows the box (the AFBA text in `specs/programs.js`, copied into this spec). Never "our lender".

6. **How does an offer that depends on selling my house work in South Carolina?** The answer first: "You add a one-page addendum to the contract, and the deal goes on only if your house closes by a date you both set." Then numbered steps (fsbo's step style, no cards):
   1. The contract has a box for it, and the South Carolina REALTORS sale contingency addendum (SCR504) is attached (row 12).
   2. You agree on the deadline: the closing day or a set number of days before it. Your house has to close by then, not just be under contract. If it has not, either side can end the deal within the days you agreed; if no one does, the deal goes on without the condition (row 13).
   3. Meanwhile you try in good faith to sell, and send the seller each contract on your house and word when it closes (row 14).
   4. The seller can keep showing the house. If a buyer comes with cash or with no house to sell, the seller sends you a notice (SCR505). Within the days agreed, you show that your house is under contract to close in time, drop the condition if you can buy without selling, step back to second place, or end the deal (row 15).
   One sentence after the steps, for the reader who searched "kick-out clause": "This is what agents elsewhere call a kick-out clause, and some here call a right of first refusal; there is no set 72 hours here, the days are written into the addendum." (row 15)
   One sentence for the seller's side, if room: the same addendum is what you sign when your buyer has a house to sell.
   No legal advice: "Your agent fills in the days, and the closing attorney can explain them."
   Proves: rows 12 to 15.

7. **Can I sell my house and stay for a while after closing?** Yes, if your buyer agrees in writing before closing; this is called a rent-back. The answer first, then:
   - The standard South Carolina REALTORS contract hands over the house empty, with the keys, codes and remotes, at closing; staying longer is a written agreement between you and the buyer (row 17).
   - A buyer with a regular home loan backed by Fannie Mae must move in within 60 days of signing the loan papers, so a rent-back longer than that does not fit that loan (row 18).
   - What we put in writing: the move-out day, the rent for each day, a deposit, who pays the power and water, and who insures what. (Wording depends on owner question 3.)
   **Mid-roll offer 3:** headline "Need a few weeks in the house after you sell?"; sentence: "We ask for the rent-back in your contract, so it is agreed before closing day."; button "Have us ask for a rent-back" → /contact/.
   Proves: rows 17, 18.

8. **What happens to my taxes if I own two homes for a while?** Two short items:
   - The 4 percent rate for the home you live in: a home that was your legal residence for any part of the year keeps it for that whole year, so both homes can have it the year you move. If you still own the old home the next year and do not live there, it is taxed at 6 percent. Apply for the new home's rate by January 15 after the year you move in (row 19; link /buyers/property-taxes/).
   - If you rent out the old home instead of selling: the tax break on the gain from a home sale needs 2 of the last 5 years lived in, so you have up to 3 years after moving out to sell. Your case goes to a tax pro (row 20; links /sell/capital-gains/, /sell/rental-property/).

9. **Questions owners ask** (open text, FAQ schema, one or two sentences each, each answer different from the sections):
   - Is it better to sell first, then buy? (Neither for everyone; name the condition and the owners' split, row 1.)
   - Can I close on both houses on the same day? (Yes; about 1 in 9 owners here recorded both deeds on the same day, row 1.)
   - What is a kick-out clause in South Carolina? (Row 15, one sentence, link to section 6.)
   - How long can a seller rent back after closing? (As long as the buyer agrees in writing; 60 days is the limit for a buyer with a Fannie Mae home loan, row 18.)
