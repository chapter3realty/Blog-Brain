## The reader and their job

Someone who wants a house, not a condo, they can walk or bike to the beach from. Often a buyer moving here or buying a second home, from a place where a beach house was out of reach. They do not know the streets, what "east of 17" or "second row" means, which towns are which, or that Myrtle Beach is a dozen beach towns in a row. They worry about three things: that it costs far more, that it floods, and that "near the beach" in a listing means a drive.

**Job, in one verb: choose** how close to the sand to buy, and in which beach town. Kind of page: a cost comparison by distance with a short list of places (`reports/Page shapes by kind of page.md`: "Know the total" plus "Pick a few places from many"). The spine is the reader's trade: each step closer to the ocean costs money and adds flood risk; where does the price jump, and where does the risk drop?

What they want first: where these houses are and what they cost. What they ask next: how much more than inland, can I walk it, will I need flood insurance, which town.

## The deciding fact

**The price jumps in the first quarter mile, then holds about level out to 2 miles.** Horry County houses within a quarter mile of the ocean sold for about $805,000 at the middle in the last year; a quarter to half a mile back, about $540,000; half a mile to 2 miles back, about $500,000; 2 to 5 miles inland, about $390,000 (row 2). Within half a mile it is about $675,000 against $390,000 2 to 5 miles inland, about 1.7 times (row 4). And the flood risk drops as fast: about half the houses within a quarter mile are in FEMA's high-risk zone, about 1 in 4 a quarter to half a mile back, under 1 in 10 past about 0.6 mile (rows 16, 17). A buyer who moves a few streets back from the ocean row keeps the walk, pays much less, and often leaves the high-risk zone.

## The findings no other site has

1. **Price by distance from the sand, from county deeds** (rows 2 to 5): the curve above, and the same comparison in each stretch of coast. Nobody has published a near-beach premium for the Grand Strand from recorded sales.
2. **The walk, measured for every house for sale** (rows 10, 10b, 10c): of 800 houses for sale within 1.5 miles of the ocean, 186 are under a quarter mile on foot from a public access (about 5 minutes), asking about $950,000 at the middle; 110 more are a quarter to half a mile (about 10 minutes), about $680,000. Of 21 houses under $400,000 within a 10-minute walk, 14 are in North Myrtle Beach and Cherry Grove. And 90 of the 420 houses within half a mile in a straight line are over a mile on foot. Built with the state's access points and a walking router.
3. **Flood risk by distance and by town** (rows 16 to 18): the high-risk share by tenth of a mile, and the surprise in it: within half a mile of the ocean, about 1 in 11 Myrtle Beach houses is in the high-risk zone, against about 4 in 10 in North Myrtle Beach and half from Surfside Beach to Murrells Inlet.
4. **Public accesses per mile** (rows 11 to 13): one about every 250 feet in North Myrtle Beach and every 350 to 400 feet in Myrtle Beach, Surfside Beach and Garden City; about one every 2,400 feet on Pawleys Island. For a house within half a mile, the nearest access is about 1,000 feet away at the middle.
5. **What the local words mean, measured** (rows 14, 15): Kings Highway runs about half a mile from the ocean in Myrtle Beach; "walk to the beach" listings are about a quarter mile out at the middle; "near the beach" reaches about 2 miles.
6. **Near the beach usually means no HOA** (row 21): about 8 in 10 houses for sale within a quarter mile report no association fee, against about 1 in 10 two to five miles inland.
