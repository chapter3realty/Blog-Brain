**Q1. When two spouses list a house with us, do we send every message and every offer to both, in writing, and have both sign every document?**
Proposed answer: yes. The law requires offers in writing to the seller and changes initialed by both sides (SC Code 40-57-135(H)); a firm owes its client loyalty and disclosure (40-57-350(A)), and when two spouses sell, both are the client. Blocks the offer "Have us run the listing with both of you, every step in writing" and one sentence in section 5.

**Q2. Do our agents give both spouses (or their attorneys) a free written estimate of what the house would sell for?**
Proposed answer: yes, free, called a price estimate and never an appraisal, with the disclosure SC Code 40-60-30(1) requires ("This valuation service may not be used for the purposes of obtaining financing in a federally related transaction."). Blocks the slim offer bar "Have us price the house for both of you, in writing."

**Q3. In practice, when divorcing sellers close, do the closing attorneys you work with hold the money until both sign a disbursement or the court rules, or do they pay it out by the settlement agreement?**
Proposed answer: they pay it as the signed agreement or court order directs, and hold any disputed part in trust (SC RPC 1.15(e)). Improves one FAQ answer; the rule alone is enough without it.

**Q4. Do we list for sellers who are still in the case, before an agreement is signed? If so, is there anything we always do (for example, wait for both attorneys to approve the listing)?**
Proposed answer: yes, with both owners signing; we do not require the attorneys' approval but welcome it. Blocks the offer "Send us your dates and we will plan the sale around them" and one sentence in section 7.
