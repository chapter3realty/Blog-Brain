## The reader and their job

A homeowner in Horry County who is separating or divorcing, or has just been told their spouse wants to. The house is often the largest thing they own together, and often where the children live. They are upset and tired. They do not know South Carolina's rules, they are afraid of losing the house or being cheated out of their share, and they fear the cost of fighting. Many have not yet talked to a lawyer. They type plain questions: "can I sell my house during a divorce", "who gets the house in SC", "how does a buyout work", "can my husband sell without my signature".

**Job, in one verb: decide** (sell and split, one buys the other out, or one keeps it for a time), and then **follow the steps**. Kind of page: decision guide with numbered steps inside it (`reports/Page shapes by kind of page.md`, decision guide and how-to). Calm and kind. The law said plainly, with the source in the sources box, and the point where a family law attorney is needed said plainly. No story, no example family, no judgement, no prediction of what a court will do.

## The deciding fact for the reader

**The two of you can choose, and if you cannot agree, a judge will.** South Carolina's Family Court divides the house as marital property whoever's name is on the deed (row 1). It weighs 15 factors, not a 50/50 rule (row 3). It can give one parent the right to live in the home for a time (row 3), order the house sold, and have the clerk of court sign the deed if a spouse will not (row 6). Then the money: on the middle $350,000 Horry County house, a sale costs a $1,295 deed fee and $3,500 per 1 percent of agent fees, while a buyout deed between spouses pays no deed fee but needs a new loan the keeper qualifies for alone (rows 16, 19, 20, 29, 30).

- *One buys the other out:* one keeps the home and pays the other for their share; no agent fee and no deed fee on the spouse-to-spouse deed; usually a new loan the keeper must qualify for alone, after at least 12 months of owning together (for a Fannie Mae loan).
- *One keeps it for a time:* the judge can give the parent with custody the right to live there for a reasonable time; both stay owners, and both still owe the loan; a written agreement or order matters for the tax rule later (row 23).

## Unique findings (two, plus one fact few pages state)

1. **A sale and a buyout worked out on the middle Horry County house** (rows 29 to 31): $150,000 of equity with $200,000 owed (an example balance); a sale costs $1,295 in deed fees and $3,500 per 1 percent of agent fees, about $74,350 each before agent fees if split equally; a buyout pays the other spouse $75,000 and needs a new loan of at least $275,000 that the keeper qualifies for alone. On the site's one $350,000 figure, so it agrees with /sell/fsbo/ and /sell/home-value/.
2. **How large the gain is on a typical house here, against the $250,000 limit** (row 33): using the metro's FHFA index on the $350,000 middle house, the whole gain is under one person's $250,000 for a house bought since mid-1996 (2016: about $183,000; 2006: about $151,000; 2021: about $120,000). So for most local couples, the timing rules (rows 22, 23, 26) matter more than the size of the gain. Nobody has published this comparison.
3. **The spouse-to-spouse deed pays no South Carolina deed fee** (row 20, SC Code 12-24-40(4)). None of the top results says so.
