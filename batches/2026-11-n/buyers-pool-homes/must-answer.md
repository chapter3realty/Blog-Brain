## Who searched this, and why

A buyer, often moving from another state, who pictures a backyard pool as part of beach living. Some are retiring here, some are families, some want a vacation home that rents. They have seen pool homes listed in Florida for less and expect the same here. They do not know that a private pool is rare on the Grand Strand below the top of the market, that most neighborhoods offer a shared pool instead, that a home inspector in South Carolina is not required to look at the pool, or what the county requires around one.

Why they typed it (CRAFT 1):
- "under $200,000 / under $300,000 with pool": a budget buyer hoping to get a pool for little. The honest answer is a count: there are very few.
- "vacation homes / rentals with private pool": many are renters or investors. A private pool helps a rental; the page links the rental pages and says the state's public-pool rule does not apply to a single-family rental's pool.
- "pool fence requirements horry county": an owner or buyer who wants to know if the fence on a house is legal, or what they must build.
- "do home inspectors check pools": a buyer under contract who just learned the inspection report skipped the pool.
- "is a pool worth it": a buyer weighing a pool home against a cheaper home in a neighborhood with a pool.

## The deciding fact (checked first)

**How many houses for sale have their own pool, and at what prices.** If pool homes were common at every price, the page would be a plain search page. They are not: of about 2,950 houses for sale in Horry County, 257 list a private pool, and only 5 ask $300,000 or less (facts rows 1 to 4). That makes the page's spine: where the pool homes are, what they cost, and the cheaper way to get a pool (a neighborhood pool) for the buyer under that price.
