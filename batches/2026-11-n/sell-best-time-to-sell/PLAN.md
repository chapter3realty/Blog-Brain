## The reader and their job

A homeowner in Horry County who has decided to sell this year, or soon, and wants to pick the month. Some have a house (often in Carolina Forest, Socastee, Conway, Surfside), some an oceanfront or golf condo they rent out, many live out of state. They have read that "spring is best" and want to know if it is true here, where summer is tourist season and winter is quiet. They fear listing in a dead month, sitting on the market, a storm wrecking the closing, or waiting and missing the market.

**Job, in one verb: decide** (when to list). Kind of page: decision guide with market data inside it (CRAFT 3). The main relation in the content is **date**: closings and prices by month, and the lag from listing to closing. One dominant shape: a month-by-month answer, then the lag that turns "best month to close" into "month to list".

## The deciding fact for the reader

Homes here close most in March through June, and homes that close in May and June sell for a middle price about 2 percent above the months around them; February closings sell about 4 percent below (rows 1, 3). A listing takes about 4 weeks to reach a contract in spring and about 5 weeks more to close (rows 7, 8). **So a house that should close in May or June goes on the market in March or April; a condo, which peaks earlier, lists in February or March** (rows 4, 5, 9). The month is worth a few percent. The year moved prices 20 percent from 2021 to 2022 and nothing from 2024 to 2025, and the asking price decides whether a home sells at all (rows 11 to 13).

## Wrong-shape check

- A national "spring is best" listicle would repeat every competitor. The reader here needs local months, and the lag that turns a closing month into a listing date.
- A market report (latest number first) would answer "is now a good time" and miss the title question; the current market is one item in section 8 and one FAQ.
- A long section per season would say the same thing four times. The seasons come in as the questions readers ask (winter, storms, tourists and school), each answering one thing.
