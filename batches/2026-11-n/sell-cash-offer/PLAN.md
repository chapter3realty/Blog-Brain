## The reader and their job

A Horry County homeowner who got a cash offer (a letter, a yard sign call, an online "instant offer") or is thinking of asking for one. Often the house needs work, or they have a deadline: a move, a job, an estate, a divorce, a tenant. They do not know how a cash buyer sets a price, what they would give up, or whether the buyer is real. They fear two things: losing a lot of money, and being taken by a stranger. They are not investors and do not know "ARV", "assignment" or "contingency".

**Job, in one verb: decide** (take the cash, list it as it is, or fix it and list it). Kind of page: decision guide (`reports/Page shapes by kind of page.md` section 9), with a worked example and a short checklist inside it. IPDAS rule for decision aids: give each option the same care, including the one that pays us nothing, and say how we are paid.

## The deciding fact for the reader

A cash offer trades price for speed and certainty, and here the price is large: **companies that bought Horry County houses from their owners and resold them within a year had paid a middle 68 percent of what they resold for** (row 5). On the middle fixed-up house, $260,000, that is an offer of about **$178,000, or $82,000 less** (row 18). Listing nets more unless repairs, fees and time cost more than that gap. And cash buyers are not rare: **at least 1 in 3 resales here had no home loan recorded, and about 3 in 4 of those buyers were people**, not companies (row 8). A seller who lists can still get a cash buyer.
