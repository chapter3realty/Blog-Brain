# /submarkets/myrtle-beach/market-common/: design

Batch 2026-11-n, Lane N. Spec: website `specs/market-common.js`. Shots: scratch `wfn/submarkets-market-common/shots/` (390, 768 and 1440 px, full page).

## What the page is

An area guide for one part of Myrtle Beach. The reader has eaten or seen a movie at The Market Common and wonders about living there. Their job is to decide whether to look here, and at which kind of home: a house in a neighborhood or a townhome on the town center's streets.

Title: `Market Common Homes in Myrtle Beach, on the Old Air Base` (56 characters). The search words come first and the hook is the air base, the deciding fact. A price in the title was planned ("Houses About $525K"), but the scorer's P4 blocks any price in the H1 (owner, PLAIN-7: "leading with prices would scare people away and even in the headline"), and the H1 must say what the title says. The prices are in the tiles and the first paragraph instead.

## Which approved treatment, and why

The town guide /submarkets/pawleys-island/ and the selling-by-owner page (batches 2026-11-a DESIGN.md), approved 2026-11-08.

- **Two-line headline** with the italic sub line at about half the H1 size ("Market Common homes in Myrtle Beach: / a town center on the old air base.").
- **Hero photo beside the headline on a wide screen,** after the sub line on a phone, as on Pawleys Island: the town center's homes over shops, the one thing a visitor already pictures. Its caption carries a fact (those apartments are rented, not sold).
- **Four plain tiles** (house price, townhome price, flood share, beach minutes), short rows on a phone.
- **The answer first:** the short answer names what it is, the prices, the HOA fee by kind, the flood share and the noise line.
- **Offers as the owner set them:** the slim bar under the short answer, an offer box after every second section, each tied to that section (price a home; check one home's flood zone, fee and tax; set up Saturday showings), and the full band at the end. Every button is a request. No two use the same words.
- **One card style, only where the reader chooses:** the three kinds of home, each with its own offer pill. Cards stack in one column at the 720 px block width, so the pill sits on one line (three columns squeezed each pill to four lines).
- **Plain lists under thin rules elsewhere:** the base's history as a short timeline, the homes for sale now, the two airport distances, what is at the town center.
- **One block width of 720 px** for the map, tables, chart, cards and offers; text at 665 px.
- **On wide screens a photo sits beside the paragraphs it explains:** Warbird Park beside the jets paragraph, the Grand Park lake beside the list of places.

## The unique parts

1. **Our map of the neighborhoods** on a NAIP 2023 aerial: the outlines, the town center, Valor Park, the runway and the beach access, named on the map with no key. A phone gets the same map cut to the neighborhoods, with fewer, larger names.
2. **Our noise map:** the airport's 65-decibel area from its 2023 layout plan, beside the town center, with the quarter-mile gap drawn and checked in the spec against the measured 441 m.
3. **Our price table by neighborhood** from county deeds, built from the data file.
4. **The walk chart:** minutes on foot to Valor Park from each neighborhood's middle.
5. **The three-area comparison:** a table on a wide screen, three plain blocks on a phone.

## What was taken away after looking at the shots

- Three-column cards with four-line pills (crowded): one column.
- A four-item list of flood counts with filler lines ("The second group"): one sentence.
- Long tile labels that wrapped to four lines on a wide screen: short labels.
- A 4-column comparison that scrolled sideways on a phone: plain blocks on a phone.
- History as four paragraphs (a wall on a phone): a six-line timeline.
- High Market's outline on the map, which the data file puts in the wrong place (NEEDS.md 13).
