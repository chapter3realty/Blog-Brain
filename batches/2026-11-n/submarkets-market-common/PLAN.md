## The reader and their job

A buyer, often moving to the area or moving up from a condo, who has been to The Market Common for dinner or a movie and wonders about living there. They know the shops; they do not know the neighborhoods' names, that it was an air base, what an HOA fee here runs, or how the airport next door affects a home.

**Their job, in one verb: decide** whether to look here, and in which part: a house in a neighborhood, or a townhome on the town center's streets.

What they would pay an expert to tell them:
- what a house and a townhome cost here, and the monthly HOA fee for each;
- how far each neighborhood really is from the shops, on foot;
- whether a lender will make them buy flood insurance;
- how loud the airport is, in facts, not reassurance;
- what a year of owning costs besides the mortgage;
- how it compares with Carolina Forest and Surfside Beach.

## The deciding fact (checked first)

The Market Common and the neighborhoods around it are built on the former Myrtle Beach Air Force Base (3,936 acres, closed 1993), inside the City of Myrtle Beach (rows 1, 34). It explains the page: a planned town center, a runway next door, Warbird Park's jets.

## The findings no other site has

1. **The airport noise answer, measured** (rows 17 to 20). We placed the airport's own 65-decibel lines (2018 and the 2038 forecast, from its 2023 layout plan) on every parcel. No home is inside; the closest is about a quarter mile outside; the loudest areas run off the runway's ends.
2. **Price and HOA fee by kind of home** (rows 11 to 14, 35): houses about $525,000 with HOA fees mostly $150 a month or less; town center townhomes about $400,000 resale, about $580,000 new, with fees mostly $400 to $600 a month. Condos are rare (37), and the homes over the shops are rentals.
3. **The walk test** (row 24): only the town center's own streets are a short walk to the shops (about 8 minutes); most neighborhoods are a 20- to 35-minute walk.
4. **Flood by neighborhood** (row 15): 2 percent of homes in the high-risk zone, all in three named places.
5. **The three-area comparison on one deed file** (rows 29, 36), with the share of houses in a high-risk flood zone (Carolina Forest 0.2 percent, The Market Common 2.5 percent, Surfside Beach 31 percent).
6. **Our map**: the neighborhoods named on the map, the town center, the lake and parks, the runway and the 65 dB line.

The town center opened in 2008 on 121 acres; the apartments over its shops are rented by the year. Today Warbird Park on Farrow Parkway shows an F-100, an A-7D and an A-10, on loan from the Air Force. The old back gate is the Bellamy Interchange at US 17 and Farrow Parkway. ## Words and rules to watch (for the writer)

- No steering words: never "family-friendly", "safe", "perfect for retirees", "quiet", "best". The noise section states the line and the average; it never says "quiet".
- Explain once, in the same sentence: HOA (homeowners association, the group that runs the neighborhood and charges the monthly fee), DNL (yearly average noise), FEMA's high-risk flood zone (the zone where a lender requires flood insurance), townhome (a home that shares side walls and sits on its own small lot).
- "Short walk" only for the town center's streets (row 24). Drive times say "with no traffic".
- The HOA fee is "listed" (listing data), and we say what it is, not what it pays for.
- No sentence over 28 words; none starting with "So"; no "Here is"; no pseudo-cleft; no em dashes.
